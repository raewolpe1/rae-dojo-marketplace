# The Five Principles

## When to Use
- When the operational content of effectuation is needed.
- When designing curriculum, coaching, or support methodology.
- When applying the logic to a specific venture decision.

## Fails When
- **The conceptual foundation is missing.** Load `effectuation-logic.md` first — the principles without the non-predictive-control frame become a checklist.

## Core Concept

Five principles, each a device for reducing the prediction a decision requires.

### 1. Bird-in-hand — start with your means
Rather than starting with a goal and finding the means to achieve it, **start with what you have: who you are, what you know, and whom you know.** Then ask what effects you can create with that. Goals emerge from means and from stakeholder commitments; they are not given in advance.
*Removes:* the need to forecast which goal is attainable.
*Requires:* that there are means. See the limit in `affordable-loss-and-uncertainty.md`.

### 2. Affordable loss — invest only what you can afford to lose
Rather than calculating expected returns and selecting the highest, **determine what you can afford to lose and commit that.**
*Removes:* the need to forecast returns entirely. This is the principle that does the most work under uncertainty, because it requires no prediction at all.
*Note:* this is a risk-*limiting* principle. Entrepreneurs are not risk-takers.

### 3. Crazy quilt — build with self-selecting stakeholders
Rather than conducting competitive analysis, **build partnerships with whoever actually commits.** Each commitment brings resources and reshapes the goal. The venture is stitched from whoever joins.
*Removes:* the need to forecast the market. In a market that does not yet exist, competitive analysis has nothing to analyse; the commitments *are* the market.

### 4. Lemonade — leverage contingencies
Rather than avoiding surprises and adhering to plan, **treat contingencies as resources.** Under uncertainty the surprise is where the information is.
*Removes:* the need to forecast disruptions.

### 5. Pilot-in-the-plane — control rather than predict
**Focus on activities within your control**, on the belief that the future is not something to predict but something to create. Building something new requires collaborating with others to create the future you want to see.
*Removes:* the residual. This is the principle underneath the other four.

**The relationship between them.** They are not a sequence and not a checklist. They are five expressions of one commitment: to act in ways that make prediction unnecessary.

## How to Apply

1. **Means inventory.** Who are you, what do you know, whom do you know? Write it down. It is the starting position.
2. **Affordable loss statement.** What can be committed and lost without ruin? That figure is the budget, and it is not derived from a forecast.
3. **Find one commitment.** Not a segment, not a persona — a named person or organisation who has committed something real. Then let it reshape the goal.
4. **Log the surprises.** Not as variances. As information.
5. **List the controllables.** Act there. Stop forecasting the rest.

## Examples

**Situation:** A founder wants to start a food business and the programme has asked for a market analysis and three-year forecast.

**Application (effectual sequence):** Means — she cooks well, she knows the church network, her cousin has a bakkie. Affordable loss — R4,000 and her Saturdays; failure at that level is survivable. Crazy quilt — she offers Saturday catering to the church network; two people commit to orders; one offers a kitchen. The commitments have now reshaped the venture from "food business" to "weekend catering for community events", which nobody forecast. Lemonade — a customer asks for a funeral catering package she had not considered; that becomes the core line. Pilot-in-the-plane — none of this required knowing the size of the Western Cape catering market.

**Result:** A venture built from commitments rather than a plan built from invented numbers.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Teach the five principles as a checklist.
**Why:** They are five expressions of one logic. Detached from non-predictive control they become slogans.

**Don't:** Apply bird-in-hand where there are no means.
**Why:** The principle presupposes a starting position. Where there is none, the intervention is upstream.

**Don't:** Read affordable loss as risk-taking.
**Why:** It is the opposite. It is a hard limit set before commitment.
