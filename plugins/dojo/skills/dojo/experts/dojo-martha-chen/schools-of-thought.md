# The Four Schools of Thought

## When to Use
- Whenever a document explains, implicitly or explicitly, why informality exists.
- When an instrument is proposed and its rationale needs surfacing.
- As the diagnostic first move on any informal economy chapter.

## Fails When
- **The theory is already explicit and argued.** Then engage it directly rather than surfacing it.

## Core Concept

Four accounts of the informal economy dominate. Each captures part of a heterogeneous reality; **none is adequate on its own**. Every policy document adopts one implicitly, and the adoption determines the instrument before any evidence is consulted.

**Dualist.** The informal sector is a **residual** — a set of marginal activities largely unconnected to the formal economy, providing income and a safety net for the poor. Origins: the ILO/Hart tradition of the 1970s. *Policy instinct:* it will shrink as the modern sector grows; meanwhile, provide credit and services. *Fits:* almost nobody, in truth. *Persists because:* it is comfortable and requires no confrontation with any interest.

**Structuralist.** Informal units and workers are **subordinated to the interests of capitalist development**, supplying cheap goods and services and absorbing risk that formal firms have shed. The connection to the formal economy is not absent — it is the mechanism. Origins: Moser, Castells and Portes. *Policy instinct:* regulate the *relationship*, not the informal unit. *Fits:* industrial outworkers, subcontracted workers, casual labour in formal supply chains — precisely.

**Legalist.** Informal work is a **rational response to a hostile and burdensome regulatory environment**; simplify registration and grant property title, and the informal will formalise and prosper. Origins: de Soto. *Policy instinct:* red-tape reduction, one-stop shops, titling. *Fits:* some own-account operators with growth intent. *Over-applied:* almost universally.

**Voluntarist.** Informal operators **deliberately avoid regulation and taxation**, having weighed the costs and chosen. *Policy instinct:* enforcement. *Fits:* a small number of larger informal operators. *Wildly over-applied to the poor*, usually by treasuries.

**The holistic alternative.** The informal workforce is heterogeneous, and different segments are described accurately by different schools. A policy that adopts one school applies its instrument to the whole population and therefore fits only the segment its school describes. The correct approach is to segment first (see `informal-economy-segmentation.md`) and then apply the account that fits each segment.

## How to Apply

1. **Name the school the document has adopted.** It will be legalist unless it is voluntarist.
2. **Name what that school cannot see.** A legalist document cannot see the outworker's contractor. A voluntarist document cannot see that the worker is poor rather than evasive.
3. **Map the schools onto the segments.** Structuralist for outworkers and subcontracted labour. Legalist for growth-intent own-account operators. Voluntarist for the small number of larger evaders. Dualist for essentially nobody.
4. **Reject the single-school architecture.** The instrument set should be as plural as the population.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's informal economy chapter opens by attributing informality to compliance burden and proposes registration simplification.

**Application:** The chapter is legalist. That is de Soto's account and it fits some own-account operators with growth intent. It is silent on the structuralist segments — the outworkers and subcontracted workers whose constraint is a contractor, not a licence — and on the reality that for many segments the regulatory environment does not burden them, it does not recognise them. Recommendation: retain the legalist instruments for the segment they fit, name that segment, and add the instruments the other segments need.

**Result:** A plural instrument set matched to a plural population.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Adopt a school without naming it.
**Why:** It will determine the instrument before the evidence is consulted.

**Don't:** Apply the legalist account as a general theory.
**Why:** It fits one segment. It is applied to all of them.

**Don't:** Reach for voluntarism where the operators are poor.
**Why:** The account requires a genuine cost-benefit choice. Survival is not a choice in the relevant sense.
