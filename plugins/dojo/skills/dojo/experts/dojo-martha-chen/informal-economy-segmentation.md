# The Multi-Segmented Model

## When to Use
- **Whenever "the informal sector" appears as a single population.** This is the core correction.
- When designing instruments for micro or survivalist enterprise.
- When a percentage figure is being attached to "informal enterprises".

## Fails When
- **The document has already segmented properly.** Rare.

## Core Concept

The informal workforce is not a mass of micro-entrepreneurs. It is a structured set of **statuses in employment**, each with a different relationship to capital, to risk, and to a counterparty.

**The WIEGO model features six statuses in employment:**

1. **Informal employers** — they do employ others. These are genuinely small business owners and the only segment for which enterprise-development instruments are straightforwardly appropriate.
2. **Informal employees** — working for formal or informal enterprises without contracts, benefits, or protection. They are workers with an employer, misclassified or unrecognised.
3. **Own-account operators** — working alone or with family, no employees. The archetype of "the informal entrepreneur", and still a minority of the whole.
4. **Casual wage workers** — paid by the task or the day. They appear in statistics as self-employed because the payment structure disguises the employment relationship.
5. **Industrial outworkers / subcontracted workers** — including homeworkers. **They depend on employers or contractors for work orders, product specification, raw materials, and product sales.** Formally independent; substantively dependent. The structuralist account describes them exactly.
6. **Contributing family workers** — unpaid, working in a household enterprise. Frequently uncounted entirely; overwhelmingly women.

**The model has been tested.** In 2004 WIEGO commissioned data analysts to test it in six developing countries — **Costa Rica, Egypt, El Salvador, Ghana, India and South Africa** — by analysing national data (Chen et al. 2005). It is not a taxonomy invented at a desk.

**Why it is the whole argument.** Each segment has a different binding constraint and a different remedy. Own-account operators need working capital, space, and freedom from harassment. Outworkers need a floor price and recognition of the relationship the contract denies. Casual wage workers need restoration of the employment status they were stripped of. Contributing family workers need to be counted, and then to be paid. Informal employees need enforcement. Informal employers — and only informal employers — need what MSME policy usually offers.

**The enterprise-centric fallacy.** A strategy that calls all six "micro enterprises" and offers all six business training and finance has addressed segment 1 and possibly 3, and has misdescribed the other four in a way that makes their actual constraints invisible.

## How to Apply

1. **Disaggregate the headline figure.** A statement that 65% of enterprises are micro or survivalist should be decomposed by status in employment before any instrument is designed.
2. **Ask for each segment: who is the counterparty?** For segments 2, 4, 5 there is one, and it holds the power. For segments 1 and 3 the counterparty is the market and the state.
3. **Match instrument to status.** Enterprise development for informal employers. Labour rights and enforcement for informal employees and casual wage workers. Price floors, contract terms and recognition for outworkers. Recognition and remuneration for contributing family workers. Working capital, space, and protection for own-account operators.
4. **Check the gender composition of each segment.** Contributing family workers and homeworkers are overwhelmingly women. A segment-blind strategy is therefore also gender-blind.
5. **Use the South African data.** The model has been tested against South African national data. This is not a transfer problem.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy defines its "Groundbreakers" segment as micro, informal and survivalist enterprises and proposes a package of business training, market access, and micro-finance.

**Application:** The segment as defined contains at least four WIEGO statuses. The package addresses own-account operators and informal employers. It offers nothing to casual wage workers or outworkers, whose constraint is the counterparty, and it renders contributing family workers invisible by counting their household's enterprise rather than them. Recommendation: sub-segment Groundbreakers by status in employment; retain the enterprise package for the two statuses it fits; add a labour-relations and recognition track for the others; and disaggregate every indicator by status and gender.

**Result:** A segment that describes its population accurately and offers each part of it something that addresses its actual constraint.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Call all informal workers micro-entrepreneurs.
**Why:** Most are not. Outworkers depend on a contractor for orders, specifications, materials, and sales — for everything except the risk.

**Don't:** Design one package for the informal segment.
**Why:** Six statuses, six constraints. One package fits one of them.

**Don't:** Count enterprises and think you have counted workers.
**Why:** Contributing family workers disappear entirely in an enterprise count, and they are overwhelmingly women.
