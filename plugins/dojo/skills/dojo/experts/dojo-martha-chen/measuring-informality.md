# Measuring Informality

## When to Use
- When informality statistics from different sources disagree.
- When a share-of-employment or share-of-enterprises figure is being used.
- When designing the evidence base for an informal economy chapter.

## Fails When
- **The problem is econometric.** This file is about classification, which is usually where the error actually is.

## Core Concept

WIEGO's publication series includes **statistical profiles of informal employment and critical analysis of data collection and classification methods**, and that pairing is deliberate: the classification usually contains the error, and no amount of analytical sophistication downstream will recover from it.

**Three recurring errors.**

**1. Enterprises versus persons.** An enterprise count and a worker count are not two views of the same thing. One enterprise may contain several contributing family workers who appear in neither count. One worker may operate what the statistics record as no enterprise. Administrative registers count units; labour force surveys count people. **These are not comparable baselines and must never be presented as though they were.** Where both appear, the relationship between them must be explained, not assumed.

**2. Survey versus administrative.** A survey asks people what they do. An administrative register records what has been registered. The informal economy is, by construction, the part that is not in the register. So administrative data cannot measure it and survey data measures it with all the error that self-report carries. A figure derived from a register is a statement about registration, not about activity.

**3. Status misclassification.** Casual wage workers appear as self-employed because they are paid by the task. Industrial outworkers appear as own-account operators because they own their machine. Contributing family workers appear as nothing. **Each of these is a classification decision that manufactures the enterprise-centric picture the data then appears to support.**

**The scale.** Informal employment represents **more than half of non-agricultural employment in most developing regions**, and well over half of the global workforce works in the informal economy — the vast majority of the workforce in developing countries. Any measurement framework that treats it as a residual category has mis-specified the main case as the exception.

**The tested instrument.** The WIEGO multi-segmented model was tested against national data in six developing countries — Costa Rica, Egypt, El Salvador, Ghana, India and South Africa — precisely to establish whether national statistical systems could support the classification. That work is the starting point for anyone building an informality evidence base.

## How to Apply

1. **State the unit on every figure.** Persons or enterprises. Survey or administrative. Never let a figure travel without it.
2. **Refuse the comparison across units.** A labour force survey figure and a tax register figure describe different objects. Present both; explain the difference; do not net them.
3. **Disaggregate by status in employment.** The aggregate is the least informative number available.
4. **Disaggregate by sex.** The status composition differs sharply by gender and the aggregate conceals it.
5. **Interrogate the classification, not just the estimate.** Ask how casual wage workers, outworkers and contributing family workers were coded. That is where the error is.
6. **Treat a register-derived informality figure with suspicion.** It measures registration.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy presents a labour force survey figure for enterprise operators alongside an administrative figure for registered establishments, and the two are read together as a formality gap.

**Application:** These are not comparable baselines. The survey counts **persons** who report operating an enterprise; the register counts **sites or units** that have registered. One person may operate several units; one unit may have several operators; and the register by construction cannot see the population the strategy is about. The difference between the two numbers is not the informal economy — it is an artefact of two incompatible measurement frames. Present both, state the frame of each, and explain the incomparability explicitly rather than inviting the reader to subtract.

**Result:** A statistical section that does not manufacture a false quantity.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Subtract an administrative count from a survey count.
**Why:** Different units, different frames. The residual is an artefact.

**Don't:** Report an aggregate informality rate as the finding.
**Why:** The composition by status and by sex is the finding. The aggregate conceals it.

**Don't:** Accept the classification as given.
**Why:** Casual wage workers coded as self-employed, and outworkers coded as own-account, produce the entrepreneurial picture the data then appears to confirm.
