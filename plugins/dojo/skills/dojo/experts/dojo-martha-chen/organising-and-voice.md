# Organising and Representative Voice

## When to Use
- When designing consultation or governance for informal economy policy.
- When informal workers appear in a stakeholder list but not in a structure.
- When asking how any gain for informal workers has ever actually been achieved.

## Fails When
- **The question is ecosystem governance for growth firms.** Route to Stam.

## Core Concept

**Informal workers can make gains through organising.** This is not an aspiration — it is the historical mechanism. Every substantial improvement in the conditions of informal workers has come through their own organisations: SEWA in India, waste picker cooperatives in Latin America, street trader associations, domestic worker unions. Not through enlightened policy delivered to them.

**WIEGO's own construction encodes this.** It is a research–policy–action network that seeks to improve the status of the working poor — especially women — in the informal economy through **stronger organisations, better data and research, and fairer policies**. Three legs. The organisations come first, and the data and the policy are in service of them.

**The consultation/representation distinction.** Informal workers appear in almost every stakeholder engagement annexure. They appear in almost no governance structure. The difference is standing: a consultee is heard; a representative decides. A chapter written after consultation with traders is still a chapter written about traders. A chapter written with traders' organisations holding a seat is a different document, and it will get the siting, the tenure, the by-laws and the infrastructure right, because they know and the planner does not.

**Why this is not a procedural nicety.** The knowledge required to design informal economy policy — where the footfall is, what the enforcement actually looks like on a Tuesday morning, what the contractor pays, which by-law is used — is held by the workers and by nobody else. It is not in the survey data. A policy process that does not have that knowledge in the room will produce a technically competent document about a place that does not exist.

**The obligation.** Where organisations of informal workers exist, speaking for informal workers is both unnecessary and a substitution. Where they do not exist, supporting their formation is a legitimate and high-return policy objective in itself — cheaper than most programmes and more durable than all of them.

## How to Apply

1. **Find the organisations.** Trader associations, waste picker cooperatives, domestic worker unions, home-based worker networks. They exist.
2. **Give standing, not consultation.** A seat with a vote in the structure that decides. Not an annexure entry.
3. **Support formation where absent.** It is a legitimate objective, and it is the mechanism by which everything else becomes durable.
4. **Route the local knowledge questions to them.** Siting, enforcement, pricing, by-laws. They are the source.
5. **Check the annexure against the structure.** If the names appear in one and not the other, note it as the finding.
6. **Disaggregate voice by segment and sex.** Trader associations do not represent homeworkers; the loudest organisation is not everyone's.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy lists informal trader associations among its consulted stakeholders and proposes an implementation structure comprising provincial departments, agencies, municipalities, and organised business.

**Application:** Informal workers were consulted and are not represented. "Organised business" in South Africa does not include them, and the assumption that it does is a category error. The governance structure will therefore make siting, tenure, enforcement and infrastructure decisions without the only people who hold the relevant knowledge, and it will make them badly. Recommendation: seat informal worker organisations in the implementation structure with the same standing as organised business; where a segment has no organisation — homeworkers, typically — fund its formation as an initiative in its own right; and disaggregate the seats by segment, because a street trader association does not speak for a home-based worker.

**Result:** A governance structure that contains the knowledge required to make its decisions.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Confuse consultation with representation.
**Why:** A consultee is heard; a representative decides. Only the second changes the document.

**Don't:** Assume "organised business" includes informal workers.
**Why:** It does not, anywhere.

**Don't:** Speak for informal workers where their organisations exist.
**Why:** It is unnecessary, and it substitutes for the mechanism that actually produces gains.
