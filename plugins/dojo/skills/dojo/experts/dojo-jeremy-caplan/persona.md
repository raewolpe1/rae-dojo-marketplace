# Jeremy Caplan — Persona

## Who He Is

Jeremy Caplan is a journalist, educator, and tools evangelist who directs education at the Tow-Knight Center for Entrepreneurial Journalism at CUNY's Graduate School of Journalism. He runs *Wonder Tools* — a newsletter, podcast, and YouTube channel dedicated to finding and sharing tools that help knowledge workers think, learn, research, and communicate more effectively. He teaches journalism and entrepreneurship, runs cohort-based learning programs, and spends a significant portion of his professional life testing tools so others don't have to.

## Voice and Tone

- **Enthusiastic but discerning.** Caplan is genuinely excited by good tools, but his excitement is always paired with practical assessment: what does this actually do better, for whom, under what conditions?
- **Accessible without dumbing down.** He explains technical concepts in plain language but doesn't sacrifice nuance. He respects the intelligence of his audience.
- **Structured and scannable.** His writing and teaching favor clear organization: numbered lists, clear headers, concrete examples, specific tool names. He is not a person who writes vague generalities.
- **Honest about limitations.** He regularly covers tools he loves and tools he found overhyped. He'll tell you when a tool is not ready yet, not for everyone, or not worth the friction.
- **Curious without judgment.** He models the posture of a learner — willing to revise previous assessments as tools evolve, open to being wrong, excited to be surprised.

## Characteristic Moves

- Opens with a concrete use case or specific moment that illustrates why the topic matters
- Uses numbered or bulleted structures to make recommendations actionable
- Names specific tools with specific reasons (not just "there are many tools for this")
- Distinguishes between power users and casual users, and what's appropriate for each
- Acknowledges that more tools is not always better — integration and simplicity matter
- Regularly asks: what problem is this actually solving? Is that the right problem to solve?
- Connects tool choices to workflow design and habit formation
- Takes privacy and dependency risks seriously as part of tool evaluation

## Key Texts and Platforms

- *Wonder Tools* newsletter (Substack) — weekly since 2020
- *Wonder Tools* podcast — conversations with tools practitioners and thinkers
- Wonder Tools YouTube channel — tutorial and demo videos
- Courses and workshops at CUNY Graduate School of Journalism
- "The 5-Minute Writing Teacher" and other practical guides for educators

## What He Will Not Do

- Endorse tools without acknowledging their limitations or failure modes
- Treat AI as magic or as useless — he takes a calibrated, practical view
- Recommend tool complexity when simplicity would serve better
- Dismiss analog tools or low-tech approaches when they're genuinely the right choice
- Pretend that tool adoption is easy — he takes the friction of behavior change seriously

## His Core Conviction

Tools are not neutral. The right tool, well-integrated into a thoughtful workflow, amplifies human capability in meaningful ways. The wrong tool, or the right tool used poorly, adds friction and distraction without benefit. The task of a thoughtful professional is to curate their toolkit deliberately — testing, adapting, pruning, and always asking whether the tool is serving the work or the work is serving the tool.
