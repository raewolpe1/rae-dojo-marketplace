# Piketty Applied to Practice

## When to Use
- When the practitioner needs a structured method for applying Piketty's inequality analysis to a specific country or context.
- When designing inequality diagnostics, fiscal assessments, or long-term equality strategies.
- When translating the data-driven approach into operational practice.

## Core Concept
Six steps constitute a Piketty-informed inequality diagnostic:

First, data assembly. Gather data on income distribution (top 1%, top 10%, middle 40%, bottom 50% shares), wealth distribution (if available), the capital-income ratio, and effective tax rates by income level. The World Inequality Database (WID.world) is the starting point; national statistical offices, tax authorities, and household surveys supplement it.

Second, r > g assessment. Estimate the rate of return on capital and compare with the rate of economic growth. If r > g, wealth is concentrating automatically. The gap quantifies the concentration pressure.

Third, regime classification. Classify the inequality regime: proprietarian, social-democratic, hyper-capitalist, hybrid? What are the colonial legacies? What ideological justifications sustain the current arrangement?

Fourth, ideology identification. Name the ideology: meritocracy, trickle-down, proprietarianism, free-market fundamentalism? Assemble the data that challenges the ideology.

Fifth, fiscal assessment. Assess the fiscal system: how progressive are income taxes? Is capital income taxed equally with labour income? Do wealth and inheritance taxes exist? What is the effective tax rate by income decile? What is the tax-to-GDP ratio?

Sixth, strategy design. Propose the long-term equality strategy: progressive taxation, educational equalisation, wealth redistribution, democratic enterprise governance. Be specific about rates, thresholds, revenue, and institutional design.

## How to Apply
1. **Start with the data.** "Before any analysis, assemble the data. If the data does not exist, building the data infrastructure is the first intervention."
2. **Diagnose r > g.** "Is capital concentrating? At what rate? The r > g diagnostic is the first-order finding."
3. **Classify and identify.** "What inequality regime? What ideology sustains it? These classifications frame the strategy."
4. **Assess the fiscal system.** "Is the tax system genuinely progressive? Where are the gaps? What reforms would make it more progressive?"
5. **Design for twenty years.** "The equality strategy should be a generational project — not a programme cycle. Design for structural change over twenty years."
6. **Deploy the data politically.** "The data is not merely diagnostic — it is political. Publish it. Present it to the public. Use it to challenge the ideology that sustains the inequality."

## Examples
**Situation:** A development organisation is conducting a country-level inequality assessment to inform its programme strategy. The organisation wants to apply Piketty's framework.
**Application:** "Apply the six-step protocol. Step one, data: access WID.world for the country. Supplement with national household survey data and, if available, tax administration data. Assemble the key metrics: top 1% income share, top 10% income share, bottom 50% income share, Gini coefficient, capital-income ratio, effective tax rates by decile. Step two, r > g: estimate the average return on capital (from corporate profits, real estate returns, financial asset returns) and compare with GDP growth. If the return exceeds growth, wealth is concentrating. Step three, regime: classify the regime — likely a hybrid of post-colonial proprietarianism and hyper-capitalism, with colonial legacies in the property distribution. Step four, ideology: identify the dominant ideology — probably meritocratic ('growth benefits everyone') and proprietarian ('property rights must be respected'). Assemble the data that challenges these claims: the top 1% capture of growth, the colonial origins of current property distributions. Step five, fiscal: assess the tax system's progressivity. Most developing countries have regressive effective systems (despite nominally progressive rates) because capital income is undertaxed, wealth is untaxed, and consumption taxes (which are regressive) generate most revenue. Step six, strategy: propose progressive income tax reform, a wealth tax, inheritance taxation, and educational equalisation — with specific rates, thresholds, and revenue projections."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Reduce the protocol to data collection.
**Why:** Data without analysis, ideology identification, and strategy design is inert. The protocol integrates all six steps — each building on the previous.

**Don't:** Skip the ideology step.
**Why:** The ideology identification is essential because it reveals why the inequality persists despite the data. The data challenges the ideology — but only if the ideology is named.
