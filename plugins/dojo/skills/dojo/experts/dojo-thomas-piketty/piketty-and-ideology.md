# Piketty and Ideology

## When to Use
- When inequality is being justified by an ideology — meritocracy, proprietarianism, trickle-down, free-market fundamentalism — and the ideology needs to be named and challenged with data.
- When the relationship between ideology and institutional design needs to be articulated.
- When *Capital and Ideology* provides the framework.

## Core Concept
Piketty's central argument in *Capital and Ideology* is that every inequality regime is sustained by an ideology — a set of beliefs, narratives, and justifications that make the inequality appear natural, just, or necessary. Without the ideology, the inequality would be experienced as arbitrary and would be politically unstable.

The major ideologies of inequality include: proprietarianism (property rights are sacred and redistribution is theft); meritocracy (the rich deserve their wealth because they earned it through talent and effort); trickle-down (inequality benefits everyone because the rich invest and create jobs); and free-market fundamentalism (markets produce optimal outcomes when unregulated). Each ideology contains a kernel of truth (property rights serve useful functions; effort should be rewarded; investment does create some jobs; markets are powerful allocation mechanisms) but extends that kernel into a justification for levels of inequality that the kernel cannot support.

The data is the weapon against ideology. When the ideology says "the rich earned their wealth through merit," the data shows that the majority of top-percentile wealth is inherited or generated through capital returns, not through labour. When the ideology says "reducing taxes on the rich stimulates growth," the data shows no correlation between top tax rates and growth rates. When the ideology says "markets produce optimal outcomes," the data shows that unregulated markets produce extreme concentration and social instability.

## How to Apply
1. **Name the ideology.** "What ideology sustains the current inequality regime? Proprietarianism? Meritocracy? Trickle-down? Name it — because naming it reveals that the inequality is sustained by belief, not by nature."
2. **Deploy counter-data.** "For each ideological claim, present the data. Meritocracy? Show the proportion of top wealth that is inherited. Trickle-down? Show the correlation (or lack thereof) between top tax rates and growth. The data dismantles the ideology."
3. **Acknowledge the kernel.** "Every ideology contains a genuine insight. Property rights do serve useful functions. Effort should be rewarded. Markets are powerful. Acknowledge the kernel while rejecting the extension."
4. **Connect ideology to policy.** "The ideology shapes the policy. Meritocratic ideology produces policies that reward 'merit' (which is often inherited advantage). Proprietarian ideology produces policies that protect property (which is often concentrated through r > g). Change the ideology and the policy becomes changeable."

## Examples
**Situation:** A policy debate about inheritance taxation features the argument that inheritance tax is unfair because parents have a right to pass their wealth to their children — and that the wealth was "already taxed" when it was earned.
**Application:** "The argument deploys proprietarian ideology: property rights are sacred, and inheritance is a natural extension of property rights. The data challenge: a significant proportion of large inheritances represents capital gains that were never taxed (due to step-up in basis at death), accumulated through r > g (returns on capital exceeding growth), and originated in advantages (education, networks, opportunities) that were socially produced. The 'already taxed' claim is often factually false — and even where it is true, the question is not whether the original earner was taxed but whether dynastic wealth concentration is compatible with equality. The ideological claim — parents have a right to pass wealth to children — contains a genuine kernel (family solidarity, intergenerational care). But extending this kernel to justify unlimited inheritance produces dynasties of wealth that have nothing to do with the heir's merit, effort, or contribution. A progressive inheritance tax with a generous exemption (protecting family homes and moderate savings) and steep rates on large estates (preventing dynastic concentration) honours the kernel while rejecting the extension."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use "ideology" as a dismissive label.
**Why:** Ideologies are not merely lies — they are belief systems that contain genuine insights alongside their justificatory function. Effective critique engages the genuine insights rather than dismissing the entire framework.

**Don't:** Present ideology critique as sufficient.
**Why:** Naming the ideology is necessary but insufficient. The ideology sustains institutional arrangements — and those arrangements must be changed through policy, not merely through intellectual critique.
