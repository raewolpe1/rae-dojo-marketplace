# Dose and Population — The Guardrail

## When to Use
- **Load this whenever Bloom's evidence is being applied to a real programme.** It is the guardrail.
- When "training", "consulting", "BDS" and "capability support" are being used interchangeably.
- When management findings are being applied to micro-enterprises.

## Fails When
- **The population really is large multi-plant firms receiving months of intensive consulting.** Then the evidence applies directly and this file is unnecessary.

## Core Concept

**Bloom's headline results attach to a specific dose delivered to a specific population, and both are far from where enterprise policy usually wants to apply them.**

| | Bloom's India experiment | A typical provincial BDS programme |
|---|---|---|
| **Dose** | Months of free intensive consulting from an international firm: diagnostic, implementation support, follow-up | 3–5 day accredited course, possibly some mentoring |
| **Population** | Large **multi-plant** textile firms | Micro and small enterprises, often survivalist |
| **Structure present** | Plant managers, middle management, shop floor, measurable monitoring/targets/incentives | Owner-operator, no management layer |
| **Result** | **+17% productivity, year one** | ~**1 additional practice in 20 taught**; no significant profit effect |

**The dose curve is steeply non-linear, and both ends have been measured — by the same people.** David McKenzie is a co-author of *"Does Management Matter?"*. He then spent a decade establishing what the cheap end does. **Both findings are correct.** The mistake is treating them as contradictory, or citing one to fund the other.

**The population point is the harder one.** The World Management Survey instrument scores **monitoring, targets and incentives**. Ask what those mean in an own-account enterprise. Monitoring whom? Targets set by whom for whom? Incentives paid to whom? **There is no structure for the instrument to measure**, which means there is also nothing for the intervention to act on. The India firms improved because they had plant managers who could be taught to collect data and middle managers to whom decisions could then be delegated — the paper reports increased decentralisation as one of the three main effects. A survivalist has nobody to decentralise to.

**Where Bloom's evidence legitimately applies in a provincial context.** Firms with roughly 50–500 employees, a management layer, and a plant or operation that can be scored. That population exists in the Western Cape, it has never been scored, and scoring it is cheap. **That** is the defensible use of this dojo.

**What to say when the citation is being misused.** Not "the evidence is mixed". Say: *this result is about a different dose delivered to a different population, and the evidence on your dose and your population exists and says something else.* Then route to McKenzie.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask the dose.** Hours, days, or months? Content delivery or implementation support? Individual or group?
2. **Ask the population.** Employees? Management layer? Plants? If the answer is owner-operator, the instrument does not apply.
3. **Match citation to proposal.** If they do not match, say so specifically — not vaguely.
4. **Route the cheap end to McKenzie.** He co-authored the India paper and measured the other end. This is corroboration, not conflict.
5. **Find the population that fits.** Mid-sized manufacturers. Score them. That is the real opportunity.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's BDS pillar cites Bloom to support accredited training for 15,000 micro-enterprises.

**Application:** State it plainly. The cited result comes from months of free intensive consulting delivered to large multi-plant textile firms and it measured monitoring, targets and incentives — a structure that does not exist in an own-account enterprise. The evidence on the proposed dose and population is McKenzie's, and it says roughly one practice in twenty and no significant profit effect. The citation is not merely weak support; it is support for a different programme. **What the strategy could legitimately take from Bloom** is the mid-sized manufacturer opportunity: score them, find the tail, and design an intensive intervention for a few dozen firms with a real management layer.

**Result:** The citation is corrected, and a defensible programme is found inside the same evidence.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat Bloom and McKenzie as disagreeing.
**Why:** McKenzie co-authored Bloom's India paper. They measured different points on a non-linear dose curve. Both are right.

**Don't:** Apply the WMS instrument to micro-enterprises.
**Why:** It scores monitoring, targets and incentives. There is no one to monitor, target or incentivise.

**Don't:** Say "the evidence is mixed" when a citation is misapplied.
**Why:** It isn't mixed. It is precise, and it is about something else. Say which.
