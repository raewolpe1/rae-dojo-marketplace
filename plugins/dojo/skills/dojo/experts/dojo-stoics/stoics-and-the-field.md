# Stoics and the Field

## When to Use
- When positioning Stoicism relative to contemporary psychology, therapy, and philosophy.
- When engaging critiques of Stoicism.
- When someone asks whether Stoicism is still relevant.

## Core Concept
Stoicism occupies a peculiar position in intellectual history: a 2,300-year-old philosophy that is more popular now than at any time since antiquity, partly because its core mechanism — the idea that changing your judgments changes your experience — was independently validated by cognitive behavioural therapy. The relationship between Stoicism and the modern world is one of influence, parallel development, and legitimate critique.

**Stoicism and CBT.** Aaron Beck and Albert Ellis both acknowledged Stoic influence on the development of cognitive behavioural therapy. Ellis cited Epictetus directly: "It is not things that disturb us, but our judgments about things" is the foundational insight of both Stoicism and CBT. The mechanism is the same: identify the automatic thought (judgment), test whether it is accurate, revise it if it is not, and observe the emotional consequence of the revision. The difference is that CBT is a clinical protocol with empirical validation, while Stoicism is a comprehensive philosophical system that includes ethics, physics, and logic alongside its psychological techniques. CBT borrowed the technique; Stoicism provides the larger framework of meaning.

**Stoicism and modern Stoicism.** The contemporary Stoic movement — Stoic Week, the Modern Stoicism organisation, popular authors like Ryan Holiday and Massimo Pigliucci — has brought Stoic practice to millions. The movement is genuine and valuable. It also tends to emphasise the self-help dimension (resilience, emotional regulation, productivity) at the expense of the ethical and communal dimensions (virtue, justice, cosmopolis). The ancient Stoics would recognise the techniques but might be puzzled by the individualism.

**Critiques of Stoicism.** Several critiques deserve engagement. First, the suppression charge: Stoicism counsels emotional suppression that produces psychological damage. The Stoic response: the philosophy distinguishes pathē from eupatheiai and aims to replace distorted emotions with accurate ones, not to eliminate feeling. But the critique has bite — popular Stoicism often does slide into suppression, and the distinction is easily lost. Second, the privilege charge: Stoicism works well for people with agency and resources but is inadequate for people facing systemic oppression. The Stoic response: Epictetus was enslaved and developed the philosophy under the most extreme constraint. But the critique has bite — telling oppressed people to focus on what they can control risks legitimising the structures that constrain them. Third, the quietism charge: acceptance of fate produces political passivity. The Stoic response: the three major Roman Stoics were among the most politically active people of their era. But the critique has bite — the contemporary emphasis on individual equanimity can obscure the Stoic commitment to justice.

**Productive pairings.** Stoicism pairs well with frameworks that address its blind spots. With existentialism (Yalom) for the meaning-making dimension the Stoics handle through cosmology that modern practitioners may not share. With IFS (Schwartz) for the internal multiplicity the Stoics did not develop — the Stoic model of mind is unified where IFS sees parts. With mindfulness (Kabat-Zinn) for the present-moment attention practices that complement Stoic judgment-examination. With Macnamara for the empirical question of whether Stoic-influenced interventions actually produce the effects they claim.

## How to Apply
1. **Acknowledge the CBT connection.** "Stoicism and CBT share a mechanism. CBT has empirical validation. Stoicism has a comprehensive ethical framework. They are complementary, not competing."
2. **Engage critiques honestly.** "The suppression, privilege, and quietism critiques each have genuine force. A Stoicism that does not grapple with them is not serious philosophy — it is comfort literature."
3. **Distinguish ancient from popular.** "Modern popular Stoicism is often a subset of ancient Stoicism — the self-help parts without the ethics and community. Read the ancients, not just the interpreters."
4. **Use Stoicism alongside, not instead of.** "Stoicism is not a complete system for modern life. It is a powerful framework that works best when combined with other perspectives — therapeutic, political, empirical."

## Examples
**Situation:** A colleague says: "Stoicism is just rich people telling poor people to accept their lot."
**Application:** The Stoics would respond: "That critique deserves engagement, not dismissal. It is true that Seneca was wealthy and Marcus was emperor — their Stoicism was practised from positions of power. It is also true that Epictetus was enslaved — his Stoicism was forged in the most constrained conditions imaginable. The philosophy does not reduce to 'accept your lot.' It says: pursue justice (cardinal virtue), fight injustice where you can (duty), and cultivate inner freedom regardless of outcome (practice). The critique has force when Stoicism is used to tell oppressed people not to resist. It loses force when Stoicism is used as the ancients intended: act justly, resist injustice, and maintain equanimity about the outcome of your resistance. The acceptance is of results, not of conditions. But the critique is a useful corrective — it reminds us that Stoic practice must include the justice dimension, not just the equanimity dimension."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Claim Stoicism is a complete system.
**Why:** It does not address structural power, unconscious processes, or collective action in the ways modern frameworks do. Presenting it as sufficient for all problems produces shallow application.

**Don't:** Dismiss modern critiques as misunderstandings.
**Why:** Some are misunderstandings. Some are genuine limitations of the framework. Treating all criticism as ignorance is itself a failure of Stoic wisdom — the wise person learns from criticism.
