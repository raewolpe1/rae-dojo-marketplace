# Climate and Development

## When to Use

- When climate and development are being treated as separate policy agendas.
- When development planning must integrate climate adaptation and mitigation.
- When the argument for inseparability needs articulating.

## Fails When

- **The user needs specific climate science.** This file addresses the development-climate nexus, not climate science.
- **The user needs specific adaptation or mitigation technologies.** This provides the strategic framing, not technical solutions.

## Core Concept

Sachs's later work centres on the inseparability of climate and development. The argument is twofold. First, climate change is a development emergency: the populations most vulnerable to climate impacts — droughts, floods, sea-level rise, disease vector shifts — are the poorest, who have the least capacity to adapt. Climate change is not a future threat to the poor; it is a present reality that worsens poverty today. Second, development pathways that ignore climate are self-defeating: investments in agriculture, infrastructure, and energy that don't account for climate constraints will fail as conditions change.

This means that every development strategy must be climate-smart. Agricultural development must include drought-resistant varieties, water harvesting, and soil conservation. Infrastructure must be climate-resilient — built to withstand changing rainfall patterns, higher temperatures, and extreme weather events. Energy systems must leapfrog fossil fuels and go directly to renewables, which are now often cheaper and more suitable for decentralised deployment in rural areas.

Sachs also argues that the rich countries' responsibility is double: they caused the climate crisis through historical emissions, and they have the resources to help poor countries adapt and develop along low-carbon pathways. Climate finance — separate from and additional to development aid — is a moral obligation, not charity.

The SDG framework, which Sachs helped architect, integrates climate (SDG 13) with all other development goals precisely because Sachs sees these as inseparable. You cannot achieve SDG 1 (no poverty) or SDG 2 (zero hunger) without addressing SDG 13 (climate action), and vice versa.

For Southern Africa, the climate-development nexus is particularly acute. The region is among the most climate-vulnerable globally: increasing drought frequency, changing rainfall patterns, rising temperatures that reduce agricultural productivity, and water scarcity that constrains all economic activity.

## How to Apply

1. **Climate-proof every development investment.** Ask: will this investment still work under projected climate scenarios? If not, redesign it.
2. **Lead with climate-smart agriculture.** For rural poverty reduction in Southern Africa, drought-resistant crops, conservation agriculture, water harvesting, and improved irrigation are not optional enhancements but core components.
3. **Leapfrog to renewables.** Don't replicate the fossil fuel pathway. Solar and wind are now cheaper for many applications and better suited to decentralised rural deployment.
4. **Make the climate finance case.** Developing countries' adaptation needs should be funded by climate finance, separate from development budgets.

## Examples

**Situation:** A Limpopo agricultural development programme promotes irrigation expansion using surface water from a river whose flow is declining due to reduced rainfall.
**Application:** This programme is not climate-smart. Investing in irrigation dependent on a declining water source is building on a disappearing foundation. The clinical diagnosis: the binding constraint is not just water access but water access under a changing climate. The prescription: shift to water-efficient irrigation (drip systems, deficit irrigation), invest in water harvesting and storage, promote drought-resistant crop varieties, and diversify livelihoods away from water-dependent agriculture in the most vulnerable areas.
**Result:** Redesign the programme around climate projections, not historical rainfall patterns.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat climate as a separate "environmental" issue in development planning.
**Why:** Climate affects every development sector — health, agriculture, infrastructure, energy, water. Siloing it in an environmental chapter means the development strategy isn't climate-resilient.

**Don't:** Use climate vulnerability as a reason for defeatism.
**Why:** Climate adaptation is achievable. The technologies exist. The question is whether the investments are made at sufficient scale.
