# Entrepreneurial Finance in Emerging Markets

## When to Use
- When a strategy has an undifferentiated "access to finance" pillar.
- When venture capital, growth equity, or fund-of-funds instruments are proposed.
- When credit guarantees or blended finance are being designed.

## Fails When
- **The subject is household or consumer credit** — different literature, different constraints.

## Core Concept

Access to capital is a genuine barrier to transformational entrepreneurship. It is also the most over-generalised finding in the field, because "access to finance" means two unrelated things for the two populations.

**For subsistence enterprise**, the constraint is working capital in small amounts at high frequency. The return on that capital can be high but the enterprise does not scale, so the finance is a livelihood smoothing instrument. Microfinance addresses this and does it moderately well. It does not produce growth firms, and financing more of it produces more survival enterprise, not more employment.

**For transformational enterprise**, the constraint is patient risk capital in amounts large enough to fund a step change — equipment, premises, a first management hire, working capital for a contract that is three times current turnover. This is the finance gap that matters for jobs, and it is the one the system is worst at filling, because it sits above microfinance and below what commercial banks will lend unsecured.

**On venture capital specifically.** VC is an instrument that presupposes its market: a deal flow of scalable ventures, a syndication network, valuation comparables, and — decisively — **an exit market**. Schoar's work on limited partners and fund performance shows that even in deep markets, returns are concentrated and access to the good funds is the scarce resource. Transplanting the vehicle into a thin market without the supporting institutions produces funds that cannot deploy, cannot exit, and cannot raise a second vintage. The vehicle is not the constraint; the market is.

**On family firms and business groups.** In many emerging markets, the firms that do scale do so inside family structures and business groups. Ownership and governance structure shape performance materially. A finance strategy that assumes an arm's-length equity market ignores the ownership form that actually dominates.

## How to Apply

1. **Split the finance pillar in two.** Working capital for livelihoods; growth capital for employment. Different instruments, different rationales, different indicators.
2. **Name the missing tier.** Usually the gap is R500k–R10m unsecured or quasi-equity — too big for micro, too small or too unsecured for banks.
3. **Before proposing a fund, establish the exit path.** Trade sale to whom? Listing where? Management buy-out financed by what? No exit, no fund.
4. **Ask whether the constraint is demand or supply.** Bankable transformational firms may be scarce because regulation and talent constrain them, not because capital is absent. Adding capital to a demand-constrained market does nothing.
5. **Design for the ownership form that exists**, including family firms, rather than the one the instrument assumes.

## Examples

**Situation:** A province proposes a venture fund to close the "equity gap" for high-growth SMEs.

**Application:** Ask three questions before anything else. What is the historical exit record in this market — how many trade sales or listings of firms in this size band in the last decade? What is the annual deal flow of ventures that could absorb the cheque size and return the fund? Who is the second-vintage investor? If the answers are thin, the instrument is wrong even if the diagnosis (equity gap) is right. Mezzanine, revenue-based finance, or a guarantee that changes bank behaviour may fit the actual market; a VC fund will not.

**Result:** A finance instrument matched to the market's institutional depth rather than imported from one with a functioning exit market.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat "access to finance" as one pillar.
**Why:** It is two constraints on two populations with almost nothing in common.

**Don't:** Propose a VC fund without an exit analysis.
**Why:** The vehicle presupposes the market. Without exits it is a grant scheme with worse governance.

**Don't:** Assume more capital means more growth firms.
**Why:** If the binding constraint is regulation or talent, capital is not binding and adding it changes nothing.
