# Cohen and Development

## When to Use
- When the development industry's own internal inequalities — compensation, power, knowledge hierarchies — need to be confronted.
- When applying Cohen's egalitarian principles to the structure and practice of development work.
- When asking the uncomfortable question that Cohen asks of all egalitarians: does your practice match your principles?

## Core Concept
Cohen's egalitarianism applies to the development industry with devastating force. The development industry is staffed by professionals who earn salaries that place them in the global top decile of income, who work on behalf of populations in the bottom decile. The industry reproduces the very inequality it claims to address: international staff paid multiples of national staff, headquarters consuming resources meant for field operations, knowledge production controlled by metropolitan institutions while local knowledge is marginalised.

The camping trip test applies: if the development industry were a group of friends working on a shared project (reducing poverty), would the friends from wealthy countries demand five times the compensation of the friends from poor countries? Would the friends who designed the project claim authority over the friends who implemented it? Would the friends who wrote the report take credit while the friends who did the work remained unnamed?

Cohen's analysis reveals three forms of inequality within the development industry. Compensation inequality: international staff paid market rates that reproduce the global wage hierarchy. Knowledge inequality: metropolitan institutions producing and controlling the knowledge that governs development practice. Power inequality: donors, headquarters, and international staff controlling decisions that affect national staff and communities.

## How to Apply
1. **Apply the camping trip test.** "Would this compensation structure, this power hierarchy, this knowledge arrangement be acceptable among friends working on a shared project? If not, why is it acceptable in a development organisation?"
2. **Interrogate the incentive arguments.** "When the organisation justifies salary differentials by 'market rates,' apply Cohen's challenge: the talented person's demand for premium pay is a choice, not a natural fact. The organisation should challenge this choice, not capitulate to it."
3. **Assess the ethos.** "Does the organisation operate with a community ethos (mutual concern, shared sacrifice, non-exploitation) or with a market ethos (self-interest, competition, extraction of advantage)? The ethos determines whether the organisation's egalitarian mission is genuine."
4. **Connect internal to external inequality.** "An organisation that reproduces inequality internally — between staff levels, between national and international, between headquarters and field — is unlikely to challenge inequality externally. The internal structure shapes the external practice."

## Examples
**Situation:** A development organisation's strategic plan commits to "reducing inequality and promoting inclusive development." Meanwhile, the CEO's compensation is 20 times the median national staff salary, the headquarters budget absorbs 40% of total expenditure, and all senior technical positions are filled by international staff.
**Application:** "The strategic plan commits to reducing inequality. The organisational structure reproduces it. Cohen's question: if you're an egalitarian organisation, how come your CEO earns 20 times your median national staff? The incentive argument will be deployed: the CEO's experience and networks justify the premium. But the incentive argument, interrogated, reveals that the CEO's premium is produced by their bargaining position in the global labour market — not by a genuine 20:1 differential in contribution. The headquarters budget absorbs 40% — which means that for every dollar donated for poverty reduction, 40 cents funds the metropolitan infrastructure of the organisation. The international monopoly on senior positions reproduces the colonial knowledge hierarchy: metropolitan expertise governing local practice. The organisation's strategic plan is sincere. Its structure contradicts it. Cohen would say: reform the structure or stop claiming to be egalitarian. The personal-choice question applies to organisations as well as to individuals."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use the critique to paralyse.
**Why:** The development industry, despite its contradictions, produces genuine good. The critique should reform, not paralyse. The goal is a more egalitarian development industry, not the abolition of development.

**Don't:** Reduce the analysis to personal guilt.
**Why:** The structural inequalities of the development industry are structural — produced by the system, not by individual moral failure. The remedy is structural reform, not personal confession.
