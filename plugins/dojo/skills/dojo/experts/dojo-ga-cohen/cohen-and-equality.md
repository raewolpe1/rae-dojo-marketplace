# Cohen and Equality

## When to Use
- When equality of opportunity, procedural equality, or equality before the law is being presented as sufficient — as if achieving these forms of equality constitutes justice.
- When arguing that genuine equality requires equality of condition, not merely equality of formal treatment.
- When maintaining the philosophical demand for radical equality against the temptation to settle for less.

## Core Concept
Cohen distinguishes between partial equalities (which improve on inequality but fall short of justice) and radical equality (which justice demands). Equality of opportunity gives everyone the same starting line but does not address the prior inequalities (family wealth, social capital, genetic endowment) that determine who can run fastest. Equality before the law treats everyone the same in court but does not address the structural conditions that determine who ends up in court. Progressive taxation redistributes some wealth but accepts the inequality it partially corrects.

Cohen's radical equality is equality of condition: a society in which the conditions of each person's life are substantively equal — in resources, in opportunities, in well-being, in the social conditions that make a good life possible. This is demanding. Cohen acknowledges it may not be fully achievable. But he insists that the philosophical demand must be maintained: partial equality is better than inequality, but it is not justice. The egalitarian must never be satisfied with partial equality while claiming to have achieved justice.

## How to Apply
1. **Name the partial equality.** "This programme achieves equality of access — everyone can apply. But does it achieve equality of condition? Can the poorest applicant and the wealthiest applicant genuinely compete? If not, equality of access launders inequality through a meritocratic process."
2. **Trace prior inequalities.** "Equality of opportunity assumes a level playing field. Ask: is the field level? What prior inequalities — in wealth, in education, in social capital, in cultural capital — shape who can seize the opportunity?"
3. **Maintain the demand.** "Accept partial equality as an improvement while insisting it is not justice. The philosophical demand for genuine equality must be maintained even when practice falls short."
4. **Design for equalisation, not just equal treatment.** "Equal treatment of unequals produces unequal outcomes. Genuine equality may require unequal treatment — more resources, more support, more attention for those who start from a position of greater disadvantage."

## Examples
**Situation:** A scholarship programme provides equal access to all applicants from disadvantaged backgrounds. The selection process is merit-based — the best applicants receive scholarships. The programme reports it achieves equality of opportunity.
**Application:** "The programme achieves formal equality of opportunity — all disadvantaged applicants can apply. But the merit-based selection rewards the cultural capital that some disadvantaged applicants possess and others do not: familiarity with application processes, academic literacy, institutional confidence, and prior educational quality. The 'most meritorious' applicants from disadvantaged backgrounds are typically those whose disadvantage is least severe — those who attended better schools, had more educated parents, or possessed cultural capital that the less disadvantaged applicants lack. The programme has provided equality of access while reproducing inequality through the selection mechanism. Genuine equality would require: equalising the conditions of competition (support for application, remedial education, mentoring), adjusting the criteria of selection (recognising forms of merit the standard criteria miss), and ultimately questioning whether merit-based selection is the appropriate mechanism for achieving equality."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Dismiss partial equalities.
**Why:** Equality of opportunity is better than no equality. Progressive taxation is better than flat taxation. Partial equalities are improvements — they are just not justice.

**Don't:** Treat equality as sameness.
**Why:** Equal concern may require unequal treatment — giving more to those who need more. Equality is about equal concern for every person's well-being, not about identical treatment.
