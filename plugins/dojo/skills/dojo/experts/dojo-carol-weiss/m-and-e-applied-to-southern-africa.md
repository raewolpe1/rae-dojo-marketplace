# M&E Applied to Southern Africa

## When to Use
- When applying Weiss's evaluation concepts to South African or broader Southern African contexts.
- When analysing the strengths and weaknesses of South Africa's government-wide monitoring and evaluation system.
- When designing theory-based evaluations for programmes operating in the Southern African development context.

## Fails When
- **You apply Weiss generically.** The value of this file lies in connecting Weiss's specific concepts — programme theory, evaluation use, political context, enlightenment — to the specific dynamics of Southern African evaluation practice.
- **You ignore the institutional landscape.** South Africa has one of the most developed government M&E systems in the developing world. Weiss's analysis should engage this reality, not bypass it.

## Core Concept
South Africa's monitoring and evaluation landscape offers a particularly instructive case for applying Weiss's frameworks. Since 2009, the Department of Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (DPME) has built a government-wide evaluation system (GWES) that has commissioned over 80 national evaluations across government departments. The system includes a National Evaluation Policy Framework, a national evaluation plan, and standards for evaluation practice. This institutional infrastructure is unusual among developing countries and provides a significant base for evaluation influence.

Weiss's frameworks illuminate both the strengths and limitations of this system. The GWES has made evaluation a routine part of government practice — evaluation is no longer exceptional but expected. However, applying Weiss's analysis of evaluation use reveals important dynamics.

**On programme theory:** Many GWES evaluations have adopted theory of change as an evaluation framework, but the quality of theoretical specification varies enormously. Some evaluations articulate genuine causal theories with testable mechanisms; others produce elaborate diagrams that describe programme logic without specifying how change occurs. Weiss would observe that the institutional requirement for theories of change has produced compliance without consistently producing the analytical rigour that theory-based evaluation requires.

**On evaluation use:** The GWES includes improvement plans that require government departments to respond to evaluation findings. This mechanism creates a formal pathway for instrumental use. But Weiss's analysis predicts — and South African experience confirms — that the political dynamics of government frequently override the instrumental pathway. Departments may formally accept evaluation recommendations while implementing them partially or not at all, especially when findings challenge politically important programmes. The more consequential pathway of influence, consistent with Weiss's framework, is conceptual use: evaluations are changing how senior officials think about programme design, even when specific recommendations are not implemented.

**On the political context:** Evaluation in South Africa operates within a political environment that includes party-political dynamics, centre-provincial tensions, and the institutional politics of government departments protecting their programmes and budgets. Weiss's analysis of evaluation in the political context applies with particular force: the decision to evaluate certain programmes and not others, the choice of evaluation questions, and the handling of findings are all shaped by political considerations.

**On enlightenment:** South Africa's evaluation system has been operating long enough for enlightenment effects to be observable. The cumulative body of government evaluations has shifted the discourse within government about what constitutes evidence, what questions evaluations should answer, and what standards of rigour are expected. This is enlightenment in action — not the use of specific findings for specific decisions, but the gradual reshaping of the evaluation culture within government.

## How to Apply
1. **Use the GWES as both asset and context.** The existence of a government-wide evaluation system creates opportunities (institutional demand for evaluation, established processes, growing capacity) and constraints (bureaucratic requirements, political dynamics, compliance pressure).
2. **Push for genuine theory-based evaluation.** Within the GWES framework, advocate for evaluations that specify mechanisms and test causal chains, not just evaluations that include theory of change diagrams.
3. **Design for multiple use pathways.** Given the political constraints on instrumental use, design evaluations with an eye to conceptual use and enlightenment — produce findings that change how people think about problems, not just whether specific programmes continue.
4. **Engage provincial and local dynamics.** Much programme implementation occurs at provincial and local levels where evaluation capacity, political dynamics, and institutional contexts differ significantly from the national level.
5. **Account for the capacity context.** Evaluation capacity in Southern Africa is unevenly distributed. Applying Weiss's frameworks requires adapting to contexts where evaluation infrastructure, skills, and institutional support vary significantly.

## Examples
**Situation:** An evaluator is commissioned to evaluate a provincial SMME development programme in the Free State. The programme has been running for five years with limited evidence of impact, but it has strong political support.
**Application:** Weiss's framework suggests beginning with the programme theory: what causal mechanisms does the programme assume connect SMME support activities to business growth and employment? In the Free State context, the programme assumes that mentoring, access to finance, and business development services create viable enterprises. A theory-based evaluation would test each link: did mentoring produce improved business practices (mechanism: knowledge and network transfer)? Did access to finance produce investment (mechanism: capital constraint alleviation)? Did improved practices and investment produce growth (mechanism: market competitiveness)? Given the political support for the programme, Weiss's analysis of the political context predicts that negative findings will face resistance. The evaluator should design for multiple use pathways: the immediate improvement plan (instrumental use), the conceptual contribution to how the province thinks about SMME development (conceptual use), and the cumulative body of evidence about what works in SMME support in secondary cities and towns (enlightenment).

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Apply Weiss's American-context findings about evaluation use without adaptation.
**Why:** The institutional, political, and capacity contexts differ significantly. South Africa's government-wide evaluation system creates dynamics that did not exist in the American federal evaluation context Weiss studied. The frameworks travel; the specific findings about use patterns require contextual adaptation.

**Don't:** Critique the South African M&E system as if theory-based evaluation were easy to implement.
**Why:** Building a government-wide evaluation system is an enormous institutional achievement. The gap between current practice and Weiss's ideal of theory-based evaluation is real, but closing it requires capacity building, institutional change, and sustained investment — not just methodological critique.
