# Entrepreneurial Ecosystems as a Distinct Cluster Type

## When to Use
- Whenever "ecosystem" and "cluster" are being used interchangeably.
- As the foundation file for any Autio answer.
- When distinguishing Autio's approach from Stam's.

## Fails When
- **The user needs a measurable element framework and an index.** That is Stam's contribution, not Autio's. Route accordingly.

## Core Concept

**The starting complaint:** entrepreneurial ecosystems command increasing attention from policy makers, academics and practitioners, **yet the phenomenon itself remains under-theorized.** Specifically, the conceptual similarities and differences of entrepreneurial ecosystems relative to **clusters, "knowledge clusters," regional systems of innovation, and "innovative milieus" remain unclear.**

That is not a general call for rigour. It is a specific charge: economic geography has been describing co-located, interacting, mutually reinforcing firm populations for forty years under at least four names, each with substantial theory. A fifth term arrived, policy adopted it enthusiastically, and nobody established what it picked out that the other four did not.

**The resolution.** Drawing on research on industrial districts and agglomerations, clusters, and systems of innovation, entrepreneurial ecosystems **differ from traditional clusters** in four respects:

1. **By their emphasis on the exploitation of digital affordances.**
2. **By their organisation around entrepreneurial opportunity discovery and pursuit** — rather than around an industry or a technology.
3. **By their emphasis on business model innovation** — rather than technology innovation.
4. **By facilitating generic business knowledge** — they are the only cluster type that does; the others trade in industry or technology knowledge.

**The summary formulation:** an entrepreneurial ecosystem is **a unique type of cluster: one that specialises in harnessing digital affordances created by digitalisation, mixing these with the more conventional spatial affordances created by proximity.**

**Note what this concedes and what it claims.** It concedes that an EE *is* a cluster — it does not float free of economic geography. It claims that it is a **distinct type**, with a different organising principle, a different knowledge commodity, and a different innovation locus. That is a stronger and more testable claim than "ecosystems are important".

**The four-question test.** Digital affordance? Spatial affordance? Organised around opportunity rather than industry? Generic knowledge circulating? Four yeses: an entrepreneurial ecosystem. Otherwise: a cluster, a knowledge cluster, an innovation system, or a milieu — all legitimate objects with well-developed policy literatures that should be used rather than reinvented.

## How to Apply

1. **Run the four-question test** before accepting the ecosystem label.
2. **Name the alternative if it fails.** Cluster, knowledge cluster, innovation system, milieu. Each has a policy literature.
3. **Do not treat "cluster" as a demotion.** Much of a regional economy is clusters and cluster policy is mature.
4. **Use Autio for the identity question and Stam for the quality question.** Different jobs.
5. **Watch for the fifth-noun problem.** If the term is doing no work the other four could not, it is decoration.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy has an "entrepreneurial ecosystem" chapter covering agri-processing, tourism, oil and gas services, and tech.

**Application:** Run the test on each. Agri-processing: co-located firms sharing industry knowledge and a value chain — that is a **cluster**, and cluster policy applies. Oil and gas services: same, plus a supplier development problem — cluster. Tech: probably a genuine entrepreneurial ecosystem — digital affordances, opportunity-organised, generic knowledge circulating. **The chapter is therefore three cluster strategies and one ecosystem strategy filed under one heading**, and because the heading is "ecosystem", the three clusters are getting ecosystem instruments that do not fit and the one ecosystem is getting sector treatment that structurally cannot deliver generic knowledge.

**Result:** Four objects correctly named, each with the policy literature that fits it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use "ecosystem" for any co-located firm population.
**Why:** Co-location is a cluster. The EE needs digital affordance and generic knowledge.

**Don't:** Treat the cluster verdict as a failure.
**Why:** Cluster policy is well developed. Being a cluster and using cluster policy beats being called an ecosystem and using nothing coherent.

**Don't:** Blend Autio and Stam.
**Why:** Same object, different theories, different jobs. Autio: is it one? Stam: how good is it?
