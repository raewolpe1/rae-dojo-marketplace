# Digital Affordances and Spatial Affordances

## When to Use
- When digitalisation and place are both in play.
- When a digital economy or digitalisation initiative is proposed.
- When someone claims geography no longer matters, or that it entirely does.

## Fails When
- **The question is broadband/infrastructure provision as such.** That is a framework condition — route to Stam.

## Core Concept

**An affordance is what an environment makes possible for an actor.** The concept does the work here because it avoids both determinism and irrelevance: digitalisation does not *cause* entrepreneurship, and it does not leave the geography of entrepreneurship unchanged. It changes **what is possible**.

**Digital affordances** — created by digitalisation. What becomes possible: reach without proximity; distribution at near-zero marginal cost; modular recombination of components built by strangers; radical collapse of the minimum scale required to enter a market; instant access to global generic knowledge; and testing a business model against real customers before building anything.

**Spatial affordances** — created by proximity. The conventional ones, and they have not gone away: tacit knowledge exchange that does not survive transmission; trust built by repeated face-to-face contact; reputation that circulates through a community; serendipitous recombination — the encounter you did not plan; and access to specialised local labour.

**The claim: the entrepreneurial ecosystem is the mixture.** It is a unique type of cluster, one that **specialises in harnessing digital affordances created by digitalisation, mixing these with the more conventional spatial affordances created by proximity.**

**Why the mixture is the whole point, and not a hedge.**

- **Digital affordances alone give you a market, not an ecosystem.** Anyone anywhere can use the same tools. There is no local anything, and no reason for policy to have a geography.
- **Spatial affordances alone give you an industrial district.** A cluster in the classical sense. Well understood since Marshall, and cluster policy applies.
- **The two together give you the entrepreneurial ecosystem** — a place where the digital affordance collapses the cost of trying, and the spatial affordance supplies the trust, tacit knowledge and serendipity that make trying informed rather than random.

**The diagnostic that follows.** Ask of any proposed ecosystem: **is proximity doing anything the internet could not do?** If not, there is no spatial affordance, and the "ecosystem" is a set of individuals using the same global tools who happen to share a postcode. That is not a policy object with a local instrument.

**And the reverse:** is digitalisation doing anything here? If not, this is a cluster, and the ecosystem framing adds nothing.

**The implication for a place with a spatial economy problem.** Digital affordances are the one thing that can reach a location the physical economy has bypassed — reach without proximity is precisely the affordance that does not care where you are. But the spatial affordance still has to come from somewhere, which is why "give them connectivity and devices" produces users rather than ecosystems. **The connectivity is necessary and it is not the intervention.** The intervention is whatever supplies trust, tacit exchange and serendipity locally — and that is a physical, social, and expensive thing.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what each affordance is contributing.** Both, or you do not have an ecosystem.
2. **Test the proximity question.** Is co-location doing anything the network could not? If not, no spatial affordance.
3. **Do not read digitalisation as the abolition of geography.** The mixture is the object.
4. **Beware connectivity-as-strategy.** It supplies the digital affordance and none of the spatial one.
5. **In a bypassed location, the digital affordance is the opportunity and the spatial affordance is the missing input.** Design for the second.

## Examples

**Situation:** A township digital economy initiative proposes connectivity, devices and digital skills training.

**Application:** That supplies the digital affordance — genuinely valuable, and the affordance most likely to reach a location the physical economy has bypassed, because reach without proximity does not care where you are. But an entrepreneurial ecosystem is the *mixture*, and nothing in the initiative supplies the spatial affordance: trust, tacit exchange, reputation circulation, serendipitous encounter. Without it, the output is people using global tools individually — a market, not an ecosystem, and one in which they compete with everyone else on earth using the same tools with better capital. What would supply the spatial affordance: a physical place where they are repeatedly in the same room; peers at different stages; someone who has done it before; and enough recurrence for reputation to mean something. That is unglamorous, physical and comparatively expensive, and it is the actual intervention.

**Result:** A digital initiative that builds an ecosystem rather than a user base.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat connectivity as an ecosystem strategy.
**Why:** It supplies one of two affordances. The other is the local, expensive, social one.

**Don't:** Claim digitalisation makes place irrelevant.
**Why:** The EE is the mixture. Remove proximity and you have a market.

**Don't:** Assume co-location supplies the spatial affordance automatically.
**Why:** Proximity affords trust, tacit exchange and serendipity only where there is recurrence. A shared postcode is not recurrence.
