# Measuring Entrepreneurship — Indices as Theory Made Arithmetic

## When to Use
- **When GEM, TEA, GEDI or GEI figures are cited, relied on, or contested.**
- When survey-based and register-based counts disagree.
- When an index is being used to justify or attack a strategy.

## Fails When
- **The question is general survey methodology.** This is about what indices assume.

## Core Concept

**An index is a theory made arithmetic.** It weights what its framework holds to matter. That is not a flaw to be apologised for — it is what an index *is*. But it means an index must never be presented as a neutral fact, because the moment a reader opens the methodology, an argument you did not need to have is lost.

Autio speaks here with unusual standing: he **co-founded the GEDI Institute and co-authored the Global Entrepreneurship and Development Index**, and has a long association with the **Global Entrepreneurship Monitor**. He built the instruments. He knows what they assume.

**What GEM is, precisely.** A **survey instrument built on a framework**. It measures what its framework holds entrepreneurship to be — principally early-stage activity, attitudes, aspirations, and perceived framework conditions — gathered through **population surveys of individuals** and **expert panels**. It is **not a census of firms** and does not claim to be.

**What TEA is, precisely.** A rate of **individuals engaged in early-stage entrepreneurial activity**. A specific construct with a specific definition. **Most disputes about GEM numbers are disputes about the construct, not about the fieldwork** — and are therefore resolved definitionally, not statistically.

**The incomparability that generates most contests.** A GEM figure counts **persons, by survey**. A tax or business register counts **units, administratively**. When they disagree, both are right: they are measuring different objects. Someone objecting that a GEM figure is implausible against a register is **correct that they differ and wrong that this is an error.** The correct response is definitional: state the construct, the unit, the instrument, the sample and the year.

**The deeper point for a national systems of entrepreneurship index.** In the work with Acs and Szerb, the measurement issues *are* the policy issues: an index's weighting decides which countries look good, which pillar looks binding, and therefore what gets funded. An index is not a thermometer. It is an argument with a number attached — which is fine, provided it is presented as one.

**The defensive posture, which is stronger than defence.** Do not defend an index as objective. Present it as what it is: a theory-laden instrument, transparent about its theory, measuring a defined construct. That posture is very hard to attack, because it concedes in advance the only thing an attacker has.

**And the offensive corollary.** When an index is cited *against* you, ask what it assumes. When it is cited *for* you, ask the same question first — before someone else does.

## How to Apply

1. **State the construct, unit, instrument, sample and year** with every figure. Every time.
2. **Never net a survey figure against a register figure.** Different objects; the residual is an artefact.
3. **Present the index as theory-laden and transparent**, not as objective. It is the defensible posture.
4. **When contested, answer definitionally.** Most attacks are construct disputes wearing statistical clothing.
5. **Interrogate the weighting** of any composite index before relying on it.
6. **Treat a draft as a draft.** An unpublished index figure has no standing, however convenient.

## Examples

**Situation:** The GEM-derived figures in a draft strategy are being challenged by a reviewer who notes they do not reconcile with administrative enterprise counts.

**Application:** The reviewer is right that they differ and wrong that one is therefore wrong. GEM's TEA is a rate of **individuals** engaged in early-stage entrepreneurial activity, from a **population survey**, on a defined construct. An enterprise register counts **registered units** administratively, and by construction cannot see the informal activity GEM's survey does see. They are measuring different objects and the difference is not an error to be reconciled — it is a definitional fact to be stated. Response: report both, state the construct and unit of each, explain the incomparability explicitly in a footnote, and decline to derive any quantity from the difference between them. Then, separately: if the index is still in draft, say it is a draft, and do not let a convenient number acquire standing it has not earned.

**Result:** A statistical section that survives review because it does not overclaim.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Defend an index as objective.
**Why:** It is a theory made arithmetic. Concede that first and the attack has nowhere to go.

**Don't:** Reconcile a survey rate with a register count.
**Why:** Persons versus units, survey versus administrative. The difference is definitional, not an error.

**Don't:** Let a draft index figure acquire standing.
**Why:** Draft means draft. Convenience is not publication.
