# EE Policy Is Not Cluster Policy and Is Not SME Policy

## When to Use
- **When auditing the instruments in an ecosystem strategy.** This is the payload file.
- When asking what ecosystem policy should actually contain.
- When the strategy's instruments look familiar.

## Fails When
- **The object genuinely is a cluster or an established SME population.** Then cluster policy and SME policy are correct, mature, and should be used.

## Core Concept

**The explicit policy conclusion:** policymakers need to adopt **novel approaches to stimulate entrepreneurial ecosystems that differ from those in place to develop industrial clusters or support already-established small- and medium-sized companies.**

Read that twice, because it names the two things nearly every enterprise strategy actually contains, and says neither will do.

**The three policy families, and what each assumes:**

| Policy family | Object | Assumes | Typical instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cluster policy** | Co-located firms in an industry | The industry exists; firms are in it; the problem is linkage, upgrading, shared inputs | Supplier development, shared facilities, sector associations, value chain programmes, skills for the industry |
| **SME policy** | Established small and medium firms | The firms exist and are trading; the problem is growth constraints | BDS, access to finance, market access, compliance support, procurement set-asides |
| **EE policy** | Opportunity pursuit across sectors | The firms may not exist yet; the problem is the rate and quality of new configurations | ? |

**Why the first two do not substitute.** Cluster policy assumes an industry and organises by it — which structurally cannot circulate generic knowledge (see `generic-vs-industry-knowledge.md`). SME policy assumes an existing trading firm with a growth constraint — which is not what an ecosystem is producing; it produces attempts, most of which fail, and the ones that work do so by configuring something new. **Both policies presuppose the thing the ecosystem is trying to bring into existence.**

**What EE policy contains instead, derived from the four distinguishing features:**

- **From digital affordances:** lower the cost of trying. Access to tools, components, platforms and cloud; and the connectivity that makes them usable.
- **From spatial affordances:** create recurrence. Physical venues where the same people meet repeatedly, so that trust, reputation and tacit exchange can form. This is the expensive, unglamorous part and it is the part that is always missing.
- **From opportunity organisation:** convene by stage across sectors, not by sector across stages.
- **From business model innovation:** fund runway to iterate, not R&D to invent; and measure configurations, not patents.
- **From generic knowledge:** get experienced operators into the room, regardless of their industry, with standing rather than as guest speakers.

**The uncomfortable observation.** EE policy is cheap in capital and expensive in attention. It is mostly convening, venue, recurrence and people — none of which produces a ribbon-cutting, all of which produces the thing. Which is a large part of why strategies default to the two policies that do produce ribbon-cuttings and then name the result an ecosystem.

**The honest caveat.** "Novel approaches" is a conclusion about what is *needed*, not a demonstration that any specific approach *works*. Autio establishes that cluster and SME instruments do not fit the object. He does not supply an evidence base that the alternatives deliver. For that, route to McKenzie — and expect thin returns, because almost none of this has been tested.

## How to Apply

1. **Classify every instrument.** Cluster, SME, or EE? Tabulate it. The table is the finding.
2. **Expect the answer to be cluster and SME.** It nearly always is.
3. **Keep them — and rename them.** They are good policies for their objects. The problem is the label, not the instrument.
4. **Add the EE instruments deliberately:** recurrence, cross-sector stage-based convening, operators with standing, runway finance.
5. **Say that the EE instruments are untested.** Autio establishes the need, not the efficacy. Attach an evaluation.

## Examples

**Situation:** An ecosystem strategy's instruments are: incubators, supplier development, BDS, access to finance for small firms, sector value chain support, and a business plan competition.

**Application:** Tabulate. Supplier development and sector value chain support: **cluster policy**. BDS, access to finance, and most incubation: **SME policy**. The business plan competition is the only instrument in the list that is arguably EE — it selects on opportunity across sectors — and note that it is also the one with the strongest impact evidence (route to McKenzie). So the strategy contains six instruments, five of which are the two policy families that Autio's finding says will not stimulate an ecosystem, and one that might. Recommendation: keep all six, relabel five as what they are, and build out the missing EE layer — recurrence, cross-sector convening by stage, operators with standing — while being explicit that this layer is theoretically motivated and empirically untested, and evaluating it.

**Result:** An honest instrument table and a real ecosystem layer.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Call cluster policy ecosystem policy.
**Why:** It presupposes an industry and circulates industry knowledge. Different object, different commodity.

**Don't:** Call SME policy ecosystem policy.
**Why:** It presupposes an existing trading firm. The ecosystem is trying to produce those.

**Don't:** Claim the EE instruments are evidence-based.
**Why:** Autio establishes what the object requires. Nobody has shown the requirements can be met by policy. Say so and test.
