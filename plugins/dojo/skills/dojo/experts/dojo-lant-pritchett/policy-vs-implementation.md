# Policy vs Implementation

## When to Use
- When the discussion treats policy quality as the central variable.
- When evaluating why reform produces less impact than expected.
- When designing development interventions.

## Fails When
- **Policy quality is genuinely the binding constraint** — in some contexts it is.

## Core Concept

The development discourse over-attends to policy design and under-attends to implementation. Policy quality matters, but in many developing-country contexts the implementation gap is the larger constraint. Two countries can adopt the same policy and produce dramatically different outcomes because of differences in implementation capability.

The empirical observation: developed and developing countries often adopt similar policies; the developed countries implement; the developing countries struggle to. The discourse's attention to policy design produces sophisticated policies in environments lacking the capability to operationalise them.

Several factors produce the imbalance:

**Donors fund policy.** Donor cycles, evaluation timeframes, and reporting requirements favour visible policy adoption over slow implementation building.

**Consultants design policy.** The consulting industry's business model favours policy advice over implementation embedding. Policy advice is portable; implementation building is local and long.

**Politicians announce policy.** Policy adoption is the unit of political achievement; implementation is the unit of governance achievement. The political incentives favour announcement.

**Implementation is invisible.** Policy design is highly visible (white papers, legislation, plans); implementation work is invisible (training, organisational routine, problem-solving over years). Visibility shapes funding and attention.

**Implementation is hard to measure.** Policy adoption is observable; implementation quality is harder. What is hard to measure is harder to manage.

The corrective requires:

**Diagnose the constraint honestly.** Is the binding constraint policy quality or implementation capability? In most middle-income developing countries, the latter is larger.

**Fund implementation.** Sustained investment in implementation capability rather than reform after reform.

**Build implementation indicators.** Measure what actually happens at the point of delivery, not just what policies have been adopted.

**Sequence sophistication to capability.** Don't demand sophisticated implementation from organisations without basic capability.

**Use PDIA** for implementation building.

For South Africa: a country with strong policy design tradition and substantial implementation gap. Many sophisticated policies (NDP, IPAPs, NHI, JET, master plans) coexist with substantial implementation failure. Reform that adds more policies without addressing implementation will not change the underlying picture.

## How to Apply

1. **Diagnose policy-implementation balance** explicitly.
2. **Identify the binding implementation constraint** in the specific area.
3. **Invest in implementation capability** rather than more policy reform.
4. **Match policy sophistication to implementation capability.**

## Examples

**Situation:** South Africa's National Development Plan (NDP) was adopted in 2012. Implementation has been substantially below the plan's ambitions.

**Application:** The plan is intellectually substantial. The implementation gap reflects the binding constraint. Additional policy reform — revised plans, additional master plans, sector strategies — does not address it. Implementation capability building (state institutions delivery capability, intergovernmental coordination, accountability) is the binding constraint.

**Result:** Attention reoriented from planning to implementation building.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use the framework to dismiss policy work.
**Why:** Policy design has its place. The argument is about balance, not against policy work.

**Don't:** Treat implementation as merely administrative.
**Why:** Implementation is the substantive work. Its complexity is comparable to policy design's complexity.
