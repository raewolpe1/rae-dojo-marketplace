# The State as Organisation

## When to Use
- When state reform is being treated at macro level.
- When the analytical unit is "the state" rather than specific organisations.
- When designing capability-building interventions.

## Fails When
- **The question is genuinely macro-political** — constitutional design, regime type, political economy. State-as-organisation is operational.

## Core Concept

States are organisations — collections of specific entities (ministries, agencies, SOEs, regulatory bodies, municipalities) with specific mandates, structures, capabilities, and cultures. State reform happens at the organisational level. The macro framing ("strengthen the state") cannot be operationalised without organisational specifics.

The implications:

**Diagnose at organisational level.** Not "the South African state is weak" but "the Department of Water and Sanitation has these specific capability gaps in these specific functions."

**Build capability at organisational level.** PDIA applies to specific organisations solving specific problems, not to the state as a whole.

**Reform sequencing happens at organisational level.** Different organisations have different capabilities, different load-bearing demands, and different priorities for capability building.

**Capability variation across organisations is substantial.** Within the same state, some organisations may be highly capable (often: central bank, treasury technical functions, certain regulatory bodies) while others struggle. The variation is informative — what enables capability in the capable organisations?

**Organisational culture, recruitment, and incentives** shape capability as much as structure or budget. The behavioural elements often matter more than the formal architecture.

Diagnostic questions at organisational level:

What problems is this organisation supposed to solve?

What is its actual capability to solve these problems?

What is the gap between mandate and capability?

What organisational features (talent, culture, processes, leadership) produce or constrain capability?

What is the immediate next step to build capability?

The state-as-organisation framing pushes diagnosis and reform from the macro to the operational, from the political to the organisational. It is consistent with the broader state-capability and PDIA frameworks but emphasises the organisational unit.

## How to Apply

1. **Specify the organisation** in question — not the state in general.
2. **Diagnose capability at organisational level** — specific functions, specific gaps.
3. **Apply PDIA at organisational level.**
4. **Compare across organisations** within the same state to identify enabling features.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African state reform debates often proceed at macro level.

**Application:** Reframe at organisational level. SARS (revenue) capability declined and recovered, with specific organisational causes and corrections. Eskom (electricity) capability collapsed, with specific organisational causes. Treasury (fiscal) has retained capability through specific features. Health department capability varies sharply across provinces. The state is not one capability story; it is many organisational stories.

**Result:** Reform engagement at the operational level where it can be effective.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat the state as monolithic.
**Why:** It is heterogeneous. Engagement requires organisational specificity.

**Don't:** Avoid macro-political questions entirely.
**Why:** Macro factors shape organisational capability. The organisational lens complements rather than replaces the macro lens.
