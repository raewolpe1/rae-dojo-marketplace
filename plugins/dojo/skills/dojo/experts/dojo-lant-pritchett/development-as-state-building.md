# Development as State-Building

## When to Use
- When development is being framed as something that can be achieved through policy reform alone.
- When the time horizon of development is being underestimated.
- When state capability is being treated as a side issue.

## Fails When
- **The framing is being used to defer all action** indefinitely. State-building should happen alongside other development work.

## Core Concept

Development is the multi-decade building of state capability alongside economic transformation. It is not a set of policies that can be installed; it is not a state of macroeconomic stability that can be achieved; it is not a level of institutional sophistication that can be imported. It is a sustained organisational and political process over decades, in which state capability builds alongside economic and social transformation.

The empirical record. Today's developed countries all underwent multi-decade state-building processes — Britain over the 18th and 19th centuries; the United States over the 19th and early 20th; Germany, France, Japan over comparable periods. East Asian developmental states built capability over 30-50 years alongside economic transformation. China has built substantial state capability over 45 years of reform. The state-building element is central in every case.

The framework's implications:

**Development is slow.** Real state capability takes decades to build. Programmes designed for 5-year horizons cannot deliver development outcomes; they can deliver specific interventions within the longer process.

**Development requires political continuity.** Multi-decade state-building requires political coalitions that can sustain the project across administrations. Frequent political turnover that resets the state-building work prevents the multi-decade accumulation.

**Development is locally specific.** Each country's state-building is shaped by its specific history, political economy, capability base, and challenges. Universal templates do not work.

**Development requires resources.** Sustained state-building requires fiscal resources, talent, organisational investment. Cheap state-building doesn't exist.

**Development requires political consensus.** Across democracies, multi-decade projects require enough cross-party agreement to survive electoral changes. Highly polarised politics make state-building difficult.

**Development is not driven primarily by aid.** Aid can support specific interventions but cannot drive state-building, which is fundamentally an internal political-organisational process.

The framework reframes the question of "what should we do for development?" The question is not "what policies should we adopt" but "how do we build the state that can adopt and implement good policies sustainably over decades?"

For South Africa: the post-1994 democratic state-building project has produced both substantial achievements and substantial difficulties. State capability rebuilding after the state-capture decade is a multi-decade project requiring political continuity, sustained investment, and integration with the broader economic and social transformation. The orthodox "complete the reforms" framing misses this.

## How to Apply

1. **Frame development as multi-decade state-building.**
2. **Design for the long horizon** — sustained investment, political continuity, organisational learning.
3. **Resist short-term framings** that underestimate the project's scale.
4. **Build political coalition** for the multi-decade work.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African policy debates often have short horizons — election cycles, ministerial terms, donor programme cycles.

**Application:** The development project is multi-decade. Specific instruments within it can have shorter horizons, but the strategic framing must hold the long view. Cross-party consensus on core elements; institutional protection of strategic continuity; sustained investment commitments.

**Result:** A development project framing that matches the actual time horizon required.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use the framework to oppose specific reforms.
**Why:** Specific reforms within the multi-decade project are appropriate.

**Don't:** Imagine state-building is just slow reform.
**Why:** It is qualitatively different — the building of organisational capability over time, not the rapid installation of forms.
