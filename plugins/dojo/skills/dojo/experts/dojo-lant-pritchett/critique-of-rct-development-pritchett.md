# Critique of RCT Development (Pritchett Version)

## When to Use
- When RCT-based development economics is being positioned as the dominant methodology.
- When micro-empirical findings are being asked to answer structural questions.
- When state-capability and structural-transformation questions are being neglected.

## Fails When
- **The question genuinely fits RCT methodology.** Some questions do.

## Core Concept

The Pritchett critique of RCT-based development economics shares ground with Chang and Deaton but has a distinctive emphasis. Pritchett's critique is centred on the structural-transformation and state-capability questions that RCTs cannot address.

The argument:

**RCTs answer specific micro-intervention questions well.** The methodology produces credible internal validity on questions like the effect of a specific cash transfer design or the impact of a specific health programme. Within scope, the work is valuable.

**The questions RCTs cannot address are the central questions of development.** What industrial policy a country should pursue. How state capability is built. What growth strategy works. How institutions develop. These are structural questions that RCT methodology cannot address — they are not amenable to random assignment at the relevant scale.

**The discipline's attention has shifted toward what RCTs can answer**, away from what they cannot. This shift has narrowed development economics. The structural-transformation literature (industrial policy, growth diagnostics, capability development, state-building) has been displaced in graduate training, top journals, and policy attention by the micro-empirical literature.

**The shift has reduced the discipline's policy relevance.** The big questions developing-country policymakers face — what growth strategy to pursue, how to build state capability, how to address structural unemployment — are not the questions the RCT literature addresses. The discipline's contribution to these questions has weakened.

**The political economy of the shift is convenient for the development industry.** Donors can fund and report on specific micro-interventions with measurable impact; the structural transformation work is harder to fund and measure. Academic careers are easier to build on RCT publications than on structural-transformation work. The incentives align toward the micro work.

**The state-capability angle.** Pritchett's specific focus: RCTs largely take state capability as given. They evaluate interventions delivered through whatever capability exists; they do not engage with how to build capability. The most consequential development question — how does a country build the state that delivers all the interventions — is outside the RCT toolkit.

The implications:

**Methodological pluralism.** RCTs for the questions they can answer; historical, institutional, structural methods for the questions they cannot.

**Restore attention to structural questions.** Industrial policy, growth diagnostics, state capability, institutional development — these need their share of disciplinary attention.

**Evaluate development advice by question relevance**, not by methodological status. The right question matters more than the most rigorous method.

For South Africa specifically: structural questions dominate. The RCT literature contributes valuable findings on specific interventions (cash transfers, health programmes, education interventions) but does not answer the central strategic questions about industrial transformation, state capability rebuilding, or growth strategy.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the question's scale and type.** Micro-intervention or structural?
2. **Match methodology to question.** RCT for micro; institutional/structural for macro.
3. **Resist methodological imperialism** in either direction.
4. **Restore structural questions** to development discourse.

## Examples

**Situation:** A donor-funded development strategy for South Africa centres on micro-intervention impact evaluation.

**Application:** The strategy will produce findings on specific programmes but will not address the structural questions (industrial transformation, state capability, growth strategy) that are South Africa's binding development challenges. Reframe: use RCT findings for the specific programme design where appropriate; use structural and institutional analysis for the strategic questions; integrate.

**Result:** A balanced methodological approach matched to the question landscape.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Dismiss all RCT work.
**Why:** Within scope it is valuable.

**Don't:** Treat RCT methodology as the gold standard.
**Why:** Different questions need different methods. The gold standard for a question is the method best suited to answering it.
