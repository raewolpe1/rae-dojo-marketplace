# Multiple Pathways of Development

## When to Use
- When debating which development model a country should follow.
- When responding to claims that there is a universal sequence to development.
- When designing country-specific development strategy.

## Fails When
- **Some patterns are genuinely universal** — basic state functions, predictable legal framework, macroeconomic stability are largely universal preconditions.

## Core Concept

Different countries have reached development through different sequences and architectures. The orthodox model — open trade, stable macroeconomics, institutional reform, FDI attraction, gradual catch-up — is one pathway. Other successful pathways include:

**The East Asian developmental state pathway** (Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Singapore). Strong state coordination, selective protection, performance-conditioned industrial policy, capability building over decades. Substantially different from the orthodox model.

**The China reform pathway.** Gradual reform under continuing state ownership and direction; SEZs as protected experimentation; sequenced opening; substantial industrial policy. Different from both East Asian developmental states and the orthodox model.

**Continental European postwar pathway.** Mixed economy with substantial state direction, codetermination, sector-level bargaining, social market structures. Different from the Anglo-Saxon orthodox model.

**Nordic pathway.** Strong social democracy, universal welfare, high taxation, active labour market policy, strong unions, broad-based prosperity. Different again.

**The American pathway.** Substantial industrial policy through procurement and basic research, weak labour institutions, financialised capitalism, regional and racial inequality. The pathway diverges from the orthodox "American model" rhetoric that the US itself promotes.

The implications:

**Country-specific pathway choice** matters more than adoption of universal templates.

**Pathways differ in their feasibility for different countries** — political-economic conditions, capability bases, demographic patterns shape which pathways are viable.

**Pathway elements can be combined.** Countries can take aspects of multiple pathways — East Asian industrial policy, Nordic social institutions, Continental codetermination — adapted to their conditions.

**The orthodox model is one pathway, not the destination.** Treating it as universal both misrepresents the historical record and constrains policy thinking.

For South Africa: the pathway choice has been substantially contested. Aspects of developmental state, social democracy, and orthodox liberalisation have all been pursued. The integration has been incomplete; the result has been an incoherent mixed model. A more deliberate pathway choice — informed by capability constraints, political economy, and historical-comparative analysis — would produce a more coherent strategy.

## How to Apply

1. **Resist the universal-pathway framing.**
2. **Examine multiple historical and contemporary pathways** for relevant lessons.
3. **Design a country-specific pathway** integrating relevant elements.
4. **Be transparent about pathway choice** as political-economic, not technical.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African development strategy debates often default to orthodox-model framing.

**Application:** Open the pathway question. The East Asian developmental state model has elements that match South African conditions (industrial policy, capability building, manufacturing transformation) but requires state capability rebuilding. The Nordic social-democratic model has elements that match (broad social protection, education quality, labour market institutions) but requires fiscal capacity. The Continental codetermination model has elements that match (worker voice, sector bargaining) and could complement transformation. A South African pathway integrates relevant elements across these models, adapted to the country's specific conditions.

**Result:** A pathway choice that is deliberate and integrates the best available lessons.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use the framework to defer pathway choice indefinitely.
**Why:** Eventually a pathway has to be pursued. The framework supports informed choice, not avoidance.

**Don't:** Treat every pathway as equally feasible for every country.
**Why:** Country-specific conditions constrain feasibility. Pathway choice is real.
