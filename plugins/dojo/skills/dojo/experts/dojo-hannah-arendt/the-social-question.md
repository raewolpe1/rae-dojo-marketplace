# The Social Question

## When to Use
- When examining whether the administration of material needs — food, health, shelter, income — has consumed or displaced political deliberation in a development context.
- When the tension between urgency (people need food now) and politics (people need voice in their future) becomes acute.
- When development institutions treat governance as a technical problem of service delivery rather than a political challenge of collective self-determination.

## Core Concept
In *On Revolution* (1963), Arendt argues that the French Revolution failed because it was overwhelmed by the social question — the sheer urgency of poverty. When the masses irrupted into the revolutionary public realm, they brought with them the imperatives of biological necessity: hunger, suffering, the need for bread. These imperatives are absolute — they cannot wait for deliberation — and when they enter the political realm, they consume it. Politics becomes the administration of necessity rather than the exercise of freedom. The American Revolution, by contrast, succeeded (in Arendt's reading) because it was not overwhelmed by mass poverty and could focus on the creation of political institutions.

The analysis is controversial and has been justly criticised for its blindness to American slavery and its idealisation of the founding. But the structural insight remains powerful: when politics is reduced to the management of material needs, something essential about political life — the deliberation of equals about their shared future — is lost. The social question does not destroy politics through malice but through urgency. When people are starving, the demand for bread overrides the capacity for deliberation.

For development practice, this is perhaps Arendt's most uncomfortable insight. Development is overwhelmingly concerned with the social question — with poverty, health, education, food security, economic growth. This concern is legitimate and urgent. But Arendt would ask: has the social question consumed development's capacity for politics? Have development institutions become so focused on delivering services and measuring outcomes that they have lost sight of the political dimension — the question of who decides, who governs, who has voice? The risk is that development becomes the most sophisticated system ever devised for administering necessity — and the most thorough system for preventing the political life that would allow people to determine their own futures.

## How to Apply
1. **Name the tension honestly.** "People need food now. People also need voice in their future. These are not the same need, and addressing one does not address the other. The programme that delivers services without creating political space has addressed the social question while ignoring the political question."
2. **Watch for the administration takeover.** "When every conversation is about delivery — targets, logistics, supply chains, disbursement rates — and no conversation is about politics — who decides, whose voice is heard, what future is being built — the social question has consumed the political."
3. **Refuse the false binary.** "The choice is not between feeding people and engaging them politically. It is about whether the way you feed people also creates or destroys the conditions for political life. A food programme can be designed as a space for deliberation — or as a delivery pipeline."
4. **Protect the political within the social.** "In every programme that addresses material needs, ask: where is the political space? Where do participants deliberate about their shared conditions, not as beneficiaries selecting from a menu, but as citizens shaping their community?"

## Examples
**Situation:** A national social protection programme provides unconditional cash transfers to the poorest 20% of households. The programme is administratively efficient — it uses digital payments, biometric identification, and regular recertification. It has measurably reduced extreme poverty. But communities report that the programme has changed local political dynamics: traditional community solidarity mechanisms have weakened, local governance structures have been bypassed by the national programme, and participants see themselves as recipients of the state rather than agents of their community.
**Application:** "The programme has brilliantly addressed the social question — it has reduced poverty through efficient administration. But it has consumed the political relationships that previously structured community life. The traditional solidarity mechanisms — whatever their limitations — were political: they involved deliberation, obligation, and mutual accountability within the community. The national programme replaces these with a bureaucratic relationship between the individual and the state: you qualify, you receive, you are recertified. No deliberation, no mutual obligation, no political engagement. The participants are better fed and worse positioned politically. They are recipients, not citizens. The community's capacity for collective action — the political capacity to shape their shared conditions — has been weakened because the social question has been answered from above rather than addressed from within. This does not mean the programme should stop. It means the programme should recognise that solving the social question is not the same as creating political life — and that a community of well-fed recipients is not the same as a community of citizens."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use this analysis to argue against addressing poverty.
**Why:** Arendt takes the social question seriously — she acknowledges that hunger is an absolute demand that cannot wait. The point is not that material needs should be ignored but that addressing them is not the same as creating political freedom. Both are needed.

**Don't:** Romanticise pre-programme political life.
**Why:** Traditional solidarity mechanisms may have been exclusionary, patriarchal, and coercive. The political life they represented was imperfect. The point is that replacing imperfect politics with no politics is not an improvement.
