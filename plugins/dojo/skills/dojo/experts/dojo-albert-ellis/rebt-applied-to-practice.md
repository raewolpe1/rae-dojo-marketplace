# REBT Applied to Practice

## When to Use
- When someone wants concrete daily practices from REBT.
- When the theory needs to be translated into homework and exercises.
- When the three modalities of REBT techniques — cognitive, emotive, behavioural — need to be taught.

## Core Concept
Ellis insisted that therapy happens between sessions, not during them. The session identifies the irrational belief and begins the disputation. The homework is where the change occurs. REBT homework spans three modalities.

Cognitive techniques include the REBT Self-Help Form — a structured worksheet where the person identifies the activating event, the irrational beliefs, disputes each belief, and formulates effective new philosophies. Daily disputation — spending ten minutes each morning identifying and challenging the musts that operated the previous day — is a powerful maintenance practice. Rational coping statements — short, memorable phrases that counter specific irrational beliefs — serve as in-the-moment interventions: "I strongly prefer success but I don't need it," "This is difficult, not unbearable," "Their opinion does not define my worth."

Emotive techniques include rational-emotive imagery (REI), where the person vividly imagines the feared situation, allows themselves to feel the unhealthy emotion, and then actively shifts to the healthy alternative while maintaining the image. The shift from anxiety to concern, from depression to sadness, from rage to annoyance — experienced internally while imagining the triggering situation — builds emotional muscle memory. Shame-attacking exercises are another emotive technique that forces experiential learning rather than intellectual understanding.

Behavioural techniques include in vivo desensitisation — doing the feared thing rather than just thinking about it. The procrastinator writes for fifteen minutes. The socially anxious person initiates a conversation. The perfectionist submits work they know is imperfect. These actions directly contradict the irrational belief and provide experiential evidence that the feared consequence does not materialise or is survivable.

Ellis recommended that clients practice disputation daily, do REI twice daily, complete at least one behavioural assignment per week, and use rational coping statements throughout the day. The combination of cognitive, emotive, and behavioural work produces faster and more durable change than any single modality alone.

## How to Apply
1. **Daily disputation.** Ten minutes each morning. What musts operated yesterday? Dispute each one.
2. **REBT Self-Help Form.** When a disturbance occurs, work through A-B-C-D-E on paper.
3. **Rational-emotive imagery.** Twice daily. Imagine the situation, feel the unhealthy emotion, shift to the healthy alternative.
4. **Rational coping statements.** Write three to five on a card. Review them when triggered.
5. **Behavioural homework.** One action per week that directly contradicts an irrational belief.
6. **Shame-attacking exercise.** One per week when approval-seeking is a target.
7. **Track progress.** Note which beliefs recur, which disputes work best, and where resistance remains.

## Examples
**Situation:** A therapist wants to design a four-week REBT homework programme for a client with social anxiety.
**Application:** Week 1: Identify the core demand ("I must be approved of") using the Self-Help Form after each anxious episode. Practice REI twice daily — imagine a social situation, feel the anxiety, shift to concern. Rational coping statement: "I prefer approval but I do not need it." Week 2: Continue daily disputation. Add a mild shame-attacking exercise — ask a stranger an unusual question. Process the result in session. Week 3: Initiate one conversation with someone the client would normally avoid. Continue REI and disputation. Add a second shame-attacking exercise. Week 4: Speak up in a meeting or group setting. Not perfectly. Just speak. Continue all practices. Review in session what beliefs shifted and which remain stubborn. The programme combines all three modalities — cognitive (disputation, Self-Help Form), emotive (REI, shame-attacking), and behavioural (conversation, speaking up) — in an escalating sequence.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Assign homework without explaining why.
**Why:** REBT homework is philosophically grounded. The person needs to understand the rationale — otherwise it becomes a compliance exercise rather than a self-directed change process.

**Don't:** Rely on cognitive techniques alone.
**Why:** Ellis was explicit that intellectual insight without emotive and behavioural action produces elegant understanding and zero change. All three modalities are necessary.
