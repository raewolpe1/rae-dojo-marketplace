# Irrational Beliefs

## When to Use
- When someone needs to understand the patterns of irrational thinking Ellis identified.
- When specific irrational beliefs need to be named and explained.
- When the hierarchical structure of irrationality needs to be taught — demandingness as core, others as derivatives.

## Core Concept
Ellis originally identified twelve irrational beliefs that he saw as common sources of emotional disturbance. Over time, he refined these into a more parsimonious model with three core demands and three derivative irrationalities.

The three core demands are: "I must perform well and be approved of by significant others" (demands on self), "Others must treat me fairly, kindly, and well" (demands on others), and "The conditions of my life must be comfortable, easy, and give me what I want without too much effort" (demands on the world/life). These three musts are the engine of virtually all emotional disturbance. Everything else is a derivative.

The three derivative irrationalities that flow from frustrated demands are: awfulizing ("This is not just bad — it is absolutely terrible, the worst thing that could happen"), low frustration tolerance ("I cannot stand this — it is unbearable"), and global rating of self or others ("Because this happened, I am/they are totally worthless").

The structure matters because it shows that disputation should target the demand first. If you only dispute the derivative — "It's not really awful" — without addressing the underlying demand — "It must not have happened" — the person will generate new derivatives from the same demand. Kill the demand and the derivatives lose their power.

Ellis later consolidated his original twelve beliefs into these patterns, recognising that the specific content varies infinitely but the structure is always the same: a rigid demand, followed by awfulizing, low frustration tolerance, or global rating when the demand is frustrated.

## How to Apply
1. **Listen for the demand.** Behind every disturbance is a must, should, or ought. It may be implicit. "I'm so angry she did that" often conceals "She must not have done that."
2. **Identify which domain.** Is the demand on self, on others, or on life conditions?
3. **Check for derivatives.** Is the person awfulizing? Displaying low frustration tolerance? Globally rating themselves or others?
4. **Target the demand first.** If you only address derivatives, the demand will regenerate them.
5. **Help the person see the pattern.** Once they recognise the demand-derivative structure, they can catch it themselves.

## Examples
**Situation:** A man says, "I applied for a promotion and didn't get it. I'm a complete failure. I can't stand working here anymore. This is the worst thing that could have happened."
**Application:** All three derivatives are visible in a single statement. Global self-rating: "I'm a complete failure" (one event → total judgment of self). Low frustration tolerance: "I can't stand it" (he is in fact standing it). Awfulizing: "the worst thing that could have happened" (worse than death? worse than illness? worse than the loss of a child?). All three flow from an unstated demand: "I must get promoted. If I don't, it means I'm incompetent." The intervention targets the demand: "You would have preferred the promotion — that's rational. But where does it say you must get it? And how does not getting one promotion make you — in your entirety — a failure? You're taking one event and using it to rate your entire being. That's what's causing the depression — not the rejection."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present irrational beliefs as something only "sick" people have.
**Why:** Ellis insisted that irrationality is a normal human tendency with a biological basis. Everyone holds irrational beliefs. Awareness and disputation are ongoing practices, not signs of pathology.

**Don't:** List beliefs abstractly without connecting to the person's situation.
**Why:** Abstract lists are intellectual. The power comes from the person recognising their own specific irrational belief in the moment it is operating.
