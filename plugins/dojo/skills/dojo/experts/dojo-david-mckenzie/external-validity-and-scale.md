# External Validity, Displacement and Scale

## When to Use
- When an international result (Sri Lanka, Togo, Nigeria, Mexico) is being used to justify local design.
- When a pilot is being scaled.
- When the target market may be saturated.

## Fails When
- **The question is philosophical/methodological transferability** — route to Cartwright, whose treatment is deeper.

## Core Concept

The evidence base in this dojo is drawn overwhelmingly from Sri Lanka, Togo, Nigeria, Mexico, Ghana, Malawi and Brazil. **None of it is South African.** That is the first and largest external validity problem and it should be stated on every use.

**Three specific transfer risks.**

**1. Market saturation and displacement.** The Nigerian competition found that YouWiN! firms did not materially destroy or generate jobs in other firms outside the experimental sample — accounting for spillovers changed the employment estimate by around 100 jobs. But Nigeria at that time was a large, growing, absorptive market. **A saturated market is exactly the condition under which the no-displacement result fails.** South African township retail is dense and demand-constrained. Helping one spaza take custom from another is a transfer, not a creation. Any employment estimate built on Nigerian spillover results imported into a saturated local market is optimistic by an unknown margin, and the margin should be measured, not assumed.

**2. General equilibrium at scale.** A pilot is partial equilibrium: a few hundred firms treated in an economy that does not notice. At scale, prices, wages, competition and input supply all respond. McKenzie & Puerto's work on business training with market-level randomisation in Kenya `[CHECK]` is the design that addresses this — randomising at the *market* level rather than the firm level, so that displacement shows up in the estimate. Firm-level randomisation cannot see it by construction.

**3. Delivery fidelity.** The Togo personal initiative result came from a specific curriculum delivered with research-grade fidelity to 500 firms. A provincial rollout to 15,000 through a procured panel of providers is not the same treatment. Effect sizes degrade with delivery quality, and the degradation is usually not measured.

**The honest posture.** External validity is not a reason to ignore the evidence — it is the strongest evidence that exists and the alternative is nothing. It is a reason to (a) state the transfer assumption explicitly, (b) discount the expected effect, and (c) build the local test into the rollout rather than assuming the transfer.

## How to Apply

1. **State the origin of every effect size.** "In Togo, with 500 firms, under research-grade delivery…" — never let a number float free of its context.
2. **Ask whether the market is saturated.** If yes, the displacement question is first-order and the Nigerian spillover result does not transfer.
3. **Randomise at the market level** where displacement is plausible. Firm-level randomisation is blind to it.
4. **Discount for delivery.** A procured rollout will not match research-grade fidelity. Assume degradation and measure it.
5. **Build the test into the rollout.** Phase the geography, randomise the order, and you get an evaluation for the price of a scheduling decision.
6. **Distinguish "no local evidence" from "no evidence".** The first is a call for a local test; the second is false.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy cites the 30% Togo personal initiative result to justify a provincial rollout to 15,000 firms.

**Application:** The citation is legitimate and it is the best evidence available — but three discounts apply. Togo microenterprises are not Western Cape microenterprises; the delivery was research-grade to 500 firms and yours will be procured at 30 times the scale; and the seven-year follow-up shows the effect dissipating for women. Recommendation: phase the rollout across districts with randomised order, which produces a credible local estimate at essentially no additional cost, and gives you an answer within two years rather than an assumption for ten.

**Result:** The international evidence is used to choose the intervention; local evidence is generated to size it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Import an effect size without its context.
**Why:** The number is attached to a country, a sample, a curriculum, a cheque size, and a delivery model.

**Don't:** Assume the Nigerian no-displacement result transfers to saturated markets.
**Why:** Non-saturation is the condition that produced it.

**Don't:** Randomise at firm level where displacement is plausible.
**Why:** The design cannot see the effect you most need to measure.

**Don't:** Treat "no South African evidence" as a reason to ignore the international evidence.
**Why:** The alternative to imperfect evidence is not perfect evidence. It is guessing.
