# Leadership and Institutional Amnesia

> **SOURCED.** Verified verbatim from the Guardian (Nigeria) interview, 29 March 2026. See `evidence-base-the-2026-interview.md`. **This file did not exist before the interview was obtained; the dojo had missed the dimension entirely.**

## When to Use
- **When a strategy assumes that having a plan is the hard part.**
- When development outcomes are attributed to resources or institutions alone.
- When explaining why the last plan didn't happen.

## Fails When
- **The question is institutions and growth in general.** Route to **Acemoglu** or **Robinson** — and see the disagreement below, which is real.

## Core Concept

**His philosophy, stated as such:**

> ***"nations fail or succeed not because of resources, but because of choices, and leadership."***

**Read the phrasing.** "Nations fail" is Acemoglu and Robinson's title, and he knows it. **He is putting *choices and leadership* where they put *institutions*.** That is not a rejection of institutions — his whole corpus is institutional — but it is an insistence on **agency** that the institutional literature tends to design out. See the tension section below.

**And it is not a late-career reflection. It came out of the doctoral work:**

> *"My thesis looked at technology acquisition in Nigeria and that working through the different factors that shape the trajectory of nations, **leadership stood out as an indispensable ingredient for nation building and economic development.**"*

**The Nigeria diagnosis — and it is unsparing, about his own country:**

> *"Nigeria's early post-independence blueprints for industrialisation were, on paper, **not dissimilar in ambition from those of East Asia**. Yet we must confront the stark lesson: **a vision without the requisite, sustained leadership action is merely a daydream.** While Development Plans were articulated, the nation lacked **continuity, political will, and, crucially, the transformational leadership** to see them through. **Policies were crafted, investments were voted, and human capital was, at times, developed—only to be dissipated by short-term political calculus, mismanagement, and a recurring cycle of institutional amnesia.** This has led us to our present crossroads: a moment of reckoning laid bare by every major development indicator."*

And, flatly: ***"We need to reflect on our past and how we ended up a failed industrial state."***

**Unpack the three failure mechanisms he names, because they are distinct and they have distinct remedies:**

| Mechanism | What it is | What it defeats |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-term political calculus** | The electoral or factional horizon is shorter than the industrial one | Any plan requiring more than one term |
| **Mismanagement** | Capability to execute is absent or captured | Execution, even where will exists |
| **Institutional amnesia** | **The system forgets what it learned** — each cycle restarts | **Accumulation itself** |

**"Institutional amnesia" is the one to sit with.** It is not that nothing was done. **Policies were crafted. Investments were voted. Human capital was developed.** And then it was *dissipated* — not defeated by an enemy, not outcompeted, **forgotten**. A system with amnesia cannot accumulate capability, and capability is the only thing that accumulates. That is why the differences are *persistent* (see `institutions-and-technical-change.md`): institutions are durable, and so is their absence — **and so is the habit of forgetting.**

**"No country industrialises by accident; it happens through concerted design. It happens through strong leadership."**

**The uncomfortable corollary for a strategy document.** He is the author of the AfCFTA Advisory Council's method, and it is a direct response to this diagnosis:

> *"We emphasize coherence and accountability. **Every recommendation must be accompanied by measurable targets, implementation champions, and timelines.**"*

**An "implementation champion" is a named person.** Not a department, not a workstream — a person who is accountable and who persists. **That is what a system with institutional amnesia requires and what a strategy document almost never supplies.** Kaplan's engaged-academic test asks whether the institution *can* act; this asks **who, by name, will make it.**

## The tension with Acemoglu and Robinson — do not resolve it

Both are in this collection. The disagreement is real and it is worth staging.

- **Acemoglu & Robinson:** inclusive versus extractive **institutions** explain why nations fail. Leadership is largely endogenous — leaders do what the institutional configuration permits and rewards.
- **Oyelaran-Oyeyinka:** *"nations fail or succeed not because of resources, but because of choices, and leadership."* Nigeria's institutions did not prevent the plans from being written — they were written, and ambitious. **What failed was the sustaining of them, and that is agency.**

**The honest reading is that they are answering different questions.** A&R explain the long-run distribution of outcomes across countries; he explains **why a specific country with adequate plans and real human capital dissipated them anyway.** A&R would say: because the institutions rewarded dissipation. He would say: because nobody stayed the course, and that was a choice, and it could have been otherwise.

**Which matters for a strategy, because they imply different work.** If it is institutions, you reform institutions and wait. If it is leadership and continuity, **you name champions, set timelines, and build the memory** — which is cheaper, faster, and within a department's reach.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what happened to the last plan.** Not whether one existed — what happened to it. If the answer is "it was superseded," that is amnesia.
2. **Name an implementation champion for every recommendation.** A person. His own council's rule.
3. **Attach measurable targets and timelines.** Same rule.
4. **Build the memory deliberately.** What in the institution will still know this in five years, when the people have moved?
5. **Do not treat a good plan as an achievement.** Nigeria's blueprints were as ambitious as East Asia's.
6. **Stage the A&R tension rather than picking.** Institutions and agency; the strategy needs a view.

## Examples

**Situation:** A provincial strategy runs to 2035 and lists 90 initiatives across three implementation phases.

**Application:** Apply his test to the document itself. **How many of the 90 have a named implementation champion?** Not an owning department — a person, accountable, who will still be there. **How many have measurable targets and timelines?** And the question the document cannot ask itself: **what happened to the previous provincial economic strategy, and who remembers what it learned?** If the answer is that it was superseded and the learning did not carry, then the binding constraint on this strategy is not analysis — it is **institutional amnesia**, and a ninety-initiative document with no named champions is the mechanism by which amnesia occurs. *"A vision without the requisite, sustained leadership action is merely a daydream."*

**Result:** Fewer initiatives, each with a name attached, and an explicit account of what the last strategy learned.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat the plan as the deliverable.
**Why:** Nigeria's post-independence blueprints were, on paper, as ambitious as East Asia's. That is his point, and it is about a document exactly like yours.

**Don't:** Assign an initiative to a department and call that accountability.
**Why:** His council's rule is **implementation champions** — people. Departments have amnesia; people can be asked.

**Don't:** Read this as displacing institutions.
**Why:** His whole corpus is institutional. He is adding agency, not substituting it. Run him against Acemoglu and Robinson and report the disagreement.
