# African Clusters and Collective Learning

> **`[CHECK]`.** Built from summary material on the cluster volume. **Obtain the book before relying on specifics.**

## When to Use
- **Whenever cluster policy is designed**, especially for small or informal firms.
- When the cluster model is imported from Italian industrial districts.
- Township economy chapters.

## Fails When
- **The cluster is composed of large formal firms.** Different dynamics; the Italian literature may fit.

## Core Concept

**The book:** *Industrial Clusters and Innovation Systems in Africa: Institutions, Markets and Policy*, edited with **Dorothy McCormick** (United Nations University Press, 2007).

**The cases:** the **Suame cluster in Ghana**; the **Kamukunji and Kariobangi clusters in Kenya**.

**The fact that determines everything else: these clusters are dominated by micro and small enterprises.** `[CHECK]`

**Why that matters more than it sounds.** The cluster literature most policymakers arrive with was built on **Italian industrial districts** — firms of a certain scale, formal inter-firm contracting, sectoral institutions, a functioning state, an established standards regime. An African cluster of micro enterprises has a different composition, different institutions and different learning dynamics. **It is not the Italian model at smaller scale.** Applying that template is the commonest error in cluster policy on this continent, and it is what produces shared facilities nobody uses.

**What the study analysed: the role of institutional and infrastructural conditions and collective learning in the adoption of new technologies.** `[CHECK]` Three terms, each doing work:

- **Institutional conditions.** What exists that supports technical change. Frequently very little.
- **Infrastructural conditions.** A technology the power supply cannot support is not adopted, whatever its merits. In the data this was determinative, not a caveat.
- **Collective learning.** The one that reorganises policy.

**The collective learning finding, and its implication.** In a cluster of micro enterprises, **no single firm holds the capability.** The man with the lathe knows the lathe. The man three units down knows the electrics. The apprentice knows where the part is because he was sent for it two hundred times. **The capability is held by the cluster, distributed across it, and it moves by proximity — apprenticeship, watching, the person next door having seen this before.**

**Therefore the object of the intervention is the cluster, not the firm.** This sits badly with enterprise support as normally practised, which is built to fund firms. **You may support a hundred firms individually and leave the thing that actually learns entirely untouched.**

**The convergence.** Lundvall's DUI mode is what this is — tacit, interactive, know-how and know-who — observed in an African MSE cluster. And Hausmann's collective knowhow claim is the same structure: capability held in a combination of people, not in any of them. **Three traditions, one finding.**

**And a fourth, now sourced.** The **Local Innovation and Production Systems (LIPS)** approach — the Brazilian RedeSist framework that CeSTII has adapted for measuring innovation in South Africa's informal sector — **"places the unit of analysis in the set of agents, at the collective level, going beyond the individual organizations, sectors or productive chains, establishing a close relationship between the territory and the economic activities"** (Mustapha, Petersen, Jegede, Bortagaray & Kruss 2022, *Innovation and Development* 12(2): 325–342, citing Cassiolato et al. 2017). A LIPS is defined as **"(a) group(s) of economic, political and social agents localised in the same area, performing related economic activities, in which formal and informal interdependence and consistent linkages usually result in cooperation and learning processes, with a potential to generate the increase of productive and innovative capabilities"** (Lastres & Cassiolato 2005, 7).

**That is this file's finding, arrived at independently from Latin American structuralism and operationalised as a measurement instrument for South Africa.** The collective unit is not a rhetorical preference — it is the design decision in the only informal-innovation instrument anyone has specified for this country. Note also that **Oluseye Jegede**, a co-author of that paper, works on exactly this in Nigeria: clustering and the scaling-up of clustered enterprises in the informal sector (Jegede et al. 2017; Jegede 2020a). **See the Kraemer-Mbula dojo, `evidence-base-cestii-measurement-programme.md`, for the instrument.**

**For a township economy chapter this is the most directly relevant work in the roster**, because the firms are the same kind of firms.

**And the collective unit has just reappeared in a new substrate: data.** **UNIDO Policy Brief No. 30 (March 2026)** — **not his** — argues: **"While small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) might not individually produce enough data to train AI models, if companies pool their data, together they can develop AI models tailored to their needs."** Same structure, new material. No single small firm holds enough; pooled, they do; and **the state's job is to build the thing that permits pooling** — "secure platforms for industry collaboration to develop AI applications using local data."

**The working precedent is provincial.** China's **Industrial Brain** programme in **Shandong** — a province — "built a single unified system to manage industrial data", running **80 pilot projects by end-2024, 12 recognised as provincial demonstration cases**, with sibling programmes in **Zhejiang and Shanghai**. Its service platform includes **"industry landscape mapping"** and **"industry monitoring"** — which is Sutton's Enterprise Map as live government software rather than a book. **Caveat: China, no evaluation reported, and pilot counts are an activity metric.** See `evidence-base-ai-and-industrial-catching-up.md`.

## His own hub prescription — VERIFIED (Guardian, 29 March 2026)

Four enablers for technology hubs and innovation ecosystems: *"**Affordable and reliable electricity, broadband connectivity, coordinated research funding, commercialisation programmes and urban innovation clusters co-located with universities and manufacturing zones.**"*

**And the warning, which is the sharpest line in the interview about African innovation policy:**

> *"policies must shift from rhetoric to **predictable funding mechanisms**, so **hubs don't vanish when donor projects end.**"*

**That is the failure mode, named.** Not that hubs don't work — that they are donor-funded and evaporate. **Predictable funding is the design variable**, and it is a question a provincial strategy can actually answer about its own instruments: *what happens to this when the funding cycle ends?*

He also wants clusters **co-located with universities and manufacturing zones**, and *"industrial clusters that connect companies with investors"* — and, on universities: *"**Universities should become laboratories for problem-solving, not just degree factories.**"*

## How to Apply

1. **Check the model's provenance.** If it is Italian districts, it does not fit MSE clusters.
2. **Ask what the infrastructure permits** before assessing any technology.
3. **Treat the cluster as the unit.** Not the firm.
4. **Find the collective learning channels** — apprenticeship, proximity, the man next door — and ask what would strengthen them.
5. **Test the firm-level instrument.** Would it touch the thing that learns?
6. **Get the book.** `[CHECK]` — this file is built from summaries.

## Examples

**Situation:** A township economy chapter proposes shared facilities, a business association, and individual enterprise support to a concentration of small manufacturers and repairers.

**Application:** The shared facility and the association are Italian-district instruments and they may or may not fit; the enterprise support is a firm-level instrument, and in an MSE cluster the firm is the wrong unit. **The capability is distributed across the cluster and moves by proximity and apprenticeship.** So ask a different set of questions. What currently transmits knowledge between these units — apprenticeship, informal training, the mechanic who has seen it before? What would strengthen that? Does the **infrastructure** support the technologies being proposed, because if not they will not be adopted at any subsidy. And which **institutions** would carry a technical upgrade — is there a standards body, a trainer, a supplier network? **Suame, Kamukunji and Kariobangi are the closest studied analogues to this population that exist. Read them.**

**Result:** A cluster intervention aimed at the thing that actually learns.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Import the Italian district model.
**Why:** African clusters are MSE-dominated with different institutions. The template produces facilities nobody uses.

**Don't:** Fund individual firms and call it cluster policy.
**Why:** The capability is held by the cluster. Firm-level support may not touch it.

**Don't:** Propose technology the infrastructure cannot support.
**Why:** In the cluster data, infrastructural conditions were determinative.
