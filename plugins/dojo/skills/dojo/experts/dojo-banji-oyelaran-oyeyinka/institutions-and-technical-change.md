# Institutions and Technical Change

> **`[CHECK]` on the book.** Built from summary material, not from reading it. **The "adaptive governance" and "institutional amnesia" material below is SOURCED** — verified verbatim from the Guardian (Nigeria) interview, 29 March 2026.

## When to Use
- When performance differences between firms, industries or countries need explaining.
- When institutions are treated as background conditions.
- When technical change is not happening despite investment and policy.

## Fails When
- **The question is institutions and growth in general.** Route to Acemoglu or Robinson, already in the collection. This is narrower: institutions and **technical change**, in Africa, at industry level.

## Core Concept

**The book:** *Learning to Compete in African Industry: Institutions and Technology in Development* (Ashgate, 2006), based on research carried out at UNU-INTECH.

**The question it sets:** why are the differences in economic performance of firms, industries and countries in Africa so **persistent**?

**Persistent is the operative word.** Not large — large would be unremarkable. **Durable.** Surviving policy changes, commodity cycles, aid programmes, and governments.

**The answer: the differences have institutional roots.** `[CHECK]` And that is a claim about causation, with a mechanism: **institutions support technical change, or they fail to.** Where the institutions that would carry technical progress are absent or malformed — training systems, engineering associations, standards bodies, universities that talk to firms, associations that transmit practice — **technical change does not occur**, regardless of investment, regardless of policy pronouncement, and regardless of how earnestly firms wish it.

**Why the differences persist, then: because institutions are durable, and so is their absence.** That is the whole explanation and it is uncomfortable, because it means the fast instruments do not work.

**The second claim: technological progress is central to competitiveness in a global context.** `[CHECK]` Not an eventual luxury for economies that have solved the basics. The condition of competing at all.

**And the term he uses for what institutions must do — VERIFIED.** His doctoral research *"connected industrial policy to institutional learning, convinced me of the importance of **adaptive governance**."* Institutions that carry technical change must themselves **learn** — which is why their failure mode is **institutional amnesia**: *"Policies were crafted, investments were voted, and human capital was, at times, developed—only to be dissipated by short-term political calculus, mismanagement, and a recurring cycle of institutional amnesia."* **A system that forgets cannot accumulate, and capability is the only thing that accumulates.** See `leadership-and-institutional-amnesia.md`.

**The third: initial conditions matter.** Levels of literacy, natural and resource endowment, the structure of industry. See `initial-conditions.md`.

**How to use this diagnostically.** The move is to ask, of any technical change you want to happen: **which institution would carry it?** Not which firm, and not which policy — which institution. Who trains the people? Who sets and enforces the standard? Who transmits the practice between firms? Who connects the university to the shop floor? **If the answer to each is nobody, the technical change will not occur, and adding money does not create an institution.**

**The relationship to the rest of the roster.** This is Lundvall's innovation system with the emphasis moved to institutional presence and quality, in a context where the institutions are frequently **absent** rather than merely narrow — which is a different problem from the one the Nordic literature was built to address, and it is Kraemer-Mbula's decontextualisation point arriving from a different direction.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask which institution would carry the technical change.** Name it. If none, that is the finding.
2. **Distinguish absent from malformed.** Building and reforming are different tasks.
3. **Read persistence as evidence.** Durable differences imply durable causes; look institutionally.
4. **Do not expect money to create institutions.** It funds them once they exist.
5. **State the initial conditions.** They condition what the institutions can do.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy proposes technology upgrading in a value chain and cannot explain why previous attempts failed.

**Application:** Ask which institution would carry the upgrade. **Who trains the technicians?** If the TVET system does not teach this technology, nobody. **Who sets and certifies the standard?** If no standards body covers it, the upgrade cannot be verified and buyers will not pay for it. **Who transmits the practice between firms?** If there is no association and no supplier network, each firm must discover it alone. **Who connects the university to these firms?** If nobody, the codified knowledge stays in the university. **Four institutional absences, and the previous attempts failed against all four** — which is why they failed repeatedly and why the failure was read as a firm-level problem. The upgrade requires institution-building, which is slow, and that is the honest finding.

**Result:** An explanation for repeated failure, and a diagnosis that names buildable things.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat institutions as background.
**Why:** They are the mechanism. Technical change does not occur without them, whatever else is present.

**Don't:** Read persistent differences as a firm-level problem.
**Why:** Durable differences have durable causes. Firms turn over; institutions do not.

**Don't:** Expect funding to substitute for an absent institution.
**Why:** Money funds institutions that exist. It does not create them.
