# Inter-Organisational Linkages — Survey Them

> **`[CHECK]`.** Built from the working paper abstract. Verify before attribution.

## When to Use
- When an innovation system is being mapped or asserted.
- When university–industry or supplier linkage programmes are proposed.
- When someone says the system's actors are connected.

## Fails When
- **The linkages have been surveyed.** Then read the survey.

## Core Concept

**The study:** *Systems of Innovation and Underdevelopment: An Institutional Perspective*, UNU-INTECH, June 2005. **Based on recent field survey data collected in three African countries**, examining **inter-firm and inter-organisational collaboration in African industry.** `[CHECK]`

**Three sets of interactions were analysed:**

1. **Firm–firm linkages** — including **user–supplier** and **subcontracting** relationships.
2. **Firm–university linkages.**
3. **Firm–industrial association linkages.**

**That enumeration is the instrument, and it is the point.** An innovation system is a set of relationships. You can assert them from an organogram, or you can **survey them** and find out. He surveyed them.

**Why this is more than method.** A strategy that maps its innovation system by listing institutions — the university, the science council, the agency, the association — has drawn an **organogram, not a system**. The system is whether those boxes are *connected to firms*, and in what direction, and with what content. **In much of Africa the honest answer is that they are not**, and that absence is the finding — it is what "systems of innovation and **underdevelopment**" names.

**The three linkages, read as a diagnostic:**

| Linkage | What it carries | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| **Firm–firm** (user–supplier, subcontracting) | Tacit know-how; specification; demand pressure | The firm learns from nobody — see Lundvall |
| **Firm–university** | Codified knowledge; testing; graduates who stay | The science base is disconnected from production |
| **Firm–association** | Practice transmission; collective voice; standards | Each firm discovers everything alone |

**Note that the first is the one that carries the most and the one policy attends to least.** University linkage programmes are popular because universities are legible and procurable. The user–supplier relationship is where the learning actually is, and it appears in the market access chapter rather than the innovation one.

**For a provincial application.** Nobody has surveyed the linkages in the Western Cape's firm population, and particularly not in townships. **Until someone does, any claim about the provincial innovation system is an assertion.** The instrument exists — he built it, on three African countries — and it is a survey, not a research programme.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask whether the linkages have been observed.** If not, the system has been asserted.
2. **Survey the three types.** Firm–firm, firm–university, firm–association.
3. **Expect firm–firm to matter most and be attended to least.**
4. **Read absence as the finding.** Disconnection is what underdevelopment means here.
5. **Do not draw an organogram and call it a system.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy maps the provincial innovation system as a diagram of institutions with arrows between them.

**Application:** The arrows are asserted. Ask what evidence exists that any firm in the province has a relationship with any of those institutions — and specifically, run the three-linkage survey. **Firm–firm:** do firms have user–supplier relationships that carry specification and feedback, or only transactions? **Firm–university:** do any firms outside a handful of large ones interact with a university at all? **Firm–association:** do the associations transmit practice, or only lobby? The likely finding is that the first exists and is unrecognised, and the second and third barely exist for the firm population the strategy is about. **That finding is more useful than the diagram**, and the instrument to produce it is a survey he has already designed and run in three countries.

**Result:** An innovation system map based on observed relationships.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Draw the institutions and connect them with arrows.
**Why:** That is an organogram. The system is whether firms are actually connected.

**Don't:** Prioritise firm–university linkage because it is legible.
**Why:** Firm–firm carries more and gets less attention. That is the standing misallocation.

**Don't:** Treat disconnection as an absence of data.
**Why:** It is the finding. "Systems of innovation and underdevelopment" is the title.
