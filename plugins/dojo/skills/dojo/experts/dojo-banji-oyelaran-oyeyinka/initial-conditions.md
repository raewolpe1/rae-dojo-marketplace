# Initial Conditions

> **`[CHECK]`.** Built from the book's summary material. Verify before attribution.

## When to Use
- When a strategy could have been written about anywhere.
- When a development model is being imported.
- As a standing check on any industrial or innovation strategy.

## Fails When
- **The initial conditions are stated and reasoned from.** Then engage the reasoning.

## Core Concept

**In *Learning to Compete*, alongside the institutional argument, the role of initial conditions is emphasised: levels of literacy and natural endowment, the structure of industry, and resource endowment.** `[CHECK]`

**Why this is a discipline and not a preamble.** Initial conditions determine what is possible. A strategy that does not state them is reasoning about a generic economy — and there is no generic economy. **It is also, in practice, the fastest way to detect an imported strategy:** the document that does not state its initial conditions was written for somewhere else, because a document written from here would have had to begin with them.

**The four to state:**

1. **Levels of literacy** — and, one would add now, the actual distribution of skills. This bounds what firms can absorb and what institutions can be staffed.
2. **Natural endowment** — what is here, physically.
3. **Resource endowment** — what can be drawn on, including the resource base that a linkage strategy would build from.
4. **The structure of industry** — what exists, at what scale, in what configuration. This is the one most often omitted and it is the most consequential.

**How it connects to the rest of the framework.** Initial conditions **condition what the institutions can do**. A training institution cannot produce technicians from a population without the schooling to enter it. A standards body cannot enforce standards firms cannot meet. **The institutional argument and the initial-conditions argument are one argument**: institutions carry technical change, and initial conditions determine what institutions can be built and what they can carry.

**Where this bites for South Africa.** The initial conditions here are unusual and are usually stated as a list of problems rather than as an analytical starting point. High unemployment alongside a sophisticated financial sector. World-class firms alongside a large informal economy. An industrial base built under conditions nobody would reproduce. Extreme inequality in the skills distribution. **These are not context. They are the parameters**, and a strategy that treats them as background while importing its instruments from elsewhere has made the error this file names.

**The relationship to Lin.** Lin's endowment structure is a version of this claim, formalised into a selection rule — and Oyelaran-Oyeyinka's is broader (it includes literacy and industrial structure, not only factor proportions) and less operational (it yields no rule). **Both say: start from what is here.** Neither yields the same answer.

## His own three barriers — VERIFIED (Guardian, 29 March 2026)

Asked directly what most obstructs African industrialisation, he named three:

1. **"Infrastructure deficits, particularly energy and logistics"**
2. **"Skills mismatch, where education does not produce technically capable graduates"**
3. **"Fragmented policy frameworks, often reactive rather than strategic"**

And the answer: *"the solution lies in **industrial ecosystems—coordinated investment in power, transport, skills, and finance around specific sectors. No country industrialises by accident; it happens through concerted design. It happens through strong leadership.**"*

**⚠️ "Industrial ecosystems" here means coordinated sectoral investment — NOT Stam's or Autio's entrepreneurial ecosystem. Same word, different object. Do not merge them.**

Note that all three barriers are **initial conditions in his sense**: infrastructure and skills are the endowment you start from, and a fragmented policy framework is the institutional condition. **And note the third — "reactive rather than strategic" is a description of a document like yours, and it is the charge to answer.**

## How to Apply

1. **State the four.** Literacy and skills; natural endowment; resource endowment; industrial structure.
2. **Reason from them**, rather than reciting them and proceeding to the imported instrument.
3. **Use the omission as a detector.** No initial conditions means the strategy came from elsewhere.
4. **Connect to the institutional question.** Initial conditions bound what institutions can be built.
5. **Treat South Africa's oddities as parameters, not problems.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's context chapter recites statistics and its instrument chapters could have been written for any middle-income economy.

**Application:** The initial conditions have been stated and then not used, which is the commonest form of the error. Test it: **does any instrument in the document change if the literacy distribution changes? If the industrial structure were different? If the resource endowment were different?** If not, the context chapter is decoration and the instruments were imported. The discipline is to reason forward: given a skills distribution like this, what can an institution actually be staffed to do? Given an industrial structure like this — dominated by micro firms, with a formal corporate sector that is world-class and disconnected — which linkages could exist at all? **Those answers constrain the instrument set, and that is what stating initial conditions is for.**

**Result:** Instruments derived from the starting position rather than adjacent to a description of it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** State the initial conditions and then ignore them.
**Why:** That is the common form of the error. Reason forward from them or don't state them.

**Don't:** Treat South Africa's structural oddities as problems to be noted.
**Why:** They are the parameters. The whole analysis runs on them.

**Don't:** Assume an institution can be built regardless of the skills distribution.
**Why:** Initial conditions bound what institutions can be staffed and what they can carry.
