# National Firms and the FDI Trap

> **SOURCED.** Verified verbatim from the Guardian (Nigeria) interview, 29 March 2026. See `evidence-base-the-2026-interview.md`.
>
> **⚠️ This file contains the sharpest direct contradiction in the collection. Read the Sutton section.**

## When to Use
- **Whenever FDI attraction is proposed as a route to capability.**
- When Sutton's FDI-as-capability-channel finding is cited.
- When asking why local firms don't get the work.

## Fails When
- **The question is FDI regulation or investment promotion mechanics.** Different subject.

## Core Concept

**The claim, verbatim:**

> ***"The strategic opportunity that Africa missed is to rely almost exclusively on foreign multinationals. This dependency inadvertently supplanted national firms, and the 'Technological Learning' process was lost. This is why today, roads, bridges and basic engineering projects are contracted to foreign firms."***

**Three moves in one sentence, and each does work.**

1. **"Supplanted."** Not *complemented*, not *crowded out* in the neutral economic sense — **took the place of**. The multinational did not sit beside the national firm; it occupied its position.
2. **"The 'Technological Learning' process was lost."** His scare quotes. **The thing destroyed was not market share — it was the learning.** A national firm that never gets the contract never learns, and the learning is the only thing that compounds.
3. **The evidence is the present tense.** *"This is why today, roads, bridges and basic engineering projects are contracted to foreign firms."* Not aerospace. Not semiconductors. **Roads and bridges** — the most basic engineering there is, sixty years after independence, still contracted out. **That is what the loss of technological learning looks like on the ground, and it is checkable by driving anywhere in Nigeria.**

**The prescription is sequenced, and the sequence is the whole thing:**

> *"nurture their own **'National Firms'** while attracting FDIs and **once they have acquired sufficient technological capacities**, invest in applied research that solves national challenges."*

**Nurture national firms *while* attracting FDI — simultaneously, not instead.** And applied research comes **after** capability, not before. He is not an autarkist. He is saying **the domestic firm must be built in parallel or the FDI takes its place.**

**The Korea proof, which he offers directly:**

> *"in the mid-1960s liberalisation by the country **deliberately excluded foreign direct investment initially** owing to fear of Japanese competition. **Korea has fewer multinationals than almost any other late-industrialising country, and this strengthened the state's capacity to discipline private firms and build domestic science and technology capability.**"*

**Read the causal chain: fewer multinationals → stronger state capacity to discipline domestic firms → domestic S&T capability.** The absence of multinationals is not a cost he is conceding; **it is the mechanism.** A state that governs its own firms can demand performance of them. A state whose industrial base is foreign-owned can only make requests.

## ⚠️ THE COLLISION WITH SUTTON — do not smooth this over

**Both went and looked at African firms. They disagree about what FDI does.**

| | **Sutton** (Enterprise Map, 2010–15) | **Oyelaran-Oyeyinka** (Guardian, 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Finding** | In Ethiopia, **the stock of new FDI projects could generate as many jobs as all fifty leading domestic firms combined** | Reliance on foreign multinationals **"supplanted national firms, and the 'Technological Learning' process was lost"** |
| **FDI is** | **A capability channel** — read the register as a capability inventory | **A capability trap** — it takes the position the national firm needed |
| **Policy** | Strengthen investment promotion to international best practice; improve the effectiveness of manufacturing multinationals | Nurture national firms in parallel; Korea excluded FDI initially and that was the point |
| **Evidence** | 50 frontier firms × 5 countries, profiled | Nigeria's industrial history, first-person, plus Korea |

**Do not resolve this cheaply. But here is the reconciliation that survives both:**

**They are counting different things.** Sutton counts **capability present in the territory** — what can be made here, and how many people it employs. That is real and it is measurable and FDI supplies it. Oyelaran-Oyeyinka counts **capability learned and retained by nationals** — what remains when the investor leaves. **FDI can deliver the first while destroying the second**, and that is precisely what "**autonomous**" means in *national autonomous technology capacity* (see `national-technology-capacity.md`).

**Which means the question is not "is FDI good?" but "does this FDI leave learning behind?"** And that question has an answer for any specific project:

- **Does a national firm get any of the work, or all of it goes to the investor's own supply chain?**
- **Does anyone local end up able to do the thing unsupervised?**
- **When the contractor withdraws, does anything continue?** — **which is Ajaokuta, exactly.** The contractors withdrew and construction ceased.

**Sutton's own Tanzanian factory is the bridge:** *if you don't start out with a firm that has the appropriate capabilities, installing capital equipment won't help.* **Oyelaran-Oyeyinka's addition is that if the foreign firm does the work, the domestic firm never acquires the capabilities in the first place — so Sutton's threshold is never crossed, and the reason is the FDI.**

**Run them together on any FDI or local content proposal. The disagreement is the analysis.**

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what the FDI leaves behind in national firms** — not jobs, not value, **learning**.
2. **Apply the withdrawal test.** If the investor left tomorrow, what continues? At Ajaokuta the answer was nothing, and the plant was never even finished.
3. **Look at who gets the basic work.** Roads and bridges are his indicator. If foreign firms do the simple things, the learning never started.
4. **Sequence: national firms in parallel, not after.** He is explicit — *while* attracting FDI.
5. **Run Sutton against him.** Sutton counts capability present; he counts capability retained. Both are true and the gap between them is the policy question.
6. **Note the Korea mechanism**, which is uncomfortable: fewer multinationals → more state capacity to discipline domestic firms.

## Examples

**Situation:** A provincial strategy proposes to attract FDI into a priority value chain and to run a local content programme alongside it.

**Application:** Sutton says do it and get the investment promotion agency to international best practice — the FDI register is a capability inventory and the multinationals are the route to the middle tier. Oyelaran-Oyeyinka says watch what it does to your national firms, because Africa's missed opportunity was relying almost exclusively on foreign multinationals, which supplanted domestic firms and destroyed the technological learning. **Both are right and the local content programme is where they meet.** So make it the test: **does a national firm end up able to do the work unsupervised?** Not *did it get a contract* — that is procurement. **Did it learn?** If the local content programme delivers volume to domestic suppliers without transferring the difficult work, it is satisfying Sutton's job count and confirming Oyelaran-Oyeyinka's trap simultaneously. **The design variable is which work the national firm gets, not how much.**

**Result:** A local content programme with a learning test rather than a volume target.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Cite Sutton's FDI finding without this file.
**Why:** He counts capability present. Oyelaran-Oyeyinka counts capability retained. Different quantities, opposite policies.

**Don't:** Read this as opposition to FDI.
**Why:** He says nurture national firms **while** attracting FDIs. The sequencing and the parallelism are the argument.

**Don't:** Measure local content by value or volume.
**Why:** His indicator is whether the national firm can do it unsupervised. Roads and bridges still contracted to foreign firms is the failure, and volume would not have detected it.
