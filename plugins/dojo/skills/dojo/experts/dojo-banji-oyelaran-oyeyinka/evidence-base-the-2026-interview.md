## THE EVIDENCE

### ⚠️ AJAOKUTA — the founding conclusion, and where it came from
- **Verbatim:** *"First, witnessing the collapse of early industrial experiments in Nigeria in the 1980s showed me that **national autonomous technology capacity—not raw investment dollars—was the missing piece. At the Ajaokuta Steel plant where I started my career, the moment the foreign contractors withdrew, construction ceased and ultimately that was the end of the project.**"*
- **This dojo previously carried the abstract version — "he watched Nigeria's early industrial experiments collapse." The concrete version is much stronger and it is the whole framework in one image.** He **started his career at Ajaokuta Steel**. The foreign contractors withdrew. **Construction ceased.** Not production — *construction*. The plant was never finished.
- **Why it is the perfect case:** the three-question test (`national-technology-capacity.md`) asks *when it breaks, who fixes it?* At Ajaokuta the answer was: **nobody, and the thing was never even built.** The capital was voted. The plant was specified. The contractors came, and when they left there was no national capacity to continue — not to run it, not even to finish it. **That is what "autonomous" means, learned at 25 on a construction site.**
- **Caveat:** Ajaokuta is Nigeria's most politically loaded industrial project and the literature on it is contested and vast. **He is describing what he saw, not offering a full causal account.** Use it as the origin of his conviction, which is what he presents it as, not as a settled history of the project.

### The three pivotal moments — his own enumeration
- **Verbatim:** *"three stand out."*
  1. **Ajaokuta** — above.
  2. **His doctoral research**, *"which connected industrial policy to institutional learning, convinced me of the importance of **adaptive governance**. My thesis looked at **technology acquisition in Nigeria** and that working through the different factors that shape the trajectory of nations, **leadership stood out as an indispensable ingredient for nation building and economic development.**"*
  3. **The UN and the AfDB** — *"revealed how **fragile progress can be when finance is unavailable to build infrastructure and innovation capacity; and when these are not aligned with high-skill human capital.**"*
- **Caveat:** note that **leadership emerges from the doctoral work, not from later disillusion.** It has been in the framework from the start. This dojo missed it entirely — see `leadership-and-institutional-amnesia.md`.

### ⚠️ THE FDI TRAP — the position that collides with Sutton
- **Verbatim:** *"**The strategic opportunity that Africa missed is to rely almost exclusively on foreign multinationals. This dependency inadvertently supplanted national firms, and the 'Technological Learning' process was lost. This is why today, roads, bridges and basic engineering projects are contracted to foreign firms.**"*
- And the prescription: nations must *"nurture their own **'National Firms'** while attracting FDIs and **once they have acquired sufficient technological capacities**, invest in applied research that solves national challenges."*
- **Caveat — this is a direct collision with Sutton and it should not be smoothed over.** See `national-firms-and-the-fdi-trap.md`.

### The Korea analysis — harder-edged than this dojo had him
- **Verbatim:** *"many people erroneously believe that countries like South Korean conglomerates like Samsung just evolved as a matter of accident. No. **Korea's industrialization was built on domestically owned chaebols** (small family businesses) that have become global juggernauts."*
- *"in the mid-1960s liberalisation by the country **deliberately excluded foreign direct investment initially** owing to fear of Japanese competition. **Korea has fewer multinationals than almost any other late-industrialising country, and this strengthened the state's capacity to discipline private firms and build domestic science and technology capability.** Government-chaebol cooperation was essential to the subsequent economic growth and astounding successes that began in the early 1960s."*
- **The figures:** *"In 2025, the revenue of the **top four chaebols (Samsung, SK, Hyundai, and LG) was 41 per cent of the South Korean GDP**, and the **top 30 chaebol were 78 per cent of GDP.**"*
- **Caveat:** **the 41%/78% figures are `[CHECK]`** — stated in an interview without a source, and revenue-to-GDP ratios are a contested statistic (revenue is not value added; conglomerate revenue includes overseas operations and double-counts intra-group sales). **Do not put them in a DEDAT document without a source.** The *structural* claims — domestic ownership, initial FDI exclusion, few multinationals, state capacity to discipline firms — are standard in the late-industrialisation literature and are safe.
- **Note the phrase "strengthened the state's capacity to discipline private firms."** That is Amsden's discipline argument, and it is a much stronger statist position than "Africa needs industrial policy." **He is saying the *absence* of multinationals is what made the Korean state able to govern its own firms.**

### The three barriers, and the ecosystem answer
- **Verbatim:** the most pressing barriers to industrialisation in Africa are *"**infrastructure deficits, particularly energy and logistics**; **skills mismatch, where education does not produce technically capable graduates** and **fragmented policy frameworks, often reactive rather than strategic**."*
- *"the solution lies in **industrial ecosystems—coordinated investment in power, transport, skills, and finance around specific sectors. No country industrialises by accident; it happens through concerted design. It happens through strong leadership.**"*
- **Caveat:** "industrial ecosystems" here means **coordinated sectoral investment**, not Stam's or Autio's entrepreneurial ecosystem. **Different concept, same word.** Do not merge them.

### AfCFTA
- **Verbatim:** *"The AfCFTA provides the continental platform to scale industries beyond narrow national markets. By **harmonising standards, reducing intra-African tariffs, and aligning infrastructure**, AfCFTA can transform Africa's **1.4 billion people** into a coherent industrial market capable of attracting investment and nurturing regional value chains."*
- And the Advisory Council's method — **this was `[CHECK]` and is now verified:** *"We emphasize coherence and accountability. **Every recommendation must be accompanied by measurable targets, implementation champions, and timelines.** We're also building **cross-country learning networks to help nations adapt best practices, not reinvent the wheel.**"*

### Innovation culture and hubs
- **Verbatim:** *"Innovation thrives where curiosity meets support. We must **democratise research, making it relevant to real social and industrial challenges** and this where we need **strong involvement of local firms**. **Universities should become laboratories for problem-solving, not just degree factories.** Governments, in turn, must **de-risk experimentation through innovation funds, startup grants, and public-private partnerships.**"*
- The four enablers for hubs: *"**Affordable and reliable electricity, broadband connectivity, coordinated research funding, commercialisation programmes and urban innovation clusters co-located with universities and manufacturing zones.**"* And: *"policies must shift from rhetoric to **predictable funding mechanisms**, so **hubs don't vanish when donor projects end.**"*
- **Caveat — that last line is the sharpest observation in the interview about African innovation policy** and it is a warning to anyone designing a hub: **the failure mode is not that hubs don't work, it is that they are donor-funded and evaporate.** Predictable funding is the design variable.

### ⚠️ THE AI CAUTION — VERIFIED VERBATIM
- **Verbatim:** *"**A future shaped by Artificial Intelligence (AI) is going to make industrial catching up very difficult.**"*
- **This dojo has carried that sentence as `[CHECK]` since it was built. It is now verified.** Attribute as: *Oyelaran-Oyeyinka, Guardian (Nigeria) interview, 29 March 2026.*
- **Note the context**, which the dojo did not have: the sentence sits **immediately after** the hybrid-future passage and **immediately before** the Nigeria failure diagnosis. He is not making a technology-determinism argument. **He is making a leadership argument with AI as the aggravating factor** — Nigeria's blueprints were as ambitious as East Asia's; what was missing was sustained leadership; and AI raises the cost of that absence.

### The hybrid future — VERIFIED, and it matches the UNIDO brief exactly
- **Verbatim:** *"**The future will be hybrid—a fusion of digital and physical production. Africa's competitive strength will lie in agribusiness and resource-based industrialisation, powered by green technology, regional integration, and digital platforms. Innovation will define who moves from consumer to creator.**"*
- And on 4IR and traditional sectors: *"By **modernising rather than abandoning traditional sectors. Agriculture, for instance, can absorb digital technologies—drones, analytics, and agro-processing—to create new value chains. **The Fourth Industrial Revolution does not replace the old economy; it reprograms it.**"*
- **Caveat — a genuinely important convergence.** **"The Fourth Industrial Revolution does not replace the old economy; it reprograms it"** is the same claim as UNIDO Policy Brief No. 30's finding that *"the main industrial opportunity offered by AI lies in using it to boost the productivity and efficiency of existing industries."* **He said it in March 2026; the UNIDO brief said it in March 2026; neither cites the other.** See `evidence-base-ai-and-industrial-catching-up.md`. Two independent arrivals at the same conclusion is the strongest form this collection has.

### His proudest AfDB work
- **Verbatim:** *"two initiatives stand out: **The Industrialise Africa Strategy**, which mobilised billions in co-financing for manufacturing zones and infrastructure; and the **Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones**, which blend agriculture with modern industry."*
- **Correction:** this dojo previously called these "Agro-Industrial Processing Zones." The name is **Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zones (SAPZ)**.
- **Caveat:** "mobilised billions in co-financing" is his own characterisation, unsourced. `[CHECK]`

### Intellectual lineage — new, and it links three other dojos
- **Verbatim:** *"Books by **Joseph Schumpeter, Albert Hirschman, Chris Freeman, Dr. Pius Okigbo**, a great Nigerian economist shaped my understanding of innovation and development while **John Kenneth Galbraith** influenced my attitude as a public intellectual. My lecturers and supervisors at the **Science Policy Research Unit (SPRU), University of Sussex** and **Prof. Adedotun Philips, NISER, Ibadan** taught me to **think critically but act pragmatically**."*
- **Caveat — read the list.** **Chris Freeman founded SPRU and co-founded the national systems of innovation approach alongside Lundvall.** **Hirschman** is the source of the linkage argument that runs through his resource-based industrialisation work. **Schumpeter** is Keun Lee's tradition. **This is not a roster of tastes; it is the lineage of the framework, and it puts him one step from Lundvall by supervision.**

### Method, and the thing he wants remembered
- **Verbatim:** *"academia taught me to **look for patterns behind problems**. In policy work, that translates into **identifying structural constraints rather than chasing symptomatic fixes**. My research background guides me to base recommendations on evidence and comparative learning—**understanding why South Korea or Malaysia succeeded, but also why Africa must chart its own path rather than imitate.**"*
- *"my deepest wish is that my work demonstrates how **Africa's transformation must be knowledge-led, innovation-driven, and institutionally grounded**. If my scholarship and advisory work have helped bridge the gap between theory and practice—**between policy documents and tangible factories, startups, and research networks**—then I would consider that a legacy worth leaving."*
- Next: *"perhaps establishing a **Pan-African Center for Innovation Policy, Industrialisation, Technological Change and Development**. I also plan to **document Africa's overlooked industrial experiments—less as nostalgia, more as instruction.**"*

### Biography — verified additions
- **First class in chemical engineering, best graduating student**, University of Ife (now Obafemi Awolowo University); master's at **Toronto**, whose chemical engineering department he notes was *"ranked third in all North America"*; the switch to development economics was *"a divinely guided move **not out of an inability to continue in engineering**."*
- Trained masters and doctoral students at **NISER** and **UNU-INTECH, Maastricht**.
- **Faith and family are central and he says so repeatedly**: *"Family, faith, and the mentorship of young scholars give me strength"*; *"A strong family foundation rooted in scriptural principles helps steer the Journey"*; *"Being alive is by God's amazing Grace."*
- The two hurdles as the first professor in the field: **conceptual misunderstanding** — *"why a nation needs experts who have deep connections to engineering and economics"*, and colleagues who *"could not initially see how technological capability building relates to economic structure or **how innovation policy differs from industrial policy**"* — and **institutional inertia**.
- The friend's remark, which he still quotes: *"'You are a brilliant fellow, how will you even get a job dabbling into this thing nobody understands.'"*
- On Africa's youth: *"watching the continent's youth confidently design, build, and export knowledge-based solutions is deeply gratifying... **Despite all our troubles, we have several unicorns mostly fintech doing remarkably well.**"*
- **Caveat — note the collision with Kaplan.** Kaplan's March 2024 TIPS brief: *"Over the past five years, Nigeria has produced five technology unicorns. South Africa has produced none."* **Oyelaran-Oyeyinka is citing those Nigerian unicorns as evidence of progress; Kaplan cited them as evidence of South African failure. Same firms, two national arguments.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy wants to cite his AI caution to argue that the province should not pursue an AI agenda.

**Application:** That inverts him. The sentence is verified — *"A future shaped by Artificial Intelligence (AI) is going to make industrial catching up very difficult"* — but read what surrounds it. It follows *"The future will be hybrid—a fusion of digital and physical production"* and *"The Fourth Industrial Revolution does not replace the old economy; it reprograms it."* And it precedes his account of why Nigeria failed: blueprints as ambitious as East Asia's, undone by the absence of sustained leadership. **He is saying AI raises the penalty for the absence of capability and leadership — not that the technology is a reason to stand back.** The strategy he supports is the reprogramming one: agriculture absorbing drones, analytics and agro-processing; agribusiness and resource-based industrialisation with digital platforms on top. **Which is, to the word, the Western Cape's actual base.**

**Result:** An AI position that quotes him correctly and points at agri-processing rather than away from technology.
