# Principles-Focused Evaluation

## When to Use
- When the programme or initiative is defined by its guiding principles rather than by a fixed model, manual, or protocol.
- When evaluating networks, coalitions, or multi-site initiatives where local adaptation is expected and principles provide the coherence across sites.
- When assessing whether an organisation's stated principles actually guide action or are merely aspirational statements.

## Fails When
- **The initiative has a clear programme model.** If the programme has a detailed implementation manual and operates the same way everywhere, conventional evaluation of fidelity and outcomes is more appropriate.
- **The principles are not principles.** Vague aspirational statements ("we value excellence") are not evaluable principles. The GUIDE framework helps determine whether stated principles are genuine guides to action.
- **You apply it without stakeholder engagement.** P-FE requires engaging stakeholders in assessing whether principles are meaningful and operational, not just evaluator judgment.

## Core Concept
Some initiatives are guided not by detailed programme models but by principles — general rules, guidelines, or values that inform action across diverse and changing contexts. Principles-focused evaluation provides a framework for evaluating whether these principles are meaningful, whether they are being followed, and whether they are producing the intended results.

The GUIDE framework provides five criteria for assessing the quality of principles. A good principle provides meaningful **Guidance** — it tells people what to do and what not to do. It is **Useful** — people actually consult and apply it when making decisions. It is **Inspiring** — it motivates commitment and action. It is **Developmentally adaptable** — it can be applied across diverse and changing contexts without losing its meaning. And it is **Evaluable** — you can assess whether it is being followed and whether it is producing results.

P-FE evaluates at three levels: Are the principles being adhered to (fidelity)? How are they being operationalised in practice (implementation)? Are they producing the intended outcomes (effectiveness)? This three-level assessment captures both whether people are following the principles and whether the principles themselves are good guides to action.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify the guiding principles.** What principles does the initiative claim to be guided by? Are they documented? Are they known to participants?
2. **Apply the GUIDE framework.** Assess each principle against the five criteria. Principles that fail the GUIDE test need strengthening before they can be evaluated.
3. **Assess adherence.** Through observation, interviews, and document review, determine whether the principles are actually guiding action in practice.
4. **Assess operationalisation.** How are participants interpreting and applying the principles? What does each principle look like in practice across different contexts?
5. **Assess effectiveness.** Are the principles producing the intended results? What evidence connects principle-guided action to outcomes?

## Examples
**Situation:** A national youth development network operates across multiple provinces. Each provincial chapter adapts programming to local contexts, guided by five core principles: youth agency, community embeddedness, evidence-informed practice, equity, and sustainability.
**Application:** P-FE evaluates at three levels. GUIDE assessment: are the five principles genuinely actionable? "Youth agency" is evaluable (observable actions like youth-led programming). "Sustainability" is vague and needs operational definition. Adherence: through site visits and interviews, assess whether provincial chapters actually use the principles in programming decisions. Effectiveness: connect principle-adherent practice to youth outcomes across diverse contexts.
**Result:** The evaluation strengthens the principles themselves (sharpening those that fail GUIDE), documents how strong chapters operationalise them, and generates cross-site learning about which principle applications produce the best outcomes.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Evaluate principles as if they were a programme model.
**Why:** Principles guide action; they do not prescribe it. Evaluating principle adherence is not the same as evaluating model fidelity. The same principle may look different in different contexts while being equally well applied.

**Don't:** Accept vague values as evaluable principles.
**Why:** "We believe in excellence" is not a principle. It provides no guidance, is not evaluable, and cannot be assessed for adherence. Apply the GUIDE framework to distinguish genuine principles from aspirational platitudes.
