# Blue Marble Evaluation

## When to Use
- When evaluating initiatives that address global challenges — climate change, sustainable development, pandemics, inequality, migration — where local and global are interconnected.
- When the evaluand involves complex networks of stakeholders working across national boundaries, sectors, and scales.
- When conventional evaluation frameworks cannot capture the systemic, cross-boundary, and long-term nature of the change being pursued.

## Fails When
- **The programme is genuinely local and bounded.** Blue Marble evaluation is designed for global-local interconnection. For a local programme with clear boundaries, it adds unnecessary complexity.
- **You use global framing to avoid local accountability.** Invoking planetary complexity is not a licence to avoid being specific about what the programme achieved, for whom, at what cost.
- **The initiative talks about systems change but acts at the project level.** If the intervention is actually a conventional project with global rhetoric, evaluate the project, not the rhetoric.

## Core Concept
Blue Marble evaluation takes its name from the iconic photograph of Earth from space — a reminder that we inhabit one interconnected planet. It provides a framework for evaluating transformative initiatives that work toward a sustainable future across national boundaries, sectors, and issues.

Four overarching principles define the Blue Marble perspective: think and act globally, attend to and engage with the whole interconnected system (GLOCAL), apply integration and boundary-crossing to understand issues and act, and transform systems for a sustainable future. Twelve operating principles provide more specific guidance for evaluation practice, organised around global thinking, systems framing, transformation orientation, and evaluation rigour.

Blue Marble evaluation is rooted in utilization-focused, developmental, and principles-focused evaluation. It extends these approaches to the global scale, addressing challenges that traditional evaluation frameworks were not designed for — challenges where the evaluand is a complex network rather than a bounded programme, where outcomes are emergent and long-term, and where sustainability and equity are central concerns.

The practical implication for evaluators working on development programmes in South Africa or other developing contexts is that local programmes increasingly operate within global systems — supply chains, climate impacts, migration patterns, pandemic responses, financial flows. Evaluation that treats the local programme as self-contained misses the systemic connections that shape its outcomes.

## How to Apply
1. **Map the global-local connections.** How does the local initiative connect to global systems? What global forces shape local outcomes? What local actions contribute to global change?
2. **Apply Blue Marble principles.** Use the four overarching principles and relevant operating principles to frame the evaluation — not as a checklist but as lenses for seeing systemic connections.
3. **Evaluate across boundaries.** Design the evaluation to capture cross-boundary dynamics — between sectors, between scales, between stakeholders with different perspectives.
4. **Centre sustainability and equity.** Blue Marble evaluation explicitly centres long-term sustainability and equity as evaluation criteria, not just short-term programme effectiveness.

## Examples
**Situation:** A South African climate adaptation programme works with coastal municipalities to build resilience to sea-level rise and extreme weather events, funded by a multilateral climate fund.
**Application:** Conventional evaluation would assess whether municipalities implemented adaptation plans and achieved resilience indicators. Blue Marble evaluation adds systemic dimensions: how do local adaptation actions connect to global climate systems? Are adaptation strategies equitable across communities? Do local interventions contribute to or undermine broader sustainability goals? How do global funding mechanisms shape local implementation? The evaluation connects local municipal action to the global climate challenge, evaluating both local results and systemic contribution.
**Result:** The evaluation captures not just whether the programme "worked" locally but whether it contributed to meaningful systems change — building genuine resilience, promoting equitable adaptation, and generating learning for the global climate adaptation community.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Apply Blue Marble framing to every evaluation.
**Why:** Most evaluations are properly local. Blue Marble evaluation is for initiatives that genuinely engage with global-local interconnections. Inflating a local programme to global scale obscures rather than illuminates.

**Don't:** Use systems complexity as an excuse for vague findings.
**Why:** Even in complex global systems, evaluation must be specific about what happened, what was learned, and what should change. Complexity demands more precision, not less.
