# Trust in Numbers

## When to Use
- When interpreting any official or published statistic.
- When designing data systems.
- When using data for policy advocacy.

## Fails When
- **The framework slides into nihilism about all data.** Some data is more reliable than others; the work is calibration.

## Core Concept

A theme across Deaton's work is the difficulty of producing reliable numbers and the corresponding need for calibrated trust in them. The key points:

**Numbers are constructed.** Every published statistic involves methodological choices — sampling, weighting, classification, aggregation. The choices affect the number. The same underlying reality can produce different numbers under different methodologies.

**Construction is often political.** What gets measured reflects political-economy decisions about what matters. What doesn't get measured is often what those in power don't want measured (informal-sector activity, gender-based violence in certain contexts, environmental damage).

**Comparability across time and place is harder than it looks.** Changes in measurement methodology break time series; differences in measurement across countries undermine cross-country comparison. Headline statistics often present incomparable data as comparable.

**Recent revisions are common and consequential.** GDP estimates are routinely revised, sometimes substantially. Poverty estimates change when methodology updates. The published number is rarely the final word.

**Statistical agencies face political pressure.** In some contexts, statistical-agency independence is partial. Numbers that contradict political preferences face pressure. The 2024 controversy over India's poverty statistics, the Argentine inflation-measurement crisis, and various other cases show the pattern.

**Survey methodology matters substantially.** Recall periods, item lists, question phrasing, sample design all shape what surveys find. Two surveys of the same population can produce different results from methodological differences alone.

**Indicators take on a life of their own.** Once an indicator is published and used in policy, the indicator itself becomes a target — and gets gamed, manipulated, or distorted. Goodhart's Law: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

**The right response is not nihilism but calibration.** Different numbers warrant different levels of trust. National-statistical-agency vital-registration data is generally more reliable than donor-funded one-off surveys. Multiple-source triangulation is more reliable than single-source. Methodology-transparent estimates are more reliable than black-box ones. The work is to calibrate appropriately.

**For South Africa specifically:** Stats SA is among Africa's more credible statistical agencies but faces budget pressures and methodological challenges. The Census 2022 had substantial undercount issues. Vital registration is incomplete in some areas. NIDS-CRAM during COVID provided important data but with its own methodological limits. The country's data ecosystem is comparatively strong but requires calibrated engagement.

## How to Apply

1. **Always engage methodology** before engaging conclusions.
2. **Use multiple sources** to triangulate.
3. **Be alert to political-economy** of measurement.
4. **Distinguish levels from trends** — trends are more reliable if methodology is constant.

## Examples

**Situation:** A debate about South African unemployment trends.

**Application:** Stats SA's Quarterly Labour Force Survey provides the main data. Methodology has changed substantially since the 1990s. International comparability requires care. Narrow and expanded definitions tell different stories. The right approach: use the trend with methodology consistent; supplement with other indicators (NIDS, administrative data); be honest about what the data can and can't support.

**Result:** A data engagement that supports rather than overstates the conclusions.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat any single statistic as definitive.
**Why:** Construction matters; triangulation strengthens inference.

**Don't:** Use methodological complexity to dismiss inconvenient data.
**Why:** Calibrated trust is the goal; selective dismissal is bad faith.
