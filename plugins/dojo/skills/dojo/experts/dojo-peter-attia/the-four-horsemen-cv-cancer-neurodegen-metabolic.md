# The Four Horsemen

## When to Use
- When the user asks what they should actually be afraid of.
- When prioritising a longevity strategy — these are the targets.
- When explaining why metabolic health sits upstream of everything else.
- When someone is scattering effort across trivial risks and missing the big four.

## Fails When
- **The user wants the detailed prevention plan for one horseman.** Route to apoB/screening (cardiovascular), screening (cancer), sleep/metabolic/exercise (neurodegenerative), glucose-insulin (metabolic).
- **You treat the four as separate silos.** Metabolic dysfunction amplifies the other three; they share mechanisms and risk factors.

## Core Concept
If you do not die of an accident, an overdose, suicide, or an acute infection, you will almost certainly die of one of four things. Attia calls them the Four Horsemen, and a longevity strategy is, concretely, a strategy against this specific list.

**Horseman 1 — Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD).** Heart attacks and strokes. The single largest killer in the developed world. It is also, in Attia's view, the most preventable of the four, because we understand its principal causal driver — apoB-containing lipoproteins — and we have safe, effective, cheap tools to lower them. The disease is built silently over decades as apoB particles penetrate the artery wall and accumulate as plaque. The tragedy is that the first symptom is frequently the event itself: roughly half of first major cardiac events occur in people who had no prior warning. This is the horseman where early, sustained intervention has the clearest payoff.

**Horseman 2 — Cancer.** The second great killer, and the one where prevention is hardest because the biology is heterogeneous and the causal levers are fewer. The two big modifiable risk factors are smoking and metabolic dysfunction (obesity and insulin resistance raise risk for many cancers). Beyond risk reduction, Attia's emphasis is on early detection — finding cancer when it is still localised and curable rather than after metastasis, because survival is overwhelmingly determined by stage at diagnosis. This is why he favours aggressive, earlier-than-guideline screening (earlier colonoscopy, and considering advanced modalities) for those whose risk warrants it.

**Horseman 3 — Neurodegenerative disease.** Alzheimer's and related dementias. The horseman people fear most, because it takes the self before it takes the body. There is no cure, which makes prevention and risk reduction the entire game. The major modifiable levers are the same ones that serve the other horsemen — exercise (the most powerful), sleep (critical for amyloid clearance), metabolic health, and cardiovascular health (vascular contribution to cognitive decline is large). Knowing your ApoE genotype sharpens how hard you push these levers, since the e4 allele substantially raises risk.

**Horseman 4 — Metabolic dysfunction.** Type 2 diabetes and the whole spectrum that precedes it — insulin resistance, hyperinsulinemia, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Attia singles this one out because it is not merely a fourth disease sitting beside the others — it is *upstream* of them. Insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia independently raise the risk of cardiovascular disease, multiple cancers, and Alzheimer's (sometimes called "type 3 diabetes" for the metabolic contribution to neurodegeneration). Get metabolic health right and you lower the tide for the other three horsemen simultaneously. This is why metabolic markers — fasting insulin, an OGTT with insulin, triglycerides, visceral fat — get such weight in his screening.

The strategic insight is that these four are not random. They share risk factors (inactivity, poor metabolic health, poor sleep, smoking) and mechanisms (chronic inflammation, insulin resistance, oxidative stress). The same core interventions — exercise as the most potent, then metabolic health, sleep, nutrition, and the targeted lipid and screening work — push back on all four at once. You do not need four separate programmes. You need one well-built programme that happens to defeat four diseases, plus a few horseman-specific instruments (apoB lowering for ASCVD, screening for cancer, ApoE-informed intensity for dementia).

The honest asymmetry across the four: cardiovascular disease is the most preventable and gets the most decisive tools; cancer prevention is weaker but early detection is powerful; neurodegeneration has no cure so the leverage is entirely upstream; and metabolic dysfunction is the master variable that, managed well, lowers all of them. That ranking is why a longevity plan spends so much energy on lipids, fitness, and metabolic health — they buy down the largest, most tractable portions of total risk.

## How to Apply
1. **Name the target.** Anchor any longevity plan on these four, not on diffuse "wellness."
2. **Treat metabolic health as the master lever.** Improving it lowers risk across the other three; check insulin and glucose early.
3. **Match the tool to the horseman.** ASCVD → apoB/Lp(a) and lipid lowering. Cancer → risk reduction + early detection. Neurodegeneration → exercise, sleep, metabolic and vascular health, ApoE awareness. Metabolic → glucose/insulin, exercise, body composition.
4. **Exploit the shared base.** Exercise, sleep, and metabolic health pay off against all four — build these first.
5. **Use family history to set intensity.** A strong family history of any horseman raises the aggressiveness of screening and intervention for it.

## Examples
**Situation:** A 55-year-old wants to "do everything" but is overwhelmed and chasing minor risks (a trendy toxin scare).
**Application:** Redirect to the four that actually account for mortality. The trace-toxin worry is rounding error next to apoB, fitness, and metabolic health. Build the shared base, then add the horseman-specific instruments.
**Result:** Effort concentrates where the hazard actually is.

**Situation:** Someone with strong family history of Alzheimer's asks what to do, fearing nothing can be done.
**Application:** No cure means prevention is the whole game, and the levers exist: exercise (most potent), sleep for amyloid clearance, tight metabolic and cardiovascular control, and ApoE genotyping to calibrate intensity. The same work also lowers the other three horsemen.
**Result:** Fear converts into a concrete, evidence-based programme.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat the horsemen as four unrelated projects.
**Why:** They share risk factors and mechanisms; metabolic dysfunction feeds the other three. One good programme addresses all four.

**Don't:** Over-index on cancer's scary reputation while ignoring the more preventable cardiovascular disease.
**Why:** ASCVD kills more and is more tractable. Allocate effort to where prevention works best.

**Don't:** Assume neurodegeneration is pure fate.
**Why:** There's no cure, but the modifiable risk is substantial — inaction is the real fatalism.
