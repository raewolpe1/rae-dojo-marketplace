# Medicine 3.0

## When to Use
- When the user wants Attia's overall philosophy before any specific number.
- When explaining why he screens and intervenes decades earlier than guidelines.
- When distinguishing healthspan (years lived well) from lifespan (years lived).
- When someone asks why "feeling fine" is not reassurance.

## Fails When
- **The user wants a specific protocol or value.** Medicine 3.0 is the frame, not the prescription — route to screening, apoB, exercise, etc.
- **You frame it as anti-establishment.** It is not anti-medicine. Medicine 2.0 is miraculous at acute care; the argument is that it is the wrong tool against slow chronic disease.
- **You make it sound like biohacking.** It is conservative, evidence-weighted clinical reasoning applied earlier, not a gadget culture.

## Core Concept
Medicine 3.0 is Attia's name for a reorientation of medicine around prevention, individual risk, and the right endpoint. To understand it you have to see what it replaces.

**Medicine 1.0** was pre-scientific — Hippocratic guesswork and humours. **Medicine 2.0** is the medicine of the germ theory, the randomised trial, and the hospital: extraordinary at acute, fast problems — trauma, infection, a heart attack in progress. It is the reason a bacterial pneumonia is no longer a death sentence. But Medicine 2.0 has a structural blind spot. It is built to wait for disease to present, name it, and treat it. Against the chronic diseases that actually kill most people in the developed world, that model fails, because those diseases are not events — they are slow processes that begin decades before they are diagnosable. By the time atherosclerosis, cancer, Alzheimer's, or type 2 diabetes earns its name, the process has usually been running for ten, twenty, thirty years and has already won most of the ground.

Medicine 3.0 changes four things.

**First, it is preventive rather than reactive.** It acts on the disease process while it is still subclinical — while you still feel completely fine — rather than waiting for symptoms. "Come back when something's wrong" is exactly the wrong instruction for a disease that is silent until it is advanced.

**Second, it is risk-weighted to the individual.** Population guidelines are designed for cost-effectiveness at scale and for the average person. But you are not the average person. Your family history, your Lp(a), your ApoE genotype, your metabolic markers define a personal risk that may be far above or below the average. Medicine 3.0 sets the aggressiveness of screening and intervention to *your* risk, not the population's.

**Third, it treats the patient's own goals as the endpoint.** The objective is not an abstract "health." It is the specific physical and cognitive capacity the patient wants to retain — which is why the planning device is the Marginal Decade and the Centenarian Decathlon.

**Fourth, and most fundamentally, it optimises healthspan, not just lifespan.** Lifespan is how long you live. Healthspan is how long you live *well* — free of physical decline, cognitive decline, and disease. Adding years of frailty and dementia is not the goal; in fact it can be the opposite of the goal. The aim is to compress morbidity: stay fully functional to the very end, then decline rapidly, rather than spending a long, slow descent in a diminished state.

The practical consequence of all this is a tolerance for acting under uncertainty when the asymmetry is favourable. If an intervention is cheap, safe, and the downside of inaction is one of the Four Horsemen, Medicine 3.0 acts on probabilistic evidence rather than waiting for the certainty that, in chronic disease, only arrives too late. The cost of an extra test or a well-tolerated drug is small; the cost of a preventable heart attack or a preventable dementia is everything. That asymmetry — not recklessness — is what justifies moving early.

This is the frame that organises every other topic in this skill. apoB, VO2max, screening, protein, sleep — each is an instrument of the same project: reduce the lifetime probability of the diseases that disable and kill, and protect the function that makes a long life worth having.

## How to Apply
1. **Start every health question with: what is the long process here, and where is it now?** Not "do I have a disease?" but "where am I on the curve, and what's the trajectory?"
2. **Reframe "I feel fine" as uninformative.** Subclinical disease is, by definition, asymptomatic. Reassurance has to come from data, not feeling.
3. **Set aggressiveness by individual risk, not the guideline floor.** A guideline is a population minimum; weight up where the person's specific risk warrants.
4. **Choose the endpoint deliberately.** Define what "living well" concretely means for this person, then work backward to today's decisions.
5. **Use the asymmetry test for early action.** Cheap + safe + large downside of inaction = act now on probabilistic evidence.

## Examples
**Situation:** A 48-year-old with no diagnoses says "my doctor sees no reason to do anything — everything's normal."
**Application:** "Normal" in Medicine 2.0 means "no disease named yet," which is not the same as "low future risk." Medicine 3.0 asks what the trajectory is: get apoB, Lp(a), fasting insulin, a fitness measure. The absence of a diagnosis at 48 is the opportunity, not the all-clear.
**Result:** The person shifts from waiting to measuring, and acts while the leverage is highest.

**Situation:** Someone wants to "wait for more certain evidence" before lowering an elevated apoB at 50.
**Application:** Apply the asymmetry. apoB lowering is cheap, the drugs are safe and well-studied, and the downside of waiting is cumulative arterial exposure you can't recover. Certainty in chronic disease arrives as the event itself. Act.
**Result:** Early, sustained lowering — the lifetime dose is reduced rather than waited out.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat Medicine 3.0 as rejecting conventional medicine.
**Why:** It depends on Medicine 2.0's tools and trials. It applies them earlier and more individually; it does not discard them.

**Don't:** Let it become a licence for unproven biohacks.
**Why:** The framework is evidence-weighted. Acting early under favourable asymmetry is not the same as chasing every supplement or gadget.

**Don't:** Optimise lifespan while ignoring healthspan.
**Why:** Years added in decline can be a cost, not a benefit. The endpoint is function, not merely duration.
