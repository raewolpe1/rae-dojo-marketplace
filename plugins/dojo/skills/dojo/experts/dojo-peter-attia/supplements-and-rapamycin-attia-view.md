# Supplements and Rapamycin — the Attia View

## When to Use
- When someone asks what supplements or longevity drugs to take.
- When grading the evidence: proven vs promising vs unsupported.
- When countering supplement-industry hype with an empirical stance.
- When the question is about rapamycin, metformin, NAD precursors, etc.

## Fails When
- **The question is about lipid drugs for heart disease.** Route to apoB — statins/PCSK9 are outcome-proven and handled there.
- **You present unproven longevity drugs as established.** The discipline is grading evidence honestly; overstating it is the failure.

## Core Concept
Attia's stance on supplements and longevity pharmacology is defined by one principle: **grade the evidence honestly, and act in proportion to its strength.** This makes him simultaneously more willing than naturalists to use real drugs and far more skeptical than the wellness industry about supplements. The organising move is to sort interventions into proven, promising, and unsupported — and never to launder one category as another.

**Proven, outcome-backed drugs — used confidently where individual risk warrants.** This is where Attia is aggressive. **Statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors** for lowering apoB have decades of hard cardiovascular-outcome evidence; he uses them without hesitation (detailed in the apoB file). **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (semaglutide, tirzepatide) have strong evidence for weight loss and metabolic improvement and he regards them as genuinely useful tools for obesity and metabolic disease, while noting the need to preserve muscle mass alongside them (the lean-mass-loss caveat). These are not "longevity drugs" in the speculative sense — they treat specific Horsemen with proven outcomes, and that earns confident use.

**Rapamycin — the most interesting promising candidate, explicitly not proven.** Rapamycin (an mTOR inhibitor) is the geroscience molecule with the strongest preclinical longevity signal — it extends lifespan in multiple animal models, including mice, more reliably than almost any other intervention, and the mechanistic story (mTOR's role in aging, autophagy, cellular senescence) is compelling. Attia finds it the single most interesting longevity drug and has discussed using it off-label himself at intermittent dosing. But he is scrupulous about the caveat: **there is not yet randomised human evidence that rapamycin extends human lifespan or healthspan.** He holds it as *promising, not proven* — a reasoned bet under uncertainty for those who choose it with eyes open and medical supervision, not an established recommendation. This is the exact distinction his whole method demands: a strong animal signal and good mechanism is a reason for interest and careful trials, not a reason to claim proof.

**Metformin — interesting but downgraded.** Once a leading longevity candidate (the TAME trial premise), Attia has become more cautious, partly because of evidence that metformin may **blunt the muscle and fitness adaptations to exercise** — and since exercise is his most potent longevity lever, a drug that interferes with it carries a real cost. He treats the longevity case for metformin in non-diabetics as weaker than once thought.

**Supplements — mostly skepticism, a few defensible.** Here Attia is firmly empirical and unimpressed by the industry. "Natural" is not an argument, the regulatory bar is low, quality control is poor, and the great majority of products have no credible evidence for longevity benefit. **NAD precursors (NR, NMN)** — much hyped, but the human longevity evidence is thin; he's skeptical of the marketing. He will use specific supplements where there's a reasonable rationale and acceptable safety: **vitamin D** where deficient, **omega-3 (EPA/DHA)** for some, **magnesium**, and others chosen for individual indication rather than blanket use. The default posture is that a supplement must *earn its place* with evidence and a specific reason — and most do not.

The throughline is the asymmetry-and-evidence discipline from Medicine 3.0. For interventions with strong outcome evidence and favourable safety (statins, GLP-1s where indicated), act confidently. For promising-but-unproven agents (rapamycin), label the uncertainty honestly and treat use as a personal bet, not a recommendation. For the unsupported majority of supplements, decline to pretend. And crucially, Attia repeatedly reminds people that **no pill rivals the proven levers**: exercise, sleep, nutrition, metabolic health, and apoB lowering deliver benefits that dwarf anything in the speculative-supplement category. The person spending energy and money optimising a supplement stack while undertraining and under-sleeping has the priorities backwards. Pills are, at best, a small supplement to the foundational behaviours — never a substitute for them.

## How to Apply
1. **Sort by evidence first.** Proven (statins, GLP-1s for indication) → use confidently; promising (rapamycin) → label as a bet; unsupported (most supplements, NAD precursors) → decline to overstate.
2. **Use rapamycin language carefully.** Strong animal data, good mechanism, no human longevity proof yet — promising, not proven; only with supervision.
3. **Downgrade metformin for non-diabetics.** Note the evidence it may blunt exercise adaptations — a real cost given exercise's primacy.
4. **Make supplements earn their place.** Use specific ones for specific indications (e.g., vitamin D if deficient, omega-3); reject blanket stacks and "natural" as an argument.
5. **Keep pills in proportion.** No supplement rivals exercise, sleep, nutrition, and apoB lowering — fix those before optimising a stack.

## Examples
**Situation:** "Should I take NMN and resveratrol for longevity?"
**Application:** The human longevity evidence for NAD precursors is thin and the resveratrol story has largely not held up. Attia would decline to endorse them and redirect the energy and money to the proven levers — training, sleep, metabolic health, apoB.
**Result:** Resources move from hype to high-yield basics.

**Situation:** "You mentioned rapamycin — should I just start it?"
**Application:** It's the most interesting candidate, with strong animal data and a good mechanism, but no proof it extends human lifespan. That makes it a personal, supervised bet under uncertainty — not an established recommendation. Get the proven foundations dialed first.
**Result:** The uncertainty is named honestly; any use is informed and supervised.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present rapamycin (or metformin, NAD) as proven longevity drugs.
**Why:** The human longevity evidence isn't there; overstating it is exactly the imprecision Attia opposes.

**Don't:** Accept "natural" or industry marketing as evidence.
**Why:** The regulatory bar is low and most supplements have no credible longevity data; each must earn its place.

**Don't:** Let a supplement stack substitute for the foundational behaviours.
**Why:** Exercise, sleep, nutrition, and apoB lowering dwarf any pill; priorities inverted is the common error.
