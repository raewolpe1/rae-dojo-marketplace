# The Ten Elements

## When to Use
- Whenever "ecosystem" appears without specification.
- When mapping an existing pillar structure onto a defensible framework.
- As the first move in any ecosystem review.

## Fails When
- **The unit is a firm, not a territory.** The ecosystem is a territorial construct.

## Core Concept

An entrepreneurial ecosystem consists of **all the interdependent actors and factors that enable and constrain entrepreneurship within a particular territory**. That is a definition. The analysis begins when the actors and factors are named.

**Framework conditions (4)** — the deep, slow, largely national conditions within which the system sits:
1. **Formal institutions** — rule of law, property rights, regulatory quality, contract enforcement.
2. **Culture** — societal attitudes to entrepreneurship, risk, failure, and who is permitted to be an entrepreneur.
3. **Physical infrastructure** — transport, energy, connectivity, premises.
4. **Demand** — the market for what the ecosystem's firms sell. Public and private. **Almost universally omitted from strategies.**

**Systemic conditions (6)** — the interactive, network-based conditions that a regional actor can plausibly build:
5. **Networks** — connections among entrepreneurs and between entrepreneurs and other actors; the channel through which everything else flows.
6. **Leadership** — the individuals and institutions who provide direction and legitimacy for the ecosystem itself.
7. **Finance** — capital, differentiated by phase.
8. **Talent** — the human capital available to firms, including managerial and technical.
9. **New knowledge** — R&D, universities, technological and market knowledge.
10. **Support services / intermediaries** — BDS, accelerators, incubators, professional services.

**The chain.** Elements → **outputs** (entrepreneurial activity) → **outcomes** (aggregate value creation: productivity, income, employment, well-being).

**The three propositions.**
- **Co-evolution** — elements evolve in interdependence; a systems view is required, not a checklist.
- **Upward causation** — ecosystem quality predicts entrepreneurial output, particularly the rate of high-growth firms.
- **Downward causation** — successful entrepreneurs reinvest in and reshape the ecosystem. Entrepreneurship is not only the output of the system; entrepreneurs are important players in creating the ecosystem and keeping it healthy.

**Phase differentiation.** The ecosystem context differs by phase of the entrepreneurial process (Stam & Bosma 2015). This is most obvious for **finance**: nascent entrepreneurs, start-ups, moderately growing firms, high-growth firms and unicorns have substantially different finance needs. An undifferentiated finance element is under-specified.

## How to Apply

1. **Map the strategy's pillars onto the ten elements.** Produce the crosswalk as a table. The unmapped elements are the finding.
2. **Look for demand.** It is nearly always absent, and it is a first-order framework condition. Public procurement is the most direct instrument and is usually buried in a market access clause.
3. **Differentiate finance by phase.** Five phases, five different instruments.
4. **Check that support services have not been mistaken for the ecosystem.** Intermediaries are one element of ten. Most strategies are 60% intermediaries.
5. **Ask where downward causation lives.** If entrepreneurs only receive, the model is half-implemented.

## Examples

**Situation:** A seven-pillar provincial MSME strategy.

**Application:** Crosswalk the seven pillars to the ten elements. Typical result: governance/coordination maps to leadership; intelligence/evidence maps loosely to new knowledge; regulatory maps to formal institutions; infrastructure/spatial maps to physical infrastructure; market access maps partially to demand and partially to networks; finance maps to finance; capability/BDS maps to talent plus support services. **Unmapped: culture (unless a cross-cutting theme carries it), and demand as a systematically addressed condition rather than a market access sub-clause.** That crosswalk is a defensible annexure and an argument for a demand chapter.

**Result:** The pillar structure is validated against an external framework and the gaps become explicit.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat "ecosystem" as a synonym for "everyone involved".
**Why:** It is a specified set of elements with a causal chain, or it is a picture.

**Don't:** Omit demand.
**Why:** It is a framework condition. Firms with no customers do not grow regardless of how well supported they are.

**Don't:** Leave finance undifferentiated.
**Why:** Nascent, start-up, growing and high-growth firms have substantially different finance needs.
