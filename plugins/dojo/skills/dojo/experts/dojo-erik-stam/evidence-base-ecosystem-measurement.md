# Evidence Base — Ecosystem Measurement

> **Provenance.** Compiled July 2026 from the sources named in each entry. Verify against the primary source before quoting in a client deliverable. Entries marked `[CHECK]` have not been verified to the primary source and must not be quoted without checking.

## When to Use
- Designing an ecosystem measurement instrument or index.
- When a strategy proposes a concentrated single-pillar bet.
- When asked what the ecosystem approach has actually established.

## Fails When
- **The exercise is qualitative ecosystem mapping** for stakeholder engagement — different purpose, no index required.

## Headline

| Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Ecosystem quality relates to high-growth firm prevalence | Strong relationship, Dutch provinces | **Supported (association)** |
| Individual elements relate to high-growth firm prevalence | **No** | **Not supported** |
| Elements are interdependent | Strong interrelationships | Supported |
| Ecosystems can be measured and indexed | Yes — instrument exists | Supported |
| The approach has deep causal evidence | **No — "rather limited"**, per Stam himself | **Not supported** |

## THE EVIDENCE

### The measurement instrument and the index
- **Study:** Stam & van de Ven (2021), *Small Business Economics* 56(2): 809–832, "Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Elements". DOI 10.1007/s11187-019-00270-6. (Earlier as Utrecht University School of Economics working paper 19-07, June 2019.)
- **Country:** Netherlands (12 provinces).
- **Intervention:** Not an intervention — a measurement exercise. A systems framework for studying entrepreneurial ecosystems; a measurement instrument for its elements; an entrepreneurial ecosystem index composed from them.
- **Finding:** **The prevalence of high-growth firms in a region is strongly related to the quality of its entrepreneurial ecosystem.** **Strong interrelationships among the ecosystem elements reveal their interdependence and the need for a systems perspective.**
- **Caveat:** Association, not causation. One country. A small, rich, densely governed, institutionally homogeneous country — arguably the least demanding possible test of a framework intended for territorial variation.

### The inconvenient result — the index relates, the elements do not
- **Study:** Stam (2017), "Measuring Entrepreneurial Ecosystems", Utrecht University School of Economics discussion paper 17-11.
- **Country:** Netherlands (12 provinces).
- **Intervention:** Element measurement, index composition, relation to output measured by high-growth firm indicators.
- **Finding:** **The prevalence of high-growth firms relates to the overall value of the entrepreneurial ecosystem index, but not to individual elements.**
- **Caveat:** **This is the single most policy-relevant finding in the file and the one most often ignored.** It undercuts the standard policy move of identifying a bottleneck element and funding it. The systems reading — that elements co-evolve and only the configuration matters — is consistent with the interdependence result, but it is an uncomfortable basis for a budget allocation.

### The scale of regional variation
- **Study:** Stam & van de Ven working paper (2019), as above.
- **Country:** Netherlands.
- **Finding:** After a quality check, **394 high-growth firms** remained in the sample (**331 in 2014**). **There are huge differences across regions, even within a small country**: in 2014 the absolute number of high-growth firms ranged from **1 to 75** across regions, and even in relative terms there was a **15-fold difference** between the lowest-ranked region (**0.003%**) and the highest. On the index itself the variation is much larger: the bottom region (**Drenthe**) has an index value that is **less than 0.01% of the value of the top-performing region (Utrecht)**.
- **Caveat:** The index variation being far larger than the output variation is worth attention — it suggests the index is not linearly related to output, and cautions against reading index movements as proportional to expected results. Note the small absolute numbers: 394 high-growth firms across a national economy is a thin denominator.

### The three propositions
- **Study:** Stam & van de Ven (2021), as above; summarised by ANDE `[CHECK]`.
- **Finding:** Three propositions: **co-evolution** (ecosystem elements evolve in interdependence, requiring a systemic view); **upward causation** (ecosystem quality predicts entrepreneurial output, particularly the rate of high-growth firms); **downward causation** (successful entrepreneurs reinvest in and reshape the ecosystem itself).
- **Caveat:** `[CHECK]` on the exact formulation, which is drawn from a secondary summary. The substance is consistent with the published paper.

### The ten-element specification
- **Study:** Stam (2014), "The Dutch Entrepreneurial Ecosystem", SSRN (written 29 July 2014); Stam (2015), *European Planning Studies* 23(9): 1759–1769.
- **Country:** Netherlands.
- **Finding:** The model comprises **framework conditions** (formal institutions, culture, physical infrastructure, demand) and **systemic conditions** (networks, leadership, finance, talent, new knowledge, support services) that affect **entrepreneurial outputs** (entrepreneurial activity) and **outcomes** indicating value creation (productivity, income, employment and well-being). Applied to the Netherlands, the model is used to analyse how entrepreneurship evolved, why solo self-employment rose, and how the ecosystem could be adapted to increase productive entrepreneurship.
- **Caveat:** The Dutch application is to a high-income economy with near-full formal employment. The "rise of solo self-employment" question there is about labour market restructuring; in South Africa the same indicator would be about labour market exclusion. **The element specification transfers; the interpretation of the indicators does not.**

## How to Apply

1. **Build the instrument before the strategy.** Ten elements, measured, by district, as a baseline. Without it there is no ecosystem analysis, only assertion.
2. **Report the index, not the elements, against output.** That is what the evidence supports.
3. **Resist the bottleneck argument.** If a single-element bet is proposed, note that the individual elements did not relate to high-growth firm prevalence in the only rigorous measurement we have.
4. **Expect enormous sub-national variation.** A 15-fold range across Dutch provinces implies a much larger range across Western Cape districts. A single provincial index will conceal the thing you most need to see.
5. **Check the denominator.** High-growth firms are rare — 394 in the whole Netherlands after quality checks. A district-level high-growth indicator may have a denominator of single digits and will be statistically useless.
6. **State the external validity limit every time.** One small rich country, association not causation, by the framework's own author's assessment "rather limited" causal depth.

## Examples

**Situation:** A province proposes to concentrate its MSME budget on the finance pillar, on the grounds that finance is the binding constraint.

**Application:** The proposal assumes single-element causation. The best available measurement — twelve Dutch provinces, a validated ten-element instrument — found that high-growth firm prevalence relates to the *overall index* and not to individual elements, and that the elements are strongly interrelated. That does not prove the concentrated bet is wrong; it means the ecosystem literature does not support it, and a different argument is needed. If firm-level evidence shows finance binding for a specific population, make that argument (route to Schoar and McKenzie) rather than an ecosystem one.

**Result:** The concentrated bet either finds a defensible basis or is replaced by a portfolio.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Claim the ecosystem literature supports fixing your bottleneck element.
**Why:** The elements did not relate individually to output. Only the index did.

**Don't:** Present the Dutch association as causal evidence.
**Why:** Stam himself describes the approach's causal depth and evidence base as rather limited.

**Don't:** Report a single provincial ecosystem score.
**Why:** The intra-territorial variation is the finding. A 15-fold range across Dutch provinces means far more here.
