# The Sympathetic Critique

## When to Use
- When the ecosystem approach is being cited as established theory.
- When another region's model is being imported.
- When a strategy overclaims what ecosystem thinking supports.
- As the honesty check on any ecosystem argument — including Stam's own.

## Fails When
- **The user needs the framework applied**, not interrogated. Do not use this file to obstruct; use it to calibrate.

## Core Concept

Both words in the title are meant.

**Sympathetic.** The entrepreneurial ecosystem approach **speaks directly to practitioners**. That is a genuine virtue and the reason it spread through regional policy. It correctly starts with the entrepreneurial actor while emphasising context. It correctly identifies that entrepreneurs are not merely outputs but players who create and maintain the system. And it correctly captures the transition regional policy is making — from the quantity of entrepreneurship to its quality, and thence toward policy for an entrepreneurial economy rather than entrepreneurship policy as a silo.

**Critique.** The approach's **causal depth and evidence base are rather limited**. This is the author's own assessment of his own field, in print, in the paper that supplied the causal scheme the field lacked. The specific deficiencies:

- **It became popular before it became rigorous.** The concept remained loosely defined and loosely measured while being adopted as policy doctrine.
- **The causal scheme was supplied late and remains a starting point.** The 2015 paper offers a causal scheme of how framework and systemic conditions lead to entrepreneurial activities as output and new value creation as outcome — explicitly as "a much more rigorous and relevant starting point for subsequent studies", not as an established model.
- **The evidence is one small rich country.** The measurement work is Dutch. Association, not causation. Twelve provinces. 394 high-growth firms.
- **It is congenitally vulnerable to cargo-culting.** A framework of named elements invites the belief that assembling the elements assembles the outcome. Silicon Valley had these elements; therefore install these elements. The co-evolution proposition says exactly why that fails — the elements evolve in interdependence, they are not components to be procured.
- **Interaction analysis is underdeveloped.** The framework provides a scheme for analysing interactions between elements. Doing it well remains hard, and most applications do not attempt it.

**The correct posture.** Use the framework — it is the best specification available and vastly better than the metaphor. State its limits every time. And treat any strategy that claims the ecosystem literature *proves* something as having overrun the evidence, including where the claim is flattering to the framework.

## How to Apply

1. **Cite the limitation alongside the framework.** "Stam's own assessment is that the approach's causal depth and evidence base are rather limited." This protects the deliverable.
2. **Refuse the import.** The framework travels; the configuration does not. Co-evolution means a Dutch or Israeli or Californian ecosystem cannot be assembled elsewhere from its parts list.
3. **Downgrade "proves" to "associates with".** Every time.
4. **Ask whether the application has done the interaction analysis.** Almost none have. Note it as a limitation rather than pretending otherwise.
5. **Use it to calibrate ambition, not to obstruct.** The alternative to a limited framework is no framework.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy cites the entrepreneurial ecosystem literature to justify a package of interventions, presenting the approach as established.

**Application:** Add a calibration paragraph. The framework is the best available specification of the territorial context of entrepreneurship, and its element structure is defensible. Its causal depth and evidence base are, by its principal author's own published assessment, rather limited — the empirical work is an association in twelve Dutch provinces, and it relates the *composite index* rather than individual elements to high-growth firm prevalence. The strategy should therefore use the framework to organise its thinking and to structure its measurement, and should not claim that the literature establishes that its interventions will work. That claim, if it can be made at all, must come from the intervention-level evidence (route to McKenzie), not from ecosystem theory.

**Result:** A strategy whose epistemic claims match its evidence, and which is therefore defensible under review.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Present ecosystem theory as established causal knowledge.
**Why:** Its own author says the causal depth and evidence base are rather limited.

**Don't:** Import an ecosystem configuration.
**Why:** Co-evolution. The elements evolved together in a specific context; they are not a parts list.

**Don't:** Use the critique to reject the framework.
**Why:** It is sympathetic for a reason. The framework is the best specification available and the alternative is the metaphor.
