# Evidence Base — Outputs versus Outcomes

> **Provenance.** Compiled July 2026 from the sources named in each entry. Verify against the primary source before quoting in a client deliverable. Entries marked `[CHECK]` have not been verified to the primary source and must not be quoted without checking.

## When to Use
- Designing or auditing an indicator set, results chain, or target framework.
- Whenever entrepreneurial activity is being reported as a result.

## Fails When
- **The question is evaluation design or attribution** — route to McKenzie; **or utilisation-focused evaluation** — route to Patton.

## Headline

**The chain has four levels and most strategies collapse the middle two.**

| Level | Contents | Typical strategy error |
|---|---|---|
| **Elements** | The ten conditions | Well covered |
| **Outputs** | Entrepreneurial activity | **Skipped** |
| **Outcomes** | Productivity, income, employment, well-being | Targeted |
| — | | Nothing bridges outputs to outcomes |

## THE EVIDENCE

### The chain, as specified
- **Study:** Stam (2014), "The Dutch Entrepreneurial Ecosystem", SSRN; Stam (2015), *European Planning Studies* 23(9): 1759–1769.
- **Country:** Netherlands.
- **Finding:** The model specifies framework conditions and systemic conditions that affect **entrepreneurial outputs (entrepreneurial activity)** and **outcomes indicating value creation (productivity, income, employment and well-being)**.
- **Caveat:** The chain is a causal *scheme*, offered explicitly because the field lacked one. Stam presents it as "a much more rigorous and relevant starting point for subsequent studies" — a starting point, not an established causal model.

### The quality turn — what "output" now means
- **Study:** Stam (2015), *European Planning Studies*, "Entrepreneurial Ecosystems and Regional Policy: A Sympathetic Critique".
- **Country:** Conceptual / European regional policy.
- **Finding:** **Regional policies for entrepreneurship are going through a transition from increasing the *quantity* of entrepreneurship to increasing the *quality* of entrepreneurship.** The next step is a transition from entrepreneurship policy towards **policy for an entrepreneurial economy**. The approach starts with the entrepreneurial actor but emphasises the context of *productive* entrepreneurship.
- **Caveat:** This is a normative claim about where policy is going, supported by observation of European regional policy. It is the reason "number of enterprises supported" is no longer an acceptable output indicator.

### What the output is measured by, in practice
- **Study:** Stam (2017), UUSE discussion paper 17-11; Stam & van de Ven (2021), *Small Business Economics* 56(2): 809–832.
- **Country:** Netherlands.
- **Finding:** The output of entrepreneurial ecosystems is measured with **different indicators of high-growth firms**. The prevalence of high-growth firms relates to the overall ecosystem index.
- **Caveat:** High-growth firm prevalence is a defensible quality-adjusted output measure, but it has a thin denominator (394 high-growth firms in the whole Netherlands after quality checks) and it is not the only possible one. It also embeds a judgement: that the ecosystem's purpose is growth firms, which Schoar would endorse and Chen would contest.

### Phase differentiation in the chain
- **Study:** Stam & Bosma (2015). `[CHECK]` — full citation unverified.
- **Finding:** The ecosystem context should be **differentiated per phase of the entrepreneurial process**. This is probably most relevant for the **finance** element, with nascent entrepreneurs, start-ups, moderately growing firms, high-growth firms and unicorn firms having **substantially different finance needs**.
- **Caveat:** `[CHECK]`. Directly applicable to segmented strategies: the segments imply different positions in the entrepreneurial process, which implies different element configurations, not just different instruments.

### The Dutch interpretive warning
- **Study:** Stam (2014), SSRN.
- **Country:** Netherlands.
- **Finding:** The Dutch analysis addresses "why the rate of **solo self-employment** has increased" and how the ecosystem could be adapted to increase productive entrepreneurship.
- **Caveat:** **Critical for South African use.** In the Netherlands, rising solo self-employment is a labour-market restructuring phenomenon (flexibilisation, the *zzp'er*). In South Africa the same indicator would reflect labour market exclusion. The element framework transfers; **the interpretation of indicator movements does not.** A rise in own-account activity means opposite things in the two contexts.

## How to Apply

1. **Insert the output layer explicitly.** Elements → outputs → outcomes. If a results chain runs from initiatives to jobs, it has skipped the level where the ecosystem actually operates.
2. **Quality-adjust the output measure.** Not "enterprises supported". Not "new businesses registered". Prevalence of high-growth firms, or an equivalent quality-adjusted measure, is the defensible output.
3. **Reserve productivity, income, employment and well-being for the outcome level** — and accept that they are not attributable to the ecosystem strategy in any given year.
4. **Do not read output indicators as outcomes.** Entrepreneurial activity going up is not, by itself, value creation. It may be distress.
5. **Differentiate by phase.** Especially finance.
6. **Re-interpret every imported indicator for the local labour market.** Rising self-employment in a high-employment economy and in a high-unemployment one are different phenomena with the same number.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's M&E framework counts enterprises supported, enterprises registered, and participants trained, and reports against provincial jobs and growth targets.

**Application:** Every indicator listed is at the *element* or activity level; none is at the output level; the targets are at the outcome level. There is therefore nothing in the framework that could tell you whether the ecosystem is working, because the ecosystem's output — the quality-adjusted rate of entrepreneurial activity — is not measured. Insert an output layer: prevalence of high-growth firms by district, plus a quality-adjusted entrepreneurial activity measure. Then the chain is complete and the strategy becomes falsifiable.

**Result:** An M&E framework that can detect ecosystem performance rather than only programme delivery.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Report entrepreneurial activity as an outcome.
**Why:** It is the output. Value creation is the outcome. The conflation is the field's central accounting error.

**Don't:** Count enterprises supported.
**Why:** Policy has moved from quantity to quality. That indicator belongs to the paradigm the field has left.

**Don't:** Import Dutch indicator interpretations.
**Why:** Rising solo self-employment there is flexibilisation; here it is exclusion. Same number, opposite meaning.
