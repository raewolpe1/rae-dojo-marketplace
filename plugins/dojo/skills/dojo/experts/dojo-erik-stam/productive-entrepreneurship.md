# Productive Entrepreneurship

## When to Use
- When enterprise counts, TEA rates, or start-up numbers are proposed as objectives.
- When asked what the ecosystem is *for*.
- When distinguishing entrepreneurship worth supporting from entrepreneurship that merely exists.

## Fails When
- **The unit is a single firm.** This is an aggregate welfare standard.

## Core Concept

The foundational question is not how much entrepreneurship a territory has, but **what makes entrepreneurship truly productive**.

**The standard.** Productive entrepreneurship creates **not just firm-level success but aggregate welfare** — boosting innovation, job creation, and productivity. The alternative is entrepreneurship that generates activity without lasting value: churning capital, redistributing existing custom, or — at the extreme, following Baumol's original distinction — unproductive and destructive entrepreneurship that captures rents rather than creating them.

**Why this reframes ecosystem policy.** The ecosystem approach starts with the entrepreneurial actor but emphasises the *context of productive entrepreneurship*. The quality of the entrepreneurial ecosystem determines whether start-ups generate broad-based economic and social value or merely churn capital with little lasting impact. So the ecosystem is not a machine for producing entrepreneurs. It is a machine for producing *productive* entrepreneurs, and a territory with a high rate of unproductive entrepreneurship has a bad ecosystem, not a good one.

**The policy consequence.** This is why the field's transition from quantity to quality is not a refinement but a reversal. A policy that increases the number of enterprises without regard to their productivity may make things worse — more firms competing for the same custom in the same saturated markets, more capital tied up in activity that generates no aggregate value, and a self-congratulatory indicator moving in the right direction while the outcome does not.

**The uncomfortable corollary for high-unemployment economies.** Where wage employment is scarce, entrepreneurship rates are high and mostly unproductive — necessity-driven, own-account, market-saturating. The ecosystem framework will read this correctly (as a low-quality ecosystem producing high-quantity, low-quality output) only if the output measure is quality-adjusted. If the output measure is a count, the framework will read a distressed economy as a vibrant one.

## How to Apply

1. **Set the standard explicitly.** State that the objective is productive entrepreneurship — aggregate welfare — and not entrepreneurship as such.
2. **Quality-adjust every output measure.** Counts are not evidence of productive entrepreneurship.
3. **Ask about displacement.** Entrepreneurship that takes custom from an incumbent is redistribution, not creation. In saturated markets this is the dominant mode.
4. **Watch for rent-seeking.** Where public procurement is a major demand source, entrepreneurship can be productive (delivering better at lower cost) or unproductive (capturing the tender). The ecosystem shapes which.
5. **Read high entrepreneurship rates as a diagnostic, not an achievement.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy targets an increase in the number of registered enterprises in the province.

**Application:** The target is in the quantity paradigm, which regional policy has been leaving. It is also potentially perverse: in saturated township markets, additional entrants redistribute custom rather than create value, so the target can be met while the outcome deteriorates. Replace with a quality-adjusted output target — prevalence of high-growth firms, or firms crossing an employment threshold — and treat the enterprise count as a context statistic rather than an objective.

**Result:** A target aligned with what the ecosystem is for.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Target enterprise numbers.
**Why:** The standard is aggregate value creation, not activity. More firms can mean less value.

**Don't:** Read a high entrepreneurship rate as ecosystem health.
**Why:** In a high-unemployment economy it is mostly necessity-driven and unproductive. The ecosystem framework should diagnose that, not celebrate it.

**Don't:** Ignore the rent-seeking channel where procurement is the main market.
**Why:** Baumol's point: the same entrepreneurial energy goes to creation or to capture depending on the payoff structure the institutions set.
