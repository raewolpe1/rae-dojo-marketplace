# Crum Applied to Practice

## When to Use
- When someone needs to apply mindset science across multiple professional contexts.
- When building a comprehensive approach that integrates multiple findings from Crum's research programme.
- When translating the academic research into actionable professional practice.

## Core Concept
Crum's research programme synthesises into five practical principles for anyone designing programmes, managing teams, or communicating about health, stress, and performance.

The first principle is that every intervention creates mindsets. Whether intentionally or not, every programme, every communication, every management practice installs beliefs about the experience it addresses. A wellness programme creates mindsets about stress. A nutrition programme creates mindsets about food. A training programme creates mindsets about difficulty. These mindsets are part of the treatment effect. Audit them. Design them deliberately.

The second principle is accuracy as the design constraint. Effective mindset shifts are grounded in truth. The stress-is-enhancing mindset works because stress genuinely has enhancing properties. The exercise-perception shift works because the activity genuinely qualifies as exercise. Design mindset components around what is accurate, not what sounds positive. Positive framing that contradicts reality produces dissonance and disengagement. Accurate framing that includes overlooked dimensions produces lasting shifts.

The third principle is structural conditions first. Mindset work pairs with environmental conditions — it never substitutes for them. If the stressor is genuinely toxic, fix the stressor. If the food supply is genuinely inadequate, fix the supply. If the workload is genuinely unsustainable, fix the workload. Then — and only then — address the narrative about the manageable demands that remain.

The fourth principle is measurement of both subjective and objective outcomes. Because mindset operates at the intersection of subjective appraisal and objective biology, evaluation designs must capture both dimensions. Self-report measures confirm that the target belief shifted. Physiological or behavioural measures confirm that the shift produced downstream effects. Without both, the causal chain is incomplete.

The fifth principle is that the messenger is part of the message. Mindset interventions delivered by credible, congruent messengers — people whose visible behaviour matches the mindset they communicate — are more effective than the same content delivered by incongruent sources. A manager who preaches stress-is-enhancing while visibly burning out sends a contradictory signal. The behaviour of respected figures shapes group mindsets more than formal communications.

## How to Apply
1. **Start with the mindset audit.** "Before designing anything new, audit the mindsets your current practices create. What does your wellness programme communicate about stress? What does your training programme communicate about difficulty? What does your feedback system communicate about mistakes?"
2. **Redesign for accuracy.** "For each mindset you have identified, ask: is this the complete, accurate story? If your programme communicates 'stress is toxic,' the accurate story is 'stress has both enhancing and debilitating properties, and the conditions and the narrative determine which predominate.'"
3. **Ensure structural alignment.** "Does your environment support the mindset you want to create? If not, fix the environment first. A stress-is-enhancing message in a chronically overworked team is gaslighting."
4. **Design the messenger.** "Who will deliver the mindset message? Are they credible? Do they model the mindset themselves? Train messengers not just in content but in congruence."
5. **Measure the chain.** "Measure the mindset (did the belief shift?), the mechanism (did behaviour or physiology change?), and the outcome (did the desired result occur?). Test mediation."

## Examples
**Situation:** A development consultancy is redesigning its approach to staff wellbeing after a year of high turnover and burnout complaints.
**Application:** Crum would say: "Start with the audit. What mindsets does your current wellbeing approach create? If you have installed an Employee Assistance Programme, stress awareness workshops, and mindfulness sessions, the implicit message is: 'Your work is harmful and you need professional help to survive it.' That message, delivered consistently, installs the stress-is-debilitating mindset across your firm. Step one: fix any genuinely structural problems — unsustainable workloads, inadequate staffing, poor management. These are not mindset problems and mindset solutions will not address them. Step two: redesign your communications. Instead of 'how to cope with the stress of consulting,' frame it as 'how to channel the intensity of demanding work.' Instead of 'stress management,' use 'performance energy.' This is not euphemism — it is accuracy. The demands of consulting genuinely produce energy, focus, and mobilisation alongside discomfort and fatigue. Your current messaging highlights only the negative half. Step three: train your partners and senior managers. They are the primary mindset installers in your firm. Every time a partner says 'I know this is burning you all out,' they reinforce the debilitating narrative. Train them to say: 'This engagement is demanding, and the intensity is what makes the work consequential. Let us make sure we have recovery built into the project plan.' Step four: measure. Track not just turnover and sick days but staff beliefs about stress and work — and test whether changes in those beliefs mediate changes in the outcomes you care about."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Apply the principles as a checklist.
**Why:** Each context is different. The principles are diagnostic, not prescriptive. A stress-is-enhancing message that works in a well-managed consultancy may be harmful in an understaffed humanitarian operation. Diagnosis must precede prescription.

**Don't:** Promise guaranteed results.
**Why:** Mindset is one factor in a complex system. Shifting mindsets improves outcomes under specifiable conditions. If those conditions are not met, the shift may have no effect or may cause harm. Be honest about conditionality.
