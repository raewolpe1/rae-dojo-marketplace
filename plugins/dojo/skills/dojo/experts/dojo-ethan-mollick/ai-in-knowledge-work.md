# AI in Knowledge Work: Evidence, Applications, and Practice

## When to Use

- You want to ground an AI adoption argument in empirical evidence rather than hype or speculation.
- You need concrete, tested applications of AI in professional knowledge work — especially consulting, evaluation, education, and analysis.
- You are building the case for AI investment to decision-makers who need data, not enthusiasm.

## Core Concept

Mollick insists that the conversation about AI in knowledge work must be empirical, not speculative. The evidence base is now substantial enough to draw conclusions, and those conclusions are more interesting than either the utopian or dystopian narratives suggest.

The headline findings from multiple randomised studies converge on a consistent pattern. AI assistance improves the quality, speed, and quantity of knowledge work output by significant margins — typically 20-40% on tasks within the capability frontier. The BCG study with 758 consultants found those using GPT-4 completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster, with 40% higher quality as rated by blind evaluators. The MIT writing study found AI-assisted writers produced documents rated significantly higher in quality, with the largest gains for initially weaker writers. The Stanford customer service study found AI-assisted agents handled 14% more issues per hour, with the biggest gains for novice agents.

The distributional finding is the most important and least discussed. Across studies, AI assistance disproportionately benefits lower-performing workers. It compresses the skill gap — raising the floor more than the ceiling. This is empirically unusual for a new technology and has significant implications for equity. In the BCG study, consultants below the median saw dramatically larger performance improvements than those above it. AI provides a structural competence floor — a baseline level of organisation, completeness, and coherence — that benefits those who struggle with structure most.

For development consulting and M&E specifically, the evidence maps onto five concrete application areas. First, literature synthesis: AI can rapidly search, retrieve, and structure evidence reviews, reducing a two-week task to two days while improving structural completeness (though requiring human verification of source accuracy). Second, framework design: M&E frameworks, logical models, and theories of change have standard structural elements that AI handles competently, freeing consultant time for contextual adaptation. Third, writing and drafting: first drafts of reports, briefs, TORs, and proposals are reliably accelerated by AI, with quality improvements that scale inversely with the writer's baseline skill. Fourth, data analysis support: code generation for statistical analysis, data cleaning, and visualisation is well within the frontier for standard analytical tasks. Fifth, quality assurance: AI can serve as a first-pass reviewer, identifying structural gaps, logical inconsistencies, and missing elements in evaluation products — not replacing expert review but triaging it.

The evidence also documents clear limitations. Tasks requiring novel causal judgment, contextual interpretation of qualitative data, political economy navigation, and relationship management show minimal or negative returns to AI assistance. The productivity gains are concentrated in structured, pattern-matching-amenable tasks — and development consulting is a mix of these and the contextual, judgment-intensive tasks that remain firmly human.

## How to Apply

1. **Lead with evidence when making the case.** When arguing for AI adoption to sceptical colleagues or clients, do not lead with possibilities — lead with measured outcomes. "In a randomised experiment with 758 consultants at a major firm, AI assistance improved output quality by 40% as measured by blind expert review." Evidence moves people whom enthusiasm does not.

2. **Start with the five application areas.** For each new project, ask: where does literature synthesis, framework design, drafting, data analysis support, or quality assurance apply? These are the tested, evidence-backed applications. Start here, not with speculative use cases.

3. **Measure your own gains.** Track time spent on AI-assisted versus unassisted tasks for one month. Measure quality through your existing review processes. Build your own evidence base — it is more persuasive than external studies because it reflects your specific work and standards.

4. **Design for the distributional finding.** If AI helps weaker performers most, the highest-value organisational application is supporting junior staff. Give junior consultants AI access and guidance, and you get something close to the output of mid-level consultants at junior-level cost. This is a structural advantage for firms that adopt effectively.

## Examples

**Situation:** Rae is presenting to a client — a provincial government department — on how AI could improve their monitoring and evaluation function. The audience includes M&E officers, programme managers, and a sceptical director who has heard too many technology promises.

**Application:** Rae structures the presentation around evidence, not hype. He presents the BCG study findings — not as a Silicon Valley promise but as a measured empirical result from a rigorous experiment. He then maps those findings onto the department's specific M&E tasks.

"Your quarterly programme reports take an average of 12 person-days to produce. Based on the evidence, AI-assisted drafting could reduce this to 7-8 person-days — not by replacing the analytical work, but by handling the structural scaffolding: populating standard sections, formatting indicator tables, generating first-draft narrative summaries from the data. Your M&E officers would spend less time on document architecture and more time on the analytical interpretation that actually adds value."

He addresses the sceptical director directly: "The evidence shows this is not about replacing your team — it is about making your team more productive on the tasks that require their expertise, by reducing the time they spend on structural work that does not. And the evidence shows the biggest gains go to the officers who are newest and least experienced — which is exactly where your capacity gap is largest."

He closes with a practical proposal: a three-month pilot on one programme's quarterly reporting cycle, with before-and-after measurement of time spent and output quality. Evidence-based adoption, measured in their context, with their data.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Cite aggregate productivity gains without acknowledging the jagged frontier.
**Why:** The 20-40% improvement headline applies to tasks inside the frontier. For tasks outside it, AI assistance can make outcomes worse. Presenting only the positive headline and then having a sceptic point to an AI failure undermines credibility. Name the limitations upfront — it makes the genuine gains more credible.

**Don't:** Assume evidence from one sector transfers directly to another without testing.
**Why:** The BCG study involved management consultants doing specific analytical and creative tasks. Development M&E involves overlapping but distinct task types. The directional finding (AI helps, especially for less-experienced workers) likely transfers. The specific magnitude (40% quality improvement) may not. Test in your context, measure in your context, and build your own evidence base.
