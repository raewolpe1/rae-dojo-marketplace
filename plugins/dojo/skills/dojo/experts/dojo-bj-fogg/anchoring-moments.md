# Anchoring Moments

## When to Use
- When selecting the right existing routine to serve as a prompt for a new behaviour.
- When a habit recipe needs a reliable anchor.
- When existing anchors are failing and alternatives are needed.

## Core Concept
An anchor moment is an existing behaviour that someone already performs reliably — so reliably that it happens without conscious decision. Brushing teeth, pouring morning coffee, sitting down at a desk, turning off the car engine, closing the laptop at the end of the day. These are the foundation of the Tiny Habits method: the new behaviour is attached to the anchor, so the anchor serves as the prompt.

Good anchors have four properties. Reliability: the anchor happens every day (or every relevant day) without fail. The person does not decide to do it; they just do it. Specificity: the anchor is a concrete action with a clear endpoint, not a vague time period. "After I pour my coffee" is specific; "in the morning" is not. Location match: the anchor occurs in the same physical space where the new behaviour will happen. Anchoring a desk behaviour to a kitchen action creates a gap the habit will fall into. Frequency match: the anchor occurs at the right frequency for the desired behaviour. Daily anchors for daily habits; weekly anchors for weekly habits.

The most common mistake in anchor selection is choosing something that is not genuinely reliable. "After I go to the gym" fails as an anchor if gym attendance is itself inconsistent. "After my morning meeting" fails if meetings are cancelled or rescheduled. The test is: has this happened every relevant day for the past two weeks without exception? If not, it is not reliable enough to anchor a new habit.

Fogg identifies natural transition moments as the strongest anchors — the moments between activities when attention shifts. Finishing one task before starting another, arriving at a location, completing a meal. These transitions are natural pause points where a tiny behaviour can be inserted without disrupting flow.

## How to Apply
1. **List your reliable routines.** "What do you do every day without thinking? Morning, midday, evening — map your reliable behaviours."
2. **Test for reliability.** "Has this happened every day for the past two weeks? If you can remember a day you skipped it, it is not reliable enough."
3. **Check location match.** "Does the anchor happen where the new behaviour will happen? If not, find a closer anchor."
4. **Identify the precise moment.** "Not 'during breakfast' but 'after I put my plate in the sink.' The anchor is the completion of an action, not a time period."

## Examples
**Situation:** A field researcher wants to build a habit of reviewing her data collection notes for quality each day but cannot find a reliable moment.
**Application:** Fogg would say: "Let us map your field day. You drive to the field site — reliable? Yes, every field day. You conduct household visits — how many per day? Variable. You have lunch — where? At the field site, under a tree, every day. You drive back — reliable? Yes. You arrive at the office — reliable? Yes. Now, which of these transitions could anchor a note review? 'After I turn off the car engine at the office' — that is a concrete, reliable, daily transition. It occurs at a location where you can actually review notes — at your desk. The recipe: 'After I turn off the car engine at the office, I will open my field notebook and check one entry for completeness.' One entry. Not all of them. One. Celebrate: close the notebook and feel the satisfaction of having caught any issues while they are fresh. The anchor is the car engine — something you do every field day without thinking. The behaviour is tiny — one entry. The location matches — you are at the office where you can act on what you find."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Anchor to behaviours that are themselves inconsistent.
**Why:** If the anchor does not fire, the habit does not fire. An unreliable anchor produces an unreliable habit.

**Don't:** Anchor to emotional states.
**Why:** "When I feel motivated" or "when I feel stressed" are not anchors — they are conditions. Anchors must be concrete actions with observable endpoints.
