# Ability Chains

## When to Use
- When a behaviour is not happening because it is too difficult, time-consuming, or complex.
- When the ability element of B=MAP needs systematic analysis.
- When designing an intervention that makes behaviour easier.

## Core Concept
Fogg identifies five ability factors — the chain of elements that determine whether someone can perform a behaviour. Time: how long does it take? Money: what does it cost? Physical effort: how much bodily exertion is required? Cognitive effort: how much thinking is required? Routine fit: how well does it fit into existing patterns? A behaviour is easy when all five factors are favourable. It becomes hard when any single factor creates a bottleneck.

The ability chain metaphor is important: a chain is only as strong as its weakest link. A behaviour that is quick, free, physically easy, and fits existing routines but requires intense cognitive effort is still a hard behaviour — the cognitive effort is the bottleneck. Diagnosing which specific ability factor is the bottleneck directs the intervention precisely.

The strategic implication is Fogg's simplicity principle: if you want someone to do a behaviour, make it easier along the bottleneck dimension. If time is the constraint, shorten the behaviour. If cognitive effort is the constraint, simplify the decision. If routine fit is the constraint, redesign the timing or sequence. Do not waste effort improving ability factors that are already adequate — find the bottleneck and address it.

This connects directly to the action line in the Fogg Behaviour Model. As ability increases (the behaviour gets easier), the motivation required for action decreases. At maximum ability — a behaviour so easy it is almost effortless — almost any level of motivation is sufficient. This is why tiny habits work: they maximise ability by minimising every ability factor simultaneously.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify the bottleneck.** "Which ability factor is the constraint? Time? Money? Physical effort? Cognitive effort? Routine fit?"
2. **Address the bottleneck specifically.** "Do not improve everything — improve the factor that is actually blocking the behaviour."
3. **Reduce to tiny if needed.** "If multiple ability factors are constrained, the fastest fix is to shrink the behaviour until all factors are below threshold."
4. **Test the chain.** "After redesigning, walk through all five factors to confirm the behaviour is now above the action line."

## Examples
**Situation:** An organisation has introduced a new CRM system for tracking stakeholder engagements. Staff are supposed to log every meeting within 24 hours, but few do.
**Application:** Fogg would say: "Walk the ability chain. Time: how long does a CRM entry take? If it is fifteen minutes, that is your bottleneck — no one has fifteen spare minutes after every meeting. Fix: reduce to three fields — who, when, one-sentence summary. Two minutes maximum. Money: not a factor — the system is provided. Physical effort: not a factor — it is typing. Cognitive effort: does the CRM require categorising the engagement into a taxonomy? Choosing from dropdown menus? Remembering account numbers? Every decision point adds cognitive load. Fix: auto-populate what you can, reduce required fields, eliminate taxonomies that serve management reporting but impede data entry. Routine fit: when are staff supposed to log? If there is no natural moment, the behaviour floats and gets forgotten. Fix: anchor it to an existing routine — 'immediately after closing the meeting room door' or 'before opening the next email.' The system is failing not because staff do not care about CRM data but because the behaviour is too hard along time and cognitive effort dimensions. Fix those two and compliance will increase substantially."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Tell people to "just do it" when the behaviour is genuinely hard.
**Why:** If the behaviour requires thirty minutes, intense concentration, and breaks existing workflow, exhortation is not the solution. Redesigning the behaviour or the environment is.

**Don't:** Improve ability factors that are not the bottleneck.
**Why:** Making a behaviour physically easier when the real constraint is cognitive effort is wasted intervention. Diagnose before redesigning.
