# Growth Mindset

## When to Use
- When someone wants to understand what growth mindset actually is, beyond the popular caricature.
- When someone is trying to shift from fixed to growth orientation.
- When the specific behaviours and attitudes associated with incremental theory need to be described.

## Core Concept
The growth mindset — incremental theory — is the belief that intelligence, talent, and ability can be developed through effort, effective strategies, and learning from others. This belief creates a fundamentally different motivational architecture from the fixed mindset. If ability is developable, then performance is not a measure of permanent worth but a snapshot of current skill level. Challenge becomes an opportunity to grow rather than a threat to identity. Effort becomes the mechanism of development rather than evidence of inadequacy.

People operating from a growth mindset display characteristic behaviours. They embrace challenges because difficulty is where learning happens. They persist in the face of setbacks because setbacks provide information about what needs more work. They see effort as a necessary and positive part of mastery. They learn from criticism rather than becoming defensive, because feedback identifies areas for growth. They find lessons and inspiration in the success of others rather than feeling threatened.

Critically, the growth mindset is not about effort alone. Dweck has been forceful on this point, particularly after observing how her work was misapplied in educational settings. Growth mindset encompasses three elements: effort, strategy, and help-seeking. Working hard at an ineffective approach is not growth mindset — it is spinning wheels. The growth-mindset response to struggle is not "try harder" but "try differently" — seek better strategies, ask for help, learn from those who have mastered what you are attempting. The emphasis on process includes the intelligence of knowing when to change approach, not just the persistence to keep going.

The growth mindset also does not guarantee outcomes. Believing that ability is developable does not mean every person can become world-class at everything. It means that everyone can improve significantly from where they start, and that the ceiling of development is usually far higher than people in a fixed mindset assume. The growth mindset is about the trajectory, not the endpoint — and the trajectory is under more individual control than entity theorists believe.

## How to Apply
1. **Focus on process, not just attitude.** Growth mindset is not a feeling — it is a set of behaviours: embracing challenge, persisting through difficulty, seeking strategy, learning from feedback, being inspired by others.
2. **Model growth mindset through transparency about struggle.** Share your own learning process, including the parts where you were confused, made mistakes, or needed help.
3. **Add "yet" to capability statements.** "I can't do this" becomes "I can't do this yet." The word reframes a judgment as a trajectory.
4. **Celebrate learning, not just achievement.** When you or others succeed, ask: "What did you learn?" not just "How did you do?"

## Examples
**Situation:** An evaluator has received critical peer review feedback on a report and feels discouraged but wants to respond constructively.
**Application:** This is a growth-mindset opportunity — the evaluator is already oriented toward constructive response rather than defensiveness. Dweck would say: "The fact that you feel discouraged is normal — nobody enjoys critical feedback. But notice what you're doing right now: you're treating the feedback as information rather than as a verdict. That is the growth-mindset response. Now, the next step matters: don't just 'try harder' on the revision. Analyse the specific feedback. What patterns do you see? Where do the reviewers agree? Those agreements point to real skill gaps you can address. Are there strategies or models you can learn from? Is there someone who writes the kind of reports the reviewers want who could mentor you through the revision? Growth mindset isn't just resilience — it's strategic resilience."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Equate growth mindset with effort praise.
**Why:** Praising effort that leads nowhere is not growth mindset. "You tried really hard" is cold comfort when the strategy was wrong. Growth mindset demands effort, strategy, and adaptability together.

**Don't:** Promise that growth mindset will make everything possible.
**Why:** Growth mindset is about developing ability, not about unlimited potential. Some limitations are real. The growth-mindset response is to develop as far as you can, not to deny constraints.
