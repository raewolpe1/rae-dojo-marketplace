# Letting Go

## When to Use
- When someone is clinging to a particular outcome, expectation, or grievance and suffering because of it.
- When the mindfulness concepts of letting go and non-striving need to be explained.
- When the distinction between letting go and giving up needs to be drawn.

## Core Concept
Letting go is among the most misunderstood of the mindfulness attitudes. It is not about indifference, passivity, or emotional suppression. It is about recognising the mind's habitual grip on particular thoughts, feelings, experiences, and outcomes — and loosening that grip enough to be present to what is actually happening rather than to what the mind insists should be happening.

Kabat-Zinn observes that the mind has a powerful tendency to cling to pleasant experiences (wanting them to continue) and push away unpleasant ones (wanting them to end). Both movements take you out of the present — the clinging pulls you toward the past (when it was good) or the future (when it will be good again), and the aversion focuses on escape rather than presence. Letting go is the practice of neither clinging nor pushing — allowing experiences to arise and pass without grasping at them or fighting them.

Non-striving — a closely related attitude — is the willingness to let go of goal-orientation during practice. This is paradoxical: you sit down to meditate with some purpose (to develop mindfulness, to reduce stress), but the instruction is to let go of purpose during the practice itself. If you sit down trying to be mindful, the trying becomes an obstacle. If you sit down trying to relax, the trying produces tension. Non-striving means allowing the practice to be what it is, moment by moment, without evaluating whether it's working. This is not passive — it is an active, disciplined relinquishing of the need to achieve.

In professional life, letting go is counterintuitive because professional culture rewards grip — control, drive, tenacity. Kabat-Zinn's point is not that these qualities are wrong but that the inability to release them creates suffering. The leader who cannot let go of a decision after it's made replays it endlessly. The consultant who cannot let go of how a project "should have gone" is unavailable for how it actually went. The professional who cannot let go of a slight or a failure carries it as weight. Letting go is not weakness; it is the strength to put down what no longer needs to be carried.

## How to Apply
1. **Notice what you're holding.** Before you can let go, you need to see what the mind is gripping. "What am I holding onto right now that is causing me suffering?"
2. **Distinguish letting go from giving up.** Letting go of the outcome doesn't mean stopping the effort. It means working without being enslaved by a particular result.
3. **Practise with small things.** Let go of the need to be right in a conversation. Let go of a minor irritation. The muscle develops with practice.
4. **Use the breath as a model.** Each exhale is a natural letting go. You don't have to force it — you just stop holding.

## Examples
**Situation:** A project manager says "I can't stop thinking about the proposal that was rejected last month — I keep going over what I should have done differently."
**Application:** Kabat-Zinn would say: "Notice what the mind is doing. The proposal was rejected a month ago. That event is finished — it exists nowhere except in your thoughts. And yet those thoughts keep replaying it, revising it, arguing with it. This is the mind's grip. It's holding onto something that is over, and the holding costs you your presence in everything that's happening now. Letting go doesn't mean the rejection didn't matter. It doesn't mean you shouldn't learn from it. It means recognising that the replaying, the ruminating, the should-have-done-differently — those are not learning. They are the mind's refusal to accept what happened. Each time you notice the replay starting, you have a choice: you can follow it into the past, or you can take a breath, feel your feet on the floor, and return to this moment — which is the only moment you can actually do anything in. Letting go is not forgetting. It is putting down a weight so your hands are free for what's in front of you."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present letting go as emotional suppression.
**Why:** Letting go is not pushing feelings away. It is allowing them to be present without being controlled by them. Suppression is a form of holding on.

**Don't:** Make non-striving sound like not caring.
**Why:** Non-striving is the willingness to engage fully without attachment to a particular result. It is the most engaged way of working, not the least.
