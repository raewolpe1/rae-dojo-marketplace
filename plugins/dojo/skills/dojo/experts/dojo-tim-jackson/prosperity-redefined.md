# Prosperity Redefined

## When to Use

- When designing or evaluating development frameworks that need a definition of success beyond GDP growth
- When helping someone articulate what a good society looks like within ecological limits
- When the conversation needs to move from critique of growth to a positive vision of post-growth flourishing

## Core Concept

The deepest failure of the growth economy is not ecological — it is conceptual. We have confused the means with the end. GDP was designed as a measure of aggregate economic output, not as a measure of human welfare, social progress, or the quality of life. Yet it has become the de facto target of economic policy worldwide, the number by which governments stand or fall, the metric around which entire institutional architectures are built.

Prosperity, understood properly, is the ability to flourish as a human being within the ecological limits of a finite planet. It has material dimensions — adequate nutrition, shelter, healthcare, education — but it is not reducible to material accumulation. Beyond a threshold of material sufficiency, additional income and consumption contribute diminishing and eventually negligible amounts to wellbeing. What matters more is the quality of relationships, the sense of meaning and purpose, the ability to participate in community life, access to nature, physical and mental health, security, autonomy, and the freedom to live in a way that does not compromise the flourishing of others or of future generations.

This is not a new insight. Sen's capabilities approach, the Easterlin paradox, the extensive wellbeing literature, the OECD Better Life Index, Bhutan's Gross National Happiness, New Zealand's Living Standards Framework, and the Wellbeing Economy Alliance all point in the same direction. What Jackson adds is the ecological constraint: prosperity must be achievable within planetary boundaries, which means it cannot depend on perpetual growth in material throughput. This is not austerity. It is the recognition that a richer, more equitable, more meaningful life is available without the ecological destruction that growth requires.

For development practitioners, this reframe is operationally consequential. It shifts the design question from "how do we grow GDP?" to "what capabilities do people need to flourish, and what is the most ecologically efficient way to deliver them?" The answers often point away from growth and toward targeted investment in health, education, ecological restoration, community infrastructure, and time — the scarcest resource in the growth economy.

## How to Apply

1. **Replace GDP targets with wellbeing dashboards.** Use multidimensional frameworks — health outcomes, educational attainment, social connection, environmental quality, subjective wellbeing, time use, distributional equity — as the primary success metrics for development strategies.

2. **Apply the sufficiency test.** Before asking "how do we produce more?" ask "do we have enough, and is it distributed justly?" In many high-income contexts, the binding constraint on wellbeing is not insufficient production but maldistribution, overwork, and ecological degradation.

3. **Distinguish between development and growth.** Development — the expansion of human capabilities — does not require aggregate GDP growth. It requires targeted investment, institutional quality, and equitable distribution. This distinction is especially important for the Global South, where development needs are real but need not follow the carbon-intensive growth path of the North.

4. **Centre time as a dimension of prosperity.** The growth economy systematically converts time into money and money into consumption. Post-growth prosperity reclaims time — for care, for community, for rest, for attention to the world. Work-time reduction is a structural post-growth policy, not a lifestyle choice.

## Examples

**Situation:** A regional government is designing a 2030 development vision. The previous plan targeted 4 percent annual GDP growth. The new administration wants an alternative framework but does not know how to structure it without a single headline number.

**Application:** Propose a prosperity dashboard with five to seven dimensions, each with measurable indicators: health (life expectancy, mental health prevalence, access to care); education (attainment, adult learning participation, childhood development); social connection (loneliness prevalence, community participation, trust); ecological health (air and water quality, biodiversity indicators, carbon footprint per capita); material security (housing adequacy, food security, income floor); time (average working hours, commute time, time spent in care and community); equity (income ratio, wealth distribution, access gaps by gender and geography). Set targets for each dimension. Report annually against the dashboard. The headline is not a single number but a narrative of progress across multiple dimensions of flourishing — which is what prosperity actually is.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Define prosperity so broadly that it becomes unmeasurable or unfalsifiable. Wellbeing dashboards must have concrete, trackable indicators with clear targets. Vagueness invites everyone to claim success.
**Why:** The critique of GDP is not that measurement is bad but that we are measuring the wrong thing. Replacing a precise-but-wrong metric with a vague-but-right aspiration is not progress. Jackson insists on rigour in the positive case as much as in the critique.

**Don't:** Present post-growth prosperity as requiring sacrifice or austerity. The framing is not "less" but "different" — and in many dimensions, better. Time affluence, community richness, ecological beauty, meaningful work, reduced anxiety, and greater equality are gains, not losses.
**Why:** The austerity framing plays into the growth mythology's most powerful claim — that any alternative to growth is deprivation. Jackson's entire project is to show that the opposite is true: it is the growth economy that impoverishes, by degrading the ecological, social, and temporal foundations of genuine flourishing.
