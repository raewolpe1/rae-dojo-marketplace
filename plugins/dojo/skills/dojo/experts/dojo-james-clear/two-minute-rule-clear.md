## When to Use

When someone keeps starting a habit and abandoning it. When someone can't seem to get started. When the habit feels too big or effortful to begin. When someone asks "how do I be more consistent?"

## Fails When

The person has a reliable two-minute version that is already automatic and is asking about the next level of performance. The two-minute rule is for establishing habits, not optimising them.

## Core Concept

The two-minute rule: when starting a new habit, it should take no more than two minutes to complete.

This sounds like a trick — and it is, but it's grounded in real neuroscience. Habits form through repetition of the complete loop: cue → craving → response → reward. The brain encodes the pattern most reliably when the loop completes successfully. A habit that is too hard to start fails before the loop begins. A habit that completes in two minutes runs the full loop successfully every time.

The two-minute version is not the goal — it is the gateway. The goal of the two-minute rule is not to produce a small result; it is to establish the automaticity of beginning.

Every habit has a two-minute version:
- "Read 30 books per year" → "Read one page"
- "Do yoga three times a week" → "Get out the yoga mat"
- "Study a foreign language" → "Open the language app"
- "Write daily" → "Write one sentence"
- "Run every morning" → "Put on running shoes"

Notice that some two-minute versions don't even complete the full activity — they just start it. This is intentional. The ritual of beginning is what needs to become automatic. Once you have running shoes on and are standing outside, you will almost always run. The obstacle is always the start.

**Mastery phase:** Once the two-minute version is genuinely automatic (it fires without deliberation, feels incomplete not to do), you can extend. But only then. The extension happens naturally — you're already in it, and stopping at two minutes starts to feel artificial. Don't force extension; let the identity and momentum carry it.

**The gateway habit:** Some two-minute habits are specifically designed as gateway behaviours — their only purpose is to place you in the location and state where the full habit happens. "Put on gym clothes" is a gateway — you don't need to exercise yet, you just need to become a person in gym clothes. The rest follows.

## How to Apply

1. **Take your habit and shrink it to two minutes.** If you can't get it under two minutes, you haven't shrunk it enough. Keep going until you reach the absolute minimum.
2. **Write it out explicitly.** "My habit is: [two-minute version]." Not the goal version — the two-minute version.
3. **Do only the two-minute version for the first two weeks.** Stop at two minutes even when you want to continue. This sounds counterintuitive but it builds the pattern without triggering the exhaustion that kills new habits.
4. **Track completion, not duration.** Did you do it? That's the only question. Length is irrelevant at this stage.
5. **Let extension happen naturally.** After 2-3 weeks, you will find that stopping at two minutes feels incomplete. That is the signal to begin extending. Extend by 2 minutes per week, not in large jumps.

## Examples

**Example 1 — Morning writing habit**

A consultant wants to write every morning. She's tried "write for 30 minutes" many times. It always fails within two weeks. Two-minute version: open the document and write one sentence. That's it. Close it if you want. She does this for three weeks. By week two she never closes after one sentence — she's already writing. By week six she's doing 20-minute sessions. The two-minute rule got her through the starting problem.

**Example 2 — Learning Spanish**

He says he has no time. Two-minute version: open Duolingo. Just open it. Set a phone notification — but the habit is just opening it, not completing a lesson. The opening is automatic within a week. The lesson follows automatically. The habit is now 15 minutes. But it started as "open the app."

## Anti-Patterns

- **Treating the two-minute version as the long-term habit.** The two-minute rule is for establishing automaticity, not for producing output. If you're still doing the two-minute version after six months, you've confused the tool with the goal.
- **Skipping the two-minute phase because "I can do more."** You probably can do more today. But you won't do more on a bad day, a travel day, or a sick day. The two-minute version is your floor — the minimum that keeps the identity intact on the worst days.
- **Using the two-minute rule for a habit that's already established.** If the habit is already automatic, the two-minute rule adds nothing. Use it only for new behaviours.
