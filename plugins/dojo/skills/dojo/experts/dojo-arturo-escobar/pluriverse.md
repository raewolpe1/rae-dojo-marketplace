# The Pluriverse: A World in Which Many Worlds Fit

## When to Use

- When a development programme, policy, or strategy needs to be reframed from a single-model paradigm to one that acknowledges and enables multiple ontologies of the good life.
- When practitioners ask what comes after the critique of development — what the affirmative alternative actually looks like.
- When the conversation has reached the limits of "inclusion" and "participation" and needs to move to ontological recognition — granting other worlds standing, not just voice.

## Core Concept

The pluriverse is Escobar's central proposition: not a theory about the world but a commitment to the real co-existence of multiple worlds. The phrase originates with the Zapatistas — "a world in which many worlds fit" (un mundo donde quepan muchos mundos) — and Escobar develops it into a full ontological and political programme.

The pluriverse is not relativism. It does not say all claims are equally valid within a single reality. It says there are multiple realities — multiple ways of being human, multiple conceptions of the good life, multiple relationships to the living — and that no single world has the right to define the terms for all the others. This is the distinction between epistemological pluralism (many ways of knowing one world) and ontological pluralism (many worlds, each with its own valid way of knowing and being).

For development practice, the implications are fundamental. The mainstream paradigm — whether orthodox (GDP growth) or progressive (capabilities, human development, SDGs) — assumes a single trajectory along which all societies can be located and measured. The pluriverse refuses this scale. It asks: what if the Andean concept of sumak kawsay (living well in harmony with Pachamama) is not a "perspective on development" but a different world entirely? What if the ubuntu philosophy of relational personhood is not a "cultural value" to be respected within liberal frameworks but a competing ontology of what it means to be human?

To work pluriversally is not to abandon rigour. It is to expand what counts as rigorous — to recognise that a community's territorial plan, developed through decades of collective struggle, carries the same epistemic weight as a World Bank country strategy. The pluriverse does not reject modernity. It rejects modernity's claim to be the only world.

## How to Apply

1. **Name the world the current frame presupposes.** Before introducing the pluriverse, make visible the mono-ontology of the existing programme. What conception of the good life does the theory of change assume? What counts as progress, well-being, success? Whose categories are these?

2. **Identify the suppressed worlds.** In every development context, there are conceptions of the good life that the dominant frame renders invisible. These are not gaps in the analysis — they are other worlds. Identify them: communal economies, territorial governance, relational conceptions of well-being, spiritual relationships to land.

3. **Grant ontological standing, not just epistemological tolerance.** The pluriversal move is not to "include local perspectives." It is to recognise that these are not perspectives on your world — they are different worlds with their own validity. This means the programme categories themselves may need to change, not just the stakeholder list.

4. **Design for co-existence, not integration.** A pluriversal programme does not integrate communities into a single value chain. It creates the conditions under which multiple economic, social, and ecological logics can co-exist — what Escobar calls an "ecology of knowledges and practices."

## Examples

**Situation:** A multilateral agency is designing a biodiversity conservation programme for the Amazon basin. The programme frames biodiversity as "natural capital" to be conserved through payment-for-ecosystem-services (PES) schemes and carbon markets.

**Application:** Escobar would surface the ontological assumption: the PES frame converts the forest into a stock of measurable services — carbon sequestration, water regulation, genetic resources. This is one world's relationship to the forest. For the Indigenous peoples of the Amazon — Yanomami, Kayapo, Achuar — the forest is not "natural capital." It is a living meshwork of human and non-human persons with whom one has reciprocal obligations. The forest does not provide "services." It is a relative.

A pluriversal programme would not abandon conservation but would refuse to reduce it to the PES ontology. It would recognise Indigenous territorial governance as a conservation practice with its own logic — one that has kept the forest standing for millennia without needing a carbon market. The programme design would create space for both logics to operate, with the community holding the authority to determine which applies on its territory. This is not compromise. It is ontological co-existence.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat the pluriverse as a synonym for "stakeholder diversity" or "multicultural inclusion."
**Why:** These framings remain inside a single world — the liberal one — and grant other worlds voice without granting them standing. The pluriverse is not a more inclusive version of the same paradigm. It is a different paradigm.

**Don't:** Deploy the pluriverse as pure critique without naming the substantive alternatives.
**Why:** Escobar is insistent that the pluriverse is an affirmative project, not just a deconstructive one. If you invoke it only to say "the mainstream is wrong" without saying what buen vivir, ubuntu, communal autonomy, or territorial governance actually propose, you have reduced it to an academic gesture.
