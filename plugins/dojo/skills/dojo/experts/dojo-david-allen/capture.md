## When to Use

Use when someone has ideas or commitments slipping through the cracks, when they rely on memory for task tracking, or when they use too many disparate capture tools without a processing discipline.

## Fails When

- Capture tools are numerous but never processed
- Some inputs have no assigned capture location (they remain in the head)
- The act of capture is confused with the act of clarification

## Core Concept

Capture is the first and most fundamental step of GTD. Its purpose is to get everything out of the head and into a trusted external location as quickly as possible. Allen's rule is **ubiquitous capture** — the system must be able to receive inputs anywhere, at any time, about anything.

**Why capture matters.** The mind is optimised for generating and connecting ideas, not for storing and retrieving them. When you use mental storage for commitments, you impose a constant background tax: the brain cycles through stored items to ensure none are forgotten, consuming cognitive resources that should be available for actual thinking. Capture eliminates this tax by offloading storage to an external system.

**Ubiquitous means everywhere.** The capture system must be available in every context: at the desk, in the car, in the shower, at a conference, on a run, in conversation. This typically means a small physical notepad or index cards plus a digital option on the phone. The exact tools matter less than the habit: when something enters awareness, it exits the brain immediately into a capture tool.

**What to capture.** Everything that has your attention. This includes: tasks, promises, ideas, worries, projects, reference items you might want later, things that bother you, things you want to remember, things you need to follow up on. The capture step is not selective. Filtering is done in the clarification step — not here.

**Inboxes are for collection, not storage.** A capture inbox is a temporary holding area for unprocessed material, not an organisational system. The discipline is: capture into the inbox, then process the inbox regularly. An inbox that is never processed provides no benefit over keeping things in the head.

**How many capture tools?** As few as possible while covering all contexts. Allen recommends: one physical inbox at the desk, one notebook or notepad for on-the-go, and one digital capture tool on the phone. The critical requirement is that all inputs from all tools eventually converge into a single processing point.

## How to Apply

1. **Audit your current capture situation.** Where do tasks and ideas currently live? List every location: email, notes app, notebook, sticky notes, whiteboard, voice memos, mental lists, voicemail. Each is a capture tool, and each needs a processing discipline.

2. **Choose your minimum capture tools.** Select one per context: desk, mobile, voice. Set up physical inbox trays for paper. Select a digital capture app that is always on your phone.

3. **Practice the capture habit.** For one week, commit to externalising every single thought that represents an open loop, no matter how trivial. Notice the relief that follows.

4. **Set up processing rhythms.** Each capture tool needs a regular processing schedule. Email at defined times. Physical inbox daily or every two days. Notes app during the weekly review at minimum.

5. **Do the initial mind sweep.** Sit down and capture every open loop you are currently holding in your head — every project, promise, worry, idea, and undone task. This typically takes 30–60 minutes and is one of the most relief-producing single acts in GTD.

## Examples

**Example 1: The shower idea problem**
A strategist has her best ideas in the shower and loses most of them. Capture fix: a waterproof notepad in the bathroom (they exist). The idea is captured immediately, enters the system at the next processing point, and is not lost.

**Example 2: The overloaded notes app**
A professional has 400 items in his notes app, mixed between captured tasks, reference material, and random observations, none processed. The app is a capture tool that has become a storage system — the worst of both worlds. The fix: a single processing session to clarify every item, followed by a discipline of processing the notes app weekly.

## Anti-Patterns

**Selective capture.** Deciding in the capture moment whether something is "important enough" to write down. This defeats the purpose — the brain still holds every item it decides not to capture, and the threshold for "important" shifts unpredictably under stress.

**Capture-as-organisation.** Trying to file or categorise items at the point of capture rather than collecting them neutrally. Capture should take five seconds. Organisation happens at clarification.

**Too many capture tools.** Having eight different apps, three notebooks, and sticky notes everywhere with no single processing point. The capture system must converge — otherwise you have multiplied your inboxes without creating a system.
