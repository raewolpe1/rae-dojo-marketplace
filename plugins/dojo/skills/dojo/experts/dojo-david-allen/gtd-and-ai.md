## When to Use

Use when someone wants to integrate AI tools into their GTD practice, when AI is creating new forms of open loops (prompts, threads, outputs to review), or when someone is using AI extensively and feeling less organised as a result.

## Fails When

- AI is used to avoid the clarification step ("I'll just ask ChatGPT to clarify my todos")
- AI-generated output enters the system without being processed
- The trusted system is abandoned in favour of continuous AI interaction

## Core Concept

AI does not change the fundamental architecture of GTD. It changes the volume and velocity of incoming material, and it provides new tools for certain steps — but the five-step workflow (Capture, Clarify, Organise, Reflect, Engage) remains the correct framework for managing human commitments and attention.

**Where AI changes GTD practice:**

*Capture:* AI conversations, generated outputs, and AI-surfaced information all become capture items. A useful AI exchange that reveals a new project, a promising output that needs review, a tool recommendation that needs evaluation — all of these belong in the inbox. The failure mode is treating AI as an always-on second brain and never capturing what matters from it.

*Clarify:* AI can assist clarification. Given a loosely worded item in your inbox, a language model can help articulate next physical actions, identify projects behind single-step assumptions, or reframe vague language into actionable terms. This is a legitimate use of AI in GTD — as a clarification partner. However, AI cannot make decisions about your life. It cannot know whether you care about an item, whether it fits your current priorities, or what counts as a meaningful outcome for you. You must still do the clarification; AI can accelerate the language work.

*Organise:* AI can suggest contexts and project assignments for items you are processing. This is marginally useful. The risk is that AI-suggested organisation creates a false sense of having processed items when no decision has actually been made.

*Reflect:* AI can help surface agenda items for your weekly review — patterns in incomplete tasks, projects without next actions, items that have been languishing. AI cannot substitute for the weekly review itself, which requires your judgment, your awareness of current priorities, and your sense of what has changed.

*Engage:* AI can help execute. Drafting, researching, synthesising — these are legitimate AI contributions to the doing step. This is where AI delivers the most unambiguous value in GTD practice.

**The new capture challenge.** AI tools generate enormous quantities of output. The practitioner who uses AI extensively must develop clear habits around what to capture from AI interactions: which outputs are reference material, which generate action items, which reveal new projects. Without this discipline, AI creates new open loops as fast as old ones are closed.

**AI and the trusted system.** The purpose of a trusted system is to reliably hold everything that has your attention so your mind is free to do creative and present work. AI assistants are not a trusted system. They do not reliably hold context across sessions, they generate content on demand rather than surfacing what you have committed to, and they are proactive advisors rather than passive containers. GTD requires a trusted system that is yours and that you maintain. AI is a set of tools, not a system.

## How to Apply

1. **Treat AI conversation outputs as capture items.** At the end of any productive AI session, scan the conversation for commitments, ideas, next actions, and reference material. Capture anything that matters into your inbox.

2. **Use AI for clarification when language is the obstacle.** If an item in your inbox is vague and you cannot articulate the next action, bring it to an AI for language assistance. But you must make the decision; the AI formats it.

3. **Do not let AI-generated tasks enter your lists unchecked.** AI can suggest tasks, create project plans, and generate action lists. Each suggestion must pass through your clarification step before entering your system. AI-generated lists are inbox items, not next-action lists.

4. **Use AI to prepare for the weekly review.** Before your review, ask AI to scan recent notes, email summaries, or calendar data for open loops you may have missed. Let AI surface candidates; you decide what to include.

5. **Maintain the weekly review as a human practice.** No AI tool substitutes for your own reflective review of your projects and commitments. This step requires your judgment and cannot be delegated.

## Examples

**Example 1: The productive AI capture habit**
A consultant used AI extensively for research and drafting. She established a practice: at the end of each significant AI session, she spent 3 minutes reviewing the conversation and capturing anything that implied a commitment or action. These went into her inbox for processing in the next clarification session. Her AI use became fuel for her GTD system rather than a parallel attention drain.

**Example 2: AI as clarification partner**
A product manager had an inbox item that read "think about the Q3 roadmap." He brought this to an AI for clarification: "What would a next physical action for reviewing a product roadmap look like?" The AI offered several framings. He selected one — "Schedule 90-minute roadmap session with head of engineering before July 1" — and added this to his calendar. The AI helped with language; he made the decision.

## Anti-Patterns

**AI as second brain substitute.** Using AI conversation history, pinned threads, or ongoing chat as a substitute for a maintained GTD system. AI interfaces are ephemeral, non-systematic, and optimised for generation rather than reliable retrieval. A second brain is a deliberate, maintained archive of your own knowledge. AI is a generative tool.

**Delegating clarification to AI.** Asking AI "what should I do about this?" and treating the answer as a processed item. Clarification requires your judgment about your life, priorities, and commitments. AI can inform this judgment but cannot make it.

**Unprocessed AI output in the system.** Copying AI-generated task lists directly into a task manager without running them through the clarification step. Each item still requires the same question: "What is this? Is it actionable? If so, what is the next physical action?"
