# Gottman Applied to Practice

## When to Use
- When someone wants concrete, daily actions to strengthen a relationship.
- When the theory needs to be translated into habits.
- When a therapist needs structured homework assignments grounded in the research.

## Core Concept
Gottman's research consistently shows that relationship quality is determined not by grand gestures but by the accumulation of small, daily interactions. The practical exercises derived from his research operationalise the Sound Relationship House principles into repeatable habits.

The stress-reducing conversation is one of the most powerful daily practices. Partners spend twenty minutes at the end of each day talking about their external stressors — work, family, health, anything outside the relationship. The rules: the listening partner's job is to be supportive, not to solve. Express understanding before giving advice (and only give advice if asked). Take your partner's side. Communicate that you understand and empathise. This practice strengthens Love Maps (you stay current on your partner's world), builds fondness (you demonstrate care), and increases turning toward (you are emotionally available).

The six-second kiss replaces the perfunctory goodbye peck with a kiss long enough to register emotionally — approximately six seconds. It is a daily ritual of connection that costs almost nothing but communicates intention and presence. Similarly, ritualized partings and reunions — saying goodbye with attention, greeting each other with genuine interest — create reliable moments of turning toward.

The weekly State of the Union meeting is a structured conversation where couples check in on the relationship itself. What went well this week? What could have gone better? Is there anything unresolved? The format prevents small irritations from accumulating into resentment by providing a regular venue for addressing them. Gottman recommends starting with appreciations — each partner names five things the other did that they valued — before moving to concerns.

Appreciation practices — daily expressions of specific gratitude — directly feed the Fondness and Admiration system. Not generic praise but specific observations: "I noticed you made my coffee this morning. Thank you." The specificity communicates that you are paying attention, which is itself an act of love.

## How to Apply
1. **Start with the stress-reducing conversation.** Twenty minutes, daily. External stress only. Listen to understand, not to fix.
2. **Build rituals of connection.** The six-second kiss, the mindful greeting, the intentional goodbye. Small, consistent, genuine.
3. **Institute the weekly check-in.** Start with appreciations. Move to concerns. Use soft start-up. Practice repair.
4. **Express specific appreciation daily.** At least one specific, genuine observation of something your partner did well.
5. **Update Love Maps regularly.** Ask open-ended questions about your partner's current experience — their worries, their hopes, their stressors.
6. **Dream together.** Share individual and shared aspirations. Create shared meaning through shared goals.
7. **Repair quickly.** When ruptures occur, address them within twenty-four hours. Do not let resentment compound.

## Examples
**Situation:** A couple has completed Gottman therapy and wants to maintain their gains. The therapist assigns homework for the transition out of therapy.
**Application:** The therapist would prescribe a minimal sustainable set of daily practices: the stress-reducing conversation each evening, specific appreciation at least once daily, and a weekly State of the Union. The couple would be coached to treat these not as tasks but as rituals — things they do because they value the relationship, not because they are assigned. The six-second kiss and mindful greetings would be added as low-effort, high-impact rituals. The therapist would emphasise that these practices are not maintenance — they are the relationship. The research shows that couples who sustain these habits maintain their gains. Couples who abandon them tend to regress. The question is not whether the exercises "work" — it is whether the couple values the relationship enough to invest ten to thirty minutes daily in its care.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Turn practices into obligations that feel punitive.
**Why:** A stress-reducing conversation conducted resentfully is worse than none at all. The practices must be engaged with willingness, not compliance.

**Don't:** Attempt all practices simultaneously.
**Why:** Overwhelming a couple with a complete programme produces resistance. Start with one practice, establish it as habit, then add another.
