# The Positive Perspective

## When to Use
- When someone describes a dynamic where everything they do is interpreted negatively.
- When the 5:1 ratio needs to be explained.
- When the concept of sentiment override is relevant.

## Core Concept
Gottman's research identified a remarkably consistent finding: in stable, satisfying relationships, the ratio of positive to negative interactions during conflict is at least 5 to 1. Five moments of connection, humour, affection, interest, or empathy for every one moment of criticism, defensiveness, or irritation. This ratio does not apply to all interactions — in daily life, the ratio in happy couples is closer to 20:1. The 5:1 figure applies specifically to conflict conversations, where some negativity is inevitable and healthy.

The ratio operates through a mechanism Gottman calls sentiment override. When the overall emotional climate of the relationship is positive — when the friendship is strong and the ratio is healthy — partners give each other the benefit of the doubt. A neutral comment is interpreted positively. An ambiguous action is read charitably. This is positive sentiment override. When the emotional climate is negative — when the friendship has eroded and the Four Horsemen dominate — partners interpret everything through a lens of suspicion and hostility. A neutral comment is heard as criticism. A kind gesture is suspected of having an ulterior motive. This is negative sentiment override.

Sentiment override is not a conscious choice — it is a perceptual filter shaped by the accumulated history of the relationship. It is why couples in distress often feel that nothing they do is good enough — their partner's negative filter transforms every input into confirmation of the worst interpretation. The way to shift from negative to positive sentiment override is not to change the interpretation but to change the ratio — to deposit enough positive interactions into the emotional bank account that the filter naturally shifts.

## How to Apply
1. **Count the ratio.** Not precisely, but directionally. In your last conflict, how many positive moments (humour, acknowledgment, affection, agreement) were there for every negative one (criticism, contempt, defensiveness)?
2. **Increase the positives rather than just decreasing the negatives.** A relationship that eliminates all negativity but has no warmth is not healthy. Actively build positive interactions — appreciation, curiosity, touch, humour.
3. **Recognise negative sentiment override when it operates.** If your partner interprets everything you do as hostile, the filter is in place. Arguing about individual incidents will not work — the filter must be changed by changing the ratio.
4. **Build the friendship.** Positive sentiment override is a product of the friendship foundation — Love Maps, Fondness and Admiration, Turning Toward. Strengthen these and the filter shifts.

## Examples
**Situation:** A man buys his wife flowers. She says, "What did you do wrong?" He is hurt and angry. She says this is a joke but he feels that nothing he does is ever received positively.
**Application:** Negative sentiment override is operating. Her filter interprets the flowers — an objectively positive gesture — as evidence of guilt rather than affection. This filter did not develop overnight — it is the product of accumulated relational disappointments. Arguing about the flowers will not help. The intervention is to change the ratio across all interactions — more turning toward, more genuine appreciation, more expressions of fondness — until the emotional bank account balance shifts enough to change the filter. When the overall climate becomes positive, the flowers will be received as flowers.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Try to maintain the 5:1 ratio through forced positivity.
**Why:** Fake positivity does not register as genuine. The ratio must reflect real emotional engagement, not performed niceness.

**Don't:** Blame the partner for negative sentiment override.
**Why:** The filter is not a choice — it is a product of relational history. Telling someone to "just see things positively" ignores the accumulated experiences that shaped the filter.
