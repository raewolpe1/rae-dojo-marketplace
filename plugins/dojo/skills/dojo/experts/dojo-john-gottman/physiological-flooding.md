# Physiological Flooding

## When to Use
- When someone describes an inability to think clearly during arguments.
- When stonewalling needs to be understood as a physiological response.
- When the 20-minute rule and self-soothing practices need to be taught.

## Core Concept
Diffuse Physiological Arousal (DPA) — what Gottman calls flooding — occurs when the stress response system is activated to the point where higher cognitive functions are impaired. Heart rate rises above approximately 100 beats per minute. Cortisol and adrenaline flood the system. The prefrontal cortex — responsible for empathy, perspective-taking, humour, and creative problem-solving — goes offline. The amygdala takes over, and the person is operating from a fight-flight-freeze brain.

In this state, productive conversation is neurologically impossible. The flooded person cannot hear nuance, cannot take their partner's perspective, cannot generate repair attempts, and cannot make or receive compromises. They can only defend, attack, or shut down. Continuing to argue with a flooded partner does not produce resolution — it embeds the negative pattern more deeply into the neural circuitry.

Gottman's research showed that men flood more easily, flood to a higher intensity, and take longer to return to baseline than women, on average. This is not a character flaw — it is a physiological difference with observable hormonal and cardiovascular correlates. It partly explains why stonewalling is more common in men — withdrawal is the nervous system's attempt to reduce unbearable arousal.

The intervention is physiological, not cognitive. The 20-minute rule: when either partner recognises signs of flooding — racing heart, tunnel vision, inability to think, urge to flee — they call a structured break of at least twenty minutes. During the break, the flooded partner engages in self-soothing activities that activate the parasympathetic nervous system: slow breathing, muscle relaxation, a calming activity like reading or walking. The break must include an explicit commitment to return: "I need twenty minutes. I will come back and we will finish this."

## How to Apply
1. **Teach the physiology.** Most people do not know that their inability to think during arguments has a biological basis. Understanding flooding as DPA — not weakness or indifference — reduces shame and increases willingness to self-soothe.
2. **Establish the 20-minute rule.** Both partners agree in advance that either can call a break when flooded. The break is a minimum of twenty minutes — enough time for cortisol to clear. No exceptions.
3. **Self-soothe during the break.** Ruminating about the argument during the break maintains the arousal. The partner must do something genuinely calming — not replay the argument mentally.
4. **Return to the conversation.** The break is not avoidance. The commitment to return is what distinguishes therapeutic self-soothing from stonewalling.

## Examples
**Situation:** During an argument, a man's face goes blank. His wife says he is "checking out" and accuses him of not caring. He says he "can't do this" and leaves the room. She follows him, demanding he stay and finish the conversation.
**Application:** He is flooding. His blank face and withdrawal are not indifference — they are the nervous system's shutdown response to overwhelm. His heart rate is probably above 100 bpm and his cognitive capacity is compromised. Her pursuit, while understandable as an attachment protest, is escalating his arousal further. The intervention is twofold. He needs to learn to identify early signs of flooding and call a structured break before shutdown: "I am flooding. I need twenty minutes. I will come back." She needs to understand that his departure is not abandonment — it is self-regulation. His commitment to return — and his follow-through on that commitment — is what makes the break relational rather than avoidant.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use flooding as a permanent escape from difficult conversations.
**Why:** Self-soothing is a bridge to re-engagement, not a replacement for it. A partner who calls a break and never returns is stonewalling, not self-soothing.

**Don't:** Minimise flooding as "just being dramatic."
**Why:** DPA is a measurable physiological state with observable cardiovascular and hormonal correlates. It is not a performance.
