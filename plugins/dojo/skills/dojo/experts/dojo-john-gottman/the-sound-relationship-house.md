# The Sound Relationship House

## When to Use
- When someone wants the full structural model of relationship health.
- When a systematic framework for assessment or intervention is needed.
- When someone asks "What should we be working on?"

## Core Concept
The Sound Relationship House (SRH) is Gottman's comprehensive model of relationship stability and satisfaction. It organises the research findings into a seven-level structure supported by two weight-bearing walls: trust and commitment. Each level represents a dimension of the relationship that the research has shown to predict satisfaction and stability.

The seven levels, from bottom to top: (1) Build Love Maps — knowing your partner's psychological world: their worries, stresses, joys, dreams, and history. (2) Share Fondness and Admiration — actively expressing appreciation and respect. (3) Turn Toward Instead of Away — responding to your partner's bids for emotional connection. These three form the friendship foundation. (4) The Positive Perspective — when the friendship is strong, a positive sentiment override develops that gives the partner the benefit of the doubt during conflict. (5) Manage Conflict — not resolving conflict but managing it: using soft start-ups, accepting influence, making repair attempts, self-soothing during flooding, and dialoguing about perpetual problems. (6) Make Life Dreams Come True — supporting each other's individual goals and aspirations. (7) Create Shared Meaning — building a shared culture of rituals, roles, goals, and symbols.

The two load-bearing walls — trust and commitment — support the entire structure. Trust, in Gottman's metric, is the belief that the partner is acting to maximise the relationship's benefit, not just their own. Commitment is the belief that this relationship is permanent and worth investing in.

The model is diagnostic: assess each level and you know where the relationship is strong and where it is vulnerable. It is also prescriptive: strengthen the weakest levels and the overall structure stabilises. The critical insight is that the friendship levels are foundational — without them, the upper levels cannot hold weight.

## How to Apply
1. **Assess all seven levels.** A relationship may have strong conflict management but weak friendship, or vice versa. The assessment reveals the pattern.
2. **Build from the bottom up.** Strengthening Love Maps, Fondness and Admiration, and Turning Toward creates the foundation that makes all other work possible.
3. **Check the walls.** If trust or commitment is compromised, the entire house is unstable regardless of the levels' strength. Trust and commitment issues must be addressed directly.
4. **Use the model to prioritise.** Couples often want to work on conflict (Level 5). The therapist's task is often to redirect them to friendship (Levels 1-3) because that is where the leverage is.

## Examples
**Situation:** A couple reports that they fight well — they use soft start-ups, take breaks when flooded, and repair effectively. But they feel empty. The relationship functions smoothly but lacks warmth.
**Application:** The SRH assessment would likely reveal strong Level 5 (conflict management) but weak Levels 1-3 (friendship). They have learned the skills but have not maintained the connection. The therapist would focus on rebuilding Love Maps — when did they last ask each other about their inner worlds? — restoring Fondness and Admiration — when did they last express specific appreciation? — and Turning Toward — are they noticing and responding to each other's daily bids? A relationship can be technically competent but emotionally barren. The friendship levels are what make it feel alive.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat the SRH as a checklist to be completed.
**Why:** The levels are dynamic and interacting. Strengthening one level affects others. The model is a map for understanding, not a programme to execute mechanically.

**Don't:** Ignore the walls.
**Why:** A couple with strong levels but compromised trust or commitment has a structurally unsound relationship. The walls must be assessed and addressed.
