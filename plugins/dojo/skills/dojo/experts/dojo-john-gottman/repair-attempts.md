# Repair Attempts

## When to Use
- When someone asks what distinguishes successful couples from unsuccessful ones.
- When someone needs to understand how to recover after conflict.
- When the concept of repair as the single best predictor of relationship stability needs explaining.

## Core Concept
Gottman's research revealed a finding that surprised even him: the single best predictor of whether a relationship would succeed was not the absence of conflict, not the frequency of sex, not even the ratio of positive to negative interactions — it was the quality of repair attempts during and after conflict.

A repair attempt is any statement or action — verbal, physical, or humorous — that prevents negativity from escalating out of control. It can be as sophisticated as "I think we are getting off track — can we start over?" or as simple as a silly face in the middle of an argument. Gottman found that in relationships he calls "masters," repair attempts succeed because they are received — the partner recognises the bid for de-escalation and accepts it. In "disaster" relationships, repair attempts fail — not because they are absent but because they go unrecognised or are rejected.

The success of a repair attempt depends less on its elegance than on the state of the relationship. In relationships with a strong friendship foundation — solid Love Maps, active Fondness and Admiration, habitual Turning Toward — even clumsy repair attempts succeed because the positive sentiment override is strong enough to give the attempt the benefit of the doubt. In relationships where the friendship has eroded and negative sentiment override dominates, even sophisticated repair attempts fail because the receiving partner is too flooded, too contemptuous, or too defended to let them in.

This means that the ability to repair is both a skill and a consequence. It can be taught — couples can learn to make more deliberate repair attempts and to recognise their partner's attempts — but it also depends on the underlying quality of the friendship. The best way to improve repair is to strengthen the friendship foundation.

## How to Apply
1. **Teach couples to recognise repair attempts.** Many go unnoticed because they do not look like "relationship work" — a joke during a fight, a touch on the arm, a change of subject, a self-deprecating comment.
2. **Teach couples to make deliberate repairs.** "I think I said that wrong — can I try again?" "I know you're upset and I want to understand." "I'm getting flooded — I need a break but I'll come back."
3. **Focus on receiving rather than just sending.** The problem is often not the absence of repair attempts but the failure to receive them. A partner who is flooded or in contempt cannot register a repair. Reducing flooding and contempt enables repair.
4. **Normalise imperfect repair.** Repair does not need to be graceful. "Sorry I was a jerk" is a repair. A hug in the middle of an argument is a repair. Coming back after storming out is a repair.

## Examples
**Situation:** A couple finishes an argument about parenting. They said harsh things. Now they are in separate rooms, each stewing. Neither wants to be the first to bridge the gap. They have been here before — the silence can last days.
**Application:** Gottman would identify the impasse as a failure of repair. Both partners are probably making internal repair attempts — "I shouldn't have said that," "Maybe I was too harsh" — but neither is expressing them. The first to break the silence with any bid for reconnection is making a repair attempt. It does not need to be an apology. It can be "Do you want tea?" — which is relationship code for "I am ready to reconnect." The therapist would help the couple understand that the person who repairs first is not losing — they are leading. And the person who receives the repair by accepting the tea instead of rejecting it is completing the most important transaction in the relationship. Over time, the couple would learn to shorten the post-fight recovery period from days to hours to minutes, not by avoiding conflict but by improving repair.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat repair as a magic fix.
**Why:** Repair does not resolve the underlying issue — it resolves the emotional damage caused by how the issue was discussed. The issue itself may still need to be addressed.

**Don't:** Punish failed repair attempts.
**Why:** If a partner tries to repair and is met with contempt or rejection, they will stop trying. Protecting the repair cycle — making it safe to attempt repair — is critical.
