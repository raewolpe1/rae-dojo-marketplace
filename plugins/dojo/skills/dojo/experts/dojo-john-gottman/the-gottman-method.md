# The Gottman Method

## When to Use
- When someone wants to understand what the Gottman approach is and how it works.
- When someone is considering Gottman therapy and wants to know what to expect.
- When the overall framework needs to be explained before specific concepts.

## Core Concept
The Gottman Method is a research-based approach to couples therapy developed by John Gottman and Julie Schwartz Gottman over four decades of observational research at the University of Washington. It is distinctive for being built from data rather than theory — Gottman observed thousands of couples in his laboratory (the "Love Lab"), measured their interactions through behavioural coding and physiological monitoring, followed them longitudinally, and then identified the specific behaviours that predict relationship success or failure. The therapy was designed to target those specific behaviours.

The method rests on two pillars. The first is the Sound Relationship House theory — a structural model of healthy relationships with seven levels, from friendship at the base through conflict management in the middle to shared meaning at the top. The second is the identification of destructive interaction patterns — the Four Horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling) — and their antidotes.

Gottman therapy begins with an extensive assessment phase: a conjoint session, individual interviews with each partner, and standardised questionnaires. This produces a detailed picture of the relationship's strengths and vulnerabilities. The therapy then targets specific areas identified in the assessment — strengthening friendship, replacing the Four Horsemen with their antidotes, processing past regrettable incidents, and building shared meaning.

The approach is pragmatic and psychoeducational. Gottman therapists teach couples the research findings directly — "Here is what the data show about contempt" — and provide concrete exercises and practices. This contrasts with more process-oriented models like EFT, which work primarily through in-session emotional experience. Gottman therapy works partly through understanding and partly through structured practice.

## How to Apply
1. **Start with assessment.** The Gottman approach emphasises knowing where the couple is before intervening. The assessment phase is not optional — it tells you what to target.
2. **Build friendship before addressing conflict.** The bottom levels of the Sound Relationship House — Love Maps, Fondness and Admiration, Turning Toward — must be strengthened before conflict management work can take hold.
3. **Teach the research.** Gottman therapy is explicitly psychoeducational. Couples benefit from understanding what the data say about their behaviours — knowing that contempt predicts divorce is itself motivating.
4. **Provide concrete practices.** The method includes specific exercises — Love Map questions, stress-reducing conversations, dreams-within-conflict dialogues — that couples practise between sessions.

## Examples
**Situation:** A couple in their early forties has been married for twelve years. They report that they are "growing apart" — they do not fight much but feel like roommates. They are not sure whether therapy can help because "nothing is really wrong."
**Application:** The Gottman assessment would likely reveal that while the Four Horsemen are not rampant, the friendship foundation has eroded. The couple has probably stopped building Love Maps — they do not know each other's current inner worlds. They have probably stopped turning toward each other's bids — small moments of connection are being missed. And they may have settled into a comfortable but emotionally disengaged routine. The therapy would focus on the bottom levels of the Sound Relationship House: rebuilding the friendship through Love Map exercises, reinstating rituals of connection, and increasing the ratio of turning toward to turning away. The Gottmans describe this as "making deposits in the emotional bank account" — small, daily investments in the friendship that compound over time.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present the Gottman method as a set of rules to follow.
**Why:** The research findings are descriptive, not prescriptive. "Maintain a 5:1 ratio" is not a rule to obey — it is a pattern observed in stable relationships. The spirit matters more than the letter.

**Don't:** Focus exclusively on eliminating negatives.
**Why:** The Gottman approach is as much about building positives — friendship, fondness, admiration, turning toward — as it is about reducing the Four Horsemen. A relationship that avoids all negativity but has no positive engagement is not healthy.
