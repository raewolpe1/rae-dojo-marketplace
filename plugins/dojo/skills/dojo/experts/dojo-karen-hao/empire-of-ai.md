# Empire of AI

## When to Use
- When AI is being presented as a neutral technology and needs to be reframed as a political-economic structure — an empire with specific components: metropolitan capital, peripheral labour, resource frontiers, and ideological self-justification.
- When the structural parallels between the AI industry and historical empires illuminate the dynamics at play.
- When *Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI* (2025) provides the primary framework.

## Core Concept
The empire frame is Hao's central contribution. It holds that the AI industry is best understood not as a technology sector but as an empire in formation — structurally analogous to the colonial empires of the nineteenth century and the corporate empires of the twentieth. The parallels are specific, not merely rhetorical.

Metropolitan capital: the AI industry is concentrated in a handful of companies headquartered in the San Francisco Bay Area (and, increasingly, in Shenzhen and Beijing). These companies control the capital — the compute infrastructure, the training data, the talent, and the investor relationships — that produces frontier AI systems. The concentration is extreme: the top five companies control a share of the industry's productive capacity that exceeds the concentration in oil, telecommunications, or banking.

Peripheral labour: the AI systems produced by metropolitan capital depend on human labour performed in the Global South. Data annotation, content moderation, and RLHF feedback are outsourced to workers in Kenya, the Philippines, Venezuela, India, and elsewhere — workers who are paid poverty wages, classified as contractors, and deliberately rendered invisible. This labour is not a temporary stage; it is a permanent requirement of AI production.

Resource frontiers: the compute infrastructure that AI requires — data centres, energy, water, minerals — is sited in locations chosen for cheap energy, abundant water, weak regulation, and low land costs. These locations — Chile, Iowa, Northern Virginia, Scandinavia — become resource frontiers: sites of extraction whose environmental costs are borne by local communities.

Ideological self-justification: every empire produces an ideology that justifies the extraction. The AI empire's ideology takes two forms: utopian ("AI will solve humanity's greatest problems") and apocalyptic ("AI might destroy humanity unless the wise few govern it"). Both framings direct attention toward imagined futures and away from the present structure — concentrating power in the hands of those who claim to be either saving or safeguarding humanity.

## How to Apply
1. **Map the empire.** "For any AI deployment — in your organisation, your country, your sector — map the four components: where is the capital? Where is the labour? Where are the resources? What ideology justifies the arrangement?"
2. **Name the structural parallels.** "The AI industry's structure parallels historical empires: metropolitan centres extracting value from peripheries while narrating the extraction as civilisation, development, or progress. Name the parallel — it illuminates the dynamics."
3. **Apply the two-paths framing.** "The choice is not AI or no AI. The choice is between AI-as-empire (concentrated, extractive, unaccountable) and AI-as-governed-commons (distributed, regulated, accountable). What institutional arrangements would produce the second path?"
4. **Ground in evidence.** "The empire frame is not a theoretical claim — it is an empirical observation, documented through years of access reporting. Use the specific evidence: named companies, documented labour conditions, verified resource consumption, specific ideological claims."

## Examples
**Situation:** A regional development bank is designing an AI strategy for its member countries. The strategy proposes that countries should "participate in the AI economy" by attracting data centre investment, training AI engineers, and adopting AI applications in public services.
**Application:** "The strategy positions the member countries as participants in the AI economy — but the empire frame reveals what kind of participation is being proposed. As data centre hosts: the countries provide land, energy, and water while the compute infrastructure serves metropolitan AI companies. The value is extracted; the environmental costs are local. As engineer suppliers: the countries train talent that serves metropolitan demand — the engineers build products for Silicon Valley, not for their own countries. As application consumers: the countries adopt products designed for metropolitan markets and adapted (minimally) for local use. In each role, the countries are positioned as periphery in the AI empire: providing resources and labour, consuming products, and capturing a fraction of the value that flows to the metropolitan centre. The alternative: design the strategy for sovereignty, not for participation. Support local AI research institutions. Fund open-source AI development. Establish data-sovereignty frameworks. Negotiate infrastructure agreements that include community consent, environmental standards, and local benefit-sharing. Position the countries not as consumers of an empire's products but as builders of their own AI capacity."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Reduce "empire" to a metaphor.
**Why:** The empire frame is a structural analysis, not a rhetorical device. The parallels with historical empires — metropolitan capital, peripheral labour, resource frontiers, ideological justification — are specific and documented.

**Don't:** Deny all benefits.
**Why:** AI produces genuine goods. The empire critique does not deny this — it asks who captures the benefits, who bears the costs, and what alternative structure might distribute both more justly.
