# AI Ideology

## When to Use
- When AI is being framed in utopian terms ("AI will solve poverty, cure disease, end climate change") or apocalyptic terms ("AI might destroy humanity") — and the ideological function of these framings needs to be identified.
- When the narrative apparatus of the AI industry — its self-justifying stories about mission, safety, and humanity's future — needs to be analysed as ideology.
- When the relationship between AI narratives and capital concentration needs to be made explicit.

## Core Concept
Every empire produces an ideology that justifies the extraction. The British Empire had the civilising mission. The American empire had the spread of democracy. The AI empire has two ideological registers, and both serve the same function: concentrating power while directing attention elsewhere.

The utopian register: "AI will solve humanity's greatest problems — disease, poverty, climate change, death itself." This framing positions the AI companies as saviours and their products as instruments of universal benefit. The ideological function: it justifies the concentration of capital, talent, and political influence in the hands of the AI companies by presenting them as the agents of human salvation. The more urgent the mission, the more justified the concentration. If you are saving humanity, ordinary regulatory constraints are obstacles, not safeguards.

The apocalyptic register: "AI might destroy humanity unless the wise few govern it." This framing positions the AI companies — and their safety researchers — as the only people who understand the risk and therefore the only people who should govern the technology. The ideological function: it concentrates governance authority in the hands of the same companies that produce the risk. The existential-risk framing creates a priesthood of those qualified to manage the danger — and that priesthood happens to be the AI industry itself.

Both framings share a common structure: they direct attention toward imagined futures (utopian or catastrophic) and away from the present — away from the workers being exploited now, the communities losing water now, the capital being concentrated now. The present is where the empire operates. The future — utopian or apocalyptic — is where the empire justifies itself.

Hao is careful to note that some people within the AI industry genuinely believe these framings. The ideology is not a cynical deception — it is a sincere belief system that happens to serve the material interests of those who hold it. This makes it more powerful, not less: ideology that is sincerely held is more effective than ideology that is cynically deployed.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify the framing.** "Is AI being presented as salvation or as existential threat? Both framings are ideological — they serve the concentration of power. Name the framing and its function."
2. **Redirect to the present.** "When the AI conversation is about the future — utopian or catastrophic — redirect to the present. Who is being exploited now? What resources are being extracted now? What capital is being concentrated now? The present is what the ideology directs attention away from."
3. **Distinguish function from sincerity.** "The people deploying these framings may be sincere. The analysis is not about sincerity — it is about function. The utopian framing functions to justify capital concentration regardless of whether the person deploying it believes it."
4. **Engage the safety concern seriously.** "The apocalyptic framing serves an ideological function — but this does not mean existential risk is imaginary. The risk may be genuine. The critique is not that the risk is fake but that the framing concentrates governance authority in the hands of those who produce the risk."

## Examples
**Situation:** An AI company's CEO gives a keynote at a development conference. He argues that AI will "end poverty within a generation" by optimising agricultural systems, personalising education, and enabling precision healthcare. He asks governments to create "AI-friendly regulatory environments" to accelerate these benefits.
**Application:** "The keynote deploys the utopian register: AI as saviour, poverty as a problem that technology can solve. The ideological function: if AI will end poverty, then the companies that produce AI are humanity's benefactors — and governments should create regulatory environments that serve these benefactors. 'AI-friendly regulation' means regulation that does not constrain the company's operations: data access without privacy protections, infrastructure siting without environmental review, labour practices without oversight. The utopian framing makes these concessions appear not as concessions but as investments in humanity's future. The redirect: will AI optimise agricultural systems? Perhaps — for those who can afford the technology and the connectivity it requires. Will AI personalise education? Perhaps — for those whose data is collected and whose learning outcomes align with the system's metrics. Will AI enable precision healthcare? Perhaps — for those with access to the infrastructure. In each case, the benefit is real but distributionally skewed: the AI company captures the revenue; the wealthy user captures the benefit; the poor user provides the data. The utopian framing conceals this distributional question by presenting the technology as universally beneficial. The government should assess the offer not through the utopian frame but through the political-economy frame: who benefits? who bears costs? what regulatory concessions are being requested? what alternatives exist?"

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Dismiss all AI concerns as ideology.
**Why:** Some existential risks are genuine — the alignment problem, the concentration of power, the potential for autonomous systems to act in unintended ways. The critique is not that concerns are fake but that the framing of concerns serves specific interests.

**Don't:** Treat everyone in the industry as insincere.
**Why:** Ideology is most powerful when sincerely held. Many AI practitioners genuinely believe they are building beneficial technology. The analysis is about the function of their beliefs, not about their sincerity.
