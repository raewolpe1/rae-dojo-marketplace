# Attention and Prosoche

## When to Use
- When the fundamental problem is attention: the practitioner is not present to their own work, their own decisions, their own life.
- When scattered busyness has replaced focused engagement with what matters.
- When establishing the foundation for all other exercises — since without attention, no other exercise can be practised.

## Core Concept
Prosoche — attention, vigilance, presence of mind — is what Hadot identified as the fundamental spiritual exercise of Stoicism and, arguably, of all ancient philosophy. Before the practitioner can examine their conscience, contemplate the cosmos, or meditate on death, they must first be present — attentive to what they are doing, thinking, and choosing in each moment.

For the Stoics, prosoche was specifically attention to one's ruling faculty (hegemonikon) — vigilance over one's judgments, desires, and impulses. It was not passive awareness but active monitoring: am I judging correctly? Am I desiring what is within my control? Am I acting according to reason and nature? This constant self-monitoring was not anxious self-surveillance but a practice of freedom — the freedom that comes from governing one's own responses rather than being governed by automatic reactions.

Hadot emphasised that prosoche is not merely cognitive but moral. To be attentive is to take responsibility for one's responses. The unattentive person reacts automatically — to stimuli, to institutional expectations, to habitual patterns — and therefore does not genuinely choose. The attentive person pauses between stimulus and response, examines the judgment that shapes the response, and acts from deliberation rather than habit. This is the foundation of the philosophical life.

## How to Apply
1. **Establish the attention check.** "Before every significant decision, pause. Ask: am I attending to this — actually present to what I am doing and why — or am I processing it automatically?"
2. **Monitor judgments.** "When you react to a situation — with frustration, with enthusiasm, with anxiety — examine the judgment behind the reaction. Is the judgment accurate? Is it within your control? The reaction follows the judgment; change the judgment and the reaction changes."
3. **Practise the interruption.** "Prosoche interrupts the automatic flow. Build interruption points into your day: before meetings, before writing reports, before responding to emails. Each interruption is a moment of attention."
4. **Extend attention to others.** "Prosoche is not only self-attention but attention to others — listening to what people actually say rather than processing their input through predetermined categories."

## Examples
**Situation:** A senior evaluator realises that she has been conducting evaluations on autopilot for several years — applying the same methodology, asking the same questions, producing structurally identical reports regardless of the programme being evaluated. The evaluations are technically competent but have become mechanical.
**Application:** "You have lost prosoche — attention to your own practice. The methodology runs itself, and you process programmes through it rather than attending to each programme's distinctive character. The Stoic remedy is specific: re-establish attention at the point of judgment. Before each evaluation, sit with the programme documents and ask: what is distinctive about this programme? What does this situation actually require, as opposed to what my standard methodology provides? This is not a cognitive exercise — it is a moral one. To evaluate mechanically is to treat the programme and its participants as instances of a category rather than as particular realities deserving particular attention. The exercise is daily: each morning of fieldwork, before the first interview, pause and ask: what am I actually here to understand? Not: what does my methodology require me to ask? Each evening, review: did I attend today, or did I process? Where did I hear something I did not expect? Where did I miss something because I was not listening? This examination is prosoche applied to evaluative practice — and over weeks, it transforms the mechanical evaluator into an attentive one."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Equate prosoche with modern secular mindfulness.
**Why:** Modern mindfulness is often presented as a stress-reduction technique. Prosoche is a moral practice embedded in a philosophical way of life. It is not about feeling calm; it is about governing one's judgments and taking responsibility for one's responses.

**Don't:** Present attention as effortless.
**Why:** Prosoche requires sustained effort. The default human state is inattention — automatic response, habitual judgment, reactive emotion. Attention is a practice that must be renewed daily, not a state that once achieved is maintained without effort.
