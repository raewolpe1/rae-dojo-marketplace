# State Capability and the Disabled System

> **`[CHECK]` throughout.** Reconstructed from secondary material. Verify before attribution.

## When to Use
- When a strategy assumes the institutions that must deliver it are capable.
- When innovation or industrial policy is designed without naming the deliverer.
- When explaining an implementation gap.

## Fails When
- **The question is state capability as a general development problem.** Route to Pritchett, who is already in the collection and owns that.

## Core Concept

**Innovation policy that makes no reference to the capability of the state that must deliver it is a wish list.** `[CHECK]`

This is the through-line of Kaplan's later work and it is not a rhetorical flourish — it is the finding of a career.

**The arc gives the claim its weight.** He began by documenting what the state **contributed** to South Africa's considerable industrial development. He shifted to a critical analysis of the state as the apartheid state became **predatory and obstructionist to economic growth**. And the critical stance **survived the transition**: his many publications analysed how the considerable strengths of South Africa's NSI were **disabled in the post-Transition era by a predatory and often incompetent state**. `[CHECK]`

**Note the two failure modes named, because they are different and need different answers:**

- **Predatory** — the state extracting rather than building. A political-economy problem. Not fixable by training.
- **Incompetent** — the state unable to do the thing it intends. A capability problem. Fixable, slowly.

A strategy addressing one while facing the other will fail, and most strategies name neither.

**Why this matters for a document like yours specifically.** The strategy contains initiatives. Each initiative has an implicit deliverer — a department, an agency, a municipality, an entity. **The document almost never asks whether that institution can deliver it**, because asking is awkward when the document is being written for the institution. Kaplan's position, arrived at from six years inside a provincial department and three inside the DTI, is that the question must be asked anyway, and that not asking it is what produces strategies whose implementation records are uniformly poor and uniformly surprising to their authors.

**The positive formulation.** His argument was that the key to **productivity-led growth** lay in a **dynamic private sector working together with the state, business services, and educational and research institutions in an efficient National System of Innovation.** `[CHECK]` Note the construction: **working together**, and **efficient**. The state is one node among several and its function is to make the system work — which requires that it be able to.

**The uncomfortable test.** For each initiative in the strategy: name the institution that will deliver it, and say honestly whether it can. Where it cannot, either build the capability first or do not promise the initiative. **This will shorten the strategy considerably**, and that is the point.

## How to Apply

1. **Name the deliverer for every initiative.** Then ask whether it can.
2. **Distinguish predation from incompetence.** Different diagnoses, different remedies, and neither is usually named.
3. **Do not assume capability because the document is written for the department.** That is exactly when the question gets skipped.
4. **Use the positive formulation** — the system works when the private sector, state, business services and research institutions work together *efficiently*. The state's job is to make that work.
5. **Expect the honest audit to shorten the strategy.** That is the finding, not a failure.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy assigns 90 initiatives across a provincial department, its agencies, and municipalities.

**Application:** Run the audit. For each initiative: which institution delivers, and can it? The answer will be uncomfortable — a provincial department with a modest budget and a large existing mandate cannot deliver ninety new things, and the municipalities have their own capability position. Kaplan's stance, from inside such a department, is that this is knowable in advance and that not asking is a choice. The redesign is not to abandon the ambition but to **sequence against capability**: what can be delivered now, what requires capability that must be built first, and what should not be promised. **A strategy of twenty deliverable initiatives outperforms one of ninety undeliverable ones**, and the difference is visible only if the question is asked.

**Result:** A strategy sequenced against the capability of the institutions that must deliver it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Write innovation policy without naming the delivering institution.
**Why:** It is a wish list. This is the through-line of his later work.

**Don't:** Conflate predation and incompetence.
**Why:** One is political economy, one is capability. Different remedies entirely.

**Don't:** Skip the capability question because the client is the deliverer.
**Why:** That is precisely when it is skipped and precisely when it matters.
