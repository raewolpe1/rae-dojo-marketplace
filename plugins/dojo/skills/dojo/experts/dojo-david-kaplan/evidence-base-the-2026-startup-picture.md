## THE EVIDENCE

### The sector today
- **Reported:** **560 VC-funded startups employing >11,000 people (2024).** **525 startups secured VC funding; 951 deals in 5 years (2020–24); R13.5bn assets under management YE 2024.** Peak of **3,279 deals in 2023**; **>35 VC-backed acquisitions since 2015**. Average team size ~22. **VC concentration: 30%+ in fintech; 65.9% of all VC deals into ICT; 20.0% into health.**
- **Caveat:** The employment figure (>11,000) matches Kaplan's 2024 citation of Disrupt Africa almost exactly — **it may be the same underlying count re-dated.** Do not treat as independent corroboration. Note also the deal counts here (951 over five years) are not reconcilable at face value with the "3,279 deals in 2023" peak; different definitions are evidently in play. **Attribute to SAVCA 2025 via this report and do not net the series against each other.**

### The geography — and the township finding
- **Reported:** **88% of startups based in Cape Town & Johannesburg.** **"TOWNSHIP & RURAL UNDER-SERVED."** Founders **predominantly urban and male**; **<15% of startups have a female co-founder**. Under inclusion opportunities: **"Township & rural areas need more capital and networks"**; "Insufficient # of gender-targeted accelerators"; **"Leverage procurement mechanisms to scale market access."**
- **Caveat:** 88% for two metros, with the Western Cape the larger half on Kaplan's 2022 SAVCA figures (53.7% of value). **So the sector is a Cape Town and Johannesburg sector, and within Cape Town it is not a township sector.** That is a direct and uncomfortable input to a strategy with a township economy cross-cutting theme: the segment where the province leads the country is the segment that reaches townships least.
- The **<15% female co-founder** figure is worse than Kaplan's 2024 citation ("one in seven" ≈ 14%) — i.e. **flat, not improving**, across two years and two sources.

### VC is not the answer for most firms — anywhere
- **Reported:** **"Getting external capital, especially in the form of early-stage risk capital, is an avenue open to only a very small minority of firms, anywhere in the world. This is important to appreciate, as Venture Capital is suited to a very specific type of business, and should therefore not be seen as an option for all the capital needs of startup enterprises."**

| Region | VC-funded | **Non-VC** (self-funded, bootstrapped, unfunded) |
|---|---|---|
| **USA** | **1–2%** (600–800 funded annually among 2 million new firms) | **98–99%** |
| **RSA** | 525 startups funded 2020–24 | **98–99%** |
| UK | ~4% of British investment is VC | 96% |
| Europe (EU-wide) | 5% (EU = 5% of global VC funding) | 95% |
| India | ~3–5% | 95–97% |
| Brazil | 2–4% | 96–98% |
| Chile | <5% likely | >95% |
| AVCA (Africa) | likely 1–2% | 98–99% |

- Table sourced by the report to **AVCA, PitchBook and the World Bank**.
- **Caveat — THIS IS THE MOST ANALYTICALLY IMPORTANT ITEM IN THE FILE.** Several cells are the report's own estimates ("likely", "no direct figure"). But the top line is solid and it reframes the whole conversation: **South Africa's ">90% self-funded or informally capitalised" is not a South African failure. It is the American rate.** The US runs 98–99% non-VC.
- **What this does to Kaplan:** it does **not** refute him — he scoped his binding-constraint claim to **high-potential start-ups**, a population for whom VC genuinely is the scaling instrument. It refutes the *unscoped* version. **And note the report's own internal tension:** having established that VC reaches 1–2% of firms anywhere, it goes on to characterise SA's capital position as **"Under-scaled"** and **"under-scaled for demand."** Both moves are in the same document. The first is the disciplined one.

### The capital stack (2025)
- **Reported:** roughly **R3.3 billion in fresh VC**; a **R200 million-plus angel market (continent-wide)**; **R1.6 billion of crowdfunding globally**; and several hundred-million-rand government vehicles. **"Appears UNDER-SCALED RELATIVE TO NEED."**
  - **VC:** equity deployed R2.62bn; venture debt R670m; total new deployments R3.29bn; active portfolio under management R13.35bn; cumulative investment (14 years) R10.73bn.
  - **PE:** SAVCA members' AUM **>R213bn** — majority into buy-outs, but growth in late-stage tech and deep-tech.
  - **Angel:** Pan-Africa H1 2024 **US$200m committed**; **SA among top 3 destinations alongside Nigeria and Kenya**; no consolidated national figure.
  - **Government & DFI:** **TIA R170m** via the Industry Matching Fund since 2019, plus a **R300m High-Impact Fund launched end-2024**; **IDC R18.7bn total FY2023/24, SME-Connect R954m**; **SA SME Fund R1.4bn mandate, ~R725m committed to partner funds**; **UTF II targeting R400m for deep-tech commercialisation**.
- **Caveat:** Note the ratio that matters and that the report does not draw out: **PE AUM >R213bn against VC new deployments of R3.29bn — roughly 65:1.** Kaplan's pension-fund paradox, still standing, two years on. **Kaplan sat on the TIA board**; its R170m since 2019 against R4.6 trillion in pension assets is the same story in a different number.

### The benchmark position
- **Reported:** SA **NECI 3.9**, **GEM rank 39th–51st of 50** — "Among lowest in GEM; experienced decline driven by weaker social norms & program support." Peers: **Chile 4.9 (12th–13th)**, Egypt 4.2 (17th–20th), Brazil 4.0, Tunisia 4.0, **Morocco 3.9 (25th–30th)**, Singapore 5th globally. Kenya and Nigeria not measured in global NECI (2.24/5 and 2.33/5 regionally).
- Other indices: **Economic Freedom 64/100 ("Moderately Free")**; **Ease of Doing Business ranked 100–180**; **GEDI 24–30/100**; **Opinium SSA 26/100**.
- **StartupBlink: rank #52 globally, growth +19.5%, "Stagnant in rank."** "Better than GEM suggests — above African peers." **"Still continental leader, but losing momentum advantage. Outpaced in growth by Egypt (22%) and Kenya (33.5%)."**
- The report's verdict: **"South Africa sits in the lower tier of global entrepreneurship indices — despite having functional institutions, a developed IP regime, and moderate economic freedom... The gap is not structural potential — it is policy execution."**
- **Caveat — A DISTINCTION THAT WILL TRIP A READER, AND POSSIBLY THIS PROJECT.** **NECI is not TEA.** NECI is the National Entrepreneurship Context Index — a measure of *conditions*, built from expert panels. TEA is Total early-stage Entrepreneurial Activity — a measure of *participation*, built from population surveys. **This report is citing NECI.** A strategy that has Western Cape TEA at 15.00% and reads "South Africa is among the lowest in GEM" has silently swapped constructs — the two can diverge sharply, and a country can run above-average TEA with bottom-tier NECI. That combination would in fact be the coherent reading of a place with a lot of necessity entrepreneurship and poor conditions. **Route this to the Autio dojo — "an index is a theory made arithmetic" — before either number goes near a DEDAT document.**

### Strengths, and the three named weaknesses
- **Reported strengths:** Economic Freedom 64/100, ahead of Brazil and Tunisia; **IP system strong and developed, better than many African counterparts**; Governance (WGI) stronger than Nigeria, Kenya, Tunisia; **R&D incentives: 150% R&D tax deduction — more generous than Brazil's Lei do Bem**; OECD Inclusive Entrepreneurship frameworks exist.
- **Reported weaknesses vs global peers — three, and they are specific:**
  1. **Exchange Control: "one of the most restrictive globally — limits foreign IP and capital flows."** ← **Kaplan, confirmed.**
  2. **Immigration: "no dedicated startup visa unlike Chile, Brazil, Singapore, Kenya."** ← **Hausmann's people-as-transmission point, arriving as a policy gap.**
  3. **Tax & incentives: "R&D incentive is re-imbursive — unsuited to pre-revenue startups."** ← a start-up with no revenue cannot use a deduction. The 150% deduction is simultaneously a listed strength and structurally unusable by the target population.
- **Caveat:** Items 1 and 3 are checkable regulatory facts and align with Kaplan. Item 2 is national (Home Affairs).

### The report's own headline recommendation
- **Reported:** **"GEM indicates that South Africa has not materially expanded the underlying entrepreneurial pipeline"**, while **"StartupBlink, by contrast, reflects a more active ecosystem, but one that has not significantly improved its global positioning; suggesting growth concentrated among already-active startups rather than broad-based entrepreneurial expansion."** Therefore: **"a system that is strengthening participation within the existing startup cohort, but not yet converting South Africa's broader economic base into a scalable pipeline of new entrepreneurs."** The call: **"SHIFT THE NARRATIVE from optimising for existing startups to expanding the entrepreneurial pipeline"**, with **"clearer policy separation between high-growth startup support and broader entrepreneurship activation"** and a focus on **"scalable, tech/innovation-enabled startups — not SMEs in general."**
- **Caveat — RUN THIS PAST THE ROSTER BEFORE ADOPTING IT.** Two of its moves are excellent and one is contestable.
  - **Excellent:** "clearer policy separation between high-growth startup support and broader entrepreneurship activation," and "not SMEs in general." **That is Schoar's transformational/subsistence distinction arriving in a South African policy document, independently.** Your three-segment architecture already encodes it.
  - **Contestable:** **"expanding the entrepreneurial pipeline."** The pipeline metaphor assumes the broader economic base converts into high-growth entrepreneurs at some rate that policy can raise. **Sutton's Ethiopian finding — two of fifty frontier firms began as small manufacturers — and Schoar's non-transition finding both say the conversion barely happens.** The report may be right that participation should broaden; the *pipeline* framing for how is precisely the assumption your roster is built to test. **Do not adopt the phrase without deciding whether you believe the mechanism.**

## How to Apply

1. **Never attribute this to Kaplan.** He was dead six months before publication.
2. **Treat it as a well-evidenced advocacy brief**, because that is what it is.
3. **Use it to confirm Kaplan's regulatory diagnosis** — exchange control, still "one of the most restrictive globally," two years on.
4. **Use the 1–2% VC penetration table to discipline the capital conversation.** SA's non-VC rate is the American rate.
5. **Check NECI against TEA before citing either.** Different constructs. Route to Autio.
6. **Take the "not SMEs in general" separation; interrogate the "pipeline."**
7. **Note the township finding against your own CC2.** 88% metro; townships under-served; the province leads the country in the sector least present in its townships.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy wants to cite the 2026 report's conclusion that South Africa's start-up ecosystem underperforms.

**Application:** Cite it — the diagnosis is sound and it confirms Kaplan. But three disciplines. **First, name the construct:** the underperformance is measured on **NECI**, an expert-panel index of *conditions*, not on TEA, a population measure of *participation*. If your own GEMS work reports Western Cape TEA above the global average, you have two GEM numbers pointing in opposite directions and you must explain rather than pick. **Second, scope the capital claim:** the report itself establishes VC reaches 1–2% of new firms in the US, so "under-scaled" cannot mean "should fund most firms." **Third, do not inherit the pipeline framing** without deciding whether you accept that the broader base converts — Sutton and Schoar say it mostly does not, and your Groundbreakers/Trailblazers separation exists because of that.

**Result:** A citation that survives the reviewer who reads the methodology.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Present this as Kaplan's or as an extension of his brief.
**Why:** He died in December 2025. It is a different author with a different purpose.

**Don't:** Read "among the lowest in GEM" as a statement about entrepreneurial activity.
**Why:** It is NECI — conditions. TEA is participation. They diverge.

**Don't:** Adopt "expanding the entrepreneurial pipeline" as a strategic objective on this report's authority.
**Why:** It is the assumption Sutton and Schoar both contradict. Decide it, don't inherit it.
