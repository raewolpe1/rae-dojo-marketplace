# South Africa's NSI — Strengths, and Disablement

> **`[CHECK]` throughout.** Reconstructed from obituary material and institutional biography, not from close reading of the primary works. Verify before attribution.

## When to Use
- **Load first on any Kaplan question.** It is the premise.
- When South Africa's innovation system is characterised as underdeveloped or embryonic.
- When explaining post-transition innovation performance.

## Fails When
- **The question is the general theory of innovation systems.** Route to Lundvall, whose framework Kaplan worked within.

## Core Concept

**The claim that distinguishes Kaplan from almost everyone writing about South African innovation:**

> **South Africa's NSI had considerable strengths. Those strengths were disabled, in the post-Transition era, by a predatory and often incompetent state.** `[CHECK]`

**Read the two halves separately, because each is doing work.**

**"Considerable strengths."** Not potential, not embryonic, not a developing-country research base needing construction. **Strengths, in place.** Research institutions of real quality; firms with genuine technological capability; a science base most economies at this income level do not have; and world-class industries in places nobody looks — the **mining equipment and specialist services sector** being the standing example: technologically sophisticated and globally competitive. `[CHECK]` This is historically specific and it was not built by a policy document.

**"Disabled by a predatory and often incompetent state."** Not eroded by markets. Not outgrown. Not underfunded in the first instance. **Disabled**, and by an identifiable agent. The word is causal and it was chosen.

**Why the distinction reorganises everything downstream:**

| If the diagnosis is... | Then the remedy is... | And the strategy... |
|---|---|---|
| **Underdevelopment** | Build the system | Funds new institutions, R&D, universities |
| **Disablement** | Remove what broke it | Addresses state capability and predation first |

**Most South African innovation strategy assumes the first.** It reads as a developing country building capability. Kaplan's record says the second — which implies that the binding constraint is not the absence of institutions but the state's relationship to the ones that exist.

**The hope underneath, which is easy to miss.** Disablement is a *more* hopeful diagnosis than underdevelopment. Something disabled was working. It can, in principle, be un-disabled — whereas building a system from nothing takes generations. **He arrived at the critical position by watching, not by disposition:** he began his career documenting what the state contributed to South Africa's industrial development, and shifted to a critical analysis as the apartheid state became predatory and obstructionist. The critical stance then survived the transition, which is the uncomfortable part of the record. `[CHECK]`

**The intellectual arc, which authorises the claim.** Trained as an economist **and an economic historian**. His account is historical-institutional: he could describe how the capability was built because he had studied the building. That is why the "strengths" claim is not sentimental — he knew what was there because he had traced its construction.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what was already there** before designing anything new.
2. **Test the diagnosis.** Underdevelopment or disablement? They have opposite remedies.
3. **Name the disabling agent** if the diagnosis is disablement. Predation and incompetence are specific and addressable.
4. **Do not read this as pessimism.** The premise is strengths.
5. **Look for the historical account.** The capability has a history; the strategy usually does not know it.

## Examples

**Situation:** A provincial strategy's innovation chapter opens by describing a weak innovation base requiring development.

**Application:** Test the premise against the record. South Africa built substantial productive and innovative capability, much of which persists — and the province holds a share of it, including in places the sector list does not look. If the diagnosis is *weak base*, the strategy will fund institution-building. If the diagnosis is *disabled strengths*, it would instead ask what exists, what is preventing it from performing, and whether any of that is within provincial reach. **The two strategies share no instruments.** Establishing which diagnosis is right is therefore the first analytical task, not a framing paragraph.

**Result:** A diagnosis that determines the instrument set rather than decorating it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Describe the SA innovation system as embryonic.
**Why:** Kaplan's record says it had considerable strengths. That is the premise of everything he wrote.

**Don't:** Read the disablement claim as cynicism.
**Why:** It is the more hopeful diagnosis. Something disabled was working.

**Don't:** Attribute this claim without checking the published work.
