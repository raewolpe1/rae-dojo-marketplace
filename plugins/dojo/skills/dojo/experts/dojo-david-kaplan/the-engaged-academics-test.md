# The Engaged Academic's Test

> **`[CHECK]`.** Reconstructed from characterisations of his method in obituary and institutional material. The *method* is well attested; the formulation below is a reconstruction.

## When to Use
- When asking whether a piece of analysis is finished.
- When writing for a government client.
- When a recommendation is analytically correct and cannot be implemented.

## Fails When
- **The audience is academic.** Different standard, legitimately.

## Core Concept

**The test, in one question: can the institution this is addressed to act on it?**

**If not, it may still be true, and it is not yet finished.**

**Why this is a standard and not a platitude.** Kaplan was, above all, **an engaged academic** — he made a significant contribution to the development and implementation of policy in relation to industrial development, international trade, labour markets, science and technology and innovation. `[CHECK]` And the engagement was not advisory-from-a-distance: **first Chief Economist of the DTI (2000–2003); Chief Economist of the Western Cape Department of Economic Development and Tourism (2004–2010); NACI; the Technology Innovation Agency board; the Ministerial Committee on Science and Technology; TIPS Research Fellow to the end.** Nine years holding chief economist posts inside government.

**What that vantage teaches, and it is specific.** The gap between an analytically correct recommendation and an implementable one is usually **not a gap in understanding.** It is a gap in **capability**, in **mandate**, in **budget**, and in the twenty other things the department is already required to do this quarter. An adviser who has not sat inside tends to diagnose the gap as ignorance and to respond by explaining more clearly. That does not close it.

**The three questions the test decomposes into:**

1. **Mandate** — is this the institution's to do? (See `what-a-provincial-department-can-do.md`. Usually a good deal of the strategy is not.)
2. **Capability** — can it do it? (See `state-capability-and-the-disabled-system.md`. Ask honestly, especially when the client is the deliverer.)
3. **Bandwidth** — will it, given everything else? A department with ninety new initiatives and an existing mandate does not have ninety initiatives' worth of attention.

**The uncomfortable corollary for a consultant.** The test applies to your own deliverable. A strategy that is analytically excellent and cannot be implemented has failed by this standard, and the failure is the author's, not the department's. **This is the most demanding voice in the roster to have looking over your shoulder** — because he did the job, on this department, and he would apply it to the document rather than to the client.

**What it is not.** It is not an argument for lowering ambition to what is comfortable. It is an argument for **making the constraint explicit** — naming what cannot be done and why, rather than promising it and discovering later. A short strategy that says what will not be attempted is more useful than a long one that quietly will not attempt most of it.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask it of every recommendation.** Can the addressee act on it?
2. **Decompose: mandate, capability, bandwidth.** Most failures are one of the three, and they have different answers.
3. **Do not respond to an implementation gap by explaining more clearly.** It is rarely ignorance.
4. **Apply it to your own deliverable.** That is the point of the standard.
5. **Name what will not be done.** Explicitly. That is the useful move.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy is analytically strong, evidence-based, and contains ninety initiatives across a provincial department, its agencies and municipalities.

**Application:** Run the test on the document. **Mandate:** how many of the ninety are the province's to do? A significant share are national — regulation, the science base, immigration. **Capability:** of the remainder, how many can the assigned institution actually deliver? **Bandwidth:** the department has an existing mandate and a quarter that is already full. The honest answer will be a much smaller number, and the strategy's value depends on saying so. **The failure mode is not that the department will underperform — it is that the document promised something no reading of the department's mandate, capability or bandwidth would support**, and that is a defect in the document.

**Result:** A strategy whose commitments its addressee can honour, and an explicit account of what was not attempted.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat an implementation gap as an understanding gap.
**Why:** It is usually mandate, capability or bandwidth. Explaining harder does not help.

**Don't:** Exempt your own deliverable from the test.
**Why:** That is the whole standard, and he applied it to himself for nine years inside government.

**Don't:** Read this as a counsel of low ambition.
**Why:** It is a counsel of explicit constraint. Say what will not be done.
