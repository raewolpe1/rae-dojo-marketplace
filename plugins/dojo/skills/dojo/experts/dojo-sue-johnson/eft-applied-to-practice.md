# EFT Applied to Practice

## When to Use
- When someone wants daily, practical ways to strengthen their relationship.
- When someone is asking what they can do between therapy sessions.
- When the gap between therapy insight and daily relational life needs to be bridged.

## Core Concept
EFT is not only a therapy model — it is a framework for understanding and practising relational connection in daily life. The same principles that guide therapeutic change also guide the daily maintenance of a secure bond. The fundamental practice is simple: emotional responsiveness. Can I reach you? Will you respond to me? Do I matter to you? These questions are answered not in grand gestures but in the small, repeated interactions that constitute daily life — the way you greet your partner, the way you respond when they share something, the way you repair when you have been absent or harsh.

Johnson identifies several practices that emerge from the EFT framework for daily use. These are not techniques in the behavioural sense — they are relational habits rooted in attachment awareness.

## How to Apply
1. **The daily check-in.** Take five minutes each day to ask your partner a genuine question about their inner world: "What was the hardest part of your day?" "What are you carrying right now?" Listen without fixing. The point is not to solve the problem but to show that you are accessible and engaged.
2. **The A.R.E. audit.** Periodically ask yourself: Am I accessible to my partner? Am I emotionally responsive when they reach for me? Am I engaged — present, attentive, prioritising the relationship? These three dimensions predict relationship satisfaction better than any skill or technique.
3. **The repair conversation.** When the cycle has run — when you have pursued or withdrawn, criticised or shut down — come back. "I think our cycle got us again. I know I shut down. What I was feeling underneath was..." This is not an apology performance. It is a genuine return to connection after disconnection.
4. **The reaching practice.** Practise expressing attachment needs directly rather than through protest or withdrawal. Instead of "You never spend time with me" (pursuit), say "I miss you. Can we be together tonight?" Instead of going silent when hurt, say "Something you said landed hard. I need a moment but I want to talk about it."
5. **The physical connection ritual.** Touch is an attachment behaviour. A six-second kiss, a long hug before parting, holding hands during a difficult conversation — these are not sentimentality. They are physiological regulation through attachment contact.
6. **The gratitude practice.** Tell your partner, specifically, what they did today that made you feel connected. "When you texted me in the middle of that meeting to ask how my appointment went — I felt like I mattered." Specific emotional acknowledgment strengthens the bond more than general appreciation.
7. **The cycle-spotting practice.** When tension rises, pause and ask: "Is this our cycle?" Naming the cycle in real time interrupts the automatic escalation and creates space for a different response.

## Examples
**Situation:** A couple has completed EFT therapy with good results. They feel closer than they have in years. But three months after ending therapy, the old pattern is beginning to creep back — she is starting to feel critical, he is starting to feel distant. They are worried.
**Application:** This is normal and expected — the old cycle is deeply grooved and will resurface under stress. What they need is not more therapy but daily practice that maintains the new bond. The cycle-spotting practice — naming the pattern when it appears — interrupts the automatic escalation. The daily check-in keeps emotional contact alive when life gets busy. The repair conversation addresses inevitable ruptures before they accumulate. The reaching practice keeps both partners expressing needs directly rather than through the old pursue-withdraw strategies. These practices are not homework — they are the relational equivalent of physical exercise. The bond, like a muscle, requires regular use to maintain its strength.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Turn connection practices into obligations.
**Why:** "It is Tuesday, we have to do our check-in" kills the emotional aliveness that the practices are designed to foster. Practices should feel like genuine reaching, not like compliance.

**Don't:** Use practices as a substitute for addressing significant distress.
**Why:** Daily practices maintain a bond that is fundamentally intact. If the cycle is running at full intensity — if there are unresolved attachment injuries, ongoing betrayals, or deep disconnection — practices alone are insufficient. Professional support is needed.
