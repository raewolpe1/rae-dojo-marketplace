# EFT Beyond Couples

## When to Use
- When someone asks about EFT applications beyond couple therapy.
- When family attachment dynamics are relevant.
- When the broader social and community implications of attachment science are in play.

## Core Concept
While EFT was developed for couples, Johnson and her colleagues have extended the model to families (EFFT — Emotionally Focused Family Therapy) and have articulated a vision for attachment science as a lens for understanding social connection more broadly. The core insight translates: wherever human beings form bonds — between parents and children, between siblings, within communities, between leaders and teams — attachment dynamics operate, and the same principles of emotional accessibility, responsiveness, and engagement apply.

Emotionally Focused Family Therapy applies the EFT framework to parent-child and family-wide dynamics. The model identifies negative interaction cycles within families — the adolescent who withdraws behind screens, the parent who escalates into criticism, the sibling who becomes the identified patient to draw attention from parental conflict. As in couples EFT, the cycle is externalised, the vulnerable emotions underneath the protective positions are accessed, and new bonding interactions are facilitated. The difference is complexity — family systems involve multiple attachment relationships, coalitions, and hierarchies that must be tracked simultaneously.

Johnson has also written about the broader social implications of attachment science. In Love Sense (2013), she argues that attachment is not only the foundation of romantic love but the foundation of human social life. Communities function better when their members feel secure in their bonds. Leadership is more effective when leaders are emotionally accessible and responsive. Social isolation — which attachment science frames as disconnection from attachment figures — is a public health crisis with mortality risks comparable to smoking.

This broader application is aspirational. Johnson is careful to note that attachment science does not have all the answers to social problems, and that structural inequality, poverty, racism, and political conflict cannot be reduced to attachment dynamics. But she argues that attachment science provides a crucial piece of the puzzle — the understanding that human beings are fundamentally social creatures whose wellbeing depends on the quality of their bonds.

## How to Apply
1. **Apply the cycle model to family dynamics.** When a family presents with a "problem child," look for the negative cycle. Who pursues? Who withdraws? What attachment fear is the child's behaviour expressing?
2. **Work with parent-child attachment.** Help parents understand that their child's challenging behaviour is often an attachment protest — a bid for connection expressed through the only strategy available to them.
3. **Consider attachment in leadership and team contexts.** Leaders who are emotionally accessible and responsive create psychologically safe teams. The same A.R.E. framework that predicts relationship satisfaction predicts team cohesion and performance.
4. **Recognise the limits.** EFT beyond couples is an emerging application. Use attachment as a lens but do not reduce every family or social problem to an attachment issue.

## Examples
**Situation:** A family of four seeks therapy because their fifteen-year-old daughter has become withdrawn, hostile, and is refusing to attend school. The parents are frustrated and frightened. They describe her as "impossible" and "deliberately difficult."
**Application:** EFFT would look for the negative cycle. The daughter's withdrawal and hostility are her position in the cycle — protective responses to attachment distress. Perhaps she withdrew when her parents became absorbed in their own marital conflict — she read their preoccupation as evidence that she did not matter. Her school refusal may be an attachment protest — a strategy to force parental attention. Her hostility may cover profound loneliness and fear. The therapist would help the parents see past the behaviour to the attachment need: "What if she is not being difficult? What if she is telling you, in the only way she knows how, that she needs you?" If the parents can shift from frustration to curiosity — if they can access their own fear and love rather than their exasperation — the relational dynamic begins to change. The daughter does not need consequences or motivational strategies. She needs to know that her parents are emotionally accessible and that she matters to them.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Apply couples EFT directly to families without adaptation.
**Why:** Family systems are more complex than dyads — multiple relationships, developmental stages, and power differentials require specific adaptations. EFFT is a distinct model, not a transplant of couples EFT.

**Don't:** Reduce adolescent or child problems to attachment alone.
**Why:** Learning difficulties, neurodevelopmental conditions, peer dynamics, bullying, and other factors may be contributing. Attachment is often part of the picture but rarely the entire picture.
