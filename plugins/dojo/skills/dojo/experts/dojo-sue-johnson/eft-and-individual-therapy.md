# EFT and Individual Therapy

## When to Use
- When someone asks about using EFT principles in individual therapy.
- When someone is single or not seeking couples therapy but wants to work on attachment patterns.
- When the EFIT model needs to be explained.

## Core Concept
Emotionally Focused Individual Therapy (EFIT) is Johnson's extension of EFT principles to individual therapy, formalised in her 2019 book Attachment Theory in Practice. EFIT applies the same core logic — attachment as the organising framework, emotion as the mechanism of change, the therapist as a secure base — to work with individuals on their relationship with self, with significant others, and with the therapeutic relationship itself.

EFIT works with three relationships simultaneously. The first is the client's relationship with the therapist — the therapeutic alliance itself becomes an attachment bond that provides the security for exploration. The second is the client's relationship with their inner emotional experience — learning to access, tolerate, and make sense of primary emotions rather than being driven by secondary reactive patterns. The third is the client's relationship with significant others — present and past attachment figures, the relational patterns that shape their life.

The model follows the same three-stage logic as EFT for couples: stabilisation and emotional engagement (de-escalation), restructuring core emotional responses and internal working models (restructuring), and consolidation of new patterns (consolidation). The key therapeutic moves are familiar — tracking emotion, accessing primary affect, creating corrective emotional experiences — but the enactments are different. Instead of facilitating a conversation between partners, the EFIT therapist facilitates a conversation between the client and their own emotional experience, or between the client and an imagined or internalised other.

Johnson positions EFIT as particularly suitable for people with insecure attachment patterns — anxious preoccupation, avoidant dismissal, or disorganised/fearful attachment — who want to understand and change their relational patterns. It is also appropriate for people who are not in relationships but recognise that their attachment history shapes their capacity for connection.

## How to Apply
1. **Build the therapeutic alliance as a secure base.** In EFIT, the relationship with the therapist is not just a tool — it is itself an attachment experience. The therapist's emotional responsiveness, reliability, and attunement provide the client with a lived experience of secure bonding.
2. **Track emotion in the present moment.** As in couples EFT, the therapist follows the client's emotional process rather than their narrative content. What is happening emotionally right now, in this room?
3. **Work with inner models and imagined others.** EFIT uses techniques like empty-chair work (drawing on Greenberg's emotion-focused therapy for individuals) to help clients engage with internalised attachment figures — processing unfinished attachment business with parents, partners, or parts of the self.
4. **Connect inner patterns to relational outcomes.** Help the client see how their internal emotional organisation — the way they manage attachment distress — plays out in their relationships. The avoidant client who shuts down emotions also shuts down connection. The anxious client whose emotions overwhelm them also overwhelms their partners.

## Examples
**Situation:** A man in his late thirties, single, has a pattern of intense but short-lived relationships. He falls in love quickly, becomes deeply attached within weeks, and then begins to feel suffocated and pulls away. By the third month, he has ended the relationship. He describes himself as unable to sustain intimacy.
**Application:** EFIT would explore this pattern through an attachment lens. His rapid attachment suggests an anxious system — he bonds quickly because his need for connection is intense. His withdrawal at three months suggests an avoidant overlay — when the attachment deepens to the point where he could be truly vulnerable, his protective system activates and he exits. The therapist would help him access the emotion underneath the withdrawal. What happens inside him at the moment he begins to pull away? Perhaps terror — if he stays, he will be seen, and what has been seen has historically been rejected. Perhaps grief — an old exile who learned that full dependence on another person leads to abandonment. EFIT would work with these emotional layers, using the therapeutic relationship itself as a laboratory for safe attachment, gradually building his capacity to stay present in the face of intimacy rather than fleeing from it.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Reduce EFIT to individual therapy with attachment vocabulary.
**Why:** EFIT has a specific therapeutic structure and process. Adding attachment language to a CBT or psychodynamic framework does not make it EFIT. The experiential, emotion-focused methodology is essential.

**Don't:** Present EFIT as only for people preparing for relationships.
**Why:** EFIT addresses the person's relationship with their own emotional life, not only their romantic prospects. It is relevant for anyone whose attachment patterns create suffering, whether partnered or not.
