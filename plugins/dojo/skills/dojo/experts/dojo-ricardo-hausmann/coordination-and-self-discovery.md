# Coordination and Self-Discovery

## When to Use
- When everyone is waiting for someone else to move first.
- When a plausibly viable industry has not emerged.
- When pioneer incentives are being designed.

## Fails When
- **The industry is genuinely non-viable** given the knowhow available. Then this is not the diagnosis. See Lin on CAD.

## Core Concept

**Two distinct obstacles, two distinct answers. Confusing them is the commonest error.**

### The chicken-and-egg problem (coordination)

**New industries require capabilities that do not exist — and those capabilities do not exist because the industries that would have demanded them do not exist.**

The training does not happen because there are no jobs. The jobs do not appear because there is no trained workforce. The supplier does not invest because there is no buyer. The buyer does not come because there is no supplier. **Everyone is waiting, rationally, for someone else to move first.**

Note what this is **not**. It is not a market failure in the textbook sense — no externality, no information asymmetry, no public good. Nobody is behaving badly or irrationally. And **it is not solved by making capital cheaper**: a subsidy does not tell the supplier that the buyer is coming.

**It is a coordination problem, and coordination problems need a coordinator** — one of the few things a state is genuinely well placed to be. The instrument is convening and credible commitment, not money: getting the buyer, the supplier, the trainer and the investor into a room and making each one's move conditional on the others.

### The self-discovery problem (information externality)

**Whoever goes first and discovers that this place *can* profitably make the thing has produced information that every imitator receives for free.**

The pioneer bears the full cost of discovery — the failed attempts, the learning, the wasted capital — and captures a fraction of its value, because the moment it works, others copy. **So too little discovery happens.** Predictably, and it is nobody's fault.

**This one has a bounded answer:** a limited, **time-limited** incentive to the pioneer, compensating the externality. It is bounded by construction, which is what makes it respectable — and it distinguishes it from protection, which is not bounded and which is a different animal entirely.

### Telling them apart

| | Symptom | Answer | Bounded? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chicken-and-egg** | Everyone waiting for everyone | A **coordinator** | Ends when the loop closes |
| **Self-discovery** | Nobody wants to be the first to try | **Time-limited pioneer incentive** | Yes, by construction |
| **Non-viability** | It has been tried and it does not work | **Stop** (see Lin) | N/A |

**The provenance note.** Self-discovery is Hausmann **with Rodrik** — it is joint work, and Rodrik's dojo may carry it too. The chicken-and-egg framing is more distinctly Hausmann's.

## How to Apply

1. **Diagnose which problem.** Waiting-for-each-other, or nobody-wants-to-be-first? Different answers.
2. **For coordination: convene, don't subsidise.** The missing thing is credible simultaneous commitment.
3. **For self-discovery: bound the incentive.** Time-limited. If it does not expire, it is protection.
4. **Rule out non-viability first.** If it has been tried repeatedly and failed, neither answer applies.
5. **Note that coordination is a state's comparative advantage.** It is one of the few things it is genuinely good at.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy reports that a promising value chain has failed to develop despite available finance.

**Application:** Available finance and no development is the signature of coordination failure, not a capital constraint — which is why adding more finance has not worked. Test it: ask each participant what they are waiting for. If the processor is waiting for reliable supply, the farmer is waiting for a guaranteed offtake, the trainer is waiting for a job pipeline and the logistics provider is waiting for volume, that is chicken-and-egg, and the instrument is a **coordinator** who can make each commitment conditional on the others — not a grant. Separately, ask whether anyone has tried and been copied. If the first mover's problem is that imitators free-ride on the discovery, add a **time-limited** pioneer incentive alongside the coordination.

**Result:** A convening instrument aimed at the actual obstacle, and a bounded incentive where the externality is real.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Answer a coordination problem with cheaper capital.
**Why:** A subsidy does not tell the supplier the buyer is coming. The missing input is credible simultaneous commitment.

**Don't:** Let a pioneer incentive run open-ended.
**Why:** Then it is protection, which is a different thing with a bad record. The bound is what makes it defensible.

**Don't:** Diagnose coordination failure where the thing is simply not viable.
**Why:** Repeated failure is evidence. Lin's viability test applies first.
