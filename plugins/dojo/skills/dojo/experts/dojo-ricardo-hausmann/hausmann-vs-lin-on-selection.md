# Hausmann vs Lin on Selection — and the Rodrik Overlap

## When to Use
- **When more than one sector-selection rule is on the table.** You now hold three.
- When asked how complexity differs from GIFF.
- When managing the Rodrik overlap.

## Fails When
- **One framework is in use and its limits are understood.** No need to stage a contest.

## Core Concept

### The Rodrik overlap — manage this first

**Hausmann co-created growth diagnostics with Rodrik and Velasco, and self-discovery with Rodrik.** Rodrik is already in the collection.

| Question | Route to |
|---|---|
| What is the binding constraint on growth? | **Rodrik** — growth diagnostics is in his dojo |
| Should we do industrial policy, and how designed? | **Rodrik** |
| What is economic complexity / the product space? | **Hausmann** — Rodrik's dojo does not carry it |
| What should we diversify into? | **Hausmann** |
| Self-discovery / pioneer externality | **Either** — joint work; do not load both to agree |

**Hausmann's distinct contribution to this collection is economic complexity and the product space.** Use him for that.

### Three selection rules that do not agree

You now hold **three** rules for choosing what to target. They are genuinely different and they will not always give the same answer.

| | **Lin (GIFF)** | **Hausmann (product space)** | **Keun Lee (if built)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Question** | What does our **endowment structure** support? | What is **adjacent** to what we already make? | Where do **incumbents' advantages decay fast**? |
| **Method** | Benchmark comparators with similar endowment at ~2× income, a generation ago | Map the export basket onto the product space | Target **short-cycle** technologies |
| **Unit** | Industry | Product, finely defined | Technology sector |
| **Data** | Comparator country histories | Trade data | Patent citation cycle times |
| **Selects for** | Factor-intensity fit | Knowhow proximity | Incumbent vulnerability |
| **Blind to** | Knowhow specifics; the product space | Services, informality, non-traded | Whether you have the capability at all |

**Where they conflict, concretely.** Lin would ask what comparator economies with South Africa's endowment made at double the income — a labour-abundance-driven answer. Hausmann would ask what is next to what the Western Cape already exports — a knowhow-proximity answer. These can point in opposite directions: an industry that fits the endowment may be far in the product space (no adjacent knowhow), and an adjacent product may be a poor endowment fit.

**Do not resolve this cheaply.** They are different theories of what constrains diversification — **factor endowments** versus **collective knowhow**. Both are serious. A strategy that runs both and reports the overlap has done something genuinely useful: **the intersection is a defensible priority set, and the divergence is where the analytical work is.**

**The honest self-assessment.** The product space is inferred from **goods trade data at country scale**. For a province with a large services and informal economy and no separate trade account, that is a real limitation — arguably a more severe one than Lin's, since GIFF's comparator logic does not depend on sub-national trade data. **Sutton's objection applies to both:** he would ask what your firms can actually make, and note that trade data is a coarse instrument for answering that.

## How to Apply

1. **Route growth diagnostics to Rodrik.** Do not duplicate.
2. **Run both selection rules** where the stakes justify it.
3. **Report the overlap as the priority set** and the divergence as the analytical agenda.
4. **State the data limitation.** Trade-based, country-scale, goods-only.
5. **Never merge the three.** They are different theories of the constraint.
6. **Let Sutton adjudicate at firm level.** Both rules are inferential; he went and looked.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy needs a defensible sector priority list and has access to both frameworks.

**Application:** Run both. **GIFF:** identify comparator economies with a similar endowment structure at roughly double the income a generation ago; ask what they made. **Product space:** take the province's export basket at fine granularity; identify the adjacent possible. **Then compare.** Items appearing in both are the defensible core — they fit the endowment *and* the knowhow is nearby. Items in one only are the interesting cases and require an argument: an endowment-fit item that is distant in the product space needs a knowhow-import plan (see `people-as-the-transmission-mechanism.md`); an adjacent item that is a poor endowment fit needs an explanation of why factor prices will not kill it. **Report the divergence rather than suppressing it.** That is the analysis.

**Result:** A priority list with two independent derivations and an explicit account of where they disagree.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use Hausmann for growth diagnostics.
**Why:** It is Rodrik's, and it is in his dojo. Hausmann is here for complexity.

**Don't:** Present complexity as the settled selection method.
**Why:** Lin's rule is serious and different. So is Lee's. The field has no consensus.

**Don't:** Apply the product space to a services economy without saying so.
**Why:** It is inferred from goods trade data. The limitation is severe at provincial scale.
