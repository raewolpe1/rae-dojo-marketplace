# Unselfing

## When to Use
- When the practitioner's ego — career ambitions, methodological attachments, institutional identity — is distorting their perception of the situation they are supposed to serve.
- When self-interest is masquerading as professional judgment.
- When the discipline of redirecting attention outward, away from self, is what the situation requires.

## Core Concept
Murdoch uses the term "unselfing" to describe the moment when attention is redirected from the self to the world beyond the self. Her famous example: a person is consumed by anxious, self-focused thoughts, then looks out the window and sees a hovering kestrel. In that moment of genuine attention to the bird, the self is forgotten — the anxious ego dissolves, and the person is simply attending to reality. This is unselfing: the overcoming of ego-distortion through attention to something beyond the self.

Unselfing is not self-destruction or self-neglect. It is the correction of the ego's natural tendency to place itself at the centre of every perception, every judgment, every situation. The ego distorts by making everything about itself: the community assessment becomes about the consultant's methodology; the evaluation becomes about the evaluator's reputation; the programme design becomes about the designer's career. Unselfing restores the correct proportions: the community is at the centre, not the consultant; the programme's reality is the subject, not the evaluator's cleverness.

This is a practice, not a one-time achievement. The ego reasserts itself constantly. Unselfing must be practised repeatedly — in each encounter, each analysis, each decision — because the default direction of human attention is inward.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify where the ego is centred.** "In your analysis of this situation, who is at the centre — you or the community? If your methodology, your reputation, or your career is driving the analysis, the ego is in charge."
2. **Redirect outward.** "Set aside for a moment what you want from this situation — the publication, the successful evaluation, the happy donor — and attend to what the situation actually is. What does it require, independent of what you need?"
3. **Use beauty or nature as a prompt.** "When the ego is entrenched, sometimes indirect methods work: attend to something beautiful — a landscape, a piece of art, the face of a child — and let the attention carry you out of yourself."
4. **Practise regularly.** "Unselfing is not achieved once. It is practised daily. Before each significant professional engagement, ask: is my attention on the situation or on myself?"

## Examples
**Situation:** A consultant is designing a theory of change for a governance programme. She finds herself spending most of her energy ensuring the theory of change reflects her published framework — which will enhance her professional reputation — rather than attending to the specific governance dynamics of the country.
**Application:** "You are designing a theory of change for your career rather than for the programme. Your published framework is the ego's consolation — it provides structure, recognition, and the comfortable assurance that you are the expert. But the governance dynamics of this country are particular. They have their own history, their own power structures, their own logics — and they may not fit your framework. Unselfing here means setting aside the framework temporarily and attending to the country: what is actually happening in its governance? What are the real power dynamics, the genuine constraints, the specific opportunities? Let the theory of change emerge from what you see rather than from what you already believe. If your framework proves useful, it will be because it illuminates this particular reality, not because you imposed it. The moral discipline is: put the country at the centre, not your framework. Let the reality be the authority, not your publication."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present unselfing as the elimination of self-interest.
**Why:** Self-interest is natural and not inherently evil. Unselfing is the discipline of preventing self-interest from distorting perception. The practitioner can have a career while attending to reality — the task is to prevent the career from governing the attention.

**Don't:** Use unselfing to shame practitioners.
**Why:** Ego-distortion is universal. The point is not to condemn it but to practise overcoming it. Shame reinforces the ego rather than dissolving it.
