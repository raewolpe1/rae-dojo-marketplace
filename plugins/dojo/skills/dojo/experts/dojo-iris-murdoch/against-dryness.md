# Against Dryness

## When to Use
- When analysis has become so abstract, categorical, and technical that it no longer captures the moral reality of the situation.
- When insisting on the particular — this person, this community, this situation — against the generalising tendency of institutional analysis.
- When moral vocabulary needs enriching because technical language has impoverished what can be seen.

## Core Concept
Murdoch's essay "Against Dryness" (1961) attacked the dominant moral philosophies of her time for producing an impoverished, "dry" picture of human moral life. Both behaviourism (which reduced morality to observable actions) and existentialism (which reduced morality to dramatic moments of choice) ignored the rich, complex, continuous inner life where moral work actually happens. Against this dryness, Murdoch insisted on the "fat, substantial" reality of human moral experience — the textures of relationship, the complexity of character, the particularity of situations.

For development practice, "against dryness" challenges the institutional tendency toward abstraction. Logframes, theories of change, indicator frameworks, and results matrices are all necessary administrative tools. But they are dry — they reduce particular communities to target populations, particular persons to beneficiaries, particular experiences to data points. The dryness is not a flaw in the tools; it is their nature. The problem arises when the tools become the only way of seeing, when the categories replace the reality.

The remedy is not to abandon tools but to supplement them with rich, particular attention. For every indicator, a case study. For every target population, a named community. For every outcome, a story of how it was experienced by a particular person. This is not soft supplementary data; it is the moral substance that the categories cannot capture.

## How to Apply
1. **Insist on the particular.** "Your report refers to '5,000 beneficiaries.' Name one. Describe their experience. If you cannot, you have data without knowledge."
2. **Enrich the vocabulary.** "Add moral language to technical language. Not only 'effective' but 'just.' Not only 'efficient' but 'dignified.' Not only 'sustainable' but 'good.'"
3. **Read a novel.** "When professional analysis has become dry, read fiction. A great novel teaches attention to the particular — the irreducible specificity of this person, this situation. Bring that attention back to your professional work."
4. **Resist the tyranny of the framework.** "Frameworks are useful. They are also dry. After applying the framework, ask: what did the framework miss? What particularity was squeezed out? That omission may be the most important thing."

## Examples
**Situation:** A mid-term review of a large education programme produces a technically accomplished report: enrolment figures, attendance rates, learning outcomes by district, cost-per-child ratios, gender parity indices. The donor is satisfied. But the programme staff who read the report say it bears no resemblance to their experience of the programme.
**Application:** "The report is dry. It is technically excellent and morally empty. It captures the categories — enrolment, attendance, learning outcomes — and misses the reality: the teacher in the rural school who comes every day despite not being paid for three months, the girls who walk two hours to attend, the community that built an additional classroom with its own resources, the children who cannot learn because they are hungry. These are not anecdotes to be appended to the data; they are the moral substance of the programme that the data cannot capture. The report sees the programme through its categories and misses the programme as it is lived. Against dryness: for every district in the report, include one page describing the experience of one school — not as a case study with methodology but as a portrait with attention. Let the particular school reveal what the district averages conceal. This is not methodological weakness; it is moral seriousness."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Dismiss all abstraction as dry.
**Why:** Categories, frameworks, and indicators serve essential functions — they enable comparison, aggregation, and analysis. The point is not to abandon abstraction but to supplement it with particularity.

**Don't:** Substitute anecdotes for analysis.
**Why:** Particularity enriches analysis; it does not replace it. The goal is thick analysis — quantitative rigour with qualitative richness — not the replacement of one inadequacy with another.
