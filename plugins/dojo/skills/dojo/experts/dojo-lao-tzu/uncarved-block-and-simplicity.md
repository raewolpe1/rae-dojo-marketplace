# The Uncarved Block (Pu) and Simplicity

## When to Use
- When the user is dealing with over-engineered programmes, frameworks, indicator sets, or strategies.
- When elaboration has displaced substance.
- When teaching the discipline of returning to plainness.
- When the user is being asked to add more and the addition is itself the problem.

## Fails When
- **The user mistakes simplicity for unsophistication.** The uncarved block is not crude. It is what the artist returns to after long mastery, having shed everything unnecessary.
- **The situation requires technical complexity.** Some problems require sophisticated analysis. The teaching is about returning to plainness when elaboration has become its own end, not about refusing complexity altogether.
- **You treat pu as a style.** Performed simplicity — the rustic display, the deliberate plainness — is not pu. Pu is the substance that has shed elaboration, not the elaboration that has dressed itself as plain.

## Core Concept
Pu (樸) is the Tao Te Ching's image of original simplicity. The character refers to wood that has not yet been carved — the block before the artisan's tool has shaped it. The image carries a double meaning. On one side, it is the unformed potential before culture, education, and ambition have imposed their shapes. On the other, it is what the sage returns to after long cultivation — the wisdom that has shed everything ornamental.

The Tao Te Ching's project includes a sustained critique of elaboration. The text observes that the more elaborate the moral rhetoric, the more lost the Way. The more numerous the laws, the more numerous the criminals. The more elaborate the rituals, the further from sincerity. Each layer of elaboration was added to compensate for an earlier loss of substance — and the elaboration itself accelerates further loss.

The discipline of returning to the uncarved block is not refusal of culture. It is the practice of stripping back, of remembering what is essential, of letting go of the elaborated forms that have come between the practitioner and the substance.

For Rae's work as a consultant and evaluator, pu offers a specific critique of contemporary development practice. The proliferation of frameworks — log-frames, theories of change, results frameworks, MEL plans, OECD-DAC criteria assessments, contribution analyses — has produced an industry of elaboration. Each framework is useful for some purpose. The aggregate has become a barrier between the practitioner and the practice. The pu move is to ask: what would the work look like if we returned to the substance? What is the question we are actually trying to answer? What would suffice?

The same critique applies to indicator sets. A framework with fifty indicators has stopped knowing what matters and started counting. The pu move is to reduce — radically — to the three or five indicators that, if they moved, would tell you something important. The remaining forty-five are elaboration that the practitioner is using to feel safe, not to know.

The discipline applies inwardly as well. The practitioner who has accumulated certifications, frameworks, vocabularies, and methods has a corresponding need to display them. The pu return is to set them aside, in the act of working, and to meet the situation with the empty mind of the apprentice. The accumulation remains available. But it does not run the conversation.

Pu is the discipline of restoration. The block is restored — not literally to its uncarved state, which is impossible — but in the sense that the practitioner can return to its inner quality. From that inner quality, what is genuinely needed can be carved.

## How to Apply
1. **Ask what would happen if you removed half.** Half the indicators, half the frameworks, half the slides, half the meetings. Ask whether the work would suffer or improve. Often it would improve.
2. **Return to the question.** What is the actual question this evaluation is asking? What is the actual outcome this programme is pursuing? The elaboration was added to answer questions that may have shifted. Re-anchor in the question.
3. **Speak plainly.** Specialist vocabulary often carries less meaning than plain speech. The discipline of explaining your work in plain words to a thoughtful non-specialist will reveal where the elaboration was hiding emptiness.
4. **Apprentice yourself again periodically.** Set aside the accumulated frameworks and meet a situation as if you were new to it. Notice what you see when the frameworks are not running.
5. **Defend the substance, not the elaboration.** When asked to add another indicator, another framework, another deliverable, ask whether it adds substance or only elaboration. Defend the substance.

## Examples
**Situation:** A results framework has been negotiated with five stakeholders. It now contains thirty-two outcome indicators across four pillars with twelve cross-cutting markers. The evaluator suspects no one understands the whole thing anymore.
**Application:** The pu move is to ask each stakeholder, separately, what they actually want to know. Each will name two or three things. The aggregate, across stakeholders, will be perhaps eight to ten things — not thirty-two. The thirty-two are political accumulation. The eight to ten are the substance. Reduce the framework to those.
**Result:** The remaining framework is something stakeholders can hold in mind, can use to make decisions, and can defend to their own constituencies. The earlier framework was symbolic; this one is operational.

**Situation:** A senior consultant is asked to write a methodology section for a proposal. She produces seventeen pages of carefully cited framework alignment, philosophical positioning, and methodological justification.
**Application:** The seventeen pages are armour. The pu move is to write the methodology in two pages, in plain language, with three concrete examples of how the methods would be applied in this specific context. The two pages will land harder than the seventeen. The seventeen were written to defend; the two were written to communicate.
**Result:** The proposal is read. The methodology is understood. The reviewers can ask substantive questions because the substance is no longer buried.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Confuse simplicity with crudeness.
**Why:** The uncarved block is the substance after elaboration has been shed. It is not the absence of skill; it is the presence of skill without ornament.

**Don't:** Perform simplicity.
**Why:** Performed plainness is elaboration in a different costume. Pu is what is left when the performance has also been set down.

**Don't:** Refuse legitimate complexity.
**Why:** Some problems require technical apparatus. The discipline is to bring the apparatus in service of the substance, not in place of it.
