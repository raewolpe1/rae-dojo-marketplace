# Lyon Applied to the 55-Year-Old Male

## When to Use
- When a midlife man asks where to start or how it all fits together for him.
- When you need the whole model converged into one sequenced plan for this demographic.
- When the user is Rae's profile - a 55-year-old man building or rebuilding health.
- When a coaching or strategic answer needs a concrete, personal synthesis.

## Fails When
- **The user needs one component in depth.** Route to the specific file; this is the integration.
- **There are medical conditions in play.** Individualize with a clinician; this is the general framework for a healthy midlife man.
- **You go generic.** The value here is the sequenced, demographic-specific plan - not "eat well and exercise."

## Core Concept
This is the demographic Lyon's clinic is, almost literally, built for: a man in his mid-fifties, past the point where old habits coast, squarely inside the window where anabolic resistance, gradual testosterone decline, and metabolic drift all start to compound. The good news is that this is also the window where the model delivers the most, because the inputs are powerful and the starting point usually has room. Here is the whole triangle converged into a sequence for this man.

**Protein, first and non-negotiable.** Target roughly 1 gram per pound of ideal body weight - for most 55-year-old men that's about 150-180g per day - distributed across three or four meals at 35-40g each, because at this age the per-meal dose needs to clear a higher leucine threshold. The single highest-leverage change is breakfast: most midlife men eat almost no protein in the morning, leaving the day's first anabolic window empty after an overnight catabolic fast. Fix breakfast to 35-40g (eggs, Greek yogurt, a whey shake) before optimizing anything else. This one change, done daily, shifts the trajectory.

**Resistance training, the non-negotiable second leg.** Two to four sessions a week, every week, built on compound patterns - push, pull, hinge, squat, carry - with real load, sets taken to within a couple of reps of failure, and progressive overload tracked over time. If he's currently a "cardio guy" walking 45 minutes a day, keep the walking but understand it is not training; the muscle is wasting underneath it. Add the lifting. Include some heavier, lower-rep strength work and a little power, because power protects function and falls-risk down the line. Beginners scale to machines and good technique and progress relentlessly - the early gains in an untrained 55-year-old are large.

**Bloodwork, the third leg that makes it medicine.** Get the panel the standard physical skips: fasting insulin (with glucose for HOMA-IR), ApoB, Lp(a) once, hs-CRP, HbA1c, a full lipid panel with triglycerides, vitamin D, homocysteine, ferritin, thyroid, and total and free testosterone. Read against optimal ranges and his own trend. For a 55-year-old man, two things to watch especially: a normal glucose hiding a high fasting insulin (the early metabolic warning), and a testosterone slope where the right first move is fixing muscle, visceral fat, training, and sleep - not reaching straight for TRT.

Around the triangle, a few midlife-specific refinements. **Sleep** is foundational and often the most broken input - it governs recovery, testosterone, appetite, and insulin sensitivity; fix it early. **A short, disciplined supplement stack** - creatine 3-5g daily, vitamin D dosed to a measured level, omega-3, magnesium - adds a modest edge on top of the basics, not instead of them. **Body composition and strength baselines** (ideally a DEXA and a grip/strength measure) let him train against numbers and prove progress at a six-month re-test. And the **mindset** Lyon insists on: the decline a 55-year-old has been told to expect is largely optional; this decade is the right time to build, not concede, and the body is the only place he has to live. The sequence if it must be ranked: fix breakfast protein this week, install resistance training this week, get the comprehensive panel drawn, fix sleep, add the stack, set baselines. That is the entire model, pointed directly at this man.

## How to Apply
1. **Set protein at ~1g/lb ideal weight.** ~150-180g/day for most men this age, at 35-40g per meal, breakfast first.
2. **Install resistance training.** 2-4x/week, compound patterns, real load, progressive - keep cardio as the complement.
3. **Draw the comprehensive panel.** Fasting insulin, ApoB, hs-CRP, HbA1c, lipids, vitamin D, homocysteine, thyroid, total/free testosterone.
4. **Fix sleep and add a short stack.** Sleep first among lifestyle inputs; creatine, vitamin D, omega-3, magnesium on top.
5. **Baseline and re-test.** DEXA and strength markers now; re-measure labs and composition at six months to prove it's working.

## Examples
**Situation:** A 55-year-old man, walks daily, ~100g protein, "normal" physical, wants one place to start.
**Application:** Start with breakfast: 35-40g protein tomorrow morning. Add resistance training three days a week this week. Request the full panel - especially fasting insulin and total/free testosterone - since "normal" likely didn't include them. Then sleep, then the stack.
**Result:** Within months: more muscle, better glucose and energy, and labs that finally show the real picture.

**Situation:** A 56-year-old man with low energy assumes he needs testosterone.
**Application:** Get total and free testosterone drawn, but first run the inputs - build muscle, drop visceral fat, train, sleep, raise protein. In midlife men these often lift testosterone and energy without a prescription. If he's genuinely deficient after that, it's a clinical conversation.
**Result:** Often a natural rebound; if not, a properly informed medical decision.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Hand him a generic "diet and exercise" answer.
**Why:** The value is the sequenced, demographic-specific plan - breakfast protein and resistance training first, the right labs drawn.

**Don't:** Jump to testosterone replacement before fixing inputs.
**Why:** In midlife men, muscle, fat loss, training, and sleep often raise testosterone naturally and break the suppressive loop.
