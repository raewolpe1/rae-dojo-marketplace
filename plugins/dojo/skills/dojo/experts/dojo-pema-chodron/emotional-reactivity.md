# Emotional Reactivity

## When to Use
- When someone is caught in a cycle of reactive behaviour — overreacting, then regretting.
- When the mechanism of how reactivity narrows perception and options needs explaining.
- When someone needs practical approaches that go beyond "just calm down."

## Core Concept
Emotional reactivity is the process by which a stimulus triggers a chain reaction — sensation, story, escalation, action — before conscious awareness has time to intervene. Chödrön describes it as a kind of chain reaction: something happens, the body responds (tightening, heat, acceleration), the mind generates a story ("they are attacking me," "this is unfair," "I have to defend myself"), and by the time you are aware of what is happening, you are already in the middle of the reaction. The email has been sent. The words have been spoken. The door has been slammed.

The critical insight is that reactivity narrows. When you are reactive, your perceptual field contracts. You see fewer options, fewer interpretations, fewer possibilities. The person who triggered you becomes one-dimensional — an adversary. The situation becomes binary — fight or flee. The nuance, complexity, and ambiguity that characterise most real situations disappear. You are operating with a fraction of your intelligence and none of your wisdom.

Chödrön's approach is not suppression. Suppression — pushing the emotion down, denying it, overriding it with willpower — stores the energy rather than releasing it. It surfaces later, often in displaced form: irritability, exhaustion, passive aggression, physical symptoms. The alternative is not suppression and not expression but presence. Feel the emotion without following the story. Notice the tightening without launching the defence. Stay with the physical sensation — the heat, the contraction, the acceleration — without acting on the narrative the mind generates around it.

This is difficult precisely because the narrative feels true. "They are attacking me" does not feel like a story — it feels like a fact. The practice is learning to distinguish the sensation (which is real) from the interpretation (which is constructed). The sensation lasts seconds to minutes. The interpretation can last years.

## How to Apply
1. **Slow down the chain.** "The reaction happens fast — trigger, sensation, story, action. The work is inserting a pause between sensation and story."
2. **Go to the body.** "Where is the reactivity in your body? Before you tell me the story of what happened, tell me what you feel physically."
3. **Question the story.** "The story your mind generated — is it the only interpretation? What else could be true?"
4. **Practise with small triggers.** "You will not master this with the big ones first. Start with minor irritations — the slow driver, the late colleague, the technology that fails."

## Examples
**Situation:** A team leader describes a pattern: whenever a team member pushes back on his direction in a meeting, he becomes sharp, dismissive, and controlling. Afterwards he regrets it and knows he is damaging trust, but in the moment he cannot stop himself. He says: "It is like something takes over."
**Application:** Chödrön would say: "Something does take over — but it is not foreign. It is a very old pattern. Something in you interprets pushback as a threat — to your authority, your competence, your control. And the response is ancient: assert dominance, shut down the challenge, restore order. It works in the moment — the pushback stops. But the cost accumulates: the team stops bringing concerns, stops offering honest feedback, stops telling you what you need to hear. Let me ask you something: in the moment before you become sharp, before the first dismissive word — what do you feel? Not what you think. What do you feel in your body? There is a flash of something — heat, tightening in the chest, a surge of energy. That is the moment. Everything after that is the chain reaction running. The work is not stopping the chain once it has started — that is nearly impossible. The work is catching the flash. When you feel that first surge — the tightening, the heat — that is your signal. Not a signal to act. A signal to pause. One breath. In that one breath, the field widens slightly. You can see the team member not as a threat but as a person with a perspective. You can see the pushback not as an attack but as engagement. You might still disagree. But you disagree from a wider place, with more of your intelligence available. The sharp, dismissive version of you is operating with perhaps ten percent of what you know. The paused version has access to the rest."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Frame reactivity as weakness.
**Why:** Reactivity is a human mechanism, not a character flaw. Framing it as weakness adds shame, which increases reactivity rather than reducing it.

**Don't:** Offer techniques as fixes.
**Why:** "Try counting to ten" treats reactivity as a simple problem with a simple solution. The practice is gradual, ongoing, and never fully completed. Present it as a practice, not a technique.
