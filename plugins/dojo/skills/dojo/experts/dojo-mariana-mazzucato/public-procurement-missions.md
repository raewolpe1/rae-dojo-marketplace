# Public Procurement for Missions

## When to Use
- When designing or reforming public procurement frameworks.
- When using public purchasing power to advance mission delivery.
- When supporting domestic supplier capability development.

## Fails When
- **The procurement is being used to protect incumbent suppliers** without genuine capability development.
- **Capacity to administer mission-oriented procurement** is absent.

## Core Concept

Public procurement is one of the largest economic levers governments hold. Total public procurement in most developed economies is 10–20% of GDP. The traditional framing treats procurement as administrative — buy what's needed at lowest cost. The mission-oriented framing treats procurement as strategic — direct purchasing power toward mission delivery, supplier capability development, and value creation in priority sectors.

The mission-procurement framework has several elements:

**Specifications for mission outcomes.** Procurement specifies what the mission requires (e.g. zero-carbon energy generation, accessible healthcare, sustainable housing) rather than just inputs. Suppliers compete on mission delivery, not just on price for specified inputs.

**Local content requirements.** Procurement conditions on domestic supplier participation, capability development, and supply chain build-out. The Korean and Brazilian use of procurement for industrial development is the model.

**Performance conditions.** Continued supplier engagement depends on demonstrated capability development, mission-aligned outcomes, and operational performance.

**SME and emerging supplier support.** Procurement structured to support new entrants alongside established suppliers, with progress mechanisms.

**Innovation procurement.** Procurement of solutions to mission challenges, with suppliers competing on innovative approaches. The US DoD, DARPA, and NIH use this framework; many other governments have variants.

**Transparency and accountability.** Public procurement transparency exposes capture and rent-seeking, supports democratic accountability, and disciplines mission delivery.

The contrast with conventional procurement (lowest price, narrow specifications, established suppliers favoured) is sharp. Conventional procurement misses the strategic opportunity; mission procurement uses it.

For South Africa specifically: public procurement (including SOE procurement) is around 30% of GDP. Reformed procurement could substantially advance industrial transformation, BBBEE supplier development, local manufacturing capability, and JET implementation. The current procurement architecture is fragmented and has been substantially compromised by capture; reform requires both technical redesign and capability rebuilding.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the mission** the procurement should advance.
2. **Specify mission outcomes** rather than just inputs.
3. **Build local content, capability development, and innovation conditions** into procurement.
4. **Establish transparency and accountability** to defend against capture.
5. **Build administrative capability** for sophisticated procurement.

## Examples

**Situation:** REIPPPP (Renewable Energy Independent Power Producer Procurement Programme) in South Africa.

**Application:** REIPPPP already includes local content and supplier development conditions, but the level and enforcement could be substantially strengthened. Mission framing: procurement should not just buy renewable electricity but also build domestic renewable manufacturing capability across the value chain. Conditions: increasing local content thresholds; capability development requirements; SME supplier development; innovation procurement for specific technology challenges (battery storage, electrolysers). Transparency through public reporting of supplier performance against conditions.

**Result:** REIPPPP as both energy procurement and industrial mission instrument.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use local content requirements as protection for inefficient incumbents.
**Why:** The requirements must be tied to genuine capability development with performance and time-bound conditions.

**Don't:** Imagine procurement reform is administrative.
**Why:** It requires building substantial public-sector capability for sophisticated procurement design and management.
