# Green Industrial Strategy

## When to Use
- When designing climate transition policy.
- When the orthodox climate policy framework is carbon-pricing-led.
- When designing the industrial dimension of energy transition.

## Fails When
- **The country has limited capability to develop domestic renewable manufacturing** — strategy must be honest about capabilities.

## Core Concept

Green industrial strategy frames climate transition as the largest mission-oriented industrial policy opportunity of our generation. The transition requires reorganisation of energy, transport, buildings, industry, agriculture, and finance — each demanding sustained investment, capability building, and supply-side response that carbon pricing alone will not generate.

The framework has three components:

**Mission-orient the transition.** Specify decarbonisation pathways with bold, measurable, time-bound milestones. Cross-sectoral coordination toward the milestones. Bottom-up experimentation across technology pathways under the mission umbrella.

**Build domestic manufacturing capability** along the transition value chain. Solar PV, wind turbines and components, battery cells and packs, electrolysers for green hydrogen, transmission and distribution equipment, electric vehicle components. Each is a manufacturing opportunity that the transition is creating demand for. Domestic capability requires industrial policy support (selective protection, local content, R&D investment, skill development).

**Use mission-oriented procurement.** Public procurement of renewable infrastructure (electricity, transport, buildings) with local content requirements, capability-development conditions, and supplier development drives industrial capability accumulation alongside the infrastructure build-out.

**Mobilise patient capital.** State investment vehicles, sovereign wealth, public-private patient capital structures, and international climate finance positioned for long-horizon investment in capability building. Performance conditions tied to capability development.

**Manage the just transition.** Workers and communities in carbon-intensive sectors absorb the transition costs. Mission-aligned regional development, skill development tied to renewable manufacturing, social protection — these are mission deliverables, not afterthoughts.

The contrast with orthodox climate policy is sharp. Carbon pricing alone fixes the externality and waits for market response. Green industrial strategy actively builds the supply-side capability for the transition while creating demand through procurement.

For South Africa specifically: the JET is the central mission opportunity of the decade. The country has substantial renewable resources (sun, wind), some manufacturing base to build on (metallurgical, chemicals), a coal-dependent legacy that requires just transition management, and access to AfCFTA as a protected scale-up market. The strategic architecture: green industrial mission, capability building across the renewable value chain, mission-oriented public procurement on REIPPPP and infrastructure, patient capital through JET financing and state vehicles, just transition for coal regions.

## How to Apply

1. **Frame climate policy as industrial mission**, not just energy transition.
2. **Identify the value chain segments** where domestic capability is feasible.
3. **Use procurement, finance, and selective protection** as mission instruments.
4. **Build the just transition** into the mission design.
5. **Coordinate across sectors** — energy, manufacturing, finance, skills, regional development.

## Examples

**Situation:** South Africa's JET implementation plan.

**Application:** Reframe as mission-oriented green industrial strategy. Mission: decarbonise electricity by 2050 + build domestic renewable manufacturing capability (solar PV, wind components, battery storage, electrolysers) + just transition for coal workers and communities. Procurement conditions on REIPPPP and infrastructure tenders. Patient capital through JET financing and IDC/PIC deployment. Cross-departmental delivery structure.

**Result:** A mission-coherent JET architecture rather than fragmented energy and industrial policies.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Rely on carbon pricing alone.
**Why:** Insufficient to drive the transition at required pace. Need supply-side industrial policy.

**Don't:** Treat the just transition as separate from the industrial mission.
**Why:** It is integral. Mission delivery requires political coalition; just transition is part of coalition-building.
