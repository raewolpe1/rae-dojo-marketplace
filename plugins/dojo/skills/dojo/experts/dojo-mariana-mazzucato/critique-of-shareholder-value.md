# Critique of Shareholder Value

## When to Use
- When corporate strategy is being justified by shareholder value maximisation.
- When corporate governance reform is being debated.
- When evaluating CEO compensation, M&A activity, or capital allocation decisions.

## Fails When
- **Shareholders' interests genuinely align with broader stakeholder interests** in the specific case.

## Core Concept

Shareholder value primacy — the doctrine that corporate purpose is to maximise shareholder returns — became the dominant framing of corporate purpose from the 1970s onwards, associated with Milton Friedman ("The Social Responsibility of Business is to Increase Its Profits", 1970) and increasingly embedded in corporate law, governance, and executive compensation structures since then. The framing has produced substantial damage to the productive economy and to broader stakeholder interests.

The critique:

**Shareholders are residual claimants, not the only stakeholders.** A corporation has obligations to workers, customers, suppliers, communities, the environment, and government. Treating only shareholders as the principal misrepresents the corporation's actual stakeholder structure.

**Shareholder value maximisation has produced share buyback culture.** Trillions in corporate cash transferred to shareholders rather than productive investment.

**It has produced executive compensation structures** that incentivise share price manipulation through buybacks and accounting choices rather than productive activity.

**It has shortened corporate time horizons.** Quarterly results focus, analyst expectations, equity-tied executive pay — these reduce patient capital for long-horizon investment.

**It has eroded worker compensation and protections.** The pursuit of "shareholder returns" has involved sustained wage suppression, benefit reduction, and labour-cost shifting across many sectors.

**It has produced socially damaging corporate behaviour.** Aggressive tax avoidance, environmental externalisation, predatory market practices — all justified by shareholder value maximisation.

The historical record is informative. The post-WWII corporation in much of the developed world operated under "managerial capitalism" — broad stakeholder consideration, long horizons, retained earnings, substantial investment, broad-based wage growth. The shareholder-primacy shift coincided with the slowdown in productivity growth, the rise of inequality, and the financialisation of corporate strategy.

Alternative frames:

**Stakeholder capitalism** — corporations as nexuses of stakeholder relationships, with obligations to all major stakeholders.

**Public benefit corporations** — corporate forms that legally embed public-purpose alongside profit.

**Codetermination** — worker representation on corporate boards (German model).

**Mission-aligned corporations** — corporate purpose tied to specific public-purpose missions.

For South Africa: substantial JSE-listed corporate behaviour follows global shareholder-primacy patterns — buybacks, executive compensation arbitrage, low productive investment. King IV governance code has elements of broader stakeholder consideration, but the dominant practice remains shareholder-primacy. Reform direction: strengthen broader stakeholder governance, restrict buybacks, reform executive compensation structures, condition state procurement on broader stakeholder commitments.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the broader stakeholder structure** of the corporation in question.
2. **Evaluate corporate decisions** against broader stakeholder impact, not just shareholder return.
3. **Reform corporate governance** for broader stakeholder representation.
4. **Address compensation structures** that embed shareholder primacy.

## Examples

**Situation:** A major JSE-listed company announces a substantial share buyback alongside layoffs.

**Application:** The combination reveals the shareholder-primacy pattern. Cash is being transferred to shareholders while productive workforce is being shed. Critique: this transfers value to shareholders at worker expense, with no productive investment to justify either decision. Reform: restrict buybacks (tax, disclosure, conditional approval), strengthen worker voice in major capital allocation decisions.

**Result:** Different corporate behaviour or different governance framework constraining it.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Use the critique to oppose all shareholder returns.
**Why:** Legitimate shareholder returns from genuine value creation are appropriate. The critique is of primacy, not of returns.

**Don't:** Imagine cultural shift will suffice.
**Why:** Shareholder primacy is embedded in corporate law, compensation structures, and capital markets. Reform requires structural change.
