# Mission Economy Framework

## When to Use
- When designing comprehensive economic transformation strategy.
- When the economy needs concentration of effort across multiple sectors and policy areas toward specific deliverables.

## Fails When
- **The economy is in stable functioning** and incremental policy is appropriate.

## Core Concept

The mission economy (Mazzucato, 2021) is an economy organised around specific public-purpose missions that mobilise effort across sectors, policy areas, and stakeholders. It is broader than mission-oriented innovation policy — it extends the mission framing to industrial policy, financial system structure, public-sector strategy, and political coalition.

Core elements:

**Identify 2–4 missions** at the level of nation or region. Each must be bold, measurable, time-bound, cross-sectoral, and inspirational. South Africa candidates: climate transition + green industrialisation; comprehensive social transition (employment, education quality, basic services); spatial transformation and regional development; water and food security.

**Reorganise government around missions.** Cross-departmental teams, mission-aligned budget lines, accountability to mission delivery, public reporting on mission progress.

**Reorganise public investment around missions.** State investment banks, sovereign wealth fund deployment, infrastructure investment aligned with mission delivery.

**Reorganise public procurement around missions.** State purchasing power directed to mission-advancing suppliers, with performance conditions.

**Build political coalition around missions.** The missions become the organising principle for democratic deliberation about economic strategy.

**Enable bottom-up experimentation under mission umbrellas.** Many actors, many approaches, mission as direction-setter rather than micro-manager.

The framework is distinct from generic "grand challenges" rhetoric in its institutional specificity — it requires reorganising government, finance, procurement, and political coalition around delivery. Without institutional reorganisation, mission rhetoric is theatre.

The framework is well-suited to South Africa's developmental moment. The country needs concentrated effort across multiple structural challenges (energy, employment, services, transformation) that the current dispersed policy architecture is not delivering. A mission-economy reorganisation could provide the focus.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the missions** through democratic-political process informed by structural analysis.
2. **Reorganise government** around mission delivery — cross-departmental teams, mission budgets, mission accountability.
3. **Align finance** — state investment vehicles, SWF, DFI deployment.
4. **Align procurement** — purchasing power as mission instrument.
5. **Build political coalition** around mission deliverables.

## Examples

**Situation:** South Africa's economic development strategy is fragmented across multiple plans and instruments.

**Application:** Reorganise around 3 missions: green industrial transformation (decarbonise + build domestic renewable capability + just transition); social transformation (halve unemployment by 2035, universal quality basic services); spatial transformation (regional development closing urban-rural and historic spatial divides). Each mission has cross-departmental delivery team, mission budget, mission accountability, mission-aligned procurement, mission-aligned finance.

**Result:** A coherent economic strategy organised for delivery.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat mission economy as just renaming existing policy.
**Why:** It requires institutional reorganisation, not just relabelling.

**Don't:** Pick missions that are everyone's preferred policy areas.
**Why:** Missions are choices that imply trade-offs. The choice is the political work.
