# Patient Capital

## When to Use
- When debating the financing of long-horizon innovation or industrial investment.
- When private capital is unwilling to bear the time horizon or uncertainty required.
- When designing state investment vehicles or sovereign wealth instruments.

## Fails When
- **The investment is short-horizon by nature** — patient capital is not for all investment.

## Core Concept

Patient capital is investment willing to bear long time horizons (decades, not quarters), high uncertainty (high failure rates in individual investments), and limited liquidity (commitment without immediate exit). It is the kind of capital that funds genuine innovation, infrastructure build-out, and long-horizon industrial transformation.

The orthodox financial system under-supplies patient capital. Quarterly-results-focused public equity markets, short-tenure private equity strategies, banks constrained by Basel-type liquidity requirements, mutual funds with short performance evaluation cycles — none of these structures match the time horizons of transformative investment. The result is systematic underinvestment in patient-capital-requiring activity.

Patient capital comes from:

**Sovereign wealth funds** (Norway, Singapore Temasek, Saudi PIF, others). Long-horizon, willing to bear uncertainty, can hold positions for decades.

**Public investment banks** (KfW in Germany, BNDES in Brazil, China Development Bank, IDC in South Africa, the European Investment Bank). Mandated for long-horizon developmental investment.

**Pension funds and endowments** — when properly governed, these can be patient capital sources. Many have shortened horizons under financial-sector pressure.

**Mission-aligned philanthropy** — Gates Foundation, Wellcome Trust, others. Limited in scale but willing to bear long horizons.

**Private patient capital** — Berkshire Hathaway, family offices, and some specialised funds. The smallest source.

For developing countries: build state investment vehicles with explicit patient capital mandates; align pension fund and SWF strategies for patient deployment; use development finance institutions strategically rather than as commercial banks.

For climate transition specifically: the scale of patient capital required is enormous (trillions of dollars globally over decades). The current financial system will not provide it without state-led mobilisation. State investment vehicles, public-private patient capital structures, and pension fund reform are essential components.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify the patient capital need** — what investment, what horizon, what risk profile.
2. **Match the capital source** — sovereign wealth, public investment bank, mission-aligned, pension.
3. **Structure the investment** with appropriate time horizons and risk-bearing.
4. **Build the institutional architecture** for state-led patient capital deployment.

## Examples

**Situation:** South Africa's Just Energy Transition requires patient capital for renewable manufacturing capability development.

**Application:** The IDC, PIC (managing GEPF assets), and dedicated JET financing instruments should be positioned as patient capital sources. International climate finance should be structured for patient deployment. Performance conditions tied to capability development (rather than short-term financial returns) align the capital with the transition's actual requirements.

**Result:** A patient-capital architecture for the JET that the orthodox financial system would not provide.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Imagine patient capital can be invented through declaration.
**Why:** It requires specific institutional structures, mandate, governance, and political support.

**Don't:** Subordinate patient capital institutions to short-term financial metrics.
**Why:** This is how patient capital institutions become commercial banks, losing their distinctive function.
