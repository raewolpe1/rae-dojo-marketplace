# Financialisation Critique

## When to Use
- When debating share buybacks, executive compensation, private equity, or financial sector regulation.
- When the orthodox framing presents financial sector growth as productive.
- When evaluating corporate strategy that prioritises financial metrics.

## Fails When
- **Specific financial sector functions are genuinely productive** — payment systems, savings, insurance, productive investment finance. The critique is of financialisation excess, not finance.

## Core Concept

Financialisation is the process by which financial sector activity, financial metrics, and financial logic have become disproportionately large in the economy and disproportionately influential in corporate and policy decision-making. The trend since the 1980s across developed economies has produced:

**Disproportionate financial sector growth.** Financial sector share of GDP has risen substantially; financial sector profits as share of corporate profits have risen more.

**Shareholder primacy.** Corporate purpose has been redefined as maximising shareholder value, displacing broader stakeholder consideration. Share price has become the primary metric of corporate success.

**Share buyback culture.** US corporations have spent trillions on share buybacks in recent decades — using corporate cash to repurchase shares rather than invest in productive activity, R&D, or worker wages. The buyback transfers cash to shareholders without productive investment.

**Private equity extraction strategies.** Leveraged buyouts that load companies with debt, extract dividends, cut investment and labour costs, and sell or bankrupt the residual. The pattern transfers value from companies to private equity owners.

**Executive compensation through equity.** CEO and senior executive pay tied to share price metrics that executives can influence through buybacks. The structure incentivises financial engineering over productive activity.

**Short-termism.** Quarterly results focus, analyst expectations, and equity-tied executive pay have shortened corporate time horizons, reducing patient capital availability.

The cumulative effect is the corruption of corporate strategy and the misdirection of capital. Corporations that should be investing in productive activity instead engineer financial metrics. The productive economy underperforms while the financial economy grows.

The corrective:

**Restrict share buybacks** through taxation, disclosure, or regulation.

**Reform executive compensation** away from short-term financial metrics toward operational and stakeholder metrics.

**Restructure private equity tax treatment** (carried interest, debt deductibility) to reduce extraction incentives.

**Build stakeholder-based corporate governance** with worker, customer, and community representation.

**Tax financial transactions** to reduce excess financial-sector activity.

For developing countries, financialisation effects exist but with country-specific patterns. South Africa's concentrated financial sector, share buybacks by JSE-listed companies, and PE extraction strategies follow the global pattern with local intensification.

## How to Apply

1. **Identify financialisation patterns** in the specific economy or sector.
2. **Quantify the effects** — buyback flows, financial sector share, executive compensation premia.
3. **Design reform package** — buyback restrictions, compensation reform, tax reform, governance reform.
4. **Build political coalition** for reform.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African JSE-listed companies have engaged in substantial share buybacks while complaining about low investment levels.

**Application:** The combination is the financialisation pattern. Cash available for investment is being deployed to buybacks rather than to productive use. Reform: tax buybacks, disclose buyback flows alongside investment, require buyback justification to shareholders against alternative uses.

**Result:** Reduced financial engineering, increased pressure for productive investment.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Confuse financialisation critique with anti-finance.
**Why:** Productive finance is necessary. The critique is of financialisation excess.

**Don't:** Imagine reform is uncontested.
**Why:** Financialised interests are politically powerful. Build coalition before launching reform.
