# Van der Kolk and the Field

## When to Use
- When someone wants context for where van der Kolk fits in the broader landscape of trauma research and treatment.
- When someone encounters criticism of van der Kolk and wants a balanced perspective.
- When someone wants to understand how his work relates to other major figures in the field.

## Core Concept
Bessel van der Kolk has been one of the most influential figures in the trauma field for four decades, and — as is often the case with paradigm-shifting researchers — also one of the most controversial.

**Contributions.** Van der Kolk's central contribution is the integration of neuroscience, body-based approaches, and clinical practice into a coherent understanding of trauma. Before his work, PTSD was understood primarily as a cognitive and emotional disorder — a problem of memory and meaning. Van der Kolk shifted the paradigm by demonstrating through neuroimaging that trauma is a physiological event with measurable brain and body correlates. His advocacy for body-based treatments (yoga, EMDR, somatic experiencing, neurofeedback) expanded the treatment landscape beyond talk therapy and medication — the two pillars of conventional psychiatry. His campaign for Developmental Trauma Disorder, though not yet adopted by the DSM, has influenced how clinicians think about childhood adversity. And *The Body Keeps the Score* brought trauma literacy to a general audience on a scale no previous work had achieved.

**Controversies.** Van der Kolk has been criticised on several fronts. Some researchers have questioned whether his enthusiasm for emerging modalities (neurofeedback, MDMA-assisted therapy) sometimes outpaces the evidence base. He has been criticised for the strength of his advocacy for Developmental Trauma Disorder at a time when the research base, while growing, was not sufficient to satisfy all reviewers. In 2018, he was dismissed from his position at the Trauma Center at JRI following complaints about his management and workplace conduct — a separation that generated significant debate within the field about the relationship between a person's professional contributions and their institutional behaviour.

**Relationships with other figures.** Van der Kolk's work intersects productively with several other major contributors. Judith Herman (*Trauma and Recovery*) developed the phase-based treatment model and the concept of complex PTSD that van der Kolk draws on. Peter Levine (somatic experiencing) developed the body-based approach that van der Kolk champions. Stephen Porges (polyvagal theory) provided the neurobiological framework for understanding the autonomic hierarchy of survival responses. Dan Siegel (interpersonal neurobiology, window of tolerance) contributed regulatory concepts central to van der Kolk's treatment framework. Francine Shapiro (EMDR) developed the reprocessing technique that van der Kolk helped validate. Richard Schwartz (IFS) developed the parts-work approach that van der Kolk has increasingly endorsed.

**Limitations.** Van der Kolk's work is strongest on the biology and treatment of trauma and less systematic on its social and political dimensions. He acknowledges social context (poverty, racism, war) but his primary focus is on what happens inside the individual brain and body. For a more developed political analysis, one would turn to Judith Herman or to community-based trauma researchers.

## How to Apply
1. **Use van der Kolk for the body and brain.** His primary contribution is understanding how trauma lives in the body and what somatic approaches can do. For the relational dimension, pair with Sue Johnson or John Bowlby. For the parts dimension, pair with Richard Schwartz. For the social-political dimension, pair with Judith Herman.
2. **Read critically.** Van der Kolk writes with conviction and sometimes with advocacy that blurs the line between established evidence and emerging possibility. This passion is part of what makes him effective, but read the evidence base alongside his recommendations.
3. **Acknowledge the full picture.** Van der Kolk's workplace conduct issues are part of the public record and should not be ignored. They also do not invalidate five decades of research and clinical contribution. Hold both realities.

## Examples
**Situation:** Someone reads an article criticising van der Kolk and asks whether they should still trust his clinical insights.
**Application:** Separate the work from the workplace. Van der Kolk's neuroscience research has been replicated, his clinical observations are consistent with a broad evidence base, and his advocacy for body-based trauma treatment has been validated by subsequent research. His institutional conduct is a separate matter that reflects on his management behaviour, not on the validity of his scientific and clinical contributions. Read his work critically — as you should read all clinical writing — while acknowledging that the contributions stand on their own evidence base.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat van der Kolk as the sole authority on trauma.
**Why:** The field is broad and multi-voiced. Van der Kolk covers the neurobiology and body-based treatment dimensions brilliantly. Other dimensions (complex PTSD, social context, relational repair, parts work) are better served by other thinkers.

**Don't:** Dismiss the controversies to protect a hero.
**Why:** Intellectual honesty requires acknowledging limitations and controversies alongside contributions. Van der Kolk himself would likely argue that avoiding uncomfortable truths is a form of dissociation.
