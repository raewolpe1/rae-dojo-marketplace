# Reflexivity

## When to Use
- When the analyst's own position — their class, education, institutional affiliation, and forms of capital — is shaping what they see and what they miss, and this shaping needs to be made visible.
- When the claim to objectivity or neutrality is concealing the interested position from which the analysis is produced.
- When the development professional needs to objectivise their own position in the field they are intervening in.

## Core Concept
Bourdieu's reflexivity is not introspection. It is not the confession of personal biases or the declaration of positionality that has become ritual in qualitative research. It is the systematic objectivisation of the analyst's position in the field — the sociological analysis of the sociologist.

Bourdieu identifies three levels of bias that reflexivity must address. The social position bias: the analyst's class, gender, ethnicity, and education shape their categories of perception. The analyst sees the world through dispositions acquired through their own socialisation — and these dispositions make some things visible and others invisible. The field position bias: the analyst occupies a position within their own field (the academic field, the development field, the evaluation field) that shapes what questions are asked, what methods are valued, what conclusions are rewarded. The intellectualist bias: the analyst tends to treat the world as a spectacle to be interpreted rather than as a practical problem to be navigated — imposing the logic of theory on the logic of practice.

For development professionals, reflexivity means asking: what is my position in the development field? What forms of capital do I hold (international education, donor networks, English-language fluency, methodological expertise)? How does my habitus — the dispositions acquired through my socialisation in a particular class, a particular educational system, a particular institutional culture — shape what I see and what I miss? The consultant who does not objectivise these positions is not being objective — she is being naively complicit in the structures her analysis reproduces.

## How to Apply
1. **Objectivise the social position.** "What is your class position, educational background, and social trajectory? How do these shape your categories of perception? The middle-class consultant sees 'dysfunction' in a government ministry; the ministry official sees 'normal operating conditions.' The difference is positional."
2. **Objectivise the field position.** "What position do you occupy in the development field? Are you a donor, an implementer, an evaluator, a consultant? Each position has its own interests, its own rewards, its own blindnesses. The evaluator who measures 'impact' is rewarded for finding it — which creates a structural incentive to find what the field rewards."
3. **Name the intellectualist bias.** "Are you treating the community's practices as a text to be interpreted rather than as practical responses to practical problems? The analyst who asks 'why do these farmers plant this way?' and answers with a cultural theory may be imposing theoretical logic on a practice that has its own practical logic — one that the analyst's intellectualism prevents them from seeing."
4. **Make reflexivity methodological, not confessional.** "Reflexivity is not 'I acknowledge my privilege.' It is the systematic analysis of how your position shapes your analysis. What did your position make you see? What did it make you miss? How would the analysis differ if produced from a different position?"

## Examples
**Situation:** An international consultant is conducting a governance assessment of a district administration. She finds extensive use of personal networks, informal channels, and relationship-based decision-making. She writes in her report that the administration is "characterised by informal governance practices that undermine formal institutional frameworks."
**Application:** "Practise reflexivity before finalising this analysis. Your position: international consultant, educated in Northern institutions, embedded in the development field's orthodoxy that formal institutions are superior to informal ones. Your habitus: dispositions formed in bureaucratic environments where formal procedures are the norm and informal practices are the deviation. Your field position: consultant whose authority rests on the claim that formal institutional frameworks are the standard against which practice should be measured. Now: how does this position shape your analysis? You see 'informal governance practices that undermine formal frameworks.' The district officials see practical mechanisms for getting things done in an environment where the formal frameworks are under-resourced, poorly designed, or disconnected from local realities. Your analysis treats formal institutions as the standard and informal practices as the deviation. This is not an objective assessment — it is the product of your position in the development field, which values formality, transparency, and rule-based governance. The reflexive move is not to abandon your analysis but to acknowledge that it is positional: produced from a specific location in the field, shaped by a specific habitus, serving the interests of a specific institutional culture. And then to ask: what analysis would be produced from the district officials' position? From the community members' position? From a position outside the development field's orthodoxy about formal institutions?"

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Reduce reflexivity to personal confession.
**Why:** "I am a white, middle-class, Northern consultant" is not reflexivity. It is autobiography. Reflexivity is the systematic analysis of how your social position, field position, and intellectualist bias shape the analysis you produce. The focus is on the analysis, not on the analyst.

**Don't:** Use reflexivity to paralyse analysis.
**Why:** The fact that all analysis is positional does not mean all analysis is equally valid or that analysis is impossible. Reflexivity strengthens analysis by making its conditions of production visible. It does not invalidate it.
