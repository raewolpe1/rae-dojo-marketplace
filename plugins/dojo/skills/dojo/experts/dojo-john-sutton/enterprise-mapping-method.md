# The Enterprise Mapping Method

## When to Use
- When a strategy's evidence base is enterprise counts and demographics rather than capability.
- When a baseline study or survey is proposed.
- When nobody can say what the province's firms actually make.

## Fails When
- **The question is household or labour force survey design.** Different instrument, different unit.

## Core Concept

**The method is: go and record who makes what.**

Not a survey. Not a sample. A **standardised descriptive account of the industrial capabilities of a territory**, industry by industry, built from **profiles of roughly fifty leading firms** — the ones that, taken together, constitute the frontier of capability.

**For each firm, four questions:**
1. **What do they produce?** Specifically, by product line.
2. **How do they fit into domestic and international supply chains?** Who do they buy from, who do they sell to.
3. **Where and how did they acquire their current capabilities?** The historically decisive question, and the one nobody else asks.
4. **What constrains the next tier up?**

**Why fifty, and why the leaders.** The frontier is the analytically interesting object. It tells you the ceiling of what is currently possible in this territory, and — via question 3 — the routes by which that ceiling was reached. The median firm tells you about the labour market; the frontier tells you about industrial possibility. The fifty chosen represent almost all the largest firms in their respective sectors, so this is close to a census of the frontier, not a sample of it.

**Why it was necessary.** Accessible information on current levels of industrial capability in these countries was **limited and patchy**. Governments and potential investors were **hindered by a lack of knowledge about current domestic capabilities and the potential barriers to further growth**. The entire industrial policy debate was proceeding without the evidence. Collier's assessment of what it displaced: discussion "plagued by doctrinaire analysis resting on limited evidence."

**The dual audience, which is the design insight.** The maps were aimed at **both** policymakers seeking to foster industrial development **and** companies considering investments. That dual purpose is why they worked: a document useful only to policymakers gets read once, and a document useful to a buyer gets used. Tullow Oil used the Ghana map to find suppliers. The Ethiopia and Ghana maps were translated into Chinese; Mozambique into Portuguese — aimed squarely at the FDI sources the maps had identified.

**The cost.** Modest relative to any programme budget. The output is a book. The return, on the Ghana evidence, includes a multinational building an Enterprise Development Centre because it could finally identify local suppliers.

## How to Apply

1. **Pick the frontier, not the sample.** Fifty leading firms per priority value chain.
2. **Ask the origin question.** Where did the capability come from? This is the question that produces the surprises and it is routinely omitted.
3. **Record product lines narrowly.** Capability is defined per line; an industry-level account is not usable.
4. **Design for two audiences.** Policymakers and buyers. Publish it. Translate it into the language of your FDI sources.
5. **Expect your priors to be violated.** If they survive intact, the exercise was not worth doing.
6. **Then use it.** Take it to the buyers with local content commitments.

## Examples

**Situation:** A provincial strategy's evidence base consists of enterprise counts by size, sector and district, drawn from survey and administrative data.

**Application:** That is a demography of firms. It supports statements about how many and what type; it supports nothing about what they can make, how well, at what cost, and where the good ones got it. Every capability claim in the strategy is therefore unevidenced. Proposal: commission a Western Cape Enterprise Map — fifty leading firms across the priority value chains, profiled on the four questions, published for both DEDAT and for buyers and investors. The Ethiopian precedent suggests the origin question alone will overturn at least one load-bearing assumption in the current strategy.

**Result:** The first evidence base that can actually answer the questions the strategy assumes it has answered.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Substitute a firm survey for a map.
**Why:** A survey tells you what firms say. A map records what they demonstrably make, and traces where that came from.

**Don't:** Map the median firm.
**Why:** The frontier defines industrial possibility. The median defines the labour market — a different question with a different instrument.

**Don't:** Write it only for government.
**Why:** The dual audience is why it works. The buyer's use is the return.
