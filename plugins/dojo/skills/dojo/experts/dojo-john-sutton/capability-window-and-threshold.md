# The Capability Window and the Viability Threshold

## When to Use
- Before any capital, equipment or premises intervention.
- When firms are said to be uncompetitive against imports.
- As the analytical core of any Sutton answer.

## Fails When
- **The question is specifically about management practices as the capability content.** Route to Bloom — he measures that; Sutton frames the structure it sits inside.

## Core Concept

**Revealed capability is two numbers, per narrowly defined product line.**

- **c** — the unit variable cost: units of labour and input required per unit of output.
- **u** — the quality: anything that shifts the demand schedule outwards.

**Capability = u/c.** "Revealed" because it is read off what the firm demonstrably does in the market, not off a survey, a skills audit, or a self-assessment.

**The structure.**

- There is a **threshold**, *b*. **Below it the firm is not viable in that product line at all.** Not a weaker competitor — not a competitor. It is inactive.
- Above the threshold lies the **capability window**. Its top is defined by the **leading firm**. Active rivals sit inside it. Firms outside are not in the market.
- The window is defined **per product line**, narrowly. **A firm may be above threshold in one line and below in another**, and this is the analytically useful fact, because it locates the adjacent possible.

**Why this matters more than it first appears.** The framework **endogenises the scarcity of capability** — showing why capability must always be scarce and therefore valuable — rather than assuming it. And it produces the single most important operational rule in this dojo:

**If the firm is below the threshold, support delivered at that product line does nothing.** Finance does nothing. Equipment does nothing. Premises do nothing. Market access does nothing, because there is nothing to access the market with. The Tanzanian factory is the canonical case: if you do not start out with a firm that has the appropriate capabilities, installing capital equipment will not help.

**The useful question when a firm is below threshold** is therefore never "how do we lift it here". It is "**which line is it above threshold in, and what is adjacent to that?**" Capability moves laterally into neighbouring lines; it does not leap.

**The import-competition diagnosis.** When domestic firms cannot compete with imports, decompose. Is the gap in *c* (they cost more) or *u* (their product is worse, broadly defined)? These imply completely different interventions — the first points at scale, inputs, energy, logistics; the second at design, reliability, service, brand. Strategies routinely conflate them and then fund the wrong one.

## How to Apply

1. **Specify the product line narrowly.** "Furniture" is not a product line. "Flat-pack melamine kitchen units for the retail trade" is.
2. **Establish the firm's position.** Above or below the threshold in that line?
3. **If below — stop.** Do not fund. Ask which line it is above threshold in.
4. **If above — decompose the gap to the leader.** Is it u or c?
5. **Fund against the decomposition.** Cost gaps and quality gaps have different instruments.
6. **Look laterally for the adjacent line**, not upward within the current one.

## Examples

**Situation:** A programme proposes machinery grants to small food processors who "cannot compete with imported product".

**Application:** Take one product line — say, bottled chilli sauce for formal retail. Establish whether the firms are above the threshold: are they on a shelf anywhere, at any price, with repeat purchase? If not, they are below *b*, the machine is a monument, and the question is what they *are* viable at — perhaps informal or spaza-channel supply, or a different product entirely. If they are above threshold, decompose: is the imported product cheaper (a *c* problem — scale, inputs, energy) or better regarded (a *u* problem — labelling, shelf life, consistency, brand)? A machinery grant addresses *c*. If the gap is *u*, the grant is precisely irrelevant, and it is the commonest misdiagnosis in food processing support.

**Result:** Money goes to firms that can use it, against the gap that actually exists.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Fund a firm below the viability threshold.
**Why:** It is not in the market. The support has nothing to act on.

**Don't:** Assess capability at firm level without a product line.
**Why:** Capability is defined per narrowly defined line. The same firm is above threshold in one and below in another.

**Don't:** Treat an import-competition gap as automatically a cost gap.
**Why:** It is often a quality gap in Sutton's broad sense — reliability, service, brand — and machinery does nothing for that.
