# Evidence Base — FDI and Investment Promotion

> **Provenance.** Compiled July 2026 from IGC and LSE impact summaries of the Enterprise Map series. Verify against the primary volumes before quoting. Entries marked `[CHECK]` are unverified to the primary source.

## When to Use
- FDI attraction, investment promotion, aftercare, or local content policy.
- When linking domestic suppliers to large buyers.
- When the FDI chapter is written as a finance chapter.

## Fails When
- **The question is national FDI regulation, treaties, or screening.** Different subject.

## Headline

**FDI is not an investment statistic. It is a capability inventory and a transfer channel — and the instrument that operates it is the investment promotion agency, which is duller and more effective than anything else in the chapter.**

---

## THE EVIDENCE

### The FDI arithmetic
- **Study:** *An Enterprise Map of Ethiopia* (Sutton & Kellow, 2010).
- **Country:** Ethiopia.
- **Finding:** **The current stock of new FDI projects could generate as many jobs as the fifty leading domestic companies profiled** — i.e. as much as the entire domestic industrial frontier combined. Inflow had been increasing significantly **from a very low base**. FDI projects **spanned every sector** of Ethiopian industry. **Four countries led:** China, India, Saudi Arabia and Italy. The authors conclude that **careful monitoring and development of FDI ventures should be a key focus of policy concern over the next decade**.
- **Caveat:** From a very low base, in a period of exceptional inflow. The magnitude will not transfer to South Africa, which has a long FDI history and a large domestic corporate sector. What transfers is the framing: **count what the investors can make, not what they pledged.**

### The middle-manufacturing chain
- **Study:** LSE research impact summary of the Enterprise Map series.
- **Countries:** All five.
- **Finding:** Growth requires broadening into **middle manufacturing**; this requires **improving the effectiveness of manufacturing multinationals**; this in turn requires **strengthening national investment agencies to bring them into line with international best practice**. The publications highlight the importance of an active and broad manufacturing sector, which requires both.
- **Caveat:** A policy conclusion drawn from the mapping, not an experimental result. But note the causal chain runs *through* the multinationals — they are the mechanism by which the middle tier becomes reachable, not a competing option to domestic firm support.

### Investment promotion as the operative instrument
- **Study:** IGC case study, "Mapping industrialisation in Africa"; LSE impact case.
- **Countries:** Ethiopia, Tanzania.
- **Intervention:** Engagement with investment promotion agencies following the Enterprise Maps.
- **Finding:** The Enterprise Maps **opened doors to engage government actors and firms to implement reforms to investment agencies**. In **Ethiopia**, engagement with the **Ethiopia Investment Commission (EIC)**. In **Tanzania**, following the 2012 launch of the Tanzania Enterprise Map, engagement with the **Tanzania Investment Centre** focused on **shifting the organisational culture towards relationship building for active engagement with companies**, putting in place processes similar to the EIC's.
- **Caveat:** `[CHECK]` on the specific outcomes achieved. The transferable content is the diagnosis: **the agency's problem was organisational culture — transactional promotion versus relationship-building aftercare** — and that is a diagnosis a Western Cape agency could be tested against directly.

### The map as a commercial instrument — Tullow Oil
- **Study:** LSE research impact case, "Managing and sustaining growth in African economies"; IGC case study.
- **Country:** Ghana.
- **Intervention:** *An Enterprise Map of Ghana* (Sutton & Kpentey, 2012) used by a buyer.
- **Finding:** **Tullow Oil PLC**, which had prioritised developing local content and a sustainable supply base, **used the report to identify Ghanaian companies it could work with and integrate into its local supply chains**. The company **set up an Enterprise Development Centre** to help local SMEs develop skills and enter the oil and gas supply chain, providing **targeted support services: business training, advisory services, and access to information and markets**.
- **Caveat:** One firm, one sector, one country. But this is the clearest demonstration in the series of the map's return: **local content policy fails when the buyer cannot find the suppliers.** The map solved a search problem, and the buyer then funded the capability support itself.

### Reach
- **Study:** LSE impact case.
- **Finding:** The volumes were made publicly available for download at the IGC. The Enterprise Maps for **Ethiopia and Ghana were translated into Chinese**; the **Mozambique map into Portuguese**.
- **Caveat:** The Chinese translation is not incidental — it targets the largest source of the FDI the maps identified as the capability channel.

## How to Apply

1. **Rewrite the FDI chapter as a capability inventory.** Who is here; what can they make; what could they teach; who supplies them now and who could.
2. **Audit the investment promotion agency on aftercare, not pledges.** Is it transactional or relationship-building? That was the diagnosis in Addis and Dar.
3. **Treat multinationals as the route to the middle tier**, not as competitors to domestic firms.
4. **Solve the buyer's search problem.** Local content fails on search. A map is the instrument.
5. **Find the Tullow.** Which large buyer in the province has a local content commitment and cannot find suppliers? That is the partner who will fund the capability support.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy has an FDI pillar measured by rand value of investment attracted, and a separate local content initiative asking large firms to procure locally.

**Application:** The two pillars are the same pillar and neither is designed. The FDI pillar counts money and ignores the thing that matters — what the investors can make and teach. The local content initiative asks buyers to procure from suppliers they cannot identify, which is a search problem, not a willingness problem: Tullow *wanted* Ghanaian suppliers and needed a map to find them, then built an Enterprise Development Centre once it could. Redesign: merge them; measure the FDI stock by capability content; commission the supplier map; take it to the two or three large buyers with local content commitments; and audit the provincial investment agency against the relationship-building standard rather than the pledge count.

**Result:** An FDI chapter that transfers capability rather than counting money.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Measure FDI by value attracted.
**Why:** The capability content is the point. The Ethiopian FDI stock mattered because of what it could make and employ, not what it cost.

**Don't:** Run local content policy without a supplier map.
**Why:** Buyers fail on search, not on willingness. Tullow is the demonstration.

**Don't:** Judge an investment promotion agency on pledges.
**Why:** The reform that came out of this work was cultural — from transactional promotion to relationship-building aftercare.
