# Daring Leadership

## Core Concept
Daring leadership is not about personality, title, or position. It is a collection of four skill sets that are 100% teachable, observable, and measurable. These four skill sets emerged from Brown's seven years of research with leaders across Fortune 500 companies, the military, nonprofits, startups, and public sector organisations.

**Skill Set 1: Rumbling with Vulnerability.** The foundational skill. The willingness to lean into discomfort, have difficult conversations, show up without armour, and sit with uncertainty. Without this skill, the other three are impossible. Our capacity for daring leadership will never exceed our capacity for vulnerability.

**Skill Set 2: Living into Values.** Identifying your two core values and translating them into specific, observable behaviours. This means being clear about what you stand for and having the courage to act accordingly — especially when it is uncomfortable, when you are under pressure, or when no one is watching.

**Skill Set 3: BRAVING Trust.** Building trust through specific, behavioural components: Boundaries, Reliability, Accountability, Vault, Integrity, Non-judgement, and Generosity. Trust is not abstract — it is built in the small moments and can be diagnosed and repaired using these seven elements.

**Skill Set 4: Learning to Rise.** The Rising Strong process applied to leadership: reckoning with emotion after failure or setback, rumbling with the stories you tell yourself, and writing brave new endings. Resilient leaders are not people who never fall — they are people who have learned to rise.

The research also identified ten cultural barriers to courageous leadership — the organisational norms, behaviours, and systems that prevent leaders from being brave even when they have the individual skills. Daring leadership requires both individual courage and an organisational culture that supports it.

## How to Apply
1. **Assess which skill set needs attention.** Most leaders are weaker in one or two areas. Diagnose before prescribing.
2. **Start with vulnerability.** If the leader cannot rumble with vulnerability, the other three skill sets will not develop. This is always the starting point.
3. **Name two core values.** Not five or ten. Two. Then translate each into three or four observable behaviours.
4. **Audit trust using BRAVING.** Where is trust strong? Where is it weak? Address the specific elements.
5. **Build a rising practice.** Create regular space for reckoning with failure and learning from it — without shame.

## Examples
**Situation:** A senior government official wants to build a "culture of accountability" in their department but staff perceive accountability as punishment for failure.
**Application:** The official is confusing accountability with shame-based consequences. Daring leadership reframes accountability: it is the A in BRAVING — owning mistakes, making amends, and being held to commitments. But it requires the other BRAVING elements too, especially non-judgement (people can admit mistakes without being judged) and integrity (leaders model accountability, not just demand it). Start with vulnerability: the official should model accountability by owning their own mistakes publicly before expecting it from staff.
**Result:** Accountability shifts from a threat ("if you fail, you will be punished") to a practice ("when we fail, we own it, learn from it, and do better"). This requires the leader to go first.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Teach the four skill sets without modelling them.
**Why:** Leaders who teach vulnerability without practising it, or who demand trust without being trustworthy, create cynicism. The four skill sets must be demonstrated before they are taught. You cannot ask people to be brave if you are not willing to be brave yourself.
