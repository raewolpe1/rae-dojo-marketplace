# Engaged Feedback

## Core Concept
Brown's approach to feedback rests on one principle: clear is kind, unclear is unkind. Most feedback failures are not failures of honesty — they are failures of clarity. We sugarcoat, hint, soften, generalise, and hedge because we want to avoid discomfort. The result is that the recipient either misses the message entirely or senses the criticism without understanding it, which produces anxiety rather than improvement.

Engaged feedback has several characteristics. It is specific — naming the exact behaviour, not making character assessments. It is timely — given as close to the event as possible, not saved for an annual review. It is empathic — delivered with awareness that receiving feedback is vulnerable and that the person's worth is not in question. It is actionable — pointing toward specific changes rather than vague improvement requests. And it is mutual — the feedback giver acknowledges their own vulnerability in the process.

Brown identifies key practices: sitting on the same side of the table (metaphorically — approaching feedback as partners solving a problem together, not as judge and defendant). Acknowledging what is working before addressing what is not. Being specific about the behaviour, its impact, and what you need to see instead. Asking "what does support from me look like?" to offer partnership in the change process.

The hardest feedback is feedback about patterns rather than incidents — the colleague who consistently misses deadlines, the team member whose work quality has gradually declined, the leader whose communication style creates confusion. Pattern feedback requires the courage to name the pattern directly while maintaining empathy for the person.

## How to Apply
1. **Prepare.** Get clear about what specifically needs to change and why it matters. Be ready with examples.
2. **Deliver with clarity and empathy.** "I have some feedback about [specific behaviour]. My intention is to help us do better together."
3. **Be specific.** Not "your work needs improvement" but "the last three deliverables have had significant analytical errors in the methodology sections."
4. **Name the impact.** "The impact is that I am spending time correcting the errors, which delays delivery to the client."
5. **Offer partnership.** "What does support from me look like? Do you need more review time, more guidance on the methodology, or something else?"
6. **Follow up.** Feedback without follow-up is performative. Check in on progress and acknowledge improvement.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use "clear is kind" to justify cruelty.
**Why:** Clarity without empathy is not kindness — it is weaponised honesty. The principle is clear AND kind, not clear instead of kind. Both elements are essential.

**Don't:** Avoid feedback entirely and call it "choosing your battles."
**Why:** Chronically withheld feedback is not strategic — it is avoidant. The discomfort of giving feedback is smaller than the cost of the performance problem it would address.
