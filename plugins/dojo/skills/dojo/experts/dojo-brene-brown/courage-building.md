# Courage Building

## Core Concept
Courage is not a personality trait that some people have and others lack. It is a collection of four skill sets that can be taught, practised, measured, and strengthened: rumbling with vulnerability (the willingness to engage with uncertainty and emotional risk), living into values (aligning behaviour with deeply held values, especially under pressure), BRAVING trust (building and maintaining trust through specific behaviours), and learning to rise (recovering from failures and setbacks).

The foundational skill is vulnerability. Without it, the other three cannot be practised. Our capacity for courage will never exceed our capacity for vulnerability. This is why vulnerability is not weakness — it is the entry point for all courageous behaviour.

Courage is built through practice, not through willpower. Each small act of vulnerability — asking for help, admitting a mistake, giving honest feedback, trying something new without guarantees — strengthens the courage muscle. Over time, what once felt terrifying becomes merely uncomfortable, and what once felt impossible becomes routine.

Organisations build courage by creating cultures that support vulnerability: rewarding learning from failure rather than punishing it, celebrating honest feedback rather than penalising it, and modelling courage from the top. Leaders who are brave enough to be vulnerable give everyone else permission to do the same.

## How to Apply
1. **Start with vulnerability.** Identify one specific area where you avoid vulnerability. Practice showing up there.
2. **Build incrementally.** Small acts of courage build the capacity for larger ones. Start with low-stakes situations.
3. **Create accountability.** Find a courage partner — someone who will support you and hold you accountable for practicing.
4. **Track the skill sets.** Self-assess regularly across all four: rumbling, values, trust, rising. Where are you growing? Where are you stuck?
5. **Model for your team.** If you lead others, demonstrate the four skill sets visibly. Courage is contagious when leaders go first.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Equate courage with fearlessness.
**Why:** Courage is not the absence of fear — it is acting in the presence of fear. People who claim to be fearless are either lying or armoured. Real courage acknowledges the fear and acts anyway.
