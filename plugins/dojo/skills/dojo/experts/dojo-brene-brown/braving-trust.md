# BRAVING Trust

## Core Concept
Trust is not a single, abstract quality. It is composed of seven specific, behavioural elements that can be assessed, discussed, built, and repaired. The BRAVING inventory disaggregates trust into components that make it actionable.

**B — Boundaries.** You respect my boundaries, and when you are not clear about what is okay and what is not okay, you ask. I do the same. Trust is impossible without clear boundaries.

**R — Reliability.** You do what you say you will do. Not once — consistently. This means being aware of your competencies and limitations and not overpromising. Reliability is about follow-through, not about intention.

**A — Accountability.** You own your mistakes, apologise, and make amends. I can trust that when something goes wrong, you will not blame, deny, or deflect. You will own it.

**V — Vault.** You keep confidences. What I share with you, you hold. And — crucially — you do not share other people's stories with me. If you gossip about others to me, I know you gossip about me to others. The vault works both ways.

**I — Integrity.** You choose courage over comfort. You choose what is right over what is fun, fast, or easy. You practise your values rather than simply professing them.

**N — Non-judgement.** I can ask you for help without being judged. You can ask me for help without being judged. We can both struggle and still be worthy of connection. When asking for help is met with judgement, trust dies.

**G — Generosity.** You extend the most generous interpretation possible to my intentions, words, and actions. Before assuming the worst, you assume the best — or at least check.

The power of BRAVING is that it transforms "I don't trust you" — a conversation-ending declaration — into specific, addressable behaviours: "I need more reliability from you" or "I need to know the vault is secure." These are conversations that can actually be had.

BRAVING also works as a self-trust assessment. Change the pronouns: Did I respect my own boundaries? Was I reliable to myself? Did I hold myself accountable? Each element applies to the relationship with oneself as much as to relationships with others.

## How to Apply
1. **Diagnose specifically.** When trust feels broken, go through each BRAVING element and identify where the weakness lies. It is rarely all seven — it is usually one or two.
2. **Have the conversation.** Use BRAVING language to name the specific element: "I need more reliability — when we agree on a deadline, I need you to meet it or tell me in advance if you cannot."
3. **Build proactively.** Use BRAVING to assess new relationships: which elements are developing well? Which need attention?
4. **Assess self-trust.** Apply BRAVING to yourself: Am I keeping my own boundaries? Am I being reliable to myself? Am I practising my values?

## Examples
**Situation:** Two consultants working closely together have growing tension. Partner A feels Partner B is unreliable and occasionally shares client information inappropriately.
**Application:** Using BRAVING: the specific issues are Reliability (B does not consistently follow through on commitments) and Vault (B shares information that should remain confidential). Instead of "I don't trust you" — which Partner B will experience as a blanket attack — Partner A can say: "I need to talk about two specific things. First, reliability: when you commit to delivering a section by Thursday and it arrives Monday, it affects my ability to deliver to the client. Second, vault: when client information is shared outside our team, it puts our reputation at risk." These are specific, behavioural, and addressable.
**Result:** The conversation moves from vague mistrust to specific repair. Both partners can discuss concrete changes rather than defending against a global accusation.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Use BRAVING as a weapon.
**Why:** The inventory is designed to build and repair trust, not to catalogue someone's failings. Leading with "you fail on reliability, accountability, and vault" is an attack dressed in a framework. Lead with your own needs and the specific behaviours you need to see.
