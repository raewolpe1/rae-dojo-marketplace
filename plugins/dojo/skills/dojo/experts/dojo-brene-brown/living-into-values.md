# Living into Values

## Core Concept
Living into values is the second skill set of daring leadership. It involves two steps: first, identifying your core values — not ten, not five, but two — the values that are most central to who you are. Second, translating those values into specific, observable behaviours — what does this value look like when you practise it? What does it look like when you do not?

The discipline of choosing two values is important. When everything is a priority, nothing is a priority. Two values provide a clear anchor for decision-making under pressure. When the situation is ambiguous, when you are stressed, when the easy path and the right path diverge — your two values tell you which way to go.

Values are not values if they are only practised when it is convenient. The true test of values is whether you practise them when they are uncomfortable, costly, or unpopular. Integrity — the I in BRAVING — means choosing courage over comfort, choosing what is right over what is fun, fast, or easy, and practising your values rather than simply professing them.

Brown provides a list of over 100 values to choose from: accountability, courage, compassion, creativity, equity, fairness, family, integrity, justice, kindness, learning, reliability, respect, service, and many more. The exercise is to narrow from the full list to two — the two that, if taken away, would fundamentally change who you are.

Once identified, each value must be translated into three or four specific behaviours: what does this value look like in action? What are the three behaviours that support this value? What are the three slippery behaviours that indicate you are sliding away from it?

## How to Apply
1. **Choose two.** From a values list, identify your two core values. This is hard — it requires letting go of values you also care about.
2. **Define behaviours.** For each value, list three or four specific, observable behaviours that demonstrate it in practice.
3. **Define slippery behaviours.** For each value, list the behaviours that indicate you are drifting away from it under pressure.
4. **Create a values check-in.** Regularly assess: am I living into my values? Where am I practising them? Where am I sliding?
5. **Use values for decisions.** When facing a difficult decision, ask: which choice aligns with my two values? That is your answer.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** List values that sound good but do not actually guide your behaviour.
**Why:** Aspirational values that are never practised are performative. The exercise is about identifying what you actually stand for — not what you wish you stood for.
