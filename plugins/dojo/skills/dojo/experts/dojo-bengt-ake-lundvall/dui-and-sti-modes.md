# The DUI and STI Modes

## When to Use
- **Load this first on any Lundvall question.** It is the foundation.
- Whenever innovation is equated with R&D.
- When a firm population is described as non-innovative.

## Fails When
- **The population genuinely is research-intensive** — pharma, deep tech, university spinouts. Then STI instruments fit and the correction is unnecessary.

## Core Concept

**Two modes. The difference is the kind of knowledge.**

| | **STI** | **DUI** |
|---|---|---|
| **Full name** | Science, Technology, Innovation | Doing, Using, Interacting |
| **Driver** | Investment in R&D | By-product of ordinary activity |
| **Knowledge** | **Codified** — written, published, patentable, teachable | **Tacit** — lives in people and relationships |
| **The knows** | **Know-what, know-why** | **Know-how, know-who** |
| **Processes** | Education, training, R&D, market research | Problem-solving, learning-by-doing, learning-by-using, interaction with customers and suppliers |
| **Visible to indicators?** | **Yes** — R&D spend, patents, publications | **No** |

**The four knows.** Know-what (facts) and know-why (principles) are codifiable and transferable — you can put them in a course. Know-how (skill) and know-who (relationships) are not: they are acquired by doing and by being in a network over time. The STI mode traffics in the first pair; the DUI mode in the second.

**DUI is social, not solitary.** A common misreading is that DUI means a clever individual tinkering. It does not. Both learning-by-doing and learning-by-using **involve interaction between individuals and departments** — and outside the firm, with customers and suppliers and, where it exists, with knowledge institutions. The interaction is where the learning is.

**Why this matters more in a developing economy.** The share of firms with any R&D capacity is small everywhere and vanishingly small in an economy of micro and small enterprises. So the *entire* innovative activity of most of the firm population runs through a mode that no instrument in the standard policy toolkit addresses and no indicator in the standard set detects.

**The correction to make — and the one not to make.** The correction: an innovation pillar built on R&D and patents is designed for a fraction of a percent of firms and is blind to the rest. **The error to avoid: concluding that DUI is superior.** The empirical finding (see `evidence-base-modes-of-innovation.md`) is that **firms combining both modes out-innovate firms relying on either alone**. Most small firms are not choosing DUI over STI — they have no STI at all, and that is a genuine gap, just not the one the indicators describe.

## How to Apply

1. **Classify the firm population.** What share could plausibly run an R&D function? That is your STI-addressable population.
2. **Ask the DUI question directly.** *When did you last change what you make or how you make it, and where did the idea come from?* The answers describe the invisible mode.
3. **Read the instrument set against the modes.** All STI? Then the pillar serves the fraction.
4. **Look for the combination**, because that is what the evidence rewards — and it is what almost no policy funds.
5. **Never let "no R&D" mean "no innovation".**

## Examples

**Situation:** A survey finds that under 2% of provincial MSMEs conduct R&D, and the strategy concludes there is an innovation deficit.

**Application:** The survey measured the STI mode and found, correctly, that almost no MSMEs are in it. That is unsurprising and near-tautological — R&D is a function of firms with a certain scale and structure. It says nothing about whether these firms innovate, because their innovation, if any, runs through DUI: process adaptations, product changes driven by customers, input substitutions suggested by suppplers, methods worked out on the floor. None of it is captured. **Before concluding a deficit, measure the other mode.** Then a real finding becomes available: how many firms changed something, and where the idea came from.

**Result:** An innovation diagnosis based on both modes rather than on the absence of the minority one.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Read "no R&D" as "no innovation".
**Why:** R&D is one mode. Most firms innovate through the other one, which leaves no trace.

**Don't:** Conclude that DUI is the superior mode.
**Why:** The finding is combination. Firms doing both out-perform either pure type.

**Don't:** Treat DUI as individual tinkering.
**Why:** It is interactive by definition — between individuals, departments, and with customers and suppliers.
