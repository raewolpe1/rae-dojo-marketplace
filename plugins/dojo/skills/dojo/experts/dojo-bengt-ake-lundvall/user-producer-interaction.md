# User–Producer Interaction

## When to Use
- When procurement, supplier development or market access is being designed.
- When asking where innovation ideas actually originate.
- When a firm population has no demanding buyers.

## Fails When
- **The question is value chain governance and upgrading.** Route to Kaplan or Porter. This is narrower: the relationship as a *learning* channel.

## Core Concept

**The relationship is the learning channel. This is where the whole innovation systems framework came from.**

Before it was a national system, it was an observation: Danish producers learned from their users. Not from research. From a customer saying *this doesn't work, and here is what I need instead* — repeatedly, over years, in a relationship close enough that the complaint was honest and the producer could act on it.

**Why the relationship and not the transaction.** A one-off arm's-length sale transmits a price. It transmits no knowledge. What transmits knowledge is **repeated interaction with a trusted counterparty**: the user knows enough about the producer's constraints to complain usefully; the producer trusts the complaint enough to invest against it; both adjust. The knowledge moving through that channel is tacit — know-how and know-who — which is exactly why it needs a channel and cannot be posted.

**This is why the system is national.** Those relationships run on proximity, trust, shared language, repeated dealing, and common institutions. That is what made the system *national* — not the research budget. The insight scales to a region with the same logic.

**The policy consequence, and it is the most actionable thing in this dojo: a demanding customer is an innovation instrument.**

Consider what a firm gains from one sophisticated, demanding, repeat buyer: a specification it must rise to; honest feedback on failure; a reason to change; and a partner who will explain what was wrong. No innovation voucher does that. And the state, in a province, is frequently the largest available buyer.

**Which relocates innovation policy into the procurement chapter** — where it is not currently filed. Procurement that merely purchases at lowest price transmits nothing. Procurement that **specifies**, that gives structured feedback, that maintains a relationship across contracts, and that permits a supplier to improve rather than merely to comply, is a learning channel and therefore an innovation instrument.

**The diagnostic.** Ask a firm: *who do you learn from?* If the answer is nobody — if every customer is anonymous, price-driven and one-off — the firm is not in an innovation system whatever the map says, and no instrument aimed at the firm alone will fix it. **The missing thing is a relationship, not a capability.**

## How to Apply

1. **Ask who the firm learns from.** Name them. If nobody, that is the finding.
2. **Distinguish transaction from relationship.** Price versus knowledge. Only the second teaches.
3. **Treat the demanding buyer as an instrument** and go looking for one. Large private buyers, anchor firms, exporters.
4. **Move procurement into the innovation pillar.** Specification, feedback, continuity — that is a learning channel.
5. **Do not fix the firm in isolation.** If the missing thing is a relationship, capability support does not supply it.
6. **Look for the interaction infrastructure.** Tacit knowledge needs occasions.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy has a market access pillar (get MSMEs into supply chains) and a separate innovation pillar (R&D, universities, IP).

**Application:** These are the same pillar and the strategy has not noticed. The market access work, if it produces **relationships** rather than transactions, is the most powerful innovation instrument in the document — a demanding repeat buyer supplies specification, honest feedback, and a reason to change, which is precisely the user–producer channel the whole framework was built on. But as written it will produce transactions: a set-aside, a listing, a one-off order at a fixed price, transmitting nothing. Redesign: build supplier development with **structured feedback and continuity across contracts**, use provincial procurement to **specify rather than merely purchase**, and count the learning — did the supplier change anything as a result of the relationship? Then the market access pillar becomes the innovation pillar for the population that has no laboratory.

**Result:** The province's largest innovation instrument — its own purchasing — put to work.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat market access and innovation as separate pillars.
**Why:** For a DUI firm, a demanding buyer is the innovation instrument.

**Don't:** Procure at lowest price and expect supplier learning.
**Why:** A transaction transmits a price. Only a relationship transmits knowledge.

**Don't:** Try to fix an isolated firm's innovativeness with firm-level support.
**Why:** If it learns from nobody, the missing input is a relationship.
