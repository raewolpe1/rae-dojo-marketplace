# National (and Regional) Systems of Innovation

## When to Use
- When an innovation system is being mapped or designed.
- When the system's membership consists of universities, research councils and agencies.
- When asking who belongs in the system.

## Fails When
- **The question is an entrepreneurial ecosystem.** Different object — route to Autio (what kind of thing is it?) or Stam (how good is it?). An innovation system and an entrepreneurial ecosystem are not synonyms and should not be merged.

## Core Concept

**There are two readings of "national system of innovation" and the difference is the whole argument.**

**The narrow reading:** the system is the science-and-technology apparatus — universities, research councils, public labs, technology agencies, IP institutions, R&D-performing firms. This is the reading that most policy documents adopt, and it is the one that produces an innovation chapter listing the DSI, the NRF, the TIA, the universities and the science councils.

**The broad reading — mine:** the system encompasses **the whole of the learning that goes on in an economy, including in production and in everyday work.** Firms learning from customers. Workers learning by doing. Suppliers learning from complaints. The narrow reading is not wrong so much as it is a subset — and it is the subset containing almost none of the firms.

**Where the framework came from, which explains why it is broad.** It came out of the Aalborg tradition of studying **user–producer interaction** — watching how Danish producers learned from their users. Not from studying laboratories. The system was called *national* precisely because those relationships are national: they run on trust, proximity, shared language, repeated dealing, and common institutions. The nationality of the system is a property of the *relationships*, not of the research budget.

**Why the narrow reading is self-confirming.** Adopt it, and you populate the system with STI actors; measure it with STI indicators; fund STI instruments; and observe that the firms are not participating. The conclusion drawn is that firms are not innovative. The correct conclusion is that they are not in the system you drew.

**The membership test.** Ask of any firm in the economy: *is it in the system as you have drawn it?* For an MSME with no university contact, no IP, and no R&D — which is nearly all of them — the honest answer under the narrow reading is no. Under the broad reading the answer is yes, provided it is learning from someone. **That is not a semantic difference; it determines whether the strategy has any instrument that touches it.**

**On regional systems.** The same logic scales down, with one caution: sub-national systems are more open than national ones — the relationships cross the boundary constantly. A Western Cape innovation system is a real object, but its firms learn from suppliers in Gauteng and customers in Europe, and the system boundary is analytical rather than actual.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask which reading the document has adopted.** It will be the narrow one, implicitly.
2. **Draw the system broadly.** Then test membership: is the modal firm in it?
3. **Follow the learning relationships**, not the institutional org chart. They define the system.
4. **Do not merge with ecosystems.** Different concept, different literature. Route accordingly.
5. **Treat the regional boundary as analytical.** The relationships cross it.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's innovation chapter maps the innovation system as DSI, NRF, TIA, the universities, the science councils, and a handful of R&D-performing firms.

**Application:** That is the narrow reading, and it is the S&T apparatus rather than the innovation system. Test membership: take a typical firm in the target population — nine employees, no university contact, no IP. Under this map it is not in the system, which is why no instrument in the chapter reaches it, and why the chapter will eventually report that MSMEs are not innovative. Redraw broadly: include the learning that goes on in production and everyday work, and map the *relationships* — who does this firm learn from? Customers, suppliers, competitors, an equipment vendor. Those actors are the system for that firm, and none of them appears on the current map.

**Result:** An innovation system map that contains the firms the strategy is about.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Map the S&T apparatus and call it the innovation system.
**Why:** It is a subset, and the subset containing almost none of the firms.

**Don't:** Conclude firms are not innovative when they are not in the system you drew.
**Why:** The narrow reading is self-confirming.

**Don't:** Use "innovation system" and "entrepreneurial ecosystem" interchangeably.
**Why:** Different concepts with different literatures. Autio and Stam own the second.
