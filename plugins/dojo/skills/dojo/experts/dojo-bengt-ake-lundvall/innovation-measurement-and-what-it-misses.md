# Innovation Measurement and What It Misses

## When to Use
- Designing or auditing an innovation indicator set.
- When a benchmark or ranking is being cited.
- When low patenting is being read as low innovation.

## Fails When
- **The question is entrepreneurship indices** — GEM, GEDI. Route to Autio, who built them.

## Core Concept

**The standard innovation indicator set measures the STI mode and is structurally blind to the DUI mode.** This is not a gap to be filled at the margin; it is a category omission, and it becomes a policy failure because policy chases indicators.

**What the standard set contains, and what each sees:**

| Indicator | Sees | Blind to |
|---|---|---|
| R&D intensity | STI investment | All DUI |
| Patent counts | Codified, protectable output | All tacit knowledge |
| Publications / citations | The science base | Everything in firms |
| University–industry collaboration | STI linkage | User–producer learning |
| Technology transfer / licensing | Codified transfer | Know-how, know-who |

**Why DUI is invisible by construction, not by oversight.** Its knowledge is **tacit** — know-how and know-who. Tacit knowledge does not survive codification; that is what makes it tacit. So there is no document to count. A firm that has, over three years, transformed its process through accumulated shop-floor problem-solving and close work with a demanding customer has generated no countable artefact whatsoever.

**The benchmarking consequence.** The authors' own concluding point: the implications are **for benchmarking innovation systems and for innovation policy.** A country whose firms innovate predominantly through DUI ranks poorly on every international benchmark and may be performing well. The measurement error becomes a policy error because the ranking is taken as the diagnosis.

**The test.** Ask of any indicator set: *if DUI-mode innovation in this economy doubled next year, which of these numbers would move?* If the answer is none — and it usually is — the set cannot detect success in the dominant mode, will report failure indefinitely, and the pillar built on it will eventually be defunded for a failure that is an artefact.

**What to measure instead — and it is cheap.** Not a new international index; a survey question. **Did the firm change its product, process, marketing method or organisation in the last year, and where did the idea come from?** Source options: a customer, a supplier, a worker, a competitor, a consultant, a university, own R&D. That single question makes the invisible mode visible and locates the learning channel. Related instruments worth knowing: work organisation measures (can people solve problems? does information move horizontally?) and interaction measures (who does this firm learn from?).

**The honest limit.** Self-reported change is a noisy measure and it is not comparable to patent counts in precision. That is a real cost. It is a smaller cost than measuring the wrong mode with great precision.

## How to Apply

1. **Run the doubling test** on the existing indicator set. It will fail.
2. **Add the changed-practice question.** Cheap, survey-based, and it makes DUI visible.
3. **Add a source-of-idea question.** It locates the learning channel and tells you which relationship to support.
4. **Report benchmarks with the mode caveat.** "This ranks the STI mode" is a true and disarming sentence.
5. **Never conclude an innovation deficit from patent data alone** in an MSME economy.
6. **Accept the noise.** An imprecise measure of the right thing beats a precise measure of the wrong one.

## Examples

**Situation:** A provincial innovation pillar sets targets for R&D intensity, patents filed, and university–industry collaborations.

**Application:** Apply the doubling test. If every MSME in the province improved its processes, adapted products to customer feedback and substituted better inputs on supplier advice — DUI-mode innovation on a large scale — **not one of the three targets would move.** The pillar is therefore structurally incapable of registering the dominant form of innovation in the economy it governs. Add a fourth indicator from a firm survey: share of firms reporting a changed product, process, marketing method or organisational form in the last year, with source of idea. It is inexpensive, it is comparable over time, and it is the only one of the four that could ever move.

**Result:** An indicator set that can detect what the economy actually does.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Read a poor innovation ranking as a poor innovation system.
**Why:** The ranking measures STI. A DUI economy ranks badly and may be performing.

**Don't:** Try to fix the problem with a better patent indicator.
**Why:** The omission is categorical. Tacit knowledge has no countable artefact by definition.

**Don't:** Reject the survey question because it is self-reported and noisy.
**Why:** It is. It measures the right mode. Precision about the wrong thing is not an asset.
