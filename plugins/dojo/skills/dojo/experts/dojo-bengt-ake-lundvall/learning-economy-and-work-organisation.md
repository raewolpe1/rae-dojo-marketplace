# The Learning Economy and Work Organisation

## When to Use
- When firms are described as unable to adapt or learn.
- When work organisation appears under HR rather than innovation.
- When training is being designed.

## Fails When
- **The question is measuring management practice.** Route to Bloom — he scores it. Lundvall asks whether the organisation permits learning at all, which is a prior and different question.

## Core Concept

**The learning economy is not the knowledge economy.** Every economy has run on knowledge. What distinguishes the contemporary one is **the speed at which knowledge becomes obsolete**. Therefore the scarce and valuable thing is not the *stock* of knowledge but the **rate of learning** — and the rate of learning is a property of how an organisation is arranged.

**Work organisation is an innovation variable.** This is the claim that gets filed under human resources and lost. The DUI mode runs on problem-solving, on interaction between individuals and departments, on the person who does the job noticing something. Whether any of that happens is determined by the organisation:

- **Can people below the owner change anything?** If every decision routes to one person, there is no distributed problem-solving and no DUI capacity, regardless of how skilled the workforce is.
- **Does information move horizontally?** DUI learning is interactive between individuals and departments. Vertical-only information flow forecloses it.
- **Is learning permitted on the job?** Or is the job specified so tightly that deviation is failure?
- **Is there delegation?** Note the finding from the India management experiment (Bloom et al.): better information flow **enabled owners to delegate more decisions to middle managers**. Information and delegation move together.

**Why this bites hardest in small and family firms.** The characteristic shape — owner does everything, no second layer, no delegation beyond family — is precisely the shape that forecloses DUI. The firm may be full of skilled and experienced people whose accumulated know-how is structurally unable to reach a decision. That is an innovation problem presenting as an organogram.

**The consequence for training.** A training intervention delivers know-what and know-why to an individual. If the workplace does not permit the person to apply it — no authority to change anything, no horizontal channel to tell anyone, no delegation — **the learning does not become innovation**. Work organisation gates the return on every skills intervention in the strategy. This is why training evaluations disappoint for reasons that have nothing to do with the training.

**The radical underside, stated plainly.** If learning is the central economic process, the people doing the learning are the whole workforce — the machine operator, the driver, the person on the counter who hears the same complaint forty times. They are participants in the innovation system, not its beneficiaries. Every innovation systems framework says so. Almost every policy built on those frameworks quietly assumes otherwise and funds the laboratory.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask who is allowed to change something.** That answer is the firm's DUI ceiling.
2. **Trace horizontal information.** Does the person who notices tell the person who decides? Is there a route?
3. **Treat the organogram as an innovation diagnostic**, not an HR document.
4. **Gate training on absorption.** If the workplace forbids application, the training is consumption.
5. **Look at delegation.** Owner-does-everything is the modal constraint and it is not a skills problem.
6. **Distinguish stock from rate.** Ask how fast this organisation learns, not what it knows.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy proposes accredited skills training for MSME workers, targeting improved firm performance.

**Application:** The training delivers know-what and know-why to individuals. Whether it becomes innovation depends on something the strategy does not address: whether the workplace permits the trained person to change anything. In an owner-does-everything firm — the modal MSME shape — it does not, and the return on the training is close to zero for reasons entirely external to its quality. Recommendation: pair any skills intervention with a work organisation component, and treat the organogram as an eligibility screen. A firm where nobody but the owner can alter a process cannot convert training into performance, and funding it is funding consumption.

**Result:** Training aimed at firms that can absorb it, paired with the organisational change that lets them.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** File work organisation under HR.
**Why:** For a firm without a laboratory it *is* the innovation policy.

**Don't:** Fund training without asking whether the workplace permits application.
**Why:** Work organisation gates the return on every skills intervention.

**Don't:** Read the learning economy as the knowledge economy.
**Why:** The point is obsolescence. The rate of learning, not the stock of knowledge.
