# Innovation Policy Without Laboratories

## When to Use
- Designing innovation support for MSMEs or any non-research population.
- When every instrument in the pillar is a university, a lab, or an IP service.
- When asked what innovation policy means for a firm with nine employees.

## Fails When
- **The population is research-intensive.** STI instruments fit; use them.

## Core Concept

**The standard innovation toolkit assumes a firm with a laboratory. Most firms will never have one. What is the policy?**

**Start from what the evidence says works: the combination.** Firms combining organisational forms that promote learning with R&D efforts and co-operation with researchers at knowledge institutions are much more innovative than either pure type. So the goal for a DUI firm is **not** to build an R&D function — that is neither achievable nor what the evidence recommends. It is to **connect an existing learning organisation to a small amount of codified knowledge.**

**Four instrument families that fit a firm without a laboratory:**

**1. Cheap bridges to the science base.** A technical adviser for a week. A materials test at a university lab. A student placement. An extension officer. The point is not to make the firm scientific; it is to give a DUI firm one connection to know-why, which is the missing half of the combination. This is the highest-value and least fashionable instrument in the set.

**2. Strengthen the user–producer relationship.** Innovation in the DUI mode comes disproportionately from a demanding customer. Instruments: buyer–supplier development, procurement that specifies rather than merely purchases, supplier days, quality feedback loops. A demanding buyer is an innovation policy — see `user-producer-interaction.md`.

**3. Work organisation.** DUI capacity is a function of how work is organised: whether people below the owner can solve problems, whether information moves horizontally, whether the person doing the job may change the job. This looks like HR and it is innovation policy — see `learning-economy-and-work-organisation.md`.

**4. Interaction infrastructure.** Places and occasions where firms meet firms, and where know-who accumulates. Industry associations, clusters, shared facilities, supplier networks. The knowledge that travels here is tacit, which is why it needs a physical channel.

**What does *not* fit, and why it keeps being funded.** IP support for firms with nothing patentable. Technology transfer offices for firms that cannot absorb a transfer. Science parks for firms that cannot afford the rent. Innovation vouchers redeemable only against university services. These are STI instruments and they are funded because they are legible, procurable, and photograph well.

**The sequencing insight.** For a firm in the low-learning cluster, none of the four families will work — there is no learning organisation to connect to anything. Work organisation comes first, and it is slow. **Do not skip it and wonder why the bridge to the university produced nothing.**

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what the firm could absorb.** A lab result? A student? An adviser? A specification from a buyer? Size the bridge to the absorber.
2. **Aim at the combination, not at conversion.** You are not making the firm scientific. You are connecting it once.
3. **Treat the demanding buyer as an instrument.** It is the cheapest innovation policy available and it sits in the procurement chapter.
4. **Fund the interaction infrastructure.** Tacit knowledge needs a room.
5. **Check for the low-learning cluster.** If work organisation forbids problem-solving, fix that first or nothing lands.
6. **Delete the instruments that assume a lab.**

## Examples

**Situation:** An innovation pillar offers innovation vouchers redeemable against university research services, IP support, and access to a science park.

**Application:** All three assume a firm that can specify a research question, own protectable IP, and pay park rent. For an MSME population that is a fraction of a percent. Redesign around the combination: make the voucher redeemable against a **technical adviser or a single lab test** rather than a research project — that is the cheap bridge to know-why. Replace the IP service with **buyer-linked supplier development**, since a demanding customer generates more DUI innovation than an IP register ever will. Replace the science park with **interaction infrastructure** the firms can actually reach. And check first whether the target firms have any learning organisation to build on, because if they are in the low-learning cluster the whole sequence starts one step further back.

**Result:** Instruments a firm without a laboratory can actually use.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Try to build R&D capacity in micro-enterprises.
**Why:** Not achievable, and not what the evidence recommends. The combination is a bridge, not a conversion.

**Don't:** Fund IP support for firms with nothing patentable.
**Why:** It is an STI instrument aimed at a DUI population. It is legible and it does nothing.

**Don't:** Skip work organisation.
**Why:** For a low-learning firm there is nothing to connect the bridge to.
