# Animal Capabilities

## When to Use
- When animal welfare or rights are at issue.
- When environmental policy intersects with non-human welfare.
- When extending justice frameworks beyond the human.

## Fails When
- **The framework is treated as a sentimental concern.** Nussbaum's argument is rigorous and politically demanding.

## Core Concept

Nussbaum's *Frontiers of Justice* (2006) and the more recent *Justice for Animals* (2022) extend the capabilities approach to non-human animals. The argument:

**Animals are subjects of justice, not just objects of compassion.** Just as the capabilities approach grounds human justice in human dignity and the human capabilities, it grounds animal justice in animal dignity (suitably conceived) and species-appropriate capabilities.

**Each species has its own central capabilities.** What dignity requires varies by species. A pig's capabilities include the freedom to root, to be with other pigs, to nurture young; an elephant's include the freedom to range, to form complex social bonds, to grieve. The capabilities are species-specific in content but the structure of the framework is unified.

**The wrongs done to animals are systematic and severe.** Factory farming, environmental destruction, habitat loss, exploitation of wild animals — these are capabilities denials at industrial scale. The framework demands acknowledgement of the scale and seriousness.

**Sentience is a necessary but not sufficient marker.** All sentient animals have moral standing. But the capabilities approach asks more — what does each species need to flourish in the manner appropriate to its form of life? The question goes beyond avoiding suffering to enabling species-appropriate functioning.

**The framework displaces the contractualist tradition.** Rawlsian contractualism struggles with animal justice because animals cannot be parties to social contracts. The capabilities approach, grounded in dignity rather than contract, can encompass non-contracting beings (animals, severely disabled humans, future generations) more naturally.

**Practical implications.** End factory farming. Protect wild habitats. Reform agricultural practice. Address species extinction as a moral emergency, not just an ecological loss. Reform legal status of animals — they should not be property in the traditional sense.

**The framework engages traditions.** Many religious and cultural traditions have substantial resources for thinking about human duties to animals. The capabilities approach engages these traditions; it does not impose a single secular view.

**The South African context** includes substantial biodiversity, complex relationships between rural communities and wildlife, large-scale agricultural practice, and growing urban concern with animal welfare. The framework offers a coherent ground for engaging these alongside human-development concerns. Conservation, wildlife management, agricultural reform, and urban animal policy all benefit from the capabilities-justice lens.

## How to Apply

1. **Treat animal welfare as a matter of justice**, not optional compassion.
2. **Use species-appropriate capabilities** to assess animal flourishing.
3. **Address industrial-scale wrongs** (factory farming, habitat destruction) as systemic.
4. **Integrate animal capabilities into environmental and agricultural policy.**

## Examples

**Situation:** South African policy on wildlife management in a community-conservation area.

**Application:** The capabilities lens: the wild animals have species-specific capabilities (movement, social grouping, foraging, reproduction) that wildlife management policy can support or undermine. The framework demands that policy be evaluated on its support for these capabilities, alongside the human-development and economic considerations. The result is not "no economic use of wildlife" but rather "economic use compatible with species capabilities" — a more demanding standard than current practice meets in many places.

**Result:** A policy framework that integrates animal capabilities with human development and economic considerations.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat the framework as anti-development.
**Why:** It is about reshaping development to be compatible with animal capabilities, not opposing development.

**Don't:** Apply the human capabilities list directly to animals.
**Why:** Species-specific specification is essential. What flourishing means varies by species.
