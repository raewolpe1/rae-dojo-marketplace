# Women and Human Development

## When to Use
- When designing or evaluating women's economic empowerment programmes.
- When debating gender-specific development questions.
- When the cultural-relativist defence of patriarchy is invoked.

## Fails When
- **The framework is reduced to a checklist of women's outcomes.** It is a substantive political-philosophical commitment, not a measurement template.

## Core Concept

*Women and Human Development* (2000) is Nussbaum's most extended application of the capabilities approach to a specific population. The argument:

**Gender inequality is a capabilities crisis, not just an outcomes crisis.** Across many parts of the world, women face systematic denial of capabilities across multiple dimensions: bodily integrity (gender violence, restricted mobility, denied reproductive autonomy), affiliation (subordination in family and community), practical reason (limited educational opportunity), control over environment (restricted property rights, political voice). The pattern is structural.

**Aggregate measures hide the gender dimension.** Country-level development indicators often show progress while women's capabilities are systematically denied. Gender-disaggregated capability analysis reveals what aggregates conceal.

**The cultural-relativist defence is suspect.** When patriarchal arrangements are defended in the name of "culture", the defence often serves the interests of dominant men within the culture and silences the women whose capabilities are at stake. Listening to women — particularly women resisting the arrangements — is the empirical check.

**The capabilities-list is gender-sensitive in design.** Bodily integrity explicitly includes freedom from sexual assault and domestic violence. Affiliation explicitly includes protection against discrimination by sex. Control over environment explicitly includes property rights "on an equal basis with others". The list was designed with the gender questions in view.

**Education is foundational.** The senses-imagination-thought capability requires real education — not just literacy but the cultivation of critical reasoning, imaginative capacity, and aesthetic experience. Educating women across full lifecourse is foundational to capability development.

**Economic autonomy is necessary but not sufficient.** Income, employment, and property rights matter but they do not by themselves secure bodily integrity, affiliation on equal terms, or political voice. Capability-based women's development is multidimensional.

**Affiliation includes social bases of self-respect.** Gender-based humiliation, public shaming, and the denial of equal recognition are capabilities violations even when material outcomes are met.

**Practical reason requires institutional support.** The capability to form a conception of the good and engage in critical reflection cannot be exercised under conditions of severe deprivation or coercion. Institutional support (education, freedom of association, freedom from coerced marriage, control over reproduction) is foundational.

**The framework is global and intersectional.** Women's capabilities deficits manifest differently across class, race, geography, and disability. The capabilities lens is structurally intersectional even when not labelled that way.

For South Africa: the country has substantial formal legal protection (the Constitution, gender-based violence legislation) but massive bodily-integrity deficits (among the world's highest femicide and rape rates). The capabilities framework makes this a justice crisis, not just a policy challenge. The framework demands a level of state and social response commensurate with the constitutional commitment to dignity.

## How to Apply

1. **Disaggregate development analysis by gender** across all ten capabilities.
2. **Treat gender-based violence as a primary capabilities deficit.**
3. **Listen to women's testimony** about which capabilities are most at stake.
4. **Design programmes that address multiple capabilities together**, not just income or education in isolation.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African gender-based violence policy and programming.

**Application:** The capabilities lens: bodily integrity (the primary capability at stake) cannot be secured without simultaneous work on affiliation (social bases of women's equal standing), control over environment (women's economic autonomy and political voice), and practical reason (education that supports women's agency). The current response — primarily criminal-justice — addresses one dimension. The framework demands integration across capabilities: economic empowerment, education, psychosocial support, social-norms change, alongside criminal-justice response. The threshold concept is key: a country with high female labour-force participation but pervasive gender violence has not met the threshold of justice.

**Result:** A multidimensional gender programme grounded in the capabilities-justice framework.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Reduce women's development to income or labour-force participation.
**Why:** The capabilities are multiple and not tradable.

**Don't:** Accept cultural-relativist arguments without listening to the women whose capabilities are at stake.
**Why:** The relativist defence often serves dominant interests within the culture.
