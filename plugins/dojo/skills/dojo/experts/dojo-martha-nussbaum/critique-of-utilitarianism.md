# Critique of Utilitarianism

## When to Use
- When cost-benefit analysis is being applied to development decisions.
- When utilitarian aggregation is being defended.
- When welfare economics is being treated as the natural framework.

## Fails When
- **All utility-based reasoning is rejected.** Some aggregate measures are useful; the critique is about their philosophical status.

## Core Concept

Utilitarianism — the view that the right action is the one that maximises aggregate utility — has been the dominant framework in welfare economics and much of policy reasoning. Nussbaum's critique:

**Utilitarianism treats persons as receptacles for utility rather than as ends in themselves.** The framework counts each person's utility as a unit to be aggregated, allowing the sum to be increased by treating some persons badly if it makes others sufficiently better off. This violates the Kantian dignity commitment.

**Aggregate utility hides individual deprivation.** A society can have high average utility with substantial individual misery. The aggregate metric obscures the specific lives in which capabilities are denied.

**Utility is the wrong unit.** What people prefer or feel about their condition depends on what they have been led to expect. People in long-standing deprivation often report relatively high subjective well-being because their expectations have adapted (the "adaptive preferences" critique). Using their reported utility as the measure of justice ratifies the deprivation.

**Utility-based reasoning makes tradeoffs that capabilities reasoning forbids.** Sufficient utility gains in some dimensions can compensate for utility losses in others. But the capabilities approach insists that the threshold on each capability must be met — bodily integrity cannot be traded against income, even if the latter rises more than the former falls.

**The framework cannot adequately handle non-utility goods.** Justice, freedom, equality, dignity have value that is not reducible to the utility they produce. Utilitarianism either denies their irreducible value or smuggles in non-utility considerations under the utility label.

**The framework treats animals and future generations awkwardly.** Utility comparisons across species, across time, across very different situations of life face severe difficulties. The capabilities framework, grounded in dignity and species-appropriate flourishing, handles these more naturally.

**Cost-benefit analysis inherits the problems.** When applied to development decisions, cost-benefit reasoning aggregates monetary values that proxy for utility, inheriting the philosophical problems. It systematically undervalues capabilities that are difficult to monetise (dignity, affiliation, practical reason) and overvalues those that are easy to monetise.

**The defensible role of aggregate measures.** Some aggregate measures (GDP, HDI, poverty rates) are useful for comparative and trend analysis. The argument is not against any aggregation but against treating utility-maximisation as the foundation of justice. Aggregate measures can supplement capability assessment; they cannot replace it.

## How to Apply

1. **Resist utility-maximisation framings** in policy reasoning.
2. **Apply the capabilities threshold** rather than utility aggregation.
3. **Be alert to adaptive preferences** — reported satisfaction may not track real capability.
4. **Use cost-benefit analysis as instrumental tool, not foundation.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A South African infrastructure decision evaluated by cost-benefit analysis showing high net benefit.

**Application:** The capabilities lens: cost-benefit analysis aggregates monetary equivalents that proxy for utility. The framework asks instead: whose capabilities are advanced, whose are diminished, are thresholds maintained for each affected group? An infrastructure project with high aggregate net benefit can still produce capability deprivation for specific communities (displacement, loss of bodily integrity in construction-phase violence, loss of affiliation in community disruption) that the cost-benefit hides. The capabilities supplement to cost-benefit is necessary.

**Result:** A decision framework that uses cost-benefit instrumentally but is grounded in capabilities-justice.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat all welfare-economics analysis as illegitimate.
**Why:** Aggregate measures have instrumental value. The critique is about their foundational status.

**Don't:** Use the capabilities framework to avoid difficult tradeoffs.
**Why:** Real tradeoffs exist; the framework asks how they should be handled, not whether they exist.
