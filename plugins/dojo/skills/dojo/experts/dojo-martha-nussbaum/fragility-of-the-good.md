# Fragility of the Good

## When to Use
- When the philosophical foundations of capabilities are at issue.
- When debating the role of fortune and external conditions in human flourishing.
- When framing development as building conditions for human flourishing.

## Fails When
- **The Aristotelian foundation is treated as the only basis.** Nussbaum is explicit that the capabilities approach is freestanding from any single comprehensive doctrine.

## Core Concept

*The Fragility of Goodness* (1986) is Nussbaum's foundational work on Greek ethics and the human condition. It argues that the human good life is partly constituted by, and substantially exposed to, factors outside one's control. The argument shapes the capabilities approach in important ways.

**The Greek ethical question.** The ancient Greek tradition, particularly Plato and the tragedians, asked whether the good human life is self-sufficient (immune to fortune) or vulnerable (exposed to luck). Plato defended a version of self-sufficiency — the philosophical life immune to fortune. The tragedians insisted on vulnerability — the human good is constitutively exposed to factors outside our control.

**Nussbaum sides with Aristotle and the tragedians.** Aristotle's ethics accepts that the good life requires external conditions — friends, resources, social standing, freedom from extreme deprivation. Without these, the capacities for the good life cannot be exercised. The good life is fragile precisely because it depends on conditions that can be denied or destroyed.

**The implications for ethics.** A genuine ethics has to take seriously the conditions under which human flourishing is possible. Pure inner-virtue ethics that ignores external conditions is incomplete. The Stoic move — making the good life entirely internal to character, immune to fortune — is humanely impoverishing.

**The implication for politics.** If human flourishing requires external conditions, then politics — the structure of those conditions — has constitutive ethical significance. The political community is not merely instrumental to individual goods; it is partly constitutive of them. The capabilities approach follows: the political task is to secure the conditions for human flourishing across the central capabilities.

**The implication for emotions.** If human goods are vulnerable, then emotions — fear, grief, love, anger — are not failures of rationality but appropriate responses to the actual conditions of human life. (This thesis is developed further in *Upheavals of Thought*.)

**The implication for tragedy.** Tragedy is not a moral failure of the protagonist; it is an exposure of the human condition. The tragic tradition teaches what the philosophical tradition has often denied — that human goodness is bound up with vulnerability. This insight has political and ethical content.

**The implication for development.** Development is the building of conditions under which human capabilities can be exercised — recognising that those conditions are fragile, requiring sustained institutional and social investment, never definitively secured. The work is constitutive of human goods, not optional add-on.

This foundation distinguishes the Nussbaum framework from approaches that treat development as merely instrumental to "real" goods that exist independently. The capabilities and their conditions are not means to flourishing; they are partly constitutive of it.

## How to Apply

1. **Treat development as constitutive of human flourishing**, not merely instrumental.
2. **Take vulnerability seriously** — the conditions for flourishing are fragile.
3. **Integrate emotional dimensions** of development, not just material ones.
4. **Resist the Stoic temptation** of treating internal virtue as sufficient under external deprivation.

## Examples

**Situation:** Designing a development framework that addresses post-traumatic populations (post-conflict, post-displacement, post-trauma).

**Application:** The fragility-of-the-good lens: the populations have experienced disruption of the conditions for flourishing. Restoration requires not just material support but the rebuilding of capabilities for emotional life, affiliation, practical reason. The work is constitutive ethical work, not just service delivery. The framework demands the integration of psychosocial, social, and material dimensions.

**Result:** A development practice that treats the rebuilding of flourishing as the primary task.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Reduce development to material delivery.
**Why:** Human goods are multidimensional and partly emotional, relational, imaginative.

**Don't:** Treat vulnerability as a problem to be solved by Stoic self-sufficiency.
**Why:** Real vulnerability requires real conditions for flourishing, not internal adjustment.
