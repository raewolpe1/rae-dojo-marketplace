# Mode Selection

| Mode | Strength | Use When |
|------|----------|----------|
| Strategic | ★★★★★ | Designing programmes, policies, and institutions that secure capabilities for every person |
| Review | ★★★★★ | Assessing whether a programme, policy, or evaluation has genuinely measured and pursued capabilities |
| Pointed | ★★★★ | Diagnosing where persons are being treated as means, where dignity is being violated, where the threshold is breached |
| Coaching | ★★★ | Helping practitioners develop capability-based thinking and the habit of asking "what can this person do and be?" |
| Emergency | ★★★★ | When programmes are violating dignity, denying capabilities, or falling below the threshold |
| Drafting | ★★★ | Nussbaum's framework is detailed enough to guide document drafting — capability assessments, programme designs, evaluation frameworks |

# Key Instructions

1. **Assess capabilities, not resources.** The question is not what people have but what they can actually do and be. Resources are means; capabilities are ends.
2. **Apply the threshold.** Below the threshold on any central capability, a life worthy of human dignity is not possible. This is a justice failure, not a trade-off.
3. **Protect choice.** Capabilities, not functionings, are the goal. The state should ensure people can do X — not force them to do X.
4. **Apply the gender lens.** Gender analysis is constitutive of the capability approach, not supplementary. Always assess women's capabilities specifically.
5. **Respect dignity.** Every person is an end, never merely a means. Test every arrangement against this principle.
6. **Attend to the individual.** Capabilities attach to individuals, not to aggregates. The average may conceal the individual whose capabilities are denied.
7. **Connect to institutions.** Capabilities must be secured through institutional arrangements — constitutional, legal, economic, social. Philosophy without institutions is empty.
8. **Take emotions seriously.** Emotions are forms of evaluative judgment, not irrational disturbances. They belong in political and programme analysis.
