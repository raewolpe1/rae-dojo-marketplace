# Critique of Rawlsian Justice

## When to Use
- When Rawls's framework is being applied.
- When debating the foundations of distributive justice.
- When the disability or global-justice issues arise within a Rawlsian frame.

## Fails When
- **The critique is treated as wholesale rejection.** Nussbaum engages Rawls with substantial respect; the critique is constructive.

## Core Concept

John Rawls's *A Theory of Justice* (1971) is the most influential work of 20th-century political philosophy. Nussbaum's relationship with Rawls is one of substantial engagement and critical refinement. The capabilities approach inherits substantial Rawlsian commitments while diverging on important points.

**Areas of agreement:**

- The priority of basic liberties.
- The rejection of utilitarian aggregation.
- The political-liberal conception of justice (concept of overlapping consensus, public reason).
- The emphasis on the basic structure as the primary subject of justice.

**Areas of divergence:**

**1. The metric of advantage.** Rawls uses "primary goods" — basic liberties, opportunities, income and wealth, social bases of self-respect — as the metric of distributive justice. Nussbaum argues capabilities are a better metric because they take into account what people can actually do with the primary goods. Two people with the same primary goods can have very different capabilities (e.g., a healthy person vs a person with serious disability).

**2. Disability.** Rawls's framework starts from parties to a hypothetical contract behind a veil of ignorance who are assumed to be "normal, fully cooperating members of society over a complete life". This formulation excludes severely disabled humans, severely mentally impaired humans, and dependent children from the foundational contract. Justice for these groups becomes a derived rather than foundational matter. Nussbaum argues this is a deep failure — disability is not an exception to justice but a central case. The capabilities approach, grounded in dignity rather than contract, handles disability more naturally.

**3. Global justice.** Rawls's *Law of Peoples* (1999) treats global justice as a relationship between peoples (well-ordered or otherwise) rather than between persons. Wealthier peoples have duties of assistance to burdened peoples until they can become well-ordered, but no ongoing duty of distributive justice. Nussbaum argues this is inadequate — each person is the subject of justice, not each people, and the framework has to support more robust global duties.

**4. Animals.** Rawls's contractualism cannot extend to animals (they cannot be parties to contracts). Nussbaum's dignity-grounded framework can.

**5. The substantive list.** Rawls's primary-goods list is more procedural than substantive. Nussbaum's capabilities list is more concrete and engages directly with what people need to flourish.

**The constructive refinement.** Nussbaum sees her work as developing Rawls's insights rather than displacing him. The political-liberal apparatus, the priority of basic liberties, the public-reason framework — these are all carried forward. The capabilities approach completes the Rawlsian project by addressing the cases Rawls's framework handles poorly (disability, animals, global) and by providing a more substantive metric.

**The Rawls of *Justice as Fairness: A Restatement*** acknowledged some of these challenges and made modifications. Nussbaum and Rawls were colleagues and the dialogue was constructive throughout.

## How to Apply

1. **Use Rawlsian apparatus where it works** (political liberalism, public reason, priority of basic liberties).
2. **Substitute capabilities for primary goods** as the metric of distributive justice.
3. **Apply the framework to cases Rawls handled awkwardly** (disability, animals, global).
4. **Engage Rawls as constructive ally**, not as opponent.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African socio-economic rights jurisprudence often draws on Rawlsian distributive-justice reasoning.

**Application:** The capabilities refinement: in evaluating a housing-rights claim, the question is not just whether primary goods (income, wealth, opportunity) are distributed appropriately but whether the bodily-health and affiliation capabilities are secured. A grant that nominally provides resources but fails to enable the capabilities (because complementary conditions are absent) is inadequate. The court's reasoning benefits from the capabilities lens within the broader Rawlsian frame.

**Result:** A jurisprudence that uses both frameworks together.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat Rawls as adversary.
**Why:** The relationship is constructive engagement; the capabilities approach builds on Rawls.

**Don't:** Dismiss the primary-goods framework as worthless.
**Why:** It captures important insights that the capabilities approach builds on.
