# Disability and Justice

## When to Use
- When disability policy is being designed or evaluated.
- When the foundations of justice and disability are at issue.
- When the framework needs to be applied to cases the Rawlsian frame handles awkwardly.

## Fails When
- **The framework is reduced to legal-rights compliance.** Nussbaum's argument is more demanding — capabilities for each person with disability, not just non-discrimination.

## Core Concept

*Frontiers of Justice* (2006) places disability at the centre of justice theory. The argument:

**Disability is not an exception to justice but a central case.** Standard contractualist frameworks (Rawls's) start from parties to a hypothetical contract assumed to be "normal, fully cooperating members of society over a complete life". This formulation marginalises severely disabled humans. Nussbaum argues this is a deep failure — disability is a feature of human life that justice has to handle, not an exception.

**The capabilities approach handles disability naturally.** Because the framework is grounded in dignity (which each human has equally, regardless of capacity) rather than contract (which requires capacity), severely disabled humans are full subjects of justice. Their capabilities — appropriately specified for their condition — are claims of justice.

**Species-appropriate capabilities.** The framework distinguishes between the central capabilities at the species level and the specific way they can be exercised by individuals with particular conditions. A person with severe cognitive impairment may exercise practical reason in ways different from the typical human; the capability claim remains.

**The role of care.** Severely disabled people often require substantial care across their lives. The framework grounds robust obligations on society to provide such care without using it as grounds for diminishing the person's standing as a subject of justice. The carers' own capabilities also matter — care work has frequently been gendered and undervalued.

**Inclusion in mainstream life.** Disability policy should aim at integration into the mainstream of community life, not segregation. People with disabilities have capabilities of affiliation, practical reason, control over environment that require institutional supports — accessible environments, inclusive education, supported decision-making, accommodated employment.

**The legal-rights frame is insufficient.** Anti-discrimination law is necessary but not sufficient. The framework demands positive provision of the supports that enable capabilities — public investment, not just legal protection.

**The South African context.** The country has substantial constitutional protection (Section 9 equality clause) and policy framework (White Paper on Rights of Persons with Disabilities) but implementation is partial. Education for children with disabilities, employment, accessible environments, support services — all are substantially under-delivered. The capabilities framework demands a more substantial response.

**The intersection with other capabilities.** Disability often intersects with gender (women with disabilities face compounded capability deprivation), poverty (poverty causes and intensifies disability), and other dimensions. The framework supports intersectional analysis without abandoning the universal claims.

**The end-of-life dimension.** Severe cognitive decline at end of life raises questions the framework engages — what dignity requires for people whose capabilities are diminished, what care society owes, how to balance autonomy with the dependency of late life.

**The framework as guide.** Disability policy on the capabilities framework is more demanding than current policy in most countries. It requires substantial public investment, sustained institutional development, and the integration of disability across all policy domains.

## How to Apply

1. **Treat disability as central case of justice**, not exception.
2. **Apply the capabilities framework with species-and-individual appropriate specification.**
3. **Demand positive provision of supports**, not just legal anti-discrimination.
4. **Address intersectional dimensions** of disability with gender, poverty, age.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African basic-education provision for learners with disabilities.

**Application:** The framework demands: inclusion in mainstream schools where appropriate, with adequate support; specialised institutions where mainstream cannot serve; transport, assistive technology, support staff; teacher training in inclusive pedagogy; curriculum that engages the senses-imagination-thought capability of each learner. Current South African provision is substantially inadequate on most of these. The framework specifies the gap as a matter of justice, not policy oversight.

**Result:** An education-policy framing grounded in disability-justice.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat disability provision as charity.
**Why:** It is a matter of justice, owed regardless of public sympathy.

**Don't:** Confine disability to legal-rights frame.
**Why:** Anti-discrimination is necessary but does not deliver capabilities. Positive provision is required.
