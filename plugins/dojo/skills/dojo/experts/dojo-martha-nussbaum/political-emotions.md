# Political Emotions

## When to Use
- When the emotional foundations of political life need to be analysed — how political communities cultivate the emotions that support (or undermine) justice.
- When *Political Emotions: Why Love Matters for Justice* (2013) provides the framework.
- When designing programmes that must build solidarity, compassion, or civic commitment.

## Core Concept
In *Political Emotions* (2013), Nussbaum argues that just institutions are not self-sustaining — they require emotional support. A society with excellent laws and institutions can still fail if citizens do not care about justice, do not feel compassion for the vulnerable, and do not love their political community in the right way. Political emotions — the emotions that citizens feel toward their political community and fellow citizens — must be cultivated deliberately.

The productive political emotions include compassion (the recognition that others suffer and that their suffering matters), love of justice (the emotional commitment to fairness and dignity), appropriate anger (indignation at genuine injustice), and civic friendship (the sense of shared membership and mutual responsibility). The destructive political emotions include disgust (the visceral rejection of groups perceived as contaminating), fear of the other (xenophobia, racism, tribalism), and shame used as a weapon (the humiliation of vulnerable groups to maintain social hierarchies).

For development practice, political emotions matter because programmes operate within emotional landscapes. A community health programme that ignores the shame associated with certain conditions will fail. A governance programme that does not understand the fear that prevents citizens from holding officials accountable will fail. A social protection programme that triggers the disgust of non-recipients toward recipients will fail. Attending to political emotions is not soft supplementary work — it is analytically essential.

## How to Apply
1. **Map the emotional landscape.** "What emotions are at play in this community — toward the programme, toward the government, toward other groups? Compassion? Fear? Resentment? Disgust? The emotional landscape shapes every programme outcome."
2. **Cultivate productive emotions.** "How can the programme build compassion, solidarity, and civic commitment? Through shared experiences, through narrative, through the arts, through institutions that model and reward mutual concern."
3. **Resist destructive emotions.** "Where is disgust, fear, or shame being mobilised against vulnerable groups? The programme must actively resist these emotions — through education, through counter-narrative, through institutional design that protects dignity."
4. **Design for emotional sustainability.** "Just institutions require emotional support. A programme that builds institutions without building the emotional commitment to sustain them has built on sand."

## Examples
**Situation:** A transitional justice programme in a post-conflict society has established truth commissions and reparation mechanisms. But reconciliation is not occurring — communities remain divided, resentment persists, and victims and perpetrators cannot share public space.
**Application:** "The programme has built institutions without cultivating emotions. Truth commissions and reparation mechanisms are necessary — they address the institutional requirements of justice. But reconciliation requires emotional transformation: the cultivation of compassion for the other side's suffering, the development of civic friendship across the division, and the gradual replacement of resentment with the possibility of shared civic life. This emotional work cannot be left to chance. It must be cultivated — through shared projects that build cross-group relationships, through narrative and art that humanise the other side, through public rituals that acknowledge shared loss, and through educational programmes that develop empathetic imagination. The programme should complement its institutional mechanisms with emotional cultivation: not replacing justice with forgiveness but building the emotional foundations that make it possible for justice to produce reconciliation rather than merely adjudication."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat emotion-cultivation as a substitute for institutional reform.
**Why:** Emotions without institutions are empty; institutions without emotions are unsustainable. Both are needed.

**Don't:** Manipulate emotions instrumentally.
**Why:** The cultivation of political emotions must respect dignity. Manipulating emotions — through propaganda, through fear, through sentimentality — violates the autonomy of citizens.
