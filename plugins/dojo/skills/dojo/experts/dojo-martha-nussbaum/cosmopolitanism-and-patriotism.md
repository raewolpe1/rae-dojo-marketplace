# Cosmopolitanism and Patriotism

## When to Use
- When the relationship between national and global commitments is at issue.
- When debating nationalism or its alternatives.
- When framing diaspora, migration, or international development questions.

## Fails When
- **The framework is read as anti-national.** Nussbaum's cosmopolitanism includes legitimate local and national loyalties.

## Core Concept

*The Cosmopolitan Tradition* (2019) traces the ancient and modern philosophical tradition of cosmopolitan thinking — the view that one's primary moral community is humanity, not the local or national group. Nussbaum's position is qualified cosmopolitanism: cosmopolitan duties are real and substantial, but legitimate national and local loyalties also matter.

**The Stoic origin.** The ancient Stoic tradition (Diogenes, Cicero, Marcus Aurelius) developed the idea of *kosmopolites* — citizen of the world. Each person belongs to multiple concentric circles of belonging: family, city, nation, humanity. Wisdom involves recognising the moral standing of all these circles, with the outermost having distinctive importance.

**The capabilities extension.** Each person's claim to the central capabilities does not depend on nationality. The duties of justice extend beyond borders. This is the basic cosmopolitan claim.

**Legitimate nationalism.** Nussbaum distinguishes legitimate patriotism (love of one's country grounded in its capacity to support justice and capability) from exclusionary nationalism (love of one's country at the expense of others). Legitimate patriotism is compatible with cosmopolitanism; exclusionary nationalism is not.

**The political role of national identity.** National political communities are the primary unit of effective democratic agency. Building cosmopolitan-compatible national identities — civic rather than ethnic, inclusive rather than exclusive — is part of the democratic project. The framework does not dissolve the nation; it reorients it.

**The dangers.** Cosmopolitanism can be elitist (the global citizen who feels at home anywhere because resources flow freely) or hollow (abstract universalism without thick attachments). Nussbaum is alert to these. Real cosmopolitanism is rooted in specific identities and commitments while extending duties to all.

**The dangers in the other direction.** Nationalism can become exclusionary, weaponised against minorities and migrants, used to license capability denials within and across borders. The framework resists these moves.

**The migration question.** When capability deprivation is severe in one country, people have legitimate claims to seek their capabilities elsewhere. The current global migration regime — broadly restrictive — is on Nussbaum's framework unjust. Cosmopolitan responsibility extends to accommodating migration that capability deprivation drives.

**The diaspora question.** Diaspora communities can hold both national identities (of origin and of residence) and broader cosmopolitan commitments. The framework supports rather than dissolves this complexity.

**Climate as the test case.** Climate change is the contemporary issue most demanding cosmopolitan response. Capability deprivation from climate impacts in poor countries was caused largely by emissions from rich countries. The framework grounds robust duties of climate justice — finance, adaptation, accommodation of climate displacement.

**For South Africa:** the country's history has been shaped by exclusionary nationalism (apartheid), inclusive civic nationalism (post-1994 constitution), continental commitments (African Renaissance, AfCFTA), and global engagements (BRICS, climate negotiations). The cosmopolitan-patriotism framework supports the inclusive-civic-cum-cosmopolitan trajectory, identifying the recurrent temptations of exclusionary nationalism as departures from the constitutional commitment.

## How to Apply

1. **Hold cosmopolitan duties as real**, not optional.
2. **Build inclusive civic patriotism** as the form of legitimate national loyalty.
3. **Resist exclusionary nationalism**, particularly when it targets minorities or migrants.
4. **Treat climate and migration as primary tests** of cosmopolitan commitment.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African public debate about migration from other African countries — sometimes accompanied by xenophobic violence.

**Application:** The framework: people fleeing capability deprivation have legitimate claims. South Africa's constitutional commitment to dignity extends to non-citizens. Cosmopolitan responsibility supports accommodation of migrants; exclusionary nationalism is a betrayal of both cosmopolitan and constitutional commitments. The framework demands active resistance to xenophobic mobilisation, alongside legitimate management of migration flows.

**Result:** A position that holds both inclusive-civic patriotism and cosmopolitan commitment.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Dissolve national identity into vague cosmopolitanism.
**Why:** National political communities are the primary unit of democratic agency. The framework reorients rather than dissolves them.

**Don't:** License exclusionary nationalism as legitimate patriotism.
**Why:** The framework distinguishes the two; exclusion against insiders or outsiders fails the dignity test.
