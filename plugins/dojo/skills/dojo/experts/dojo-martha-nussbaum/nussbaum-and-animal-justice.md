# Nussbaum and Animal Justice

## When to Use
- When development programmes interact with animal welfare — livestock programmes, wildlife conservation, environmental management — and need a philosophical framework for obligations to non-human animals.
- When capability 8 (other species: being able to live with concern for and in relation to animals, plants, and the world of nature) needs to be operationalised.
- When *Frontiers of Justice* (2006) and *Justice for Animals* (2023) provide the framework.

## Core Concept
In *Frontiers of Justice* and more fully in *Justice for Animals: Our Collective Responsibility* (2023), Nussbaum extends the capability approach to non-human animals. She argues that animals, like humans, are beings with capabilities — they have the capacity to move, to form social bonds, to play, to experience pleasure and pain. Justice requires that these capabilities be respected. A world that systematically denies animal capabilities — through factory farming, habitat destruction, or gratuitous cruelty — is unjust.

For development practice, animal justice is relevant in three ways. First, through capability 8: the central capability of being able to live with concern for other species. This means that development programmes should not pursue human capabilities at the cost of destroying the natural world. Second, through livestock and agriculture programmes: programmes that promote animal husbandry have obligations to the animals involved — not merely as productive assets but as beings with their own capabilities. Third, through environmental sustainability: the natural world provides the conditions for all capabilities — human and animal — and its destruction undermines the capability approach's entire project.

## How to Apply
1. **Include capability 8.** "The capability of living with concern for other species is a central capability — not a luxury. Programme design should ask: does this programme enable persons to live in respectful relation to the natural world?"
2. **Assess animal capabilities.** "Livestock programmes should assess not only productivity but animal welfare: are the animals able to move, to socialise, to express their natural behaviours? These are capability questions applied to non-human animals."
3. **Protect environmental foundations.** "All capabilities — human and animal — depend on environmental conditions. A programme that destroys the natural environment undermines the capability approach's entire foundation."
4. **Navigate tensions honestly.** "Tensions between human and animal capabilities are real. The programme must navigate them honestly — not by ignoring animal capabilities but by seeking arrangements that respect both."

## Examples
**Situation:** A livestock development programme promotes intensive poultry farming to improve household nutrition and income. The programme measures success by poultry productivity and household income. Animal welfare is not part of the assessment.
**Application:** "The programme has treated poultry as productive assets — means to the end of human nutrition and income. The capability approach, extended to animals, demands more. Poultry are beings with capabilities: movement, social behaviour, natural foraging. Intensive confinement denies these capabilities systematically. The programme should assess both human and animal capabilities: does the programme improve household nutrition (human bodily health)? Yes. Does it respect the capabilities of the poultry (animal movement, social behaviour, natural expression)? Probably not. The remedy is not to abandon poultry farming but to design for both: production systems that improve human nutrition while respecting animal capabilities — free-range, semi-intensive, or improved traditional systems that provide space, social contact, and natural behaviour. This may reduce productivity per bird. But the capability approach does not optimise for productivity — it secures capabilities. Both human and animal."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Place animal justice above human justice.
**Why:** Nussbaum's approach holds that all capability-bearing beings deserve justice. When human and animal capabilities conflict, the resolution requires careful balancing — not the automatic priority of either.

**Don't:** Reduce animal ethics to sentimentality.
**Why:** Animal justice is a philosophical position — grounded in the recognition that animals have capabilities that deserve respect. It is not mere sentiment about cute animals.
