# Development as Freedom

## When to Use

- When a client or funder equates development with GDP growth and you need to broaden the evaluative frame.
- When designing or reviewing a programme theory of change that specifies development outcomes — to ensure the outcomes capture freedoms, not merely income or output.
- When advising a government or development agency on what "success" should mean for a strategy or programme.
- When someone argues that growth must come first and freedoms later — the "Lee thesis" that authoritarian governance is a prerequisite for development.

## Fails When

- **You need operational specifics.** Development as freedom is a conceptual framework, not an implementation manual. It tells you what to aim for, not how to build the irrigation system.
- **You treat it as merely rhetorical.** The claim that development is freedom is not a metaphor. It has concrete evaluative implications — it changes what you measure, what you prioritise, and how you judge success.
- **You use it to avoid hard choices.** "Expand all freedoms" is not a policy. The framework requires public reasoning about which freedoms to prioritise when resources are scarce.

## Core Concept

Development as freedom is the thesis that development should be understood as the expansion of the substantive freedoms that people have reason to value, and correspondingly, that the major sources of unfreedom — poverty, tyranny, poor economic opportunities, systematic social deprivation, neglect of public facilities, intolerance or overactivity of repressive states — are what development must remove.

This reframes development in two fundamental ways. First, freedom is the primary end of development. The purpose of development is not to maximise GDP but to enable people to lead lives they have reason to value. Second, freedom is the principal means of development. Free agency — political participation, economic initiative, social opportunity — is both what development aims at and the engine that drives it. A free press prevents famines. Women's agency reduces child mortality. Political participation shapes public priorities. These are not side-effects of development; they are the mechanisms through which development occurs.

Five distinct types of instrumental freedom interact and reinforce each other: political freedoms (democratic rights, free press), economic facilities (opportunities for production and exchange), social opportunities (education, healthcare), transparency guarantees (openness, trust), and protective security (social safety nets). No single type is sufficient; their interconnection is what gives the framework its power.

## How to Apply

1. **Reframe the evaluative question.** Replace "has income increased?" with "have substantive freedoms expanded?" This is not additive — it changes the entire assessment.
2. **Identify the relevant unfreedoms.** For any population or programme, specify which unfreedoms constrain people's lives: lack of healthcare, political voicelessness, gender discrimination, inadequate education, exposure to violence.
3. **Assess both ends and means.** Check whether the programme or policy expands freedoms constitutively (as outcomes worth having in themselves) and instrumentally (as enablers of further freedom).
4. **Attend to interconnection.** A programme that improves economic facilities while suppressing political freedoms is not developing fully. The instrumental freedoms reinforce each other; removing one weakens the others.

## Examples

**Situation:** A South African provincial government claims strong development performance based on 4% economic growth and declining official unemployment.
**Application:** The freedom framework asks: have substantive freedoms expanded? Growth has occurred, but life expectancy has stagnated, educational quality has deteriorated (as measured by learning outcomes, not enrolment), violent crime constrains freedom of movement particularly for women, and service delivery protests indicate failures in political voice and transparency. Income growth without freedom expansion is an incomplete — and potentially misleading — development narrative.
**Result:** The assessment should supplement income and employment data with direct measures of health capabilities, educational capabilities, personal security, and effective political participation.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat "development as freedom" as a slogan that adds rhetorical weight to an otherwise unchanged evaluation.
**Why:** The framework demands substantive changes in what you measure and how you judge. If your evaluation still uses only income and employment as final outcomes, you have not adopted the framework — you have merely cited it.

**Don't:** Argue that freedoms can only be expanded after growth has been achieved.
**Why:** This reverses the relationship. Growth is one of the means to freedom, not a prerequisite for it. Moreover, freedoms — especially political freedoms and women's agency — are themselves drivers of growth and human development. The empirical evidence does not support the sequencing argument.
