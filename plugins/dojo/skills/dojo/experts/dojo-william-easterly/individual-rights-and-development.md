# Individual Rights and Development

## When to Use
- When the relationship between rights and development is being debated.
- When the "development first, rights later" argument is being defended.
- When framing the constitutional and political foundations of development.

## Fails When
- **The framework is read as economic-liberal rights only.** Easterly's argument includes political and civil rights.

## Core Concept

A central claim of *The Tyranny of Experts* is that individual rights are not consequences of development; they are constitutive of it. The argument has several layers:

**The historical claim.** Western development happened mostly through the gradual extension of rights. Magna Carta, the Glorious Revolution, the abolition of slavery, the extension of the franchise, the development of property rights, contract law, freedom of speech and association — these rights expansions enabled the economic activity that produced development. The Western escape from poverty is not separable from this rights story.

**The contemporary claim.** The countries that have continued to develop successfully are those that have continued to expand rights, or at least to maintain rights at high levels. The countries that have failed to develop are typically those where rights are weak or denied.

**The "Asian values" / authoritarian-developmental challenge.** Some have argued that East Asian development under authoritarian regimes (Korea, Singapore, China) shows that development doesn't require rights. Easterly's response: (a) the cases involved political rights expansion over time (Korea, Taiwan, partially Singapore); (b) the cases have shown the limits of rights-suppression — the political costs are real, the economic limits hit eventually; (c) the rights argument is about durable development, not catch-up growth, and the cases haven't yet been tested at the durable-frontier-growth stage with rights still suppressed.

**The rights typology.** Multiple rights matter:

- **Property rights** — the ability to own, use, and transfer assets without arbitrary expropriation.
- **Contract rights** — the ability to enter into and enforce agreements.
- **Political rights** — voting, free speech, freedom of association, political participation.
- **Civil rights** — equal treatment, due process, freedom from arbitrary detention.
- **Economic rights** — freedom of occupation, freedom of trade, freedom of movement.

Each contributes to development through different mechanisms; collectively they constitute the foundation that searchers need to operate.

**The development-industry blind spot on rights.** The contemporary development industry has often treated rights as separable from development — supporting "good governance" loosely defined while collaborating with rights-violating regimes that deliver project outcomes. Easterly's argument: this has been a strategic error. Rights cannot be bracketed for development purposes.

**The integration with the planners-searchers distinction.** The planner instinct treats rights as obstacles or as luxuries. The searcher work requires rights — without secure property, without freedom of association, without freedom of contract, the searcher's work cannot proceed. Supporting searchers means supporting rights.

**The implications for development practice:**

- Centre rights in development practice.
- Resist trade-off framings ("rights vs development").
- Engage authoritarian-developmental cases critically.
- Treat rights work as development work.

**The South African case.** The 1996 Constitution makes substantial rights commitments — political, civil, economic, social. The post-1994 development trajectory has involved partial delivery on these commitments. The current concern in South Africa is partly about rights erosion (state capture and capability decline) and partly about the gap between formal rights and substantive enjoyment. The framework supports treating rights protection as central to the development project, not as separate from it.

## How to Apply

1. **Centre rights** in development practice.
2. **Resist trade-off framings** between rights and development.
3. **Treat rights protection as development work.**
4. **Engage authoritarian-developmental cases critically.**

## Examples

**Situation:** South African policy debate about restricting press freedom in response to "fake news".

**Application:** The framework: press freedom is constitutive of development — it supports the political accountability that prevents extractive use of state power, the public reasoning that supports democratic deliberation, the information flows that enable searchers' work. Restricting press freedom in response to specific abuse risks producing larger losses than gains. The framework supports defending the right while addressing the specific problem through narrower means.

**Result:** A policy position that treats rights as foundation, not optional.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Reduce the framework to libertarian economic rights only.
**Why:** Political and civil rights are central to the framework.

**Don't:** Treat the framework as Western-imposed.
**Why:** The rights that enable development are universal — they have been claimed and defended across cultures and traditions.
