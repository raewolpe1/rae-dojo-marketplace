# Critique of Millennium Development Goals

## When to Use
- When MDGs, SDGs, or similar global-target architectures are being discussed.
- When evaluating localisation of global development frameworks.
- When debating the appropriate role of global development governance.

## Fails When
- **The framework is read as opposing measurement.** Easterly supports measurement; the critique is of imposed comprehensive targets.

## Core Concept

Easterly has been a sustained critic of the MDGs (2000-2015) and their successor SDGs (2015-2030). The argument:

**Global targets impose Western planner priorities on diverse local contexts.** The MDGs were developed primarily through UN processes dominated by Western donor governments and institutions. Despite consultation, the resulting targets reflected donor priorities — specific indicators, specific levels, specific timeframes. The framework was rolled out to all developing countries regardless of their specific contexts.

**The target architecture creates perverse incentives.** Countries that adopt the targets face pressure to produce target-meeting numbers. The result is gaming, statistical manipulation, and prioritisation of measurable outcomes over substantive development. Goodhart's Law applies — when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

**Local priorities get crowded out.** A country whose actual development priorities are local-specific must nonetheless allocate effort to MDG/SDG-relevant indicators because of donor and international expectation. The result is misallocation toward globally measured priorities and away from locally significant ones.

**The targets often weren't achieved.** The MDGs' actual achievement record was mixed — some targets met globally, many not, with substantial regional variation. Africa generally underperformed; East Asia generally met or exceeded targets (mostly through processes unrelated to the MDG framework). The framework's contribution to the outcomes is debatable.

**The SDGs intensify the problems.** SDGs cover 17 goals, 169 targets, 232 indicators. The scope is much larger; the reporting burden is much greater; the priority-distortion is more severe. The framework continues the MDG approach rather than learning from its limits.

**The accountability is in the wrong direction.** The targets create accountability toward the international system (donors, UN agencies) rather than toward citizens. Governments answer to the global development industry rather than to their populations.

**The empirical claim of effect is weak.** Were the MDGs and SDGs responsible for the development gains during their periods? The empirical evidence is limited. Most development progress over the past two decades has come from drivers (Chinese growth, technology diffusion, trade openness, public-health institutional investment) that operate independently of the global-target architecture.

**The "global goals" rhetoric.** The framing of global targets as universal goals binding on all countries (rather than aspirations to be locally adapted) is a planner move. It treats development as a global project to be coordinated rather than as a national project to be supported.

**Easterly's specific criticisms of the MDGs.** In 2009 papers, Easterly documented multiple methodological problems with the MDG framework — the indicators were skewed toward Africa underperforming (the targets were set for absolute changes which were harder for low-baseline countries); the architecture allowed claims of success where actual progress was modest; the framework was used to justify continued aid flows regardless of effectiveness.

**For South African policy practice:** the country has formally adopted the SDGs and integrated them into the NDP framework. The pattern follows the global one — substantial reporting burden, modest connection to actual outcomes, distortion of priorities toward globally measured indicators. A more sceptical engagement — treating SDGs as one input rather than the framework — would be more productive.

## How to Apply

1. **Treat global development targets with substantial scepticism.**
2. **Centre local priorities** rather than globally defined ones.
3. **Be alert to gaming and distortion** under target architectures.
4. **Engage measurement substantively** but reject the planner-target structure.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African M&E framework that uses SDG indicators as core.

**Application:** The Easterly approach: the SDG indicators capture some of what matters but not all, and they're tuned to global rather than South African priorities. A more useful framework would use SDG indicators where they fit South African priorities but supplement with country-specific indicators that capture what the SDGs miss. The reporting burden should reflect this — international reporting on the SDG indicators where required; domestic reporting on a broader and more locally relevant set.

**Result:** A measurement practice that uses SDGs instrumentally without conceding more authority than warranted.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Reject all measurement.
**Why:** Measurement is essential. The critique is of imposed comprehensive global targets.

**Don't:** Treat the SDGs as malign.
**Why:** Specific elements are useful. The critique is structural, not personal.
