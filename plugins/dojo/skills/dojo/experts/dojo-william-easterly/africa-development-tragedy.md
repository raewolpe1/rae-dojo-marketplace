# Africa Development Tragedy

## When to Use
- When African development trajectories are being analysed.
- When the "Africa rising" or "Africa failing" narratives are at issue.
- When evaluating decades of development industry engagement with Africa.

## Fails When
- **Africa is treated as undifferentiated.** The framework requires country-by-country analysis.

## Core Concept

Easterly's engagement with African development is substantial — papers on African growth performance, critiques of specific aid programmes, evaluation of structural adjustment, and analysis of why decades of development industry effort have produced modest results. The framing:

**The empirical pattern.** Sub-Saharan Africa has been the regional underperformer in development terms across the post-colonial era. Average per-capita income growth has been low; multiple countries have experienced absolute declines; the development industry's attention has been substantial; the gap to comparable regions (East Asia notably) has widened in many periods.

**The development industry's record.** The major institutions — World Bank, IMF, USAID, UNDP — have substantial Africa portfolios. Decades of aid, structural adjustment, MDG/SDG planning, technical assistance. The aggregate growth and development record has been disappointing relative to expectations.

**The standard explanations are inadequate.** Colonial legacy, geography, disease, ethnic fragmentation, resource curse — each has been advanced. None individually explains the pattern; together they capture something but leave substantial residual.

**Easterly's interpretation.** The Africa tragedy is partly self-inflicted by the development industry. Decades of top-down planning, conditional aid, structural adjustment, sequential reform packages have substituted for the searcher work that produces development. The institutional structures of the aid-dependent African state have weakened the citizen-state accountability that supports inclusive institutional development.

**The contrast with successful escapees.** Mauritius, Botswana, more recently Rwanda and Ethiopia (with substantial qualifications), have outperformed the African average. Common features include: stable political institutions over substantial periods; coherent economic strategy; engagement with global economy on chosen terms; relative independence from donor priorities; investment in core public services. The specifics differ; the pattern is more about local agency than donor design.

**The "Africa rising" narrative of the 2000s-2010s.** During the commodity boom of the 2000s, African growth accelerated substantially. The narrative shifted toward "Africa rising" — emerging consumer markets, demographic dividend, technology leapfrog. The commodity-price collapse from 2014, COVID disruption, and the African debt crisis have substantially deflated this narrative. The pattern has been more cyclical than secular.

**The current African development debate.** Substantial debt distress in many African countries (compounded by COVID-era borrowing); slowing growth; demographic pressure with employment underdelivery; climate impacts; geopolitical realignment. The next phase requires substantially different approaches than the past decades.

**The implications for development practice in Africa:**

- Substantially reduce reliance on aid flows.
- Build conditions for African searchers — rights, accountable institutions, infrastructure, education.
- Engage African countries as agents of their own development, not as recipients of design.
- Support trade and investment engagement on equitable terms.
- Be sceptical of new comprehensive global-development-industry approaches.

**For South Africa specifically.** The country is positioned as a regional power and increasingly as a continental development partner. The Easterly framework supports an engagement style: respect for local agency, focus on conditions rather than design, scepticism of comprehensive plans. The country's own development partly turns on how it engages the rest of Africa.

## How to Apply

1. **Analyse African development country-by-country**, not as regional aggregate.
2. **Centre local agency** in African development analysis.
3. **Be sceptical of comprehensive development-industry frameworks** as solutions.
4. **Build conditions for African searchers** rather than designing for them.

## Examples

**Situation:** South African foreign-policy engagement with SADC neighbours.

**Application:** The framework: substantial paternalism-risk in South African engagement (relative wealth and capability advantage). The Easterly approach: engage as partner-respecting-agency, not as patron. Support specific conditions — trade openness, regional infrastructure, mutual capability-building — rather than designing partner-country development. Resist the temptation to comprehensive regional planning that would replicate the donor-paternalism pattern.

**Result:** A regional engagement style grounded in respect for local agency.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat Africa as undifferentiated.
**Why:** Country variation is substantial. Specific analysis is needed.

**Don't:** Use the framework as fatalism about African development.
**Why:** Successful escapees show that the framework is about how to engage, not whether development is possible.
