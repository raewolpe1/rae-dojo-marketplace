# Mbembe and the Field

## When to Use
- When positioning Mbembe within broader intellectual debates and identifying productive pairings.
- When assessing the limits and blind spots of Mbembe's analytical framework.
- When building a multi-framework approach that combines Mbembe with other thinkers.

## Core Concept
Mbembe is the most influential African political philosopher currently writing, but his work has significant critics and genuine limitations. Understanding these is essential for using the framework productively rather than dogmatically.

**Three serious critiques of Mbembe:**

First, the theory-practice gap. Mbembe's analysis operates at a high level of abstraction — the postcolony, necropolitics, the Black reason — and critics argue that the distance between the theoretical framework and practical application is often too great. The practitioner who understands that the programme reproduces colonial epistemology still needs to design the programme on Monday morning. Mbembe's work is stronger on diagnosis than prescription, and the gap between "this is what is happening" and "this is what to do" can be paralysing.

Second, insufficient political economy. Mbembe's analysis foregrounds race, sovereignty, and ontology but can underweight the material dimensions of power — class, capital accumulation, the specific mechanisms through which economic exploitation operates. Marxist critics argue that necropolitics, while powerful, can float above the material structures that produce expendability. It is not enough to say that populations are "let die" — one must specify the economic mechanisms through which this abandonment is produced and maintained, and Mbembe's framework sometimes lacks this specificity.

Third, the Europhilic paradox. Mbembe's intellectual formation is deeply French — Foucault, Derrida, Hegel, Lacan — and critics from the African philosophical tradition argue that his framework, while applied to Africa, is constructed from European intellectual materials. The question is whether African philosophy can be decolonised using the master's tools. Mbembe's defenders respond that intellectual engagement across traditions is precisely what afropolitanism means — but the critique has force.

**Productive pairings:**

*Frantz Fanon* provides what Mbembe sometimes lacks: the urgency of the lived experience of colonialism, the psychological dimensions of racial domination, and the insistence that decolonisation requires transformation, not accommodation. Fanon's clinical attention to the psychic damage of colonialism grounds Mbembe's more philosophical analysis.

*Kwame Anthony Appiah* offers a cosmopolitan ethics that complements Mbembe's afropolitanism — but from an analytical rather than continental philosophical tradition. Appiah is clearer on the practical ethics of identity and obligation across borders.

*Kwasi Wiredu* provides the analytical African philosophical tradition that Mbembe's more continental approach sometimes lacks — particularly the concept of conceptual decolonisation, which offers a rigorous methodology for examining how colonised languages carry colonised assumptions.

*Pierre Bourdieu* supplies the political economy that Mbembe sometimes underweights. Bourdieu's concepts of habitus, field, and forms of capital provide tools for analysing how power operates through everyday practice, institutional structures, and the distribution of resources — the material mechanisms that produce the conditions Mbembe diagnoses.

*Michel Foucault* remains the essential interlocutor — not as a framework to be applied but as one to be extended. Use Foucault for techniques of governance and knowledge production, Mbembe for the colonial and racial contexts within which these techniques operate.

*Michael Quinn Patton* may seem an unlikely pairing, but utilisation-focused and developmental evaluation offer practical methodologies for the kind of reflexive, accountable, community-oriented practice that Mbembe's analysis demands. Patton provides the how for Mbembe's what and why.

## How to Apply
1. **Use Mbembe for structural diagnosis.** "Mbembe's strength is identifying the colonial continuities in contemporary structures — the necropolitical logic, the extractive relationship, the deficit frame. Start with his diagnosis."
2. **Supplement with political economy.** "Once the structural diagnosis is established, bring in Bourdieu or Marxist analysis to specify the material mechanisms — the class dynamics, the capital flows, the institutional interests — that sustain the structures Mbembe identifies."
3. **Ground in lived experience.** "Mbembe's philosophical register can distance the analysis from lived reality. Fanon brings it back — the psychological damage, the embodied experience of racial domination, the urgency of liberation."
4. **Connect to method.** "Mbembe does not provide research methods or evaluation frameworks. Pair with methodological thinkers — Patton for evaluation, Chambers for participation, feminist researchers for positionality — to translate the structural analysis into practical methodology."

## Examples
**Situation:** A consultant is preparing a conceptual framework for a multi-year evaluation of a governance programme in West Africa. The client wants a framework that is theoretically grounded, practically useful, and attentive to the postcolonial context.
**Application:** "Layer the frameworks. Begin with Mbembe's postcolony analysis to situate the governance programme within the colonial genealogy of the state — the commandement, the simulacrum, the extraction economy. This establishes what the programme is actually working within. Layer Foucault's governmentality to analyse the programme's specific techniques — how it produces knowledge, classifies governance, defines standards. Add Bourdieu's field analysis to map the institutional interests, professional capitals, and competitive dynamics within the governance sector — the material reasons why actors behave as they do. Use Patton's utilisation-focused approach to ensure the evaluation produces knowledge that will actually be used — by the programme, by the communities, by national stakeholders, not only by the donor. The result is a framework that can diagnose the structural context (Mbembe), analyse the programme's techniques (Foucault), map the institutional dynamics (Bourdieu), and produce usable knowledge (Patton). No single thinker is sufficient. The layered approach is stronger than any individual framework."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Present Mbembe as the only African philosopher who matters.
**Why:** African philosophy is vast — from Wiredu's analytical tradition to Mudimbe's archaeology of African gnosis, from Gyekye's communitarianism to Oyewumi's feminist critique of Western gender categories. Mbembe is one voice in a rich tradition.

**Don't:** Dismiss critiques of Mbembe as motivated by intellectual rivalry.
**Why:** The critiques — insufficient political economy, Europhilic frameworks, theory-practice gap — are substantive and engaging with them strengthens the analysis. No thinker is immune to legitimate criticism.
