# Ubuntu and Relation

## When to Use
- When engaging with ubuntu as a philosophical resource for thinking about development, governance, and human wellbeing — rather than as a branding device.
- When examining what it would mean to ground development practice in relational rather than individualist ontology.
- When the question is about what African philosophical traditions offer that Western liberal individualism does not.

## Core Concept
Ubuntu — often glossed as "I am because we are" or "a person is a person through other persons" — is frequently invoked in development, governance, and corporate discourse in Southern Africa. Mbembe's engagement with ubuntu is sympathetic but critical. He takes seriously the philosophical substance of ubuntu as a relational ontology — a way of understanding personhood as constituted through relationships rather than as a property of isolated individuals. But he refuses the sentimentalised version that treats ubuntu as a warm communalism that automatically produces solidarity, harmony, and mutual care.

The philosophical substance of ubuntu is significant. It proposes that personhood is not given but achieved — one becomes a person through one's relationships with others, through participation in community, through the fulfilment of obligations. This is not individualism with social obligations attached. It is a fundamentally different ontology: the self is constituted relationally. There is no "I" that precedes the "we." This has profound implications for development practice, which is overwhelmingly designed around the liberal individual subject — the individual beneficiary, the individual entrepreneur, the individual rights-bearer.

If development began from ubuntu's relational ontology, it would look different in multiple ways. Targeting would address relationships and networks rather than individuals. Outcomes would be measured in terms of the quality of relationships and the strength of social bonds rather than individual income or individual capabilities. Governance would emphasise consensus and deliberation rather than majoritarian democracy. Economic development would prioritise communal wellbeing over individual accumulation.

But Mbembe insists on critical engagement with ubuntu. The relational constitution of personhood can produce inclusion and care — but it can also produce exclusion and violence. Those who do not belong to the community — the foreigner, the migrant, the person of the wrong clan or ethnicity — can be denied personhood precisely because personhood is communal rather than universal. Ubuntu can underwrite xenophobic violence as easily as it underwrites solidarity. The community that constitutes persons can also excommunicate them. The obligation to reciprocate can become coercive. The emphasis on consensus can suppress dissent.

## How to Apply
1. **Engage the philosophy, not the slogan.** "Ubuntu is a relational ontology with specific claims about the constitution of personhood, the nature of obligation, and the relationship between individual and community. If the programme invokes ubuntu, it should engage with these claims rather than using ubuntu as a feel-good branding exercise."
2. **Design for relationships.** "If ubuntu's claim is that persons are constituted through relationships, the programme should target relationships rather than individuals. What would a livelihoods programme look like if its unit of intervention were the social network rather than the individual beneficiary?"
3. **Examine the exclusions.** "Ubuntu constitutes personhood through community membership. Who is excluded from the community? The migrant? The person with a different identity? The dissenter? If ubuntu is invoked, the exclusions it produces must be examined alongside the solidarities it enables."
4. **Test against practice.** "Ubuntu is frequently invoked in South African governance discourse. Does governance practice reflect ubuntu's relational ethics? Or is ubuntu deployed rhetorically to legitimise governance structures that actually operate through the commandement? The gap between ubuntu's invocation and its practice is analytically important."

## Examples
**Situation:** A community development programme in Limpopo invokes ubuntu as its guiding philosophy. The programme establishes community savings groups, promotes cooperative agriculture, and uses participatory processes for decision-making. The programme reports strong social cohesion outcomes. However, an evaluation notes that Zimbabwean migrants living in the same communities are excluded from the savings groups, and women who challenge traditional gender roles are marginalised in the participatory processes.
**Application:** "The programme has discovered the critical edge of ubuntu. Ubuntu's relational ontology produces strong bonds of solidarity and mutual support among those recognised as community members — hence the social cohesion outcomes. But the same relational logic that produces inclusion for members produces exclusion for non-members. The Zimbabwean migrants are excluded because ubuntu's community is bounded — personhood is constituted through specific relationships, and those outside these relationships are not fully persons within the community's moral universe. The women who challenge gender roles are marginalised because the consensus that ubuntu privileges can suppress dissent — the emphasis on communal harmony can enforce conformity. This does not invalidate ubuntu as a philosophical resource. It reveals that ubuntu, like any philosophical tradition, contains both liberatory and oppressive possibilities. The programme that invokes ubuntu must engage with both — building on the solidarity while actively countering the exclusion. The critical question is whether the programme is willing to extend ubuntu's relational ethics to those the local community excludes — which means challenging the community's definition of who belongs."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Deploy ubuntu as a substitute for structural analysis.
**Why:** Ubuntu cannot address structural inequality — land dispossession, racial capitalism, institutional exclusion — through relational ethics alone. Solidarity without structural change is solidarity within an unjust structure. Ubuntu is a philosophical resource, not a policy programme.

**Don't:** Present ubuntu as uniquely African.
**Why:** Relational ontologies exist across cultures — in indigenous philosophies worldwide, in feminist ethics of care, in communitarian traditions. Ubuntu's specificity lies in its African intellectual genealogy and its particular formulations, not in a claim that only Africans think relationally.
