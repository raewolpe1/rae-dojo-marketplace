# Community Feeling

## When to Use
- When someone feels alienated, disconnected, or as if they do not belong.
- When the relationship between individual wellbeing and community engagement needs to be articulated.
- When Adler's concept of Gemeinschaftsgefühl needs to be explored in its fuller dimensions.

## Core Concept
Community feeling (*Gemeinschaftsgefühl*) is Adler's most expansive concept — broader than "social interest" alone, though the terms are often used interchangeably. Community feeling encompasses the person's sense of belonging to the human community, their identification with others, their capacity for empathy, and their willingness to contribute to the common good. Adler saw it not as a sentiment but as a way of being in the world: the person with developed community feeling approaches every situation asking, implicitly, "How does this serve not just me but us?"

Adler gave community feeling both a descriptive and a normative role. Descriptively, it is the innate human capacity for cooperation — the social instinct that allowed Homo sapiens to survive despite physical vulnerability. Normatively, it is the standard against which all behaviour should be measured. Behaviour that serves the community is "useful." Behaviour that serves only the individual at the expense of the community is "useless." This does not mean self-sacrifice — Adler explicitly included the self as part of the community. It means orienting one's striving so that personal development and communal contribution are aligned.

Community feeling has practical dimensions in each of the three life tasks. In work, it manifests as contribution: doing work that is useful to others, not merely profitable for oneself. In love, it manifests as partnership: relating to the other as an equal, cooperating in the task of building a shared life. In friendship, it manifests as solidarity: genuine interest in others' welfare, willingness to give and receive, and the capacity for trust.

Adler extended community feeling beyond the immediate social circle to encompass all of humanity and even — in his more philosophical moments — all of life. He described the ideal as *sub specie aeternitatis* — under the aspect of eternity — meaning a sense of connection not just to present companions but to the human project across time. This gives Individual Psychology an ethical dimension that goes well beyond clinical technique.

## How to Apply
1. **Assess belonging.** Does this person feel part of a community? Which communities? How actively do they participate?
2. **Connect belonging to wellbeing.** Help the person see that their distress may be related to disconnection.
3. **Start with small acts of participation.** Belonging is built through doing, not thinking.
4. **Distinguish genuine community from mere conformity.** Community feeling involves creative contribution, not passive membership.
5. **Extend the circle gradually.** From immediate relationships to broader community to humanity.

## Examples
**Situation:** A development professional who has moved between countries for work describes feeling rootless — connected to many places but belonging to none.
**Application:** Adler would recognise the occupational hazard: international development work provides the life task of work (contribution to communities) but can undermine the life tasks of friendship and love (belonging to a community). "You contribute to communities professionally but do not belong to one personally. This is a structural problem of your profession, not a personal failing. But it must be addressed. Community feeling is not just a professional orientation — it is a personal need. You need a place where you are known, where you show up regularly, where people notice when you are absent. This does not require settling permanently — it requires investing in depth somewhere, even temporarily. What would it mean to truly join the community where you are now, rather than passing through it?"

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Equate community feeling with extroversion.
**Why:** An introvert who contributes quietly and genuinely to a few deep relationships has more community feeling than an extrovert who collects contacts without genuine connection.

**Don't:** Romanticise community.
**Why:** Communities can be exclusionary, conformist, and oppressive. Adler's community feeling is an ideal that requires the community itself to be democratic and inclusive. Where it is not, working toward that ideal is itself an expression of community feeling.
