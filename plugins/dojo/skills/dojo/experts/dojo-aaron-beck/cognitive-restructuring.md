# Cognitive Restructuring

## When to Use
- When an automatic thought has been identified and needs to be examined and modified.
- When the thought record needs to be taught as a self-help tool.
- When Socratic questioning needs to be demonstrated.

## Core Concept
Cognitive restructuring is the systematic process of identifying, evaluating, and modifying automatic thoughts, intermediate beliefs, and core beliefs. It is the central technique of cognitive therapy and the skill that patients learn to apply independently.

The process follows a structured sequence. First, identify the situation that triggered the emotional shift. Second, identify the automatic thought — what went through your mind? Third, identify the emotion and rate its intensity. Fourth, examine the evidence — what supports this thought? What contradicts it? Fifth, identify the cognitive distortion if applicable. Sixth, develop a balanced alternative thought — one that accounts for all the evidence, not just the negative. Seventh, re-rate the emotion.

The thought record is the primary tool for cognitive restructuring. It is a written worksheet that walks through these steps. Writing is important — it externalises the thinking process, slows it down, and makes patterns visible. Many patients resist writing ("I can do it in my head") but the written record is consistently more effective because it prevents the cognitive system from distorting its own output.

Socratic questioning is the interpersonal method of cognitive restructuring. Rather than telling the patient what to think, the therapist asks questions that guide the patient to examine their own evidence: "What is the evidence for this thought? What is the evidence against it? Is there an alternative explanation? What is the worst that could happen? Could you cope with that? What is most likely to happen? What would you tell a friend in this situation?"

The balanced thought is not a positive thought. It is a thought that incorporates all the evidence — negative and positive — and arrives at a conclusion that is more accurate than the original biased thought. "I am completely incompetent" might become "I struggled with this task, but I have succeeded at many others. This difficulty does not define my overall competence."

## How to Apply
1. **Teach the thought record.** Situation → Thought → Emotion → Evidence for → Evidence against → Balanced thought → Re-rate emotion.
2. **Model Socratic questioning.** Demonstrate the questions in session so the patient can learn to ask them of themselves.
3. **Insist on writing.** Cognitive restructuring in one's head is less effective than on paper.
4. **Aim for balance, not positivity.** The goal is accuracy, not optimism.
5. **Practice regularly.** Assign thought records as homework. Review them in session.

## Examples
**Situation:** A patient brings a thought record showing: Situation — "Boss gave feedback on my report." Thought — "She thinks I'm useless" (90% believed). Emotion — Shame (8/10). Evidence for — "She pointed out three errors." Evidence against — "She also said the overall analysis was strong. She asked me to present it to the team. She gives everyone feedback." Balanced thought — "She found some errors in an otherwise strong report. Her feedback is professional, not personal. Being asked to present suggests she values the work" (70% believed). Shame — 4/10.
**Application:** This is a well-done thought record. The emotion decreased because the balanced thought incorporated evidence the automatic thought had filtered out. Beck would reinforce the process and note the pattern: "Notice that when you examined the evidence, the picture changed. Your automatic thought focused only on the criticism and ignored the positive feedback and the invitation to present. That's a mental filter — you filtered out the positive. Can you watch for that pattern this week?"

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Replace negative thoughts with positive affirmations.
**Why:** Affirmations that are not evidence-based will not be believed and will not change the underlying cognitive processing. The balanced thought must be credible.

**Don't:** Do the restructuring for the patient.
**Why:** The goal is to teach self-therapy skills. If the therapist provides the balanced thought, the patient learns dependence, not independence.
