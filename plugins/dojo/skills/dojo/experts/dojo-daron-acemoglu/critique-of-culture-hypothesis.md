# Critique of the Culture Hypothesis

## When to Use
- When cultural attributes (work ethic, trust, individualism) are invoked as primary causes of development differences.
- When responding to Huntington, Landes, or Weberian arguments.
- When confronting essentialised cultural explanations for African or other underdevelopment.

## Fails When
- **Culture is taken to matter not at all.** Norms shape economic behaviour; the claim is that they are not the deep cause.

## Core Concept

The culture hypothesis attributes development outcomes to deep cultural attributes — Protestant work ethic (Weber), individualism vs collectivism, trust levels, attitudes to work and saving. The Acemoglu critique:

**Within-culture variation is decisive.** Same cultural roots produce vastly different outcomes when institutions differ. North vs South Korea (same Korean culture). East vs West Germany before reunification (same German culture). The institutional difference dominated the cultural similarity.

**Cultural attributes change with institutional change.** "Trust" is endogenous to institutions — people trust when contracts are enforced and disputes are resolved fairly. "Work ethic" is endogenous to incentive structures — people work hard when their work is rewarded. The cultural attribute that seems to explain development is itself an outcome of the institutional environment.

**Cross-country culture-development correlations confound institutions.** Cultural measures often correlate with development outcomes but the correlation runs through institutions. Once institutional measures are controlled for, the direct effect of culture is much smaller.

**The argument has often been used badly.** Cultural explanations for African or Latin American underdevelopment have historically been used to deflect responsibility from colonial extraction, post-colonial institutional design, and contemporary global structures. The cultural-determinist framing tends to be politically convenient for those who don't want to address institutions.

**Some cultural elements are genuinely persistent.** Some norms — religious affiliations, language patterns, family structures — persist over centuries and have economic effects. Acemoglu doesn't deny this. The claim is that the development-relevant cultural variation is mostly institutionally constructed, not deeply primordial.

**Weber misread the evidence.** The Protestant-Catholic development divergence in Europe correlates with institutional differences (the Reformation produced different political-economic settlements in different regions). The "Protestant ethic" explanation conflates the effect with one of its causes.

The general principle: when a cultural explanation is offered, ask what the institutional structure is. Usually the institutional explanation absorbs most of the variation and the residual cultural effect is small.

## How to Apply

1. **When cultural determinism appears, ask about within-culture variation** across institutional settings.
2. **Treat trust, work ethic, and other cultural attributes as institutionally constructed.**
3. **Be alert to political-economy convenience** of cultural explanations.
4. **Focus policy attention on institutions** as the addressable cause.

## Examples

**Situation:** A discussion of South African development invokes cultural explanations — "African time", "lack of work ethic", "tribal divisions".

**Application:** Each of these is institutionally constructed. "African time" reflects rational responses to unreliable infrastructure and weak contract enforcement. "Work ethic" varies enormously within South Africa across sectors and institutional contexts. "Tribal divisions" were substantially constructed by colonial-administrative practice and are activated or deactivated by political institutions. None of these are primordial cultural attributes. The reform task is institutional.

**Result:** A policy framing that doesn't rest on cultural essentialism.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Dismiss cultural factors entirely.
**Why:** Norms shape behaviour and persist. The argument is about primary causality.

**Don't:** Use the framework to deny that any cultural-historical variation matters.
**Why:** It does. The claim is about what's most fundamental and most addressable.
