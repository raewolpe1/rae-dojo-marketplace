# Snyder Applied to African Democratic Backsliding

## When to Use
- When applying the tyranny and erosion framework to an African country or the continent's democratic trends.
- When an M&E, governance, or development engagement intersects with democratic backsliding.
- When a practitioner must decide how to work with, or whether to work with, a backsliding government.
- When the lessons need calibrating to African specifics rather than imported wholesale.

## Fails When
- **You homogenise the continent.** Botswana, Ghana, Senegal, Sudan, Zimbabwe, Rwanda, and South Africa have radically different trajectories. Aggregate-Africa statements mislead.
- **You import Western framings uncritically.** The American or European erosion story has specific features that do not transfer cleanly; the underlying mechanisms generalise, the surface particulars do not.

## Core Concept
Snyder's framework was built from European history and sharpened on American and Russian cases, but its core mechanisms are not culturally specific — they are about how power escapes accountability, and that is a universal problem. Applied to African contexts with appropriate calibration, the framework is highly useful, provided two disciplines are observed: never treat the continent as one case, and never import the surface features of the American story in place of the underlying mechanism.

**The mechanisms generalise; the particulars do not.** Anticipatory obedience, the normalisation of the abnormal, the legal-in-form capture of constraining institutions (electoral commissions, courts, auditors-general, public broadcasters), the manufacture of crisis as a pretext, and the assault on factuality are all observable across African democratic backsliding — in third-term constitutional manipulations, in the capture of electoral bodies, in the harassment of independent media and civil society, in the weaponisation of state institutions against opposition. What differs is the surface: the role of liberation-movement legitimacy, the specific colonial-institutional inheritance, the patterns of patronage, the place of traditional authority, the particular forms that media and information take. A practitioner uses Snyder for the mechanism and local knowledge for the particulars.

**The politics of inevitability and eternity have African forms.** The inevitability story in much African development discourse is the assumption that growth, urbanisation, and a rising middle class will automatically consolidate democracy — a complacency directly analogous to the Western "end of history." When that fails to materialise, the conditions for an eternity politics appear: the mobilisation of grievance (often legitimate grievance over colonialism, inequality, or external exploitation) into a future-less narrative in which the leader or party is the eternal defender of the wounded nation against enemies foreign and domestic, and in which failures are recast as the work of saboteurs. Liberation-movement parties are especially prone to a variant of eternity: the claim to permanent legitimacy as the bringer of freedom, which converts a historical achievement into a present entitlement to rule and treats opposition as betrayal of the nation itself.

**Statelessness and the destruction of institutions have acute African resonance.** Snyder's warning about the lethality of destroyed states is not abstract on a continent that has seen state collapse and the rightless zones it produces. The protection conferred by functioning institutions — citizenship, legal personhood, a working bureaucracy — is precisely what is at stake where states are hollowed or fail.

**The practitioner's position is the live question for Rae.** Working in M&E and economic development with municipalities and governments, a practitioner in a backsliding context faces Snyder's dilemmas directly. The most important is the one *On Tyranny* names: do not obey in advance, and defend the institution you are responsible for — usually the integrity of the evaluation, the audit, the data, the finding. A technically excellent M&E system can become the respectable face of a regime hollowing out accountability elsewhere; the practitioner must ask whether any independent institution remains to act on the findings, or whether the work flows into a system designed to absorb it without consequence. The defence of factuality — keeping the inconvenient finding, requiring suppression to be open and attributable — is the frontline act available to a development professional. And the corporeal-politics lesson translates into the defence of professional associations, audit institutions, and the networks of practitioners who can hold standards collectively rather than each capitulating alone.

**The Ukraine linkage is real, not abstract.** The Russo-Ukrainian war reshapes African development directly through grain and fertiliser supply, through redirected donor finance, and through the renewed contest for African alignment between Russia, China, and the West — a contest that includes the export of authoritarian information techniques and security partnerships. Democratic backsliding in Africa is not sealed off from the global authoritarian resurgence; it is connected to it through finance, arms, and information flows.

The calibration discipline is everything. Snyder himself would insist that the historian's tools must be applied to the specific case with local knowledge, not stamped onto a continent. The mechanisms are a lens; the African realities are what is seen through it.

## How to Apply
1. **Use the mechanism, supply local particulars.** Apply anticipatory obedience, capture, crisis-pretext, and the assault on truth — but read the surface through local knowledge.
2. **Disaggregate the continent.** Always specify the country and its trajectory; refuse aggregate-Africa claims.
3. **Watch for liberation-movement eternity.** The conversion of historical legitimacy into permanent entitlement, with opposition cast as betrayal.
4. **Centre the practitioner's institutional defence.** For M&E and governance work, the finding, the data, and the professional body are the institutions to defend; do not obey in advance.
5. **Trace the global linkages.** Connect local backsliding to the war's finance, grain, arms, and information effects.

## Examples
**Situation:** An evaluator in a backsliding state is invited to build a flagship M&E system for a government that has captured the electoral commission and pressured the courts.
**Application:** Ask Snyder's questions. Is there any independent institution left to act on the findings, or will they be absorbed without consequence — making the system the respectable face of unaccountable power? Locate the phase: capturing the electoral commission is not early backsliding. If the engagement proceeds, set real conditions (right to publish, data integrity, a predrawn line) before the relationship is warm, and do not pre-soften the work.
**Result:** The decision is made with eyes open, with a line drawn in the cold, rather than drifting into legitimation.

**Situation:** A ruling former-liberation party frames electoral defeat as a betrayal of the nation engineered by foreign-backed enemies.
**Application:** This is the eternity politics in its liberation-movement form: historical legitimacy converted into permanent entitlement, failure recast as sabotage, opposition delegitimised as treason. Naming the structure clarifies that this is not democratic competition but its negation, and that cataloguing the party's failures will not dislodge a grievance-based narrative.
**Result:** The dynamic is correctly diagnosed, and the response addresses the frame rather than chasing the facts.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat Africa as a single democratic context.
**Why:** Trajectories vary enormously; the country and its specifics must be named.

**Don't:** Import the American erosion story wholesale.
**Why:** The mechanisms generalise; the surface particulars (liberation legitimacy, colonial inheritance, patronage forms) are local and must be read locally.

**Don't:** Let technical excellence substitute for the question of legitimation.
**Why:** A captured regime may value a credible M&E system precisely to launder its hollowing of accountability; the practitioner must ask who can still act on the findings.
