# The Politics of Inevitability vs Eternity

## When to Use
- When diagnosing the underlying temporal logic of a political movement or leader.
- When someone asks why faith in inevitable progress collapsed into authoritarian nostalgia.
- When a politics offers only grievance and enemies, never a programme for the future.
- When teaching the central conceptual frame of *The Road to Unfreedom* (2018).

## Fails When
- **The question is about a specific mechanism.** Institutional capture, disinformation, and electoral manipulation are concrete; this frame is about the deeper relationship to time.
- **You reduce it to optimism vs pessimism.** Both inevitability and eternity are failures of politics, not moods. Neither is the healthy alternative.

## Core Concept
Snyder identifies two anti-political ways a society can relate to time, and argues that the second grows out of the collapse of the first.

**The politics of inevitability** is the belief that history has a single direction and that the good outcome is guaranteed, so that no real political choices need to be made. The dominant Western version after 1989 was the "end of history": free markets produce prosperity, prosperity produces democracy, democracy is the final destination, and the arc bends reliably toward it. This is seductive precisely because it relieves citizens of responsibility. If the future is on rails, vigilance is unnecessary, institutions can be taken for granted, and politics shrinks to administration. Inevitability is not a lie about facts so much as a lie about agency — it teaches people that nothing they do, or fail to do, can change the destination.

The problem is that the rails never existed. When the promised future fails to arrive — when the prosperity does not reach most people, when a financial crisis exposes the machinery, when inequality hardens — inevitability collapses. And because it had taught people that politics was unnecessary, it leaves them with no civic muscle, no habit of collective action, no sense that the future is theirs to build. Into that vacuum comes the second mode.

**The politics of eternity** is a politics with no future at all — only a recurring, mythic present. The nation is cast as an eternal, innocent victim, perpetually encircled by enemies within and without. There is no story of progress, no programme of improvement, because eternity has no concept of "better." The leader does not promise to make life better; he promises only to defend the wounded nation against its enemies, again and again, in a cycle that never resolves. Crucially, his failures cannot count against him, because in the logic of eternity, every failure is reinterpreted as fresh proof that the enemy is real and powerful. Corruption is not corruption; it is the nation surviving in a hostile world. A lost war is not a defeat; it is a betrayal by traitors. The leader's incompetence becomes evidence of the conspiracy arrayed against him.

Eternity feeds on manufactured nostalgia for a golden age that never existed, and on the management of grievance rather than the solving of problems. It is the temporal home of contemporary authoritarianism — Snyder reads Putin's Russia as its most developed example, and traces its export to Europe and the United States. It is deeply compatible with the assault on truth, because a politics of cyclical myth has no use for verifiable history; it needs a usable past, not an accurate one.

The recovery of freedom, for Snyder, requires recovering a third relationship to time: not the false guarantee of inevitability, not the closed cycle of eternity, but **the open present** — the sense that the future is genuinely undetermined and that what citizens do now, together, decides it. That is the temporal ground of actual politics. To live in it is to accept both responsibility and possibility: nothing is guaranteed, and therefore everything is at stake in what we choose.

## How to Apply
1. **Diagnose which mode a politics is in.** Is it promising an inevitable good future (complacency), or offering only an eternal defence against enemies (grievance)?
2. **Trace the transition.** If a society has shifted from inevitability to eternity, identify the broken promise that triggered the collapse.
3. **Test the leader's relationship to failure.** In eternity, failures become proof of the enemy. If criticism only strengthens the leader's victim narrative, you are in eternity.
4. **Recover the open present.** The constructive move is always to restore the sense that the future is contingent and choosable — that politics in the present tense is possible.

## Examples
**Situation:** Analysts are puzzled that a government's repeated economic failures seem to increase rather than erode its support.
**Application:** This is the signature of eternity. Within the grievance frame, failure is not the leader's fault but evidence that the nation's enemies are sabotaging it. No amount of bad outcomes will dislodge support, because outcomes are not the currency — the cycle of victimhood and defence is. Counter-messaging that simply lists failures will not work; it must break the frame itself by restoring a future-oriented story.
**Result:** Strategy shifts from "prove they failed" to "offer a credible future," which is what eternity cannot provide.

**Situation:** A development optimist insists that economic growth in a region will automatically produce stronger democracy.
**Application:** That is the politics of inevitability in development dress. Growth does not mechanically produce democracy; the link must be built through institutions and choices, and complacency about it leaves the door open for eternity if growth disappoints.
**Result:** The optimist's frame is replaced with one that treats democratic consolidation as contingent work, not a guaranteed byproduct.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Equate eternity with simple pessimism or inevitability with simple optimism.
**Why:** Both are failures of agency; the healthy alternative is the open present, which is neither.

**Don't:** Assume a grievance politics can be defeated by cataloguing the leader's failures.
**Why:** In eternity, failures feed the narrative. The frame, not the facts, has to be contested.

**Don't:** Treat inevitability as harmless because it is "positive."
**Why:** Its complacency is what disarms a society and clears the path for eternity.
