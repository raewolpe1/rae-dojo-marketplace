# The Russian Information War

## When to Use
- When the question is about the mechanics and aims of modern disinformation.
- When explaining "managed unreality" — the deliberate manufacture of confusion.
- When someone is disoriented by a flood of contradictory claims and wants to understand the design.
- When connecting media tactics to the politics of eternity and the war on Ukraine.

## Fails When
- **The question is about defending a specific record.** Route to the truth-as-political-act file for the practitioner's dilemma.
- **You treat it as ordinary media bias.** This is a deliberate strategy of unreality, qualitatively different from slant or partisanship.

## Core Concept
Snyder, especially in *The Road to Unfreedom* (2018), describes a distinctive contemporary form of authoritarian communication that originated in and was perfected by the Russian state, then exported. Its innovation is that it does not work like twentieth-century propaganda — the imposition of a single, coherent official line, repeated until believed. It works by the deliberate proliferation of confusion: flooding the information space with so many mutually contradictory claims that the citizen concludes nothing can be known, gives up the effort to distinguish true from false, and retreats into cynicism.

The canonical example is the destruction of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 over Ukraine in 2014. Russian state media did not offer one denial; it offered many, simultaneously and incompatibly: that the plane was shot down by a Ukrainian jet, that it was downed by a Ukrainian missile aimed at Putin's plane, that it was filled with corpses placed aboard in advance, that it was a CIA provocation, that nothing the West claimed could be trusted. The point of issuing contradictory stories is not to make any one of them believed. It is to produce the impression that the truth is unknowable, that "everyone has their version," that the difference between the investigator and the propagandist is merely which side you support. This is the technique sometimes called the "firehose of falsehood" — high volume, multiple channels, rapid, repetitive, and shameless about contradiction.

Why this is more corrosive than censorship: censorship restricts what information you can access, but it leaves intact your assumption that there is a truth being hidden. Managed unreality attacks the assumption itself. It disables the citizen's own faculty of judgement rather than merely limiting the inputs. A person who believes that facts are suppressed will look for them; a person who believes that facts do not exist will not. The aim is to manufacture a population that believes nothing — and a population that believes nothing can be governed by anyone, because it has no independent ground from which to judge power.

Snyder links this directly to the politics of eternity. A politics with no future, only a cycle of grievance and victimhood, has no use for verifiable history; it needs a usable, mythic past, not an accurate one. Managed unreality is the media form of eternity: it dissolves the shared factual present in which accountability could operate, leaving only competing myths among which the citizen chooses by tribal loyalty rather than evidence. The technique and the temporal politics reinforce each other.

Two further tactics complete the picture. **Whataboutism** — the deflection of any criticism by pointing to an alleged equivalent sin of the accuser — is designed not to win the moral argument but to establish that no one has standing to judge, that all are equally compromised, that moral and factual distinctions are naive. And the **cultivation of cynicism as sophistication** — the flattering suggestion that the truly knowing person sees through all claims equally, that believing in truth is for the gullible. This is perhaps the most insidious move, because it recruits the citizen's own sense of intelligence into the project of disabling their judgement.

Snyder's response is the opposite of cynicism. The defence is to insist that truth is knowable, that the investigation can be done, that the difference between the reporter and the propagandist is real and matters, and that the retreat into "everyone lies" is not wisdom but surrender. He pairs this with practical instruction: investigate, establish your own picture of the world from reliable long-form sources, support the institutions that produce verified facts, and refuse the manufactured symmetry that says all claims are equally suspect. The information war is won or lost in the citizen's willingness to keep believing that reality exists and can be known.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify the strategy of contradiction.** Multiple incompatible claims are a tell: the aim is unknowability, not persuasion.
2. **Distinguish it from censorship.** It attacks judgement itself, not just access to information.
3. **Name whataboutism and cynicism-as-sophistication** when they appear, and refuse them.
4. **Connect to eternity.** Managed unreality is the media form of a future-less, grievance-based politics.
5. **Respond with the affirmation that truth is knowable** and the practical defence of fact-producing institutions.

## Examples
**Situation:** Coverage of an atrocity dissolves into "claims and counter-claims," with state-aligned outlets issuing several incompatible explanations.
**Application:** The multiplicity is the tell. The goal is to make the event seem unknowable so that no judgement is possible. The correct response is to establish what the verified record actually shows and to name the contradiction-flooding as a deliberate technique, not a genuine epistemic stalemate.
**Result:** The manufactured fog is identified as a tactic, and the knowable record is restored.

**Situation:** A sophisticated colleague dismisses all reporting on a conflict as "propaganda on both sides."
**Application:** This is cynicism mistaking itself for sophistication — exactly the disposition the information war cultivates. Not all sources are equal; verification methods differ; some claims are documented and some are fabricated. The equal-suspicion stance is a surrender dressed as wisdom.
**Result:** The false sophistication is named, and the distinction between investigation and propaganda is reasserted.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat managed unreality as ordinary media bias.
**Why:** It is a deliberate strategy to disable judgement, not a slant to be balanced.

**Don't:** Accept "everyone lies" as a worldly, sophisticated stance.
**Why:** That cynicism is the precise outcome the technique is engineered to produce.

**Don't:** Try to refute each contradictory claim individually.
**Why:** The volume is the weapon; name the strategy and re-establish the verified record rather than chasing every falsehood.
