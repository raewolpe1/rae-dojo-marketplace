# Disciplinary Power

## When to Use
- When analysing how development institutions produce compliant, trained, and categorised subjects through specific techniques.
- When examining the micro-level practices — attendance registers, training schedules, assessment forms — through which programmes exercise power.
- When connecting Foucault's historical analysis of prisons, schools, and hospitals to contemporary development practice.

## Core Concept
Disciplinary power, as Foucault developed it in Discipline and Punish (1975), operates through three principal techniques: hierarchical observation (the capacity to see without being seen), normalising judgment (the measurement of individuals against a standard), and the examination (the combination of observation and normalisation into a ritual that fixes individuals in a field of documentation). Unlike sovereign power, which operates spectacularly through public punishment, disciplinary power operates quietly through routine, procedure, and administration. It does not repress — it trains. It does not prohibit — it normalises. It does not destroy — it produces.

For development practice, the relevance is immediate and concrete. The training workshop is a disciplinary technology — it arranges bodies in space, imposes a timetable, delivers standardised content, and assesses learning through pre- and post-tests. The monitoring visit is hierarchical observation — the programme officer inspects the field site, observes implementation, and produces a report that is visible to the hierarchy but not to the observed. The quarterly report is an examination — it combines observation (data collection) with normalising judgment (comparison to targets) in a documented ritual that fixes the programme in a field of accountability.

Foucault's crucial insight is that disciplinary power is productive, not merely constraining. The school does not merely confine children — it produces literate, numerate, socialised subjects. The hospital does not merely confine the sick — it produces patients who understand their condition in medical terms. The development programme does not merely provide resources — it produces subjects who can articulate their needs in the programme's categories, who conform to the programme's schedules, who demonstrate the programme's desired outcomes. This production is not inherently malicious. But it is always an exercise of power, and it always produces a specific kind of subject at the expense of other possible kinds.

## How to Apply
1. **Identify the disciplinary techniques.** "List every technique through which this programme trains, observes, and assesses its participants. Attendance registers? Training schedules? Pre/post tests? Monitoring visits? Each is a disciplinary technology."
2. **Trace hierarchical observation.** "Who observes whom? Who reports on whom? Who is visible to the hierarchy and who is not? The direction of visibility is the direction of power."
3. **Find the normalising judgment.** "Against what standard are participants being measured? What constitutes a 'good' beneficiary, a 'successful' graduate, an 'empowered' woman? The norm is where power hides."
4. **Examine the examination.** "Where does observation combine with normalisation in a documented ritual? The quarterly review, the mid-term evaluation, the beneficiary assessment — these are examinations in Foucault's precise sense."

## Examples
**Situation:** A youth employment programme requires participants to attend daily sessions, maintain a logbook of job-search activities, submit weekly reports to a mentor, and complete monthly assessments. Staff note high compliance but low employment outcomes.
**Application:** "This programme is a disciplinary apparatus in Foucault's precise sense. The daily attendance schedule organises bodies in space and time. The logbook is a technology of self-surveillance — the participant internalises the monitoring gaze. The weekly report to the mentor is hierarchical observation. The monthly assessment is the examination — observation combined with normalising judgment in a documented ritual. The programme produces a specific kind of subject: the disciplined job-seeker who documents their activities, reports to authority, and submits to regular assessment. The high compliance demonstrates that the disciplinary apparatus is effective — it is producing the subjects it is designed to produce. The low employment outcomes suggest that the labour market does not reward the kind of subject the programme produces. The programme may be training people to be compliant participants rather than effective employees. The discipline is working — but what it produces may not be what the labour market requires."

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Treat disciplinary power as inherently evil.
**Why:** Discipline produces capacity — literacy, numeracy, professional skills. The question is not whether to discipline but how, for whose benefit, and with what accountability. A training programme without any disciplinary dimension would teach nothing.

**Don't:** Apply the concept only to institutions that look like prisons.
**Why:** Disciplinary power operates in gentle institutions — the workshop, the support group, the mentoring relationship. The softness of the technique does not diminish the power it exercises.
