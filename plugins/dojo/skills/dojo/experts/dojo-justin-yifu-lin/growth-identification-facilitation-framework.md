# The Growth Identification and Facilitation Framework

> **PROVENANCE WARNING — READ.** The GIFF steps below are **reconstructed and marked `[CHECK]` throughout.** They are associated with **Lin & Monga, "Growth Identification and Facilitation: The Role of the State in the Dynamics of Structural Change", *Development Policy Review* (2011)**. **Verify every step against the paper before any use in a client deliverable.** The *logic* of the benchmark — similar endowment, roughly double the income, a generation ago — is well attested. The specific six-step enumeration is not verified here.

## When to Use
- **When priority sectors are being selected.** This is Lin's unique contribution to the collection — Rodrik and Chang have no equivalent procedure.
- When a sector list was produced by stakeholder consultation.
- When asked how to identify latent comparative advantage operationally.

## Fails When
- **The sectors are already fixed** and the question is how to support them.

## Core Concept

**The benchmark logic, which is the durable part:**

**Do not benchmark against the frontier.** A sector list produced by asking what should be grown will contain the industries wealthy countries currently occupy — semiconductors, advanced manufacturing, biotechnology. Those reflect *their* endowment structure, not yours. Targeting them is CAD by construction.

**Benchmark against your own future.** Identify countries with a **similar endowment structure** and roughly **double your income per capita**, a **generation ago**. What were they making? **Those industries are viable at the endowment structure you are heading towards.** They are within reach, and — the elegant part — **the countries that used to make them are being pushed out of them right now by their own rising wages.** The capital, equipment, buyers and know-how are looking for a destination.

**The steps `[CHECK] — VERIFY BEFORE USE`:**

1. `[CHECK]` Identify tradable goods and services produced roughly **20 years ago** in **fast-growing countries with a similar endowment structure and around double the income per capita**.
2. `[CHECK]` Among those industries, identify which **domestic private firms are already present**, and remove the constraints on their **upgrading and on new entry**.
3. `[CHECK]` For industries where **no domestic firms are present**, seek **FDI from the countries identified in step 1**, or organise **incubation programmes**.
4. `[CHECK]` Pay attention to **spontaneous self-discovery by private enterprises** — support the scaling up of successful private innovations in new industries that nobody identified.
5. `[CHECK]` In countries with **poor infrastructure and a bad business environment**, use **industrial parks or export processing zones** to overcome barriers to firm entry, FDI, and industrial clustering.
6. `[CHECK]` Provide **limited, time-bound incentives** to domestic pioneer firms and foreign investors in the identified industries, to compensate for the **non-rival information externality** they generate.

**Three features worth noting even if the enumeration proves inexact.**

**Step 4 is the humility clause.** The method identifies candidates; it does not claim to be complete. Where private firms discover something the framework missed, follow them. Any strategy using GIFF without step 4 has turned an identification aid into a plan.

**Step 5 is the second-best clause.** If you cannot fix the business environment nationally, fix one square kilometre of it. Lin recommends parks and SEZs without embarrassment, because the first-best is not on offer.

**Step 6 is the only subsidy in the framework, and it is tightly bounded.** It compensates a specific, identified externality — the pioneer's non-rival information — and it is **limited and time-bound by construction**. It is not protection. If it does not expire, the framework has been abandoned.

**The South African application, and its problem.** The GIFF benchmark asks for comparator countries with a similar endowment structure and roughly double the income per capita. **South Africa's endowment structure is unusual** — high unemployment alongside a sophisticated financial sector, world-class firms alongside a large informal economy, high inequality, and an industrial base built under conditions nobody would reproduce. **Finding the comparator set is genuinely difficult and it is where the method would be tested.** Do not assume the exercise is mechanical.

## How to Apply

1. **Verify the steps before using them.** They are `[CHECK]`. The paper is Lin & Monga (2011), *Development Policy Review*.
2. **Use the benchmark logic even if the steps prove inexact.** Similar endowment, double the income, a generation ago. That much is solid.
3. **Do the comparator work honestly.** For South Africa this is hard and it is the whole exercise.
4. **Split the identified industries by whether domestic firms are present** — the instruments differ completely (constraint removal vs FDI attraction).
5. **Keep step 4.** Follow spontaneous discovery. The method is an aid, not a plan.
6. **Time-bound step 6 or delete it.**

## Examples

**Situation:** A province has an eight-sector priority list produced through stakeholder consultation.

**Application:** That is an ambition list — consultation selects for what participants want to grow, which is what wealthy economies currently do. Run the benchmark against it. Identify comparator economies with a similar endowment structure at roughly double the income per capita a generation ago — **which for South Africa is the hard part and should not be waved through**. Ask what they were making. Then take each of the eight sectors and ask: does it appear in the benchmark set? For those that do, split by whether domestic firms are present — if yes, find and remove the constraint on their scaling; if no, go to the comparator countries and attract the firms being pushed out of that industry by their own rising wages. For those that do not appear anywhere in the benchmark set, the burden of proof is now on whoever put them on the list.

**Result:** A sector list with a derivation rather than a provenance.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Quote the six steps as verified.
**Why:** They are `[CHECK]`. Verify against Lin & Monga (2011) first.

**Don't:** Benchmark against the frontier.
**Why:** Those industries reflect someone else's endowment. That is the error the method exists to prevent.

**Don't:** Drop step 4.
**Why:** Without the self-discovery clause, an identification aid becomes a central plan, which is not what it is.
