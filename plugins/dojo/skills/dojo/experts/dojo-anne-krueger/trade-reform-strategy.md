# Trade Reform Strategy

## When to Use
- When advising a government on how to move from a protectionist trade regime toward greater openness.
- When designing the sequencing and pace of tariff reduction programmes.
- When assessing whether preconditions for successful liberalisation are in place.

## Fails When
- **You recommend trade liberalisation with an overvalued exchange rate.** This is Krueger's cardinal sequencing error. Liberalising imports while the exchange rate makes imports artificially cheap produces an import surge, destroys domestic production, generates unemployment, and discredits reform. The exchange rate must be competitive before tariffs come down.
- **You ignore the political economy of reform constituencies.** Reform succeeds when its beneficiaries — exporters, consumers, efficient producers — are mobilised politically to support the process. Reform fails when only the losers are organised.

## Core Concept
Krueger's approach to trade reform is defined by three sequencing principles and a pervasive concern with political sustainability. The principles are: stabilise before you liberalise; move the exchange rate before you move tariffs; and build reform coalitions before you announce reforms.

Macroeconomic stabilisation must precede trade liberalisation because fiscal indiscipline and inflation undermine the price signals that liberalisation is supposed to improve. If domestic prices are rising at 50 per cent per year, the incentive effects of a 20 per cent tariff reduction are swamped by macroeconomic noise. Equally, stabilisation that relies on an overvalued exchange rate as a nominal anchor will create the worst possible environment for trade reform.

Exchange rate adjustment must precede or accompany tariff reduction because the exchange rate is the most powerful relative price in the economy. A 30 per cent real overvaluation is equivalent to a 30 per cent tax on all exports and a 30 per cent subsidy on all imports — a distortion larger than any tariff schedule. Reducing tariffs while the exchange rate remains overvalued amplifies the anti-export bias rather than reducing it.

The pace of reform involves a trade-off between economic logic and political sustainability. Economic logic favours rapid, comprehensive reform: it changes many relative prices simultaneously, making it harder for any single group to organise opposition, and it delivers benefits faster. Political sustainability may require gradualism that builds evidence of success before threatening the most powerful protected interests. Krueger generally favours faster reform because gradual reform gives rent-seekers time to organise resistance.

## How to Apply
1. **Assess macroeconomic stability first.** Fiscal balance, inflation trajectory, and exchange rate competitiveness must be adequate before trade reform proceeds.
2. **Design tariff reduction as a pre-announced, binding schedule.** Credible pre-announcement allows firms to adjust; binding schedules (reinforced by WTO commitments or trade agreements) reduce the risk of reversal.
3. **Identify and mobilise reform beneficiaries.** Exporters, import-using industries, and consumers benefit from liberalisation but are often dispersed and unorganised.
4. **Use external commitments as anchors.** WTO accession, trade agreements, and IMF conditionality serve as commitment devices that raise the cost of reform reversal.

## Examples
**Situation:** Mozambique is considering a phased reduction of its tariff structure as part of SADC trade protocol implementation and AfCFTA commitments, while facing fiscal pressure and a depreciating metical.
**Application:** Krueger's sequencing framework applies directly. First question: is the macroeconomic environment stable enough? Mozambique's fiscal position is strained by hidden debt revelations and natural gas revenue uncertainty. The metical has depreciated significantly, which — unusually — may actually help: a weaker currency makes the tariff reduction less disruptive than it would be with an overvalued currency. Krueger would recommend: use the AfCFTA commitment as a binding external anchor for tariff reduction; design the reduction schedule to front-load cuts on intermediate inputs that raise costs for domestic producers and exporters; establish a transparent, rules-based schedule rather than sector-by-sector negotiations that create rent-seeking opportunities; and invest the administrative capacity saved from managing complex tariff exemptions into trade facilitation measures (customs efficiency, border infrastructure, standards compliance) that benefit all firms. The critical error to avoid is negotiating sector-specific exemptions from AfCFTA commitments — each exemption creates a rent and a constituency that will resist further liberalisation.

## Anti-Patterns
**Don't:** Assume that trade reform alone produces growth.
**Why:** Trade reform changes relative prices and reduces rent-seeking opportunities, but growth requires complementary investments in infrastructure, human capital, and institutional capacity. Krueger's framework includes these complementary conditions.

**Don't:** Recommend shock therapy in every context.
**Why:** While Krueger generally favours rapid reform, she acknowledges that the pace must account for institutional capacity, the strength of opposition, and the availability of external support. The critical requirement is credibility — a gradual programme that is credible may outperform a rapid programme that triggers reversal.
