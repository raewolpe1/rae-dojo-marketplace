# The Informal Economy as an Innovation Site

## When to Use
- **Load first on any Kraemer-Mbula question.** It is the foundation.
- When innovation and informality appear in the same strategy without connecting.
- When the informal economy is characterised purely as deficit.

## Fails When
- **The question is rights, protection, or organising.** Route to Chen. Different question, equally real.

## Core Concept

**The informal economy is a site of innovation. The measurement apparatus records it as an absence. These are not the same fact.**

The reference work is **Kraemer-Mbula, E. & Wunsch-Vincent, S. (eds), *The Informal Economy in Developing Nations: Hidden Engine of Innovation?* (Cambridge University Press, 2016)** — note the question mark in the title, and note that the book answers it.

**What informal innovation looks like concretely.** From her own chapter on **informal manufacturing of home and personal care products in South Africa** — soaps, creams, cleaning products:

- **Reformulation** in response to customer feedback — the product is too harsh, does not lather, does not keep.
- **Input substitution** as supply and price move — each substitution a technical problem solved without a laboratory.
- **Process redesign** around whatever equipment can be obtained, which is never the equipment the process was designed for.
- **Packaging development** for a market that buys in quantities and at moments no formal retailer's data would predict.

**Apply the symmetry test:** if a formal firm did these things, would we count them as product and process innovation? Yes, without hesitation. The activity is identical. The only difference is registration and the existence of a countable artefact.

**Why the apparatus is blind.** It was built to detect **codified, protectable, registered** activity: R&D spend, patents, publications, formal collaboration. Informal innovation is tacit, unprotected and unregistered by definition. **The zero is a property of the instrument.** Policy reads it as a property of the sector.

**The structural relationship to the other dojos.** This is Lundvall's DUI mode, in an informal setting — tacit knowledge, know-how and know-who, generated through doing, using and interacting. The connection is not analogical: **Edward Lorenz co-authored both the founding DUI/STI paper and Kraemer-Mbula's African MSE paper.** It is the same framework, carried deliberately to this population.

**The distinction from Chen, which must be held.** Chen: most informal workers are **not entrepreneurs** — they are own-account operators, outworkers, casual wage workers — and the policy question is protection and rights. Kraemer-Mbula: the informal economy **innovates**, and the innovation system cannot see it. **Both are true.** They are answers to different questions — who these people are and what they are owed, versus what they are doing. A strategy that collapses them gets both wrong.

## How to Apply

1. **Check whether the innovation chapter and the informal chapter reference each other.** If not, an assumption has been encoded. Name it.
2. **Apply the symmetry test.** Would this count if a formal firm did it?
3. **Go and look, or commission someone to.** The question is empirical and cheap to answer.
4. **Read the informal chapter's register.** If it is pure deficit, the strategy cannot see what is there to build on.
5. **Keep Chen's question separate.** Rights and innovation are both real and they are different.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy's Groundbreakers chapter describes the segment as survivalist, low-productivity and lacking capability, and its innovation chapter addresses universities, IP and technology transfer.

**Application:** The two chapters encode an assumption between them — that innovation is what formal firms do and informality is where innovation is not. Test it: take twenty informal manufacturers in the province and ask what they changed last year and why. The answer will contain reformulations, substitutions and process redesigns, none of which any indicator in the strategy would register. The deficit register is not wrong about incomes; it is wrong about activity, and because it is wrong about activity the strategy has nothing to build on and no instrument that touches the segment.

**Result:** A Groundbreakers chapter that describes what these firms do as well as what they lack.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat the informal economy as an innovation void.
**Why:** It innovates. The void is in the instrument.

**Don't:** Collapse this with Chen's argument.
**Why:** Most informal workers are not entrepreneurs (Chen) *and* the informal economy innovates (Kraemer-Mbula). Different questions.

**Don't:** Write the informal chapter in pure deficit register.
**Why:** You lose the ability to see the local knowledge the positive programme is built on.
