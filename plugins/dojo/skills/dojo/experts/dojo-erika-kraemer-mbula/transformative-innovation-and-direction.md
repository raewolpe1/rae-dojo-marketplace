# Transformative Innovation and the Question of Direction

## When to Use
- When a 4IR, emerging technology, or green innovation chapter is being designed.
- When innovation is treated as a quantity rather than a direction.
- When asking what the innovation is for.

## Fails When
- **The question is mission-oriented innovation at national scale.** Route to Mazzucato — closely related, and she owns the missions framing. Kraemer-Mbula's angle is the African and grassroots one.

## Core Concept

**Her chair is in *Transformative Innovation*, the Fourth Industrial Revolution and Sustainable Development** — held with **ACTS** (Nairobi) and **SPRU** (Sussex). The word "transformative" is doing specific work.

**The three frames, roughly.** Innovation policy has historically asked *how much* innovation (R&D subsidy, market failure correction), then *how well connected* (systems, ecosystems, linkages). **Transformative innovation policy asks a third question: innovation toward what?** It holds that innovation has a **direction**, that the direction is a **choice**, and that leaving it unchosen means it defaults — usually to whatever the frontier is doing and whoever can pay for it.

**Why this matters more, not less, in an African context.** Her positive programme: *putting innovation at the centre of a developmental agenda that will guide systemic change **in the desired direction***. And: *this is particularly important in current times, when our environmental and social sustainability are at stake.* The direction question is not a luxury for rich economies that have solved the quantity problem. Where resources are scarcest, spending them in an undirected way is least affordable.

**The 4IR trap, which is the live one.** An emerging-technology chapter that contains only frontier technologies is a **technology import plan**, not an innovation strategy. The programme is **blending frontier technological advances with local knowledge** — both halves. A chapter with the frontier and no local knowledge has decided, without saying so, that what is already here is nothing. That is the decontextualisation critique arriving in a new costume.

**Her own institutional work in this space `[CHECK]`:** landscape analysis and mapping of **emerging technology ecosystems in South Africa** under the UJ Chair — identifying enabling factors, risks and governance opportunities, and recommending **technology-specific roadmaps**; work on **frontier technology adoption in developing countries** with UNCTAD and CSIR-STEPRI. `[CHECK]` — titles, dates and findings unverified.

**The direction question, made concrete for a provincial strategy.** If innovation is to be directed, someone must say toward what. Candidate directions a Western Cape strategy could actually name: employment intensity rather than labour displacement; water and energy resilience; inclusion of township producers in value chains; or nothing, in which case the direction is set by whoever brings capital. **Naming it is a political act, which is why the technology-transfer framing is popular — it lets you avoid naming it.**

**The honest tension with the rest of this dojo.** Transformative innovation policy is normatively ambitious and empirically thin — it is a framework for choosing direction, not evidence that directed innovation policy works. McKenzie would ask what has been tested. Very little has. Hold both: the direction question is real and the instruments are unproven.

## How to Apply

1. **Ask what the innovation is for.** If nobody has said, the direction has defaulted.
2. **Check the frontier chapter for local knowledge.** If absent, it is an import plan.
3. **Name candidate directions explicitly** and make the choice visible rather than implicit.
4. **Treat direction as political, and say so.** The technocratic framing avoids the choice by not naming it.
5. **Do not claim the instruments are proven.** They are not. This is a framework for choosing, not evidence of efficacy.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy has a 4IR chapter covering AI, IoT and advanced manufacturing, aimed at raising provincial technology adoption.

**Application:** Two questions. First, **where is the local knowledge?** The programme is blending frontier advances with what is already here; a chapter containing only the frontier has assumed the local base is empty, which is the decontextualisation error in a new form. What do provincial firms already know how to do that these technologies could extend? Second, **toward what?** Adoption is a quantity, not a direction. AI adoption that displaces labour and AI adoption that lets a township manufacturer reach a formal buyer are both "adoption" and they are opposite policies. The strategy has not chosen, so the direction will be set by whoever brings the capital. **Name the direction, in the chapter, in a sentence.**

**Result:** A technology chapter with a stated direction and a local base.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Treat innovation as a directionless good.
**Why:** It has a direction. Unchosen means defaulted, usually to the frontier and to capital.

**Don't:** Write a frontier chapter with no local knowledge.
**Why:** That is a technology import plan. The programme is blending.

**Don't:** Present transformative innovation policy as proven.
**Why:** It is normatively serious and empirically thin. Route the efficacy question to McKenzie and expect a short answer.
