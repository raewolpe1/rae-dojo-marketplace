# Measuring Informal Innovation — Why the Instruments Cannot See It

> **Provenance.** The **why** below is Kraemer-Mbula's position, drawn from her published work. **The instrument itself is in `evidence-base-cestii-measurement-programme.md`**, sourced to Mustapha, Petersen, Jegede, Bortagaray & Kruss (2022) — **not her paper**, but the programme her critique implies, from the institution she advises. **This file previously carried a survey question of my own invention. It has been removed and replaced by the real one, which is in the evidence file.**

## When to Use
- When indicators show near-zero innovation among MSMEs.
- When a measurement claim about informality is in dispute.
- **For the diagnosis. For the instrument, go to the evidence file.**

## Fails When
- **The population is formal and research-capable.** Standard instruments fit.

## Core Concept

**The test: if informal innovation in this economy doubled next year, would any number in your framework move?** If the answer is no — and it is — the framework cannot detect the phenomenon, and its zero is an artefact.

**Why standard instruments cannot be patched — and it is not subtle.** **All national business innovation surveys implemented in Africa and elsewhere are restricted to the formal sector, targeting businesses with at least 10 employees.** The instrument is *defined* to exclude the population. This is not a coverage gap to be closed at the margin; the Groundbreakers segment sits below the threshold of every innovation survey ever run in this country.

**And the knowledge is the wrong kind for the apparatus anyway.** Standard indicators detect **codified, protectable, registered** activity: R&D spend, patents, publications, formal collaboration. Informal innovation is **tacit, unprotected, unregistered**. A better patent indicator does not help; there is nothing to patent and no one to file.

**The causal chain, which is the argument.** Measurement is not a technical annexe to policy — it determines what policy can see, and therefore what policy does:

> **"What gets measured, gets done"** — and the inverse, which is the part nobody says: **"what does not get measured, does not get done."**
> **"Policies tend to speak and respond to those phenomena that have been subjected to measurement programmes."**

From which: if the measurement programmes installed in African countries follow those designed for developed nations, **the industrial policies will follow those of developed nations too.** The imported indicator set imports the policy. That is the decontextualisation critique with its mechanism exposed, and it is why she does indicator work as well as fieldwork.

**The South African specifics you need.**
- **SESE** — the Survey of Employers and the Self-Employed — is South Africa's informal-sector instrument, modelled on the **1–2–3 survey methodology**, the ILO gold standard. **Phase one is the QLFS**, which identifies self-employed persons; **phase two is SESE**, a survey of those informal businesses. The 2017 SESE defines the informal sector as businesses **not registered for VAT, but possibly registered for income tax**.
- **Your QLFS baseline of 387,367 Western Cape enterprise operators is phase one of exactly this architecture.** SESE is phase two and it already exists.
- **Nobody has attached an innovation module to it** — and the CeSTII authors argue that a module alone would be insufficient, because "the nature of the informal sector displays such richness that it cannot possibly be sufficient merely to add a few questions on innovation to a SESE."

**What the field has and has not established.** Kraemer-Mbula and Konté (2016), analysing STI policy across African countries, **show minimal support for informal sector actors**. Petersen and Kruss (2019) reach the same conclusion for South Africa. The empirical base is thin and case-based: sectoral studies in Senegal (Konté & Ndong 2012), clustering work (Jegede et al. 2017), the three-sector case studies and surveys in **Kraemer-Mbula and Konté (2016)**, and econometric work in Kenya (Mendi & Mudida 2018) and Ghana (Fu, Mohnen & Zanello 2018). **The shortcoming is a tendency to case studies and specific sectors; a measurement programme should be based on representative data across all industrial sectors.**

**The honest limits.** Self-reported change is noisy. Recall is imperfect. "Innovation" means something different to a respondent than to a statistician — which is why the real instrument never uses the word. The construct is not internationally comparable and will not be soon. **All true — and a noisy measure of the right phenomenon beats a precise measure of the wrong one.**

## How to Apply

1. **Run the doubling test.** Report the result; it is the finding.
2. **Cite the ten-employee threshold.** It explains the zero better than any argument about tacit knowledge.
3. **Never use the word "innovation" in an instrument aimed at informal firms.**
4. **Note that QLFS → SESE is already phase 1 → phase 2.** The gap is narrower than it looks and wider than a module.
5. **Go to `evidence-base-cestii-measurement-programme.md` for the actual instrument** — and for the fact that its foundational method has already run in five Cape Town townships.
6. **Verify every magnitude to source.** This field is young and its numbers move.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy needs a baseline for innovation among township enterprises and finds no data.

**Application:** There is no data because no instrument has been pointed at them — every national innovation survey targets firms with at least ten employees, by design. That is the finding, and it is more useful than the absence. The instrument that would produce a baseline is specified, peer-reviewed and South African (see the evidence file), and its area-based stage has already been run in five Cape Town localities. Expect, when it runs, a distribution of idea-sources dominated by customers and suppliers rather than by any institution in your innovation chapter — **which is itself the policy finding**, because it relocates the innovation instrument into market access and procurement.

**Result:** A baseline that exists, and a finding about where to intervene.

## Anti-Patterns

**Don't:** Report zero innovation because your instrument found none.
**Why:** The instrument excludes firms under ten employees by design. It did not look.

**Don't:** Assume an innovation module bolted onto SESE would do it.
**Why:** The CeSTII authors considered exactly that and rejected it as insufficient. Read their reasoning before repeating the idea.

**Don't:** Quote an informal-innovation magnitude without verifying it.
**Why:** The field is young and contested. This remains the highest-risk citation area in the roster.
