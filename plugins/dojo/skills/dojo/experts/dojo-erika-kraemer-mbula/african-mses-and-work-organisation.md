## Core Concept

**The paper is titled as a question and the question is the point:**

> **Kraemer-Mbula, E., Lorenz, E., Takala-Greenish, L., Jegede, O. O., Garba, T., Mutambala, M. & Esemu, T. (2019), "Are African micro- and small enterprises misunderstood? Unpacking the relationship between work organisation, capability development and innovation", *International Journal of Technological Learning, Innovation and Development* 11(1): 1–30.**

**Note the co-author.** **Edward Lorenz** co-authored the founding **DUI/STI paper** (Jensen, Johnson, Lorenz & Lundvall, *Research Policy*, 2007). This is not an analogy or a borrowing — **it is the same framework, carried deliberately by one of its own authors to an African micro-enterprise population.** For your Groundbreakers segment this is the single most relevant paper in the literature, and its title asks exactly the question your strategy should be asking.

**The framing, which is what this file can offer with confidence.** The three variables are **work organisation**, **capability development**, and **innovation** — and the claim implicit in the title is that the relationship between them in African MSEs is **misunderstood**, i.e. that the received account gets it wrong.

**Why the received account would get it wrong.** Apply the decontextualisation critique. The received account of firm innovation assumes: a firm with functional differentiation; a management layer; an R&D function or at least a technical one; formal training pipelines; and codifiable capability. An African micro-enterprise has, typically, none of these. So the received account predicts no innovation, and its prediction is confirmed by instruments that can only see the things it assumes.

**The variable that carries the weight: work organisation.** This is Lundvall's insight in a harder setting. Whether a micro-enterprise can innovate at all depends on whether the way work is arranged permits problem-solving, whether the person doing the job may change the job, whether information moves between the people who notice and the people who decide. In an owner-does-everything firm — the modal shape — the answer is no, and **that is an organisational fact rather than a skills deficit.**

**The consequence for your strategy.** It means capability support aimed at the **owner's skills** may be aimed at the wrong variable. If the constraint is that nobody but the owner can alter anything, then training the owner more thoroughly does not create distributed problem-solving; it deepens the bottleneck. This aligns with Bloom's delegation finding from the opposite methodological direction and with Schoar's absorption point.

## How to Apply

1. **Obtain and read the 2019 paper.** It is the bridge and this file is a placeholder for it.
2. **Ask about work organisation before skills.** Who may change something? That is the DUI ceiling.
3. **Distinguish organisational constraint from skills deficit.** They call for different instruments.
4. **Use the Lorenz link explicitly** when connecting Lundvall's framework to your segment. It is a genuine intellectual lineage, not a stretch.
5. **Do not assume the received account.** The paper's title says it is a misunderstanding; find out what the misunderstanding is.

## Examples

**Situation:** A strategy proposes owner-focused business skills training to raise micro-enterprise capability and innovation.

**Application:** The intervention targets the owner's knowledge. The literature this paper sits in suggests the binding variable is **work organisation** — whether anyone other than the owner can identify a problem and act on it. In an owner-does-everything firm, more owner knowledge does not produce more distributed problem-solving; it may entrench the bottleneck, since the better-trained owner has more reason to decide everything personally. The redesign question is whether the intervention can create even a minimal second locus of decision — one person who may change one thing. **Read the 2019 paper before designing this**; it is directly on the point and its findings are not summarised here.

**Result:** A capability intervention aimed at the organisational variable rather than only at the owner's head.
